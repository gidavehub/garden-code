BIOLOGY (Questions 1 - 25)

1. The specific site for the synthesis of ribosomal RNA (rRNA) within a eukaryotic cell is the...
A) Ribosome
B) Golgi apparatus
C) Nucleolus
D) Rough Endoplasmic Reticulum

Answer: C) Nucleolus
Explanation: The nucleolus, a dense structure within the nucleus, is the primary site of ribosome biogenesis, which includes the synthesis and processing of rRNA. While ribosomes are made of rRNA and protein, they are assembled in the nucleolus. The Rough ER has ribosomes on it for protein synthesis, but it does not synthesize the rRNA itself.

2. In a dihybrid cross between two heterozygous individuals (e.g., TtYy), what is the expected phenotypic ratio of offspring showing both dominant traits?
A) 3/16
B) 1/16
C) 9/16
D) 6/16

Answer: C) 9/16
Explanation: A classic dihybrid cross (TtYy x TtYy) results in a 9:3:3:1 phenotypic ratio.

9/16 show both dominant traits (e.g., Tall and Yellow).

3/16 show one dominant and one recessive trait (e.g., Tall and green).

3/16 show the other dominant and recessive trait (e.g., short and Yellow).

1/16 show both recessive traits (e.g., short and green).

3. The reason a pyramid of energy is always upright and can never be inverted is that...
A) The biomass of producers is always greater than consumers.
B) Organisms at lower trophic levels are smaller in size.
C) There is a progressive loss of energy at each successive trophic level.
D) Decomposers recycle energy back to the producers.

Answer: C) There is a progressive loss of energy at each successive trophic level.
Explanation: The Second Law of Thermodynamics dictates that energy transformations are inefficient. At each trophic level, a significant amount of energy (around 90%) is lost as heat during metabolic processes. Therefore, the energy available to the next level is always less than the level below it, resulting in an upright pyramid shape. A pyramid of biomass can occasionally be inverted (e.g., in aquatic ecosystems), but a pyramid of energy cannot.

4. The ascent of water up the xylem column in a tall tree is primarily explained by the...
A) Root pressure theory
B) Capillarity action theory
C) Cohesion-tension theory
D) Imbibition theory

Answer: C) Cohesion-tension theory
Explanation: The cohesion-tension theory is the most widely accepted model. Tension (negative pressure) is created by transpiration (evaporation of water from leaves). This pull is transmitted down the xylem because of cohesion (water molecules sticking to each other) and adhesion (water molecules sticking to the xylem walls). Root pressure and capillarity contribute but are insufficient to explain water transport in tall trees.

5. Which of the following is the primary function of the Loop of Henle in the mammalian nephron?
A) Filtration of blood to form the glomerular filtrate.
B) Secretion of urea and other waste products into the tubule.
C) Reabsorption of glucose and amino acids.
D) Creation of a salt concentration gradient in the medulla.

Answer: D) Creation of a salt concentration gradient in the medulla.
Explanation: The Loop of Henle acts as a counter-current multiplier. The descending limb is permeable to water, and the ascending limb actively pumps out salts (NaCl). This creates a hypertonic (high salt concentration) environment in the medulla, which is crucial for the reabsorption of water from the collecting duct, thereby concentrating the urine.

6. In the life cycle of the malaria parasite, the mosquito is best described as the...
A) Pathogen
B) Definitive host and vector
C) Intermediate host
D) Reservoir

Answer: B) Definitive host and vector
Explanation: The mosquito is the vector because it transmits the parasite. It is also the definitive host because the sexual reproductive stage (sporogony) of the Plasmodium parasite occurs within the mosquito. Humans are the intermediate host where asexual reproduction (schizogony) occurs. The pathogen is the Plasmodium parasite itself.

7. Carbon fixation during photosynthesis occurs in the...
A) Thylakoid membrane
B) Stroma of the chloroplast
C) Grana
D) Cytoplasm of the leaf cell

Answer: B) Stroma of the chloroplast
Explanation: Carbon fixation is the key step of the light-independent reactions (Calvin Cycle). This entire cycle takes place in the stroma, the fluid-filled space within the chloroplast. The light-dependent reactions, which produce ATP and NADPH, occur in the thylakoid membranes (which are stacked into grana).

8. The net gain of ATP molecules from the complete aerobic respiration of one molecule of glucose is approximately...
A) 2
B) 4
C) 8
D) 38

Answer: D) 38
Explanation: While the exact number can vary slightly depending on the shuttle system used, the theoretical maximum yield is 38 ATP molecules per glucose. This includes ATP from glycolysis (net 2), the Krebs cycle (2), and oxidative phosphorylation (approx. 34). Anaerobic respiration (glycolysis alone) yields only a net of 2 ATP.

9. The rapid, saltatory conduction of a nerve impulse is made possible by the...
A) Presence of neurotransmitters at the synapse
B) High concentration of sodium ions outside the axon
C) Nodes of Ranvier and myelin sheath
D) Diameter of the axon

Answer: C) Nodes of Ranvier and myelin sheath
Explanation: The myelin sheath acts as an electrical insulator. The action potential "jumps" from one unmyelinated gap (Node of Ranvier) to the next. This process, called saltatory conduction, is much faster than the continuous propagation of the impulse along an unmyelinated axon.

10. Which of the following best describes a K-strategist species?
A) Short lifespan, many offspring, little parental care
B) Long lifespan, few offspring, significant parental care
C) Pioneer species in ecological succession
D) High population growth rate, small body size

Answer: B) Long lifespan, few offspring, significant parental care
Explanation: K-strategists (like elephants, humans) are adapted to stable environments. They have populations that hover around the carrying capacity (K). Their strategy involves investing heavily in a few offspring to ensure their survival. Options A and D describe r-strategists (like bacteria, insects).

Explanation: Down's Syndrome (Trisomy 21) is caused by the presence of a full or partial extra copy of chromosome 21. This is typically due to non-disjunction, the failure of homologous chromosomes or sister chromatids to separate properly during meiosis.

12. The conversion of atmospheric nitrogen gas (N₂) into ammonia (NH₃) by soil bacteria is known as...
A) Nitrification
B) Denitrification
C) Nitrogen Fixation
D) Ammonification

Answer: C) Nitrogen Fixation
Explanation: Nitrogen fixation is the process of converting inert atmospheric nitrogen into a biologically usable form like ammonia. This is done by nitrogen-fixing bacteria (e.g., Rhizobium). Nitrification is the conversion of ammonia to nitrates, and denitrification is the conversion of nitrates back to nitrogen gas.

13. In the mammalian heart, the structure that prevents the backflow of blood from the right ventricle to the right atrium is the...
A) Bicuspid valve
B) Tricuspid valve
C) Aortic semilunar valve
D) Pulmonary semilunar valve

Answer: B) Tricuspid valve
Explanation: The tricuspid valve is located between the right atrium and the right ventricle. It closes during ventricular contraction to prevent blood from flowing back into the right atrium. The bicuspid (or mitral) valve does the same job on the left side of the heart.

14. Which of these is a characteristic feature of wind-pollinated flowers?
A) Brightly coloured petals
B) Production of nectar
C) Large, feathery stigmas
D) Heavy, sticky pollen grains

Answer: C) Large, feathery stigmas
Explanation: Wind-pollinated flowers do not need to attract insects, so they lack bright petals and nectar. Instead, they are adapted to catch pollen from the air. This includes having large, feathery stigmas to increase the surface area for trapping light, airborne pollen grains.

15. The role of bile in digestion is...
A) To digest fats into fatty acids and glycerol.
B) To provide an acidic medium for pepsin.
C) To emulsify fats, increasing the surface area for lipase action.
D) To stimulate the pancreas to release enzymes.

Answer: C) To emulsify fats, increasing the surface area for lipase action.
Explanation: Bile, produced by the liver and stored in the gallbladder, does not contain enzymes. Its function is physical, not chemical. It breaks down large fat globules into smaller droplets (emulsification), which dramatically increases the surface area available for the enzyme lipase to chemically digest the fats.

16. Homologous structures, such as the forelimbs of a human, a bat, and a whale, are evidence for...
A) Convergent evolution
B) Analogous function
C) Divergent evolution
D) Vestigial features

Answer: C) Divergent evolution
Explanation: Homologous structures have a similar underlying anatomy due to a shared common ancestor but have been modified to perform different functions in different species. This pattern, where a common ancestral feature diversifies, is known as divergent evolution.

17. Double fertilization in angiosperms results in the formation of...
A) Two embryos
B) A diploid zygote and a triploid endosperm
C) A diploid zygote and a diploid endosperm
D) An embryo and a seed coat

Answer: B) A diploid zygote and a triploid endosperm
Explanation: In double fertilization, one male gamete (sperm nucleus) fuses with the egg cell to form the diploid (2n) zygote. The second male gamete fuses with the two polar nuclei in the central cell to form the triploid (3n) primary endosperm nucleus, which develops into the nutritive endosperm tissue.

18. The part of the brain responsible for coordinating voluntary muscle movements, posture, and balance is the...
A) Cerebrum

Answer: B) Cerebellum
Explanation: The cerebellum, located at the back of the brain, is the primary center for motor control. It does not initiate movement (that's the cerebrum) but it refines, coordinates, and ensures the smooth, balanced execution of voluntary movements.

22. Which hormone is primarily responsible for stimulating ovulation and the formation of the corpus luteum?
A) Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH)
B) Luteinizing Hormone (LH)

23. Which of the following statements correctly distinguishes mitosis from meiosis?
A) Mitosis produces four haploid cells, while meiosis produces two diploid cells.
B) Homologous chromosomes pair up (synapsis) in mitosis but not in meiosis.
C) Crossing over occurs in meiosis I, leading to genetic variation, but not in mitosis.
D) Mitosis is involved in the production of gametes, while meiosis is for growth and repair.

Answer: C) Crossing over occurs in meiosis I, leading to genetic variation, but not in mitosis.
Explanation: Crossing over, the exchange of genetic material between homologous chromosomes, is a hallmark of Prophase I of meiosis and is a major source of genetic recombination. This does not happen in mitosis. Mitosis produces two identical diploid cells for growth and repair, while meiosis produces four genetically unique haploid cells (gametes).

24. The closing of stomata in a plant is actively triggered by...
A) High turgor pressure in guard cells
B) The hormone abscisic acid (ABA) under water stress
C) An increase in light intensity
D) A high concentration of potassium ions in the guard cells

Answer: B) The hormone abscisic acid (ABA) under water stress
Explanation: When a plant is under water stress (drought), it produces abscisic acid. ABA signals the guard cells to lose potassium ions (K+). Water then flows out of the guard cells via osmosis, causing them to become flaccid and close the stomatal pore to conserve water. Options A, C, and D are associated with the opening of stomata.

25. A logistic growth curve (S-shaped curve) differs from an exponential growth curve (J-shaped curve) because it...
A) only applies to r-strategists
B) does not have a lag phase
C) accounts for environmental resistance and carrying capacity
D) represents a population that will never go extinct

Answer: C) accounts for environmental resistance and carrying capacity
Explanation: An exponential (J-shaped) curve shows population growth in an idealized, unlimited environment. A logistic (S-shaped) curve is more realistic, showing how population growth slows down as it approaches the carrying capacity (K) of the environment due to limiting factors like resource scarcity and increased competition (environmental resistance).

26. What volume of 0.5 M H₂SO₄ is required to completely neutralize 25 cm³ of 0.8 M NaOH solution?
A) 10 cm³
B) 20 cm³
C) 25 cm³
D) 40 cm³

Answer: B) 20 cm³
Explanation: First, write the balanced equation: H₂SO₄ + 2NaOH → Na₂SO₄ + 2H₂O. The mole ratio of acid to base is 1:2.
Use the formula CₐVₐ / CᵦVᵦ = nₐ / nᵦ
(0.5 * Vₐ) / (0.8 * 25) = 1 / 2
0.5 * Vₐ = (0.8 * 25) / 2
0.5 * Vₐ = 10
Vₐ = 10 / 0.5 = 20 cm³.

27. In the redox reaction: MnO₄⁻ + 8H⁺ + 5Fe²⁺ → Mn²⁺ + 5Fe³⁺ + 4H₂O, the oxidation number of manganese changes from...
A) +7 to +2
B) +4 to +2
C) +8 to +2
D) -1 to +2

Answer: A) +7 to +2
Explanation: In MnO₄⁻, oxygen has an oxidation state of -2. Let the oxidation state of Mn be x.
x + 4(-2) = -1 (the overall charge of the ion)
x - 8 = -1
x = +7
On the product side, the oxidation state of Mn in Mn²⁺ is +2. Therefore, the change is from +7 to +2 (a reduction).

29. Consider the equilibrium: 2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g); ΔH = -197 kJ/mol. Which of the following conditions will favour the forward reaction?
A) High temperature and low pressure
B) Low temperature and high pressure
C) High temperature and high pressure
D) Low temperature and low pressure

Answer: B) Low temperature and high pressure
Explanation: According to Le Chatelier's Principle:

Temperature: The forward reaction is exothermic (ΔH is negative). To favour an exothermic reaction, the temperature should be lowered.

Pressure: There are 3 moles of gas on the left (2 SO₂ + 1 O₂) and 2 moles on the right (2 SO₃). To favour the side with fewer moles of gas, the pressure should be increased.
Therefore, low temperature and high pressure favour the production of SO₃.

30. The pH of a 0.01 M solution of a weak monoprotic acid with an acid dissociation constant (Ka) of 1.0 x 10⁻⁶ is...
A) 2
B) 3
C) 4
D) 6

Answer: C) 4
Explanation: For a weak acid, [H⁺] = √(Ka * C), where C is the concentration.
[H⁺] = √(1.0 x 10⁻⁶ * 0.01)
[H⁺] = √(1.0 x 10⁻⁸)
[H⁺] = 1.0 x 10⁻⁴ M
pH = -log[H⁺] = -log(1.0 x 10⁻⁴) = 4.

35. The shape of the ammonia (NH₃) molecule is...
A) Linear
B) Trigonal planar
C) Tetrahedral
D) Trigonal pyramidal

Answer: D) Trigonal pyramidal
Explanation: In NH₃, the central nitrogen atom has three bonding pairs (with H atoms) and one lone pair of electrons. According to VSEPR theory, four electron pairs arrange themselves tetrahedrally. 36. A radioactive isotope has a half-life of 20 days. What fraction of the original sample will remain after 60 days?
A) 1/3
B) 1/4
C) 1/6
D) 1/8

Answer: D) 1/8
Explanation: Number of half-lives = Total time / Half-life = 60 days / 20 days = 3 half-lives.
After 1 half-life: 1/2 remains.
After 2 half-lives: (1/2) * (1/2) = 1/4 remains.
After 3 half-lives: (1/4) * (1/2) = 1/8 remains.
Alternatively, Fraction remaining = (1/2)ⁿ, where n is the number of half-lives. Fraction = (1/2)³ = 1/8.

45. Hess's Law states that the enthalpy change of a reaction...

50. What is the empirical formula of a compound containing 40.0% Carbon, 6.7% Hydrogen, and 53.3% Oxygen? (C=12, H=1, O=16)
A) C₂H₄O₂
B) CH₂O
C) C₂H₃O
D) CHO

Answer: B) CH₂O
Explanation:

Assume a 100g sample: 40.0g C, 6.7g H, 53.3g O.

Convert mass to moles:

Moles C = 40.0g / 12.0 g/mol = 3.33 mol

Moles H = 6.7g / 1.0 g/mol = 6.7 mol

Moles O = 53.3g / 16.0 g/mol = 3.33 mol

Divide by the smallest number of moles (3.33):

C: 3.33 / 3.33 = 1

H: 6.7 / 3.33 ≈ 2

O: 3.33 / 3.33 = 1

The simplest whole-number ratio is 1:2:1. The empirical formula is CH₂O.

PHYSICS (Questions 51 - 75)

51. A body is projected vertically upwards with a velocity of 40 m/s. What is the maximum height reached? (g = 10 m/s²)
A) 40 m
B) 80 m
C) 120 m
D) 160 m

Answer: B) 80 m
Explanation: At the maximum height, the final velocity (v) is 0. Use the equation of motion: v² = u² + 2as.
Here, u = 40 m/s, v = 0, a = -g = -10 m/s² (negative as it opposes motion), and s is the height (h).
0² = 40² + 2(-10)h
0 = 1600 - 20h
20h = 1600

51. A projectile is launched at an angle of 30° to the horizontal with an initial velocity of 100 m/s. What is the time taken to reach its maximum height? [g = 10 m/s²]
A) 2.5 s
B) 5.0 s
C) 8.7 s
D) 10.0 s

Answer: B) 5.0 s
Explanation: The time to reach maximum height (t) is given by the formula t = (u sinθ) / g, where u is the initial velocity, θ is the launch angle, and g is the acceleration due to gravity.
t = (100 * sin 30°) / 10
t = (100 * 0.5) / 10
t = 50 / 10 = 5.0 s.

52. Three resistors of 2Ω, 3Ω, and 6Ω are connected in parallel. What is the equivalent resistance of the combination?
A) 11Ω
B) 1Ω
C) 0.91Ω
D) 6Ω

Answer: B) 1Ω
Explanation: For resistors in parallel, the reciprocal of the equivalent resistance (Rp) is the sum of the reciprocals of the individual resistances.
1/Rp = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + 1/R₃
1/Rp = 1/2 + 1/3 + 1/6
Find a common denominator (6):
1/Rp = 3/6 + 2/6 + 1/6 = 6/6
1/Rp = 1
Rp = 1Ω.

53. A radioactive substance has a half-life of 20 days. What fraction of the original substance will remain after 80 days?
A) 1/4
B) 1/8
C) 1/16
D) 1/32

Answer: C) 1/16
Explanation:

Find the number of half-lives (n): n = Total time / Half-life = 80 days / 20 days = 4.

The fraction remaining after n half-lives is (1/2)ⁿ.

Fraction remaining = (1/2)⁴ = 1 / (222*2) = 1/16.

54. An object of mass 5 kg moves with a velocity of 10 m/s. It collides with a stationary object of mass 10 kg and they stick together. What is their common velocity after the collision?
A) 3.33 m/s
B) 5.00 m/s
C) 6.67 m/s
D) 10.0 m/s

Answer: A) 3.33 m/s
Explanation: This is a perfectly inelastic collision, and momentum is conserved.
Initial momentum (pᵢ) = m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = (5 kg * 10 m/s) + (10 kg * 0 m/s) = 50 kg m/s.
Final momentum (pƒ) = (m₁ + m₂)v, where v is the common final velocity.
pƒ = (5 kg + 10 kg)v = 15v.
By conservation of momentum, pᵢ = pƒ:
50 = 15v
v = 50 / 15 = 10 / 3 ≈ 3.33 m/s.

A) 168 kJ
B) 210 kJ
C) 84 kJ
D) 42 kJ

Answer: A) 168 kJ
Explanation: Use the formula Q = mcΔT, where Q is heat energy, m is mass, c is specific heat capacity, and ΔT is the change in temperature.

Calculate temperature change: ΔT = 100°C - 20°C = 80°C.

Calculate Q: Q = (0.5 kg) * (4200 J/kg°C) * (80°C) = 2100 * 80 = 168,000 J.

Convert to kJ: 168,000 J = 168 kJ.

A blood smear from a patient reveals sickle-shaped red blood cells. An individual with this trait who is heterozygous (AS) has a survival advantage in regions where malaria is endemic. This phenomenon is an example of:
A) Speciation
B) Natural selection
C) Artificial selection
D) Aseptic reproduction

Answer: B

Explanation: This is a classic example of natural selection. The malaria parasite has difficulty developing in sickle-shaped cells. Heterozygous individuals (AS) have enough normal cells to avoid severe sickle-cell anemia but enough sickle cells to resist malaria, giving them a survival advantage (heterozygote advantage) over homozygous individuals (AA and SS) in malarial zones.

If the xylem of a young plant is carefully removed in a ring around the stem, the plant will eventually die primarily due to:
A) Inability to transport manufactured food downwards.
B) Inability to transport water and minerals upwards.
C) Lack of mechanical support causing the stem to break.
D) The entry of pathogens into the exposed tissue.

Answer: B

Explanation: The xylem is the primary water-conducting tissue, transporting water and dissolved mineral salts from the roots to the rest of the plant. Removing the xylem ring cuts off this supply, leading to wilting and death from dehydration. Transporting food downwards is the function of the phloem.

Explanation: The reaction force (R) must not only support the man's weight (mg) but also provide the net force (ma) to accelerate him upwards.
Net Force = Resultant Force = R - mg.
ma = R - mg
R = mg + ma = m(g + a)
R = 80 kg × (10 m/s² + 2 m/s²) = 80 × 12 = 960 N.

A mirage is an optical phenomenon caused by:
A) Reflection of light by layers of water in the sky.
B) The constant refractive index of air.
C) The refraction of light through air layers of different densities.
D) The diffraction of light around dust particles.

Answer: C

Explanation: A mirage occurs when the ground is very hot, creating layers of air with different temperatures and therefore different densities and refractive indices. Light from the sky is bent (refracted) upwards as it passes through these layers, creating an inverted image that looks like a reflection in water.

Which of the following is NOT a vector quantity?
A) Velocity
B) Electric field strength
C) Energy
D) Momentum

Answer: C

Explanation: A vector quantity has both magnitude and direction (e.g., velocity, force, momentum). A scalar quantity has only magnitude. Energy (like work and power) is a scalar quantity; it does not have a direction associated with it.

The process of thermionic emission is the:
A) Emission of electrons from a metal surface when heated.
B) Emission of light from a substance when heated.
C) Flow of charge through a semiconductor.
D) Emission of electrons from a surface when light shines on it.

Answer: A

Explanation: Thermionic emission is the release of electrons from a heated surface (the cathode in a vacuum tube or cathode-ray tube). The thermal energy gives the electrons enough kinetic energy to overcome the work function of the metal.

An object weighs 50 N in air and 35 N when fully immersed in water. The upthrust on the object is:
A) 15 N
B) 35 N
C) 50 N
D) 85 N

Answer: A

Explanation: According to Archimedes' principle, the upthrust is equal to the loss in weight of the object when immersed in the fluid.
Upthrust = Weight in air - Apparent weight in water = 50 N - 35 N = 15 N.

The main purpose of a fuse in an electrical circuit is to:
A) Increase the voltage of the circuit.
B) Decrease the resistance of the circuit.
C) Protect the appliance from excessive current.
D) Convert alternating current to direct current.

Answer: C

Explanation: A fuse contains a thin wire designed to melt and break the circuit ("blow") if the current exceeds a safe level. This protects the appliance from damage and prevents potential fires caused by overheating.

A body moves with a constant velocity of 10 m/s for 5 seconds. Its acceleration is:
A) 0 m/s²
B) 2 m/s²
C) 10 m/s²
D) 50 m/s²

Answer: A

Explanation: Acceleration is defined as the rate of change of velocity. If the velocity is constant, there is no change in velocity, and therefore the acceleration is zero.

Heat is transferred through a vacuum primarily by:
A) Conduction
B) Convection
C) Radiation
D) Evaporation

Answer: C

Explanation: Conduction and convection both require a medium (particles) to transfer heat. Since a vacuum is empty space, heat can only travel through it via electromagnetic waves, which is the process of radiation. This is how the Sun's heat reaches Earth.

The refractive index of glass is 1.5. The speed of light in glass is: (Speed of light in vacuum c = 3.0 x 10⁸ m/s)
A) 1.5 x 10⁸ m/s
B) 2.0 x 10⁸ m/s
C) 3.0 x 10⁸ m/s
D) 4.5 x 10⁸ m/s

Answer: B

Explanation: Refractive index (n) = Speed of light in vacuum (c) / Speed of light in medium (v).
n = c / v => v = c / n
v = (3.0 x 10⁸ m/s) / 1.5 = 2.0 x 10⁸ m/s.

A car's engine does 60,000 J of work in 30 seconds. The power developed by the engine is:
A) 0.5 W
B) 2 W
C) 2,000 W
D) 1,800,000 W

Answer: C

Explanation: Power is the rate at which work is done.
Power = Work done / Time taken
Power = 60,000 J / 30 s = 2,000 W (or 2 kW).

The nature of the image formed by a plane mirror is always:
A) Real, inverted, and same size.
B) Virtual, erect, and magnified.
C) Virtual, laterally inverted, and same size.
D) Real, erect, and diminished.

Answer: C

Explanation: A plane mirror always produces an image that is virtual (cannot be caught on a screen), erect (upright), the same size as the object, and laterally inverted (left and right are swapped).
