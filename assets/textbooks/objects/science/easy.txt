B. Second-class lever
C. Third-class lever
D. Pulley system
Answer: B
Explanation: In a second-class lever, the load is located between the fulcrum (the wheel's axle) and the effort (the handles).

The freezing point of water on the Kelvin scale is
A. 0 K
B. 100 K
C. 273 K
D. 373 K
Answer: C
Explanation: The freezing point of water is 0°C. To convert Celsius to Kelvin, you add 273.15 (or approximately 273). So, 0°C = 273 K.

The rate of flow of electric charge is called
A. Voltage
B. Resistance
C. Power
D. Current
Answer: D
Explanation: Electric current is defined as the rate at which electric charge flows past a point in a circuit.

A short-sighted person needs which type of lens to correct their vision?
A. Concave lens
B. Convex lens
C. Cylindrical lens
D. Bifocal lens
Answer: A
Explanation: Short-sightedness (myopia) occurs when the eye focuses images in front of the retina. A diverging (concave) lens is used to spread the light rays out so they focus correctly on the retina.

The pitch of a sound is determined by its
A. Amplitude
B. Speed
C. Frequency
D. Wavelength
Answer: C
Explanation: Pitch is the quality that makes it possible to judge sounds as "higher" and "lower". It is directly related to the frequency of the sound wave. High frequency = high pitch.

The law of universal gravitation was formulated by
A. Galileo Galilei
B. Isaac Newton
C. Albert Einstein
D. Michael Faraday
Answer: B
Explanation: Sir Isaac Newton formulated the law of universal gravitation, which states that every particle attracts every other particle in the universe with a force that is directly proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between their centers.

The SI unit of pressure is the
A. Newton (N)
B. Joule (J)
C. Watt (W)
D. Pascal (Pa)
Answer: D
Explanation: Pressure is defined as force per unit area. The SI unit is the Pascal (Pa), which is equal to one Newton per square meter (N/m²).

An alpha particle is identical to the nucleus of a
A. Hydrogen atom
B. Helium atom
C. Lithium atom
D. Carbon atom
Answer: B
Explanation: An alpha particle consists of two protons and two neutrons bound together, which is the same composition as a helium-4 nucleus.

An electric bell works on the principle of
A. Electromagnetism
B. The motor effect
C. Static electricity
D. Chemical effect of current
Answer: A
Explanation: An electric bell uses an electromagnet that, when energized, attracts a metal arm. This movement breaks the circuit, the electromagnet turns off, and the arm springs back, completing the circuit again. This rapid on-off cycle causes the ringing.

What is the mechanical advantage of a machine if an effort of 20 N is used to lift a load of 100 N?
A. 0.2
B. 5
C. 80
D. 120
Answer: B
Explanation: Mechanical Advantage (MA) = Load / Effort. MA = 100 N / 20 N = 5.

The phenomenon of light bouncing off a surface is called
A. Refraction
B. Reflection
C. Diffraction
D. Absorption
Answer: B
Explanation: Reflection is the change in direction of a wavefront at an interface between two different media so that the wavefront returns into the medium from which it originated.

A clinical thermometer is designed to measure temperatures in the range of
A. 0°C to 100°C
B. -10°C to 110°C
C. 35°C to 42°C
D. 90°C to 110°C
Answer: C
Explanation: A clinical thermometer is used to measure human body temperature, which normally fluctuates within a narrow range. The typical range is from 35°C to 42°C (or 95°F to 108°F).

What happens to the boiling point of water when the external pressure is increased?
A. It increases
B. It decreases
C. It remains the same
D. It becomes equal to the freezing point
Answer: A
Explanation: The boiling point is the temperature at which the vapor pressure of a liquid equals the external pressure. If the external pressure is higher, the liquid needs to be heated to a higher temperature to make its vapor pressure equal to the external pressure.

In a series circuit, the total resistance is the
A. Product of the individual resistances
B. Sum of the individual resistances
C. Reciprocal of the sum of the reciprocals
D. Average of the individual resistances
Answer: B
Explanation: For resistors connected in series, the total equivalent resistance (R_total) is found by simply adding the individual resistances: R_total = R₁ + R₂ + R₃ + ...

Which of these is a property of a permanent magnet?
A. It is easily magnetized and demagnetized
B. It is made of soft iron
C. It retains its magnetism for a long time
D. Its polarity can be easily reversed
Answer: C
Explanation: A permanent magnet is made from a hard magnetic material (like steel or alnico) that retains its magnetic properties even after the external magnetizing field is removed.

If an object is at rest, the net force acting on it is
A. Increasing
B. Decreasing
C. Zero
D. Equal to its weight
Answer: C
Explanation: According to Newton's First Law of Motion, an object at rest will stay at rest unless acted upon by a net external force. This means all the forces acting on it are balanced, and the net force is zero.

The quantity that has the same unit as work is
A. Power
B. Force
C. Energy
D. Momentum
Answer: C
Explanation: Both work and energy are measured in Joules (J). Work is the transfer of energy, so they share the same unit.

A convex mirror is also known as a
A. Converging mirror
B. Diverging mirror
C. Plane mirror
D. Focusing mirror
Answer: B
Explanation: A convex mirror reflects parallel rays of light outward as if they were diverging from a focal point behind the mirror. This is why it is called a diverging mirror.

Latent heat of vaporization is the energy required to change
A. Solid to liquid at constant temperature
B. Liquid to gas at constant temperature
C. Liquid to solid at constant temperature
D. Gas to liquid at constant temperature
Answer: B
Explanation: Latent heat of vaporization is the heat absorbed per unit mass of a substance to change its state from a liquid to a gas (vapor) without any change in temperature.

The process of charging a conductor by bringing it near another charged object without touching it is called
A. Conduction
B. Friction
C. Induction
D. Polarization
Answer: C
Explanation: Electrostatic induction is a method used to create or generate a static electric charge in a material by bringing an electrically charged object near it.

The number of vibrations per second is known as
A. Period
B. Amplitude
C. Wavelength
D. Frequency
Answer: D
Explanation: Frequency is defined as the number of occurrences of a repeating event (like a vibration or oscillation) per unit of time. Its unit is Hertz (Hz).

Which of these is not part of the electromagnetic spectrum?
A. X-rays
B. Radio waves
C. Sound waves
D. Gamma rays
Answer: C
Explanation: Sound waves are mechanical waves, meaning they require a medium to travel and are caused by vibrations. The others are all types of electromagnetic radiation.

The upward movement of water in a narrow tube is an example of
A. Surface tension
B. Viscosity
C. Capillarity
D. Diffusion
Answer: C
Explanation: Capillarity or capillary action is the ability of a liquid to flow in narrow spaces without the assistance of, or even in opposition to, external forces like gravity. It is a result of cohesion and adhesion.

An object of mass 10kg is raised to a height of 2m. The potential energy gained is (g=10 m/s²)
A. 20 J
B. 50 J
C. 100 J
D. 200 J
Answer: D
Explanation: Gravitational Potential Energy (PE) = mgh. PE = 10 kg × 10 m/s² × 2 m = 200 J.

The part of a camera that corresponds to the retina of the human eye is the
A. Lens
B. Diaphragm
C. Shutter
D. Film or Sensor
Answer: D
Explanation: The film (in an old camera) or the digital sensor (in a modern camera) is the light-sensitive surface where the image is formed, which is analogous to the function of the retina in the eye.

The north pole of a magnet will repel
A. The south pole of another magnet
B. An unmagnetized iron bar
C. The north pole of another magnet
D. A piece of copper
Answer: C
Explanation: The fundamental law of magnetism states that like poles repel each other (North repels North, South repels South), and opposite poles attract each other (North attracts South).

The SI unit for momentum is
A. Ns
B. kg m/s
C. N/s
D. kg/m
Answer: B
Explanation: Momentum (p) is the product of mass (m) and velocity (v). So its unit is the unit of mass (kg) times the unit of velocity (m/s), which gives kg m/s. Ns (Newton-second) is an equivalent unit.

The transfer of heat in a vacuum can only occur by
A. Conduction
B. Convection
C. Radiation
D. It cannot occur
Answer: C
Explanation: Radiation is the transfer of energy via electromagnetic waves, which do not require a medium and can therefore travel through the vacuum of space.

An ammeter is always connected in
A. Series in a circuit
B. Parallel in a circuit
C. Series or parallel
D. A separate circuit
Answer: A
Explanation: An ammeter measures current, which is the flow of charge. To measure the flow, the ammeter must be placed in series so that all the current being measured flows through it.

A step-down transformer
A. Increases voltage and decreases current
B. Decreases voltage and increases current
C. Increases both voltage and current
D. Decreases both voltage and current
Answer: B
Explanation: A step-down transformer has more turns in the primary coil than the secondary coil. This results in a lower output voltage but a higher output current (to conserve power).

Which of these simple machines has an efficiency of 100% in theory?
A. A rough inclined plane
B. A frictionless pulley
C. A real-life lever
D. A screw jack
Answer: B
Explanation: In an ideal, theoretical scenario where there is no friction, a simple machine like a pulley would have 100% efficiency. In reality, friction always causes some energy loss.

The sun's energy is produced by
A. Nuclear fission
B. Nuclear fusion
C. Chemical combustion
D. Geothermal activity
Answer: B
Explanation: The Sun generates energy through nuclear fusion in its core, where hydrogen nuclei fuse together to form helium, releasing a tremendous amount of energy in the process.

A voltmeter is always connected in
A. Series in a circuit
B. Parallel across a component
C. A separate circuit
D. Series with the power source
Answer: B
Explanation: A voltmeter measures the potential difference (voltage) between two points. To do this, it must be connected in parallel with the component across which the voltage is being measured.

The bending of waves around an obstacle is called
A. Reflection
B. Refraction
C. Diffraction
D. Interference
Answer: C
Explanation: Diffraction is the spreading of waves as they pass through an aperture or around an obstacle. It is most pronounced when the size of the obstacle is comparable to the wavelength of the wave.

Newton's third law of motion is often called the law of
A. Inertia
B. Action and reaction
C. Acceleration
D. Gravitation
Answer: B
Explanation: Newton's third law states that for every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction.

The hydraulic press works on the principle formulated by
A. Archimedes
B. Newton
C. Pascal
D. Bernoulli
Answer: C
Explanation: A hydraulic press uses Pascal's principle, which states that a pressure change at any point in a confined incompressible fluid is transmitted throughout the fluid such that the same change occurs everywhere.

The force per unit area is known as
A. Work
B. Power
C. Pressure
D. Stress
Answer: C
Explanation: Pressure is defined as the force applied perpendicular to the surface of an object per unit area over which that force is distributed.

The color of light with the longest wavelength is
A. Violet
B. Blue
C. Green
D. Red
Answer: D
Explanation: In the visible light spectrum, red light has the longest wavelength and lowest frequency, while violet light has the shortest wavelength and highest frequency.

A half-life in radioactivity is the time taken for
A. All the atoms to decay
B. The activity to become zero
C. Half the atoms in a sample to decay
D. The substance to become stable
Answer: C
Explanation: The half-life is the characteristic time it takes for half of the unstable nuclei in a radioactive sample to undergo radioactive decay.

The speed of sound is greatest in
A. Solids
B. Liquids
C. Gases
D. A vacuum
Answer: A
Explanation: Sound travels fastest in solids, slower in liquids, and slowest in gases because it is a mechanical wave that requires a medium. The closer the particles are, the faster the vibration can be transmitted. It cannot travel in a vacuum.

If a car covers 120 km in 2 hours, its average speed is
A. 30 km/h
B. 60 km/h
C. 120 km/h
D. 240 km/h
Answer: B
Explanation: Average Speed = Total Distance / Total Time. Speed = 120 km / 2 h = 60 km/h.

An object that produces its own light is described as
A. Luminous
B. Non-luminous
C. Transparent
D. Opaque
Answer: A
Explanation: A luminous object is one that generates and emits its own light (e.g., the Sun, a light bulb). A non-luminous object reflects light from other sources.

The magnification of an object by a lens is calculated as
A. Image distance / Object distance
B. Object distance / Image distance
C. Image height + Object height
D. Focal length / Image distance
Answer: A
Explanation: Linear magnification (m) is the ratio of the height of the image to the height of the object, which is also equal to the ratio of the image distance (v) to the object distance (u). m = v/u.

The tendency of a liquid to reduce its surface area to a minimum is called
A. Viscosity
B. Capillarity
C. Surface tension
D. Adhesion
Answer: C
Explanation: Surface tension is a property of the surface of a liquid that allows it to resist an external force, due to the cohesive nature of its molecules. It causes the liquid to behave as if its surface were covered with a stretched elastic membrane.

The three ways heat can be transferred are
A. Conduction, convection, and friction
B. Conduction, radiation, and reaction
C. Convection, radiation, and expansion
D. Conduction, convection, and radiation
Answer: D
Explanation: These are the three distinct mechanisms by which thermal energy can be transferred from one place to another.

Which of the following is a scalar quantity?
A. Displacement
B. Force
C. Work
D. Acceleration
Answer: C
Explanation: Work is a scalar quantity. It has magnitude (measured in Joules) but no direction. Displacement, force, and acceleration are all vector quantities.

A. Focal point
B. Center of curvature
