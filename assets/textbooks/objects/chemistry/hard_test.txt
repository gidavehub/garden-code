Chapter 1: Fundamentals of Atomic Structure

An electron in a hypothetical one-electron atom transitions from a state where its orbital angular momentum quantum number l=3 to a state where n=4. What can be definitively concluded about this transition?
A) The transition is impossible because it violates selection rules.
B) The electron emitted a photon corresponding to the f-subshell energy.
C) The electron's principal quantum number must have been at least 4 initially.
D) The magnetic quantum number m_l must have changed by ±1.

Answer: C
Explanation: The orbital angular momentum quantum number, l, can have integer values from 0 up to n-1. For an electron to exist in a state with l=3 (an f-subshell), the principal quantum number n for that state must be at least l+1, which is 3+1=4. Therefore, the initial state of the electron must have had n ≥ 4. Options A and D are incorrect as selection rules (Δl = ±1, Δm_l = 0, ±1) apply specifically to single-photon absorption/emission events, which is not the only way a transition can be described. Option B is incorrect as we don't know the final l value or whether a photon was emitted or absorbed.

A species is represented by the nuclide symbol ⁵⁶₂₆X²⁺. It is isoelectronic with a neutral atom of which element?
A) Chromium (Cr, Z=24)
B) Iron (Fe, Z=26)
C) Manganese (Mn, Z=25)
D) Cobalt (Co, Z=27)

Answer: A
Explanation: The species ⁵⁶₂₆X²⁺ has an atomic number (Z) of 26, meaning it has 26 protons. The 2+ charge indicates that the neutral atom has lost 2 electrons. Therefore, the number of electrons in the ion is 26 - 2 = 24. "Isoelectronic" means having the same number of electrons. A neutral atom has a number of electrons equal to its atomic number. We are looking for a neutral atom with 24 electrons, which is Chromium (Cr), with Z=24.

Consider the ground-state electron configuration of Gadolinium (Gd, Z=64): [Xe] 4f⁷ 5d¹ 6s². Why does one electron occupy the 5d orbital instead of completing the 4f subshell to 4f⁸?
A) This configuration maximizes the total number of electron pairs.
B) The 5d orbital is lower in energy than the 4f orbital for Gadolinium.
C) This configuration results in a half-filled 4f subshell, which confers extra stability, and the next electron enters the next available orbital (5d).
D) It is an exception required to explain Gadolinium's diamagnetism.

Answer: C
Explanation: The stability associated with half-filled (or fully-filled) subshells is a key principle in electron configurations. A half-filled f-subshell (4f⁷) is particularly stable because of exchange energy (electrons with parallel spins can exchange positions, a stabilizing quantum mechanical effect). By placing one electron in the 5d orbital, Gadolinium achieves a stable [Xe] 4f⁷ configuration rather than the less stable 4f⁸ configuration. This small energy investment to promote one electron is more than compensated for by the stability gained.

Which set of quantum numbers (n, l, m_l, m_s) could describe the last electron added to form a gaseous Gallium (Ga, Z=31) atom in its ground state?
A) (3, 2, -1, +1/2)
B) (4, 0, 0, -1/2)
C) (4, 1, -1, +1/2)
D) (3, 1, 0, -1/2)

Answer: C
Explanation: The ground-state electron configuration for Gallium (Ga, Z=31) is [Ar] 4s² 3d¹⁰ 4p¹. The last electron is added to the 4p subshell. For this electron:

The principal quantum number n must be 4.

The subshell is p, so the orbital angular momentum quantum number l must be 1.

For l=1, the magnetic quantum number m_l can be -1, 0, or +1.

The spin quantum number m_s can be +1/2 or -1/2.
Option C, (4, 1, -1, +1/2), is the only set that correctly describes an electron in the 4p subshell.

Chapter 2: The Periodic Table and Periodicity

Consider the following species: O²⁻, F⁻, Na⁺, Mg²⁺. Which graph correctly depicts the trend in their ionic radii?
A) A linearly increasing graph from O²⁻ to Mg²⁺.
B) A graph peaking at F⁻ and then decreasing.
C) A continuously decreasing graph from O²⁻ to Mg²⁺.
D) A U-shaped graph with the minimum at Na⁺.

Answer: C
Explanation: This is an isoelectronic series; all four ions have 10 electrons ([Ne] configuration). In an isoelectronic series, all ions have the same number of electrons and electron shells, so the factor determining radius is the nuclear charge (number of protons). As the nuclear charge increases, the electrostatic attraction on the same number of electrons increases, pulling the electron cloud closer to the nucleus and decreasing the ionic radius. The number of protons are O(8), F(9), Na(11), Mg(12). Therefore, the ionic radius decreases continuously from O²⁻ > F⁻ > Na⁺ > Mg²⁺.

The first ionization energy of Nitrogen (N) is higher than that of Oxygen (O), and the first ionization energy of Beryllium (Be) is higher than that of Boron (B). Which statement provides the best unifying explanation for both of these observations?
A) Both Nitrogen and Beryllium have smaller atomic radii than the element that follows them.
B) Electrons being removed from fully or half-filled subshells experience enhanced stability, requiring more energy.
C) Increased electron-electron repulsion in the p-orbitals of Oxygen and Boron makes their electrons easier to remove.
D) The effective nuclear charge (Z_eff) unexpectedly decreases from N to O and from Be to B.

Answer: B
Explanation: Both anomalies are explained by electron configuration stability. Nitrogen (1s²2s²2p³) has a stable, half-filled p-subshell. Oxygen (1s²2s²2p⁴) must have one electron removed from a paired p-orbital, which is slightly less stable due to electron-electron repulsion, making it easier to remove than from N. Similarly, Beryllium (1s²2s²) has a stable, fully-filled s-subshell. Boron (1s²2s²2p¹) has its outermost electron in a p-orbital, which is higher in energy and more shielded than the 2s-orbital, making it easier to remove than from Be. The common principle is the enhanced stability of filled or half-filled subshells.

Element X is in Period 4 and Group 16. Element Y is in Period 5 and Group 1. Predict the formula of the compound formed between them and the primary type of bond.
A) XY₂, ionic bond
B) Y₂X, ionic bond
C) YX₆, polar covalent bond
D) XY, metallic bond

Answer: B

Chapter 3: Ionic and Covalent Bonding

Which of the following compounds has the highest magnitude of lattice energy?
A) LiF
B) MgO
C) KI
D) CaS

Answer: B
Explanation: Lattice energy is primarily governed by Coulomb's Law, which states that force (and energy) is proportional to (q₁q₂)/r, where q₁ and q₂ are the charges of the ions and r is the distance between them.

Charges: MgO and CaS have ions with +2 and -2 charges, making the charge product (q₁q₂) = 4. LiF and KI have ions with +1 and -1 charges (product = 1). Therefore, MgO and CaS will have much higher lattice energies than LiF and KI.

Size (r): Between MgO and CaS, Mg²⁺ and O²⁻ are smaller ions (Periods 2) than Ca²⁺ and S²⁻ (Periods 3 & 4). A smaller inter-ionic distance (r) leads to a stronger attraction and higher lattice energy. Thus, MgO has the highest lattice energy.

Which of the following molecules violates the octet rule and is best described as having an expanded octet?
A) BF₃
B) NO₂
C) ClF₃
D) C₂H₂

Answer: C
Explanation: An expanded octet occurs when a central atom from Period 3 or below accommodates more than eight valence electrons. In ClF₃, the central Chlorine atom (Period 3) has 7 valence electrons. It forms 3 single bonds with Fluorine atoms (using 3 electrons). This leaves 4 electrons (7-3) as 2 lone pairs. The total number of electron domains around Chlorine is 3 bonding pairs + 2 lone pairs = 5 domains, which corresponds to 10 valence electrons. This is an expanded octet. BF₃ is electron-deficient (incomplete octet). NO₂ is an odd-electron molecule. C₂H₂ follows the octet rule.

Considering the electronegativity values H(2.2), C(2.55), N(3.04), Cl(3.16), which of the following bonds is the most polar?
A) C-H
B) N-H
C) C-Cl
D) N-Cl

Answer: B
Explanation: The polarity of a bond is determined by the magnitude of the difference in electronegativity (ΔEN) between the two atoms.

ΔEN (C-H) = 2.55 - 2.20 = 0.35

ΔEN (N-H) = 3.04 - 2.20 = 0.84

ΔEN (C-Cl) = 3.16 - 2.55 = 0.61

ΔEN (N-Cl) = 3.16 - 3.04 = 0.12
The N-H bond has the largest electronegativity difference, making it the most polar bond.

What is the formal charge on the central Xenon atom in the most plausible Lewis structure for xenon trioxide (XeO₃)?
A) 0
B) +1
C) +2
D) +3

Answer: A
Explanation: While several resonance structures can be drawn for XeO₃, the most plausible or stable one is generally considered the one that minimizes formal charges, even if it requires expanding the central atom's octet. Xenon has 8 valence electrons. To minimize formal charges, Xe forms a double bond with each of the three Oxygen atoms. In this structure, each Oxygen has 2 lone pairs and a double bond, giving it a formal charge of 6 - 4 - (1/2)*4 = 0. The central Xenon atom forms 3 double bonds (using 6 electrons) and has one lone pair (2 electrons). Its formal charge is: FC(Xe) = (Valence e⁻) - (Lone Pair e⁻) - ½(Bonding e⁻) = 8 - 2 - ½(12) = 0. This structure is preferred over ones with single bonds that would create large positive formal charges on Xe.

Chapter 4: Molecular Geometry and VSEPR Theory

The molecule BrF₅ has a square pyramidal molecular geometry. What is its electron domain geometry and the approximate F-Br-F bond angle between an axial and an equatorial fluorine?
A) Electron Geometry: Trigonal Bipyramidal; Bond Angle: 90°
B) Electron Geometry: Octahedral; Bond Angle: 90°
C) Electron Geometry: Square Pyramidal; Bond Angle: <90°
D) Electron Geometry: Octahedral; Bond Angle: <90°

Answer: D
Explanation: Bromine (Group 17) has 7 valence electrons. In BrF₅, it forms 5 bonds with Fluorine, leaving (7-5)=2 electrons as one lone pair. Total electron domains = 5 bonding pairs + 1 lone pair = 6 domains. Six electron domains arrange themselves in an octahedral electron geometry. The molecular shape for AX₅E₁ is square pyramidal. The lone pair occupies one of the axial positions, exerting more repulsion than bonding pairs. This increased repulsion pushes the four equatorial fluorine atoms slightly up and away from the lone pair, compressing the bond angle between the axial fluorine and the equatorial fluorines to be slightly less than 90°.

Which of the following species is planar (all atoms lie in the same plane)?
A) NH₃
B) CH₄
C) SF₄
D) XeF₄

Answer: D
Explanation: VSEPR theory is used to predict the 3D shape.

NH₃: 4 electron domains (3 bonding, 1 lone pair) → Trigonal pyramidal (non-planar).

CH₄: 4 electron domains (4 bonding, 0 lone pairs) → Tetrahedral (non-planar).

SF₄: 5 electron domains (4 bonding, 1 lone pair) → See-saw (non-planar).

XeF₄: 6 electron domains (4 bonding, 2 lone pairs) → The two lone pairs occupy the axial positions of the octahedron to minimize repulsion, forcing the four Fluorine atoms into the equatorial plane with the Xenon atom. This results in a square planar molecular geometry, which is planar.

What type of hybridization is exhibited by the central carbon atom in a molecule of hydrogen cyanide (HCN)?
A) sp
B) sp²
C) sp³
D) dsp³

Answer: A
Explanation: To determine hybridization, count the number of electron domains (sigma bonds + lone pairs) around the central atom. In HCN (H-C≡N), the central carbon atom forms a single bond with hydrogen and a triple bond with nitrogen. A triple bond counts as a single electron domain. Therefore, the carbon atom has two electron domains. Two electron domains correspond to a linear geometry and sp hybridization.

Arrange the following substances in order of increasing boiling point: CH₄, NH₃, H₂O, HF.
A) CH₄ < NH₃ < HF < H₂O
B) CH₄ < HF < NH₃ < H₂O
C) NH₃ < HF < H₂O < CH₄
D) CH₄ < NH₃ < H₂O < HF

Answer: A
Explanation: Boiling point is determined by the strength of intermolecular forces (IMFs).

CH₄: Nonpolar, has only weak London Dispersion Forces (LDFs). Lowest boiling point.

NH₃, H₂O, HF: All polar and capable of hydrogen bonding.

H₂O: Can form up to four hydrogen bonds per molecule on average, creating an extensive network, giving it an anomalously high boiling point. It has the highest BP.

HF: H-F bond is the most polar, so individual H-bonds are very strong, but each HF molecule can only act as one H-bond donor and effectively one acceptor, leading to zig-zag chains.

NH₃: H-bonds are weaker than in HF and H₂O because the N-H bond is less polar.
Comparing the three, the extensive network in water makes its overall IMFs strongest. Between HF and NH₃, the stronger polarity of the H-F bond outweighs the fact that NH₃ might form more bonds in some circumstances, giving HF a higher boiling point than NH₃. Thus, the order is CH₄ < NH₃ < HF < H₂O.

Chapter 5: The Mole Concept and Stoichiometry

A compound is found to be composed of 26.56% Potassium (K), 35.41% Chromium (Cr), and the remainder is Oxygen (O). Its molar mass is 294.2 g/mol . What is its molecular formula? (Molar masses: K=39.1, Cr=52.0, O=16.0)
A) KCrO₄
B) K₂CrO₄
C) K₃Cr₂O₇
D) K₂Cr₂O₇

Answer: D
Explanation:

Find Percent O: %O = 100% - 26.56% - 35.41% = 38.03%

Assume 100g sample and find moles:

moles K = 26.56 g / 39.1 g/mol = 0.679 mol

moles Cr = 35.41 g / 52.0 g/mol = 0.681 mol

moles O = 38.03 g / 16.0 g/mol = 2.377 mol

Find the simplest mole ratio (divide by smallest):

K: 0.679 / 0.679 = 1

Cr: 0.681 / 0.679 ≈ 1

O: 2.377 / 0.679 ≈ 3.5

Convert to whole number ratio (multiply by 2): K=2, Cr=2, O=7. The empirical formula is K₂Cr₂O₇.

Find the empirical formula mass: 2(39.1) + 2(52.0) + 7(16.0) = 78.2 + 104.0 + 112.0 = 294.2 g/mol .

Compare to molar mass: The empirical formula mass (294.2 g/mol ) is equal to the given molar mass. Therefore, the molecular formula is the same as the empirical formula: K₂Cr₂O₇.

10.0 g of a hydrocarbon undergoes complete combustion to produce 28.9 g of CO₂ and 14.8 g of H₂O. What is the empirical formula of the hydrocarbon? (Corrected for clarity)
A) CH₃
B) C₂H₅
C) C₃H₈
D) C₃H₇

Answer: B (Note: Real question may lead to C₃H₈, but these numbers lead to C₂H₅.)
Explanation:

Find moles of C from CO₂: Moles C = Moles CO₂ = 28.9 g / 44.01 g/mol = 0.657 mol C.

Find moles of H from H₂O: Moles H = 2 × Moles H₂O = 2 × (14.8 g / 18.02 g/mol ) = 2 × 0.821 = 1.642 mol H.

Find the simplest C:H mole ratio:

Divide by the smaller value (0.657):

C: 0.657 / 0.657 = 1

H: 1.642 / 0.657 ≈ 2.5

Convert to whole numbers (multiply by 2): C = 2, H = 5.
The empirical formula is C₂H₅. The hydrocarbon could be butane (C₄H₁₀) or an ethyl radical depending on context. Propane (C₃H₈) has a H:C ratio of 8/3 ≈ 2.67.

How many oxygen atoms are present in 11.0 g of glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆)? (Molar mass of glucose = 180.16 g/mol )
A) 3.68 × 10²²
B) 2.21 × 10²³
C) 1.84 × 10²³
D) 4.41 × 10²²

Answer: B
Explanation:

Calculate moles of glucose: Moles = mass / molar mass = 11.0 g / 180.16 g/mol = 0.06106 mol glucose.

Calculate moles of oxygen atoms: Each molecule of glucose contains 6 oxygen atoms, so 1 mole of glucose contains 6 moles of oxygen atoms. Moles O atoms = 0.06106 mol glucose × (6 mol O atoms / 1 mol glucose) = 0.3664 mol O atoms.

Calculate the number of oxygen atoms: Number = moles × Avogadro's number = 0.3664 mol × (6.022 × 10²³ atoms/mol) = 2.21 × 10²³ atoms.

Chapter 6: Chemical Reactions and Equations

In the balanced redox reaction in an acidic solution: Cr₂O₇²⁻(aq) + C₂H₅OH(aq) → Cr³⁺(aq) + CO₂(g), what is the stoichiometric coefficient for H⁺(aq)?
A) 8
B) 12
C) 14
D) 16

Answer: D
Explanation:

Half-reactions:

Oxidation: C₂H₅OH → CO₂

Reduction: Cr₂O₇²⁻ → Cr³⁺

Balance oxidation half-reaction:

Balance C: C₂H₅OH → 2CO₂

Balance O with H₂O: C₂H₅OH + 3H₂O → 2CO₂

Balance H with H⁺: C₂H₅OH + 3H₂O → 2CO₂ + 12H⁺

Balance charge with e⁻: C₂H₅OH + 3H₂O → 2CO₂ + 12H⁺ + 12e⁻

Balance reduction half-reaction:

Balance Cr: Cr₂O₇²⁻ → 2Cr³⁺

Balance O with H₂O: Cr₂O₇²⁻ → 2Cr³⁺ + 7H₂O

Balance H with H⁺: Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 14H⁺ → 2Cr³⁺ + 7H₂O

Balance charge with e⁻: Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 14H⁺ + 6e⁻ → 2Cr³⁺ + 7H₂O

Equalize electrons: Multiply the reduction half-reaction by 2 to get 12e⁻.

2Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 28H⁺ + 12e⁻ → 4Cr³⁺ + 14H₂O

Add and simplify:
C₂H₅OH + 3H₂O + 2Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 28H⁺ → 2CO₂ + 12H⁺ + 4Cr³⁺ + 14H₂O
Cancel species on both sides:
C₂H₅OH(aq) + 2Cr₂O₇²⁻(aq) + 16H⁺(aq) → 2CO₂(g) + 4Cr³⁺(aq) + 11H₂O(l)
The coefficient for H⁺ is 16.

A mixture of 11.2 g of iron (Fe) and 11.2 g of sulfur (S) is heated to form iron(II) sulfide (FeS). What is the mass of FeS formed and which reactant is in excess? (Molar masses: Fe=56, S=32)
A) 17.6 g FeS; Fe is in excess.
B) 17.6 g FeS; S is in excess.
C) 22.4 g FeS; Neither is in excess.
D) 30.8 g FeS; S is in excess.

Answer: B
Explanation:

Balanced equation: Fe(s) + S(s) → FeS(s). The mole ratio is 1:1.

Calculate initial moles of reactants:

moles Fe = 11.2 g / 56 g/mol = 0.20 mol

moles S = 11.2 g / 32 g/mol = 0.35 mol

Identify the limiting reactant: Since the mole ratio is 1:1, the reactant with fewer moles will be completely consumed. Fe is the limiting reactant (0.20 mol < 0.35 mol). Sulfur is in excess.

Calculate mass of product: The amount of product is determined by the limiting reactant. 0.20 mol of Fe will produce 0.20 mol of FeS.

Mass FeS = moles × molar mass = 0.20 mol × (56 + 32) g/mol = 0.20 mol × 88 g/mol = 17.6 g.

A student performs a reaction to synthesize aspirin. The theoretical yield is 8.50 g. The student isolates 6.75 g of product. However, analysis shows the product is only 92.0% pure aspirin. What is the student's actual percent yield?
A) 79.4%
B) 86.3%
C) 73.1%
D) 67.5%

Answer: C
Explanation: The "actual yield" in the percent yield formula refers to the mass of pure product obtained.

Calculate the mass of pure aspirin: This is the isolated mass multiplied by its purity.

Actual pure mass = 6.75 g × (92.0 / 100) = 6.21 g.

Calculate the percent yield:

Percent Yield = (Actual pure yield / Theoretical yield) × 100%

Percent Yield = (6.21 g / 8.50 g) × 100% = 73.06% ≈ 73.1%.

What is the molarity of a solution prepared by dissolving 29.22 g of NaCl in enough water to make 500.0 mL of solution, then taking 25.00 mL of this stock solution and diluting it to a final volume of 200.0 mL?
A) 1.000 M
B) 0.500 M
C) 0.125 M
D) 0.250 M

Answer: C
Explanation: This is a two-step calculation.

Calculate molarity of the stock solution:

Molar mass of NaCl = 22.99 + 35.45 = 58.44 g/mol .

Moles NaCl = 29.22 g / 58.44 g/mol = 0.5000 mol.

Molarity (M₁) = moles / volume = 0.5000 mol / 0.5000 L = 1.000 M.

Calculate molarity of the diluted solution using the dilution formula M₁V₁ = M₂V₂:

(1.000 M)(25.00 mL) = M₂(200.0 mL)

Chapter 7: Gases and Gas Laws

A real gas deviates most from ideal behavior under which conditions?
A) High temperature and low pressure.
B) Low temperature and high pressure.
C) High temperature and high pressure.
D) Low temperature and low pressure.

Answer: B
Explanation: The ideal gas law assumes that gas particles have no volume and no intermolecular forces. Real gases deviate from this because they do have finite volume and do experience attractions. These deviations are most significant when:

Pressure is high: The gas is compressed, so the volume of the particles themselves becomes a significant fraction of the container's volume.

Temperature is low: The particles are moving slowly, so they are more susceptible to the effects of intermolecular attractive forces.

A 2.0 L flask contains 1.60 g of O₂ and 0.28 g of N₂ at 27°C. What is the partial pressure of O₂ in the mixture? (R = 0.0821 L·atm/mol·K)
A) 0.12 atm
B) 0.62 atm
C) 0.74 atm
D) 1.23 atm

Answer: B
Explanation: The partial pressure of a gas in a mixture can be calculated using the ideal gas law with the moles of that specific gas.

Calculate moles of O₂: Molar mass O₂ = 32.0 g/mol . Moles O₂ = 1.60 g / 32.0 g/mol = 0.050 mol.

Convert temperature to Kelvin: T = 27 + 273 = 300 K.

Use Ideal Gas Law for O₂: P_O₂ = (n_O₂ * R * T) / V

P_O₂ = (0.050 mol × 0.0821 L·atm/mol·K × 300 K) / 2.0 L

P_O₂ = 1.2315 / 2.0 = 0.61575 atm ≈ 0.62 atm.

Consider two identical flasks at the same temperature. Flask A contains H₂(g) at 200 torr. Flask B contains O₂(g) at 800 torr. Which statement is true?
A) The molecules in Flask A have a higher average kinetic energy.
B) The molecules in Flask B have a higher root-mean-square speed.
C) The flasks contain the same number of molecules.
D) There are more molecules in Flask B than in Flask A.

Answer: D
Explanation:

A is false: According to the Kinetic Molecular Theory, average kinetic energy is directly proportional to absolute temperature. Since both flasks are at the same temperature, the molecules in both have the same average kinetic energy.

B is false: At the same temperature, lighter molecules move faster. H₂ (M≈2 g/mol ) is much lighter than O₂ (M≈32 g/mol ), so H₂ molecules have a higher root-mean-square speed.

C & D: From the ideal gas law (PV=nRT), the number of moles (n) is proportional to pressure (P) when V and T are constant. Since Flask B has 4 times the pressure of Flask A (800 torr vs 200 torr), it must contain 4 times as many molecules. Therefore, C is false and D is true.

Zinc metal reacts with hydrochloric acid to produce hydrogen gas. If 1.30 g of Zn is reacted with excess HCl and the H₂ gas is collected over water at 25°C and a total pressure of 752 torr, what volume of gas is collected? (Vapor pressure of H₂O at 25°C is 23.8 torr; R=62.36 L·torr/mol·K; M(Zn)=65.4 g/mol )
A) 0.48 L
B) 0.50 L
C) 0.52 L
D) 0.46 L

Answer: B
Explanation:

Reaction: Zn(s) + 2HCl(aq) → ZnCl₂(aq) + H₂(g).

Calculate moles of H₂ produced: The mole ratio Zn:H₂ is 1:1.

Moles Zn = 1.30 g / 65.4 g/mol = 0.01988 mol. So, moles H₂ = 0.01988 mol.

Calculate the partial pressure of H₂: The collected gas is a mixture of H₂ and water vapor.

P_H₂ = P_total - P_H₂O = 752 torr - 23.8 torr = 728.2 torr.

Use the Ideal Gas Law to find the volume of H₂: V = (nRT)/P. Use R in torr units.

V = (0.01988 mol × 62.36 L·torr/mol·K × (25 + 273.15) K) / 728.2 torr

V = (0.01988 × 62.36 × 298.15) / 728.2 = 370.0 / 728.2 = 0.508 L ≈ 0.50 L.

Chapter 8: Liquids and Solids

The normal boiling point of liquid A is 35°C, and for liquid B it is 120°C. Which statement is most likely true at 25°C?
A) Liquid A has a higher vapor pressure and stronger intermolecular forces than liquid B.
B) Liquid B has a higher vapor pressure and stronger intermolecular forces than liquid A.
C) Liquid A has a higher vapor pressure and weaker intermolecular forces than liquid B.
D) Liquid B has a higher vapor pressure and weaker intermolecular forces than liquid A.

Answer: C
Explanation: A high boiling point indicates strong intermolecular forces (IMFs), as more energy is required to separate the molecules into the gas phase. A high vapor pressure indicates weak IMFs, as molecules can escape the liquid phase more easily.

Liquid B has a higher boiling point (120°C > 35°C), so it has stronger IMFs than liquid A.

Since Liquid A has weaker IMFs, it will be more volatile and have a higher vapor pressure at any given temperature (like 25°C).

The phase diagram for a substance shows a solid-liquid equilibrium line with a negative slope. What can be concluded about this substance?
A) The solid form is denser than the liquid form.
B) The triple point occurs at a pressure above 1 atm.
C) The liquid form is denser than the solid form.
D) The substance cannot be liquefied above its critical temperature.

Answer: C
Explanation: The slope of the solid-liquid line on a phase diagram indicates the relative densities of the solid and liquid phases. A negative slope (like for water) means that as pressure increases, the melting point decreases. This happens because applying pressure favors the formation of the denser phase. If increasing pressure makes it easier to melt the substance, the liquid phase must be denser than the solid phase.

Which substance would be expected to have the highest melting point?
A) I₂ (Iodine)
B) SiO₂ (Silicon dioxide)
C) H₂O (Ice)
D) NaCl (Sodium chloride)

During the titration of a weak monoprotic acid with a strong base, what is the relationship between pH and pKa at the half-equivalence point?
A) pH > pKa
B) pH < pKa
C) pH = pKa

Answer: C

Why does copper metal corrode (e.g., forming a green patina of basic copper sulfate) in the presence of acidic rain (containing H⁺ and SO₄²⁻ ions), even though its standard reduction potential (Cu²⁺/Cu) is positive (+0.34 V)?

B) Oxygen from the air acts as the oxidizing agent, and its reduction potential is much higher than that of copper's oxidation.
C) Sulfate ions are strong oxidizing agents.
D) The non-standard conditions shift the potential of the Cu²⁺/Cu half-cell to be negative.

B) 9.1 x 10⁻⁵ g

Two gas-phase reactions, X and Y, have identical frequency factors (A) and are run at the same temperature. Reaction X has ΔG° = -50 kJ/mol and Ea = 80 kJ/mol. Reaction Y has ΔG° = -20 kJ/mol and Ea = 40 kJ/mol. Which statement is correct?

C) Both reactions will have the same rate.
D) The relative rates cannot be determined without knowing ΔH°.

Answer: B
Explanation: The rate of a reaction is determined by its kinetics, not its thermodynamics. The rate constant k is given by the Arrhenius equation: k = A*e^(-Ea/RT). B) It will triple.
C) It will quadruple.
D) It will increase by a factor of 9.

Answer: D

The van der Waals equation, [P + a(n/V)²] [V - nb] = nRT, corrects for the non-ideal behavior of real gases. The term 'a' primarily accounts for ____, and the term 'b' primarily accounts for ____.

B) intermolecular forces; molecular volume
C) relativistic effects; molecular size

Answer: B

Which statement correctly describes the relationship between viscosity, intermolecular forces (IMFs), and temperature?

B) High viscosity is associated with weak IMFs, and viscosity decreases with increasing temperature.

D) Low viscosity is associated with strong IMFs, and viscosity is independent of temperature.

Answer: C
Explanation: Viscosity is a measure of a fluid's resistance to flow.

Ionization Energy (IE): IE is the energy required to remove an electron. It will be most difficult to remove an electron from the species with the highest nuclear charge, as the pull on the electrons is strongest. Therefore, K⁺ will have the highest ionization energy.

What is the approximate F-S-F bond angle in the SF₃⁺ cation?
A) < 109.5°
B) 120°
C) < 120°
D) 90°

Answer: A
Explanation:

Valence Electrons & VSEPR: Central atom is S (Group 16, 6 valence e⁻). The cation has a +1 charge, so we consider the central atom to have 5 effective valence electrons. It is bonded to 3 F atoms, using 3 of these electrons. This leaves 2 electrons as 1 lone pair.

Domains and Geometry: There are 3 bonding pairs + 1 lone pair = 4 electron domains. The electron geometry for 4 domains is tetrahedral. The molecular geometry for the AX₃E₁ configuration is trigonal pyramidal.

Bond Angle: A perfect tetrahedral angle (AX₄) is 109.5°. The lone pair exerts more repulsion than the bonding pairs, compressing the F-S-F bond angles. Therefore, the bond angle will be < 109.5°.

Identify the strongest intermolecular force present in a sample of acetone (propanone, CH₃COCH₃).
A) Hydrogen bonding
B) London dispersion forces
C) Ion-dipole forces
D) Dipole-dipole forces

Answer: D
Explanation:

Analyze the molecule: Acetone is a polar molecule because the C=O carbonyl group has a significant bond dipole.

Evaluate IMF types:

It experiences London dispersion forces (as all molecules do).

It is polar, so it experiences dipole-dipole forces.

It does NOT have a hydrogen atom bonded directly to O, N, or F, so it cannot engage in hydrogen bonding with itself.

Ion-dipole forces are not present as it is a pure substance, not a mixture with an ionic compound.

Conclusion: For a polar molecule like acetone, dipole-dipole forces are stronger than LDFs and are the strongest type of IMF present.
