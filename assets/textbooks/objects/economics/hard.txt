Chapter 1: Introduction to Economics and Basic Concepts

1. A country's Production Possibility Curve (PPC) for military goods and consumer goods experiences a biased shift outwards, pivoted along the consumer goods axis. Which of the following events would best explain this specific change?
a) A major earthquake destroys significant industrial capacity.
b) A general improvement in technology benefits all sectors equally.
c) A new manufacturing process doubles the efficiency of producing consumer goods only.
d) The government implements a large-scale military conscription, increasing the size of the army.

Answer: c) A new manufacturing process doubles the efficiency of producing consumer goods only.
Explanation: A biased or pivoted shift in the PPC indicates a change in technology or resource productivity that affects only one of the goods. In this case, the curve pivots outwards along the consumer goods axis, meaning the maximum potential output of consumer goods has increased while the maximum potential output of military goods remains unchanged. This is perfectly described by a technological improvement specific to consumer good production.
(a) would shift the entire PPC inwards.
(b) would cause a parallel outward shift of the entire PPC.
(d) represents a reallocation of resources along the existing PPC, not a shift of the curve itself.

2. In a mixed economic system, the primary justification for government intervention to provide public goods like national defense is:
a) The principle of consumer sovereignty, where consumers demand protection.
b) The market's inability to prevent the tragedy of the commons for defense resources.
c) The market's failure to provide non-excludable and non-rivalrous goods due to the free-rider problem.
d) The government's comparative advantage in producing all goods and services.

Answer: c) The market's failure to provide non-excludable and non-rivalrous goods due to the free-rider problem.
Explanation: This question gets to the core of market failure. Public goods like national defense are non-excludable (it's impossible to prevent someone from being defended) and non-rivalrous (one person's defense doesn't reduce another's). Because of non-excludability, private firms cannot effectively charge for the service, leading to the "free-rider problem" where individuals consume without paying. Consequently, private markets will not supply such goods, necessitating government provision funded through taxation.

3. If an economy is operating at a point inside its Production Possibility Curve (PPC), the opportunity cost of producing more of one good is:
a) Infinite, as resources are inefficiently used.
b) Zero, because unemployed resources can be utilized without sacrificing other goods.
c) Increasing, as shown by the concave shape of the PPC.
d) Equal to the value of the resources now being employed.

Answer: b) Zero, because unemployed resources can be utilized without sacrificing other goods.
Explanation: A point inside the PPC represents inefficiency or unemployment. This means there are idle resources (labor, capital). To produce more of one good, the economy can simply put these idle resources to work without having to divert resources away from the production of the other good. Therefore, no output of the other good is sacrificed, and the opportunity cost is zero. Opportunity cost only becomes a factor when moving along the frontier of the PPC.

4. A student has a non-refundable, pre-paid ticket for a concert tonight. A friend offers the student a free ticket to a basketball game at the same time. The student's opportunity cost of attending the concert is:
a) The price of the concert ticket.
b) Zero, because the concert ticket is already paid for.
c) The enjoyment and experience they would have received from the basketball game.
d) The price of the concert ticket plus the enjoyment of the basketball game.

Answer: c) The enjoyment and experience they would have received from the basketball game.
Explanation: Opportunity cost is the value of the next-best alternative foregone. The price of the concert ticket is a "sunk cost"—it has already been paid and cannot be recovered, so it is irrelevant to the current decision. The decision is a choice between two actions: attending the concert or attending the basketball game. By choosing the concert, the student gives up the enjoyment of the basketball game, which is the opportunity cost.

Chapter 2: Demand, Supply, and Market Equilibrium

5. The market for Good X, a normal good, is in equilibrium. If consumer incomes increase and the cost of a key input for producing Good X also increases, what is the most likely effect on the equilibrium price and quantity?
a) Equilibrium price will increase, and the effect on quantity is ambiguous.
b) Equilibrium quantity will decrease, and the effect on price is ambiguous.
c) Both equilibrium price and quantity will increase.
d) Equilibrium price will be ambiguous, and quantity will increase.

Answer: a) Equilibrium price will increase, and the effect on quantity is ambiguous.
Explanation: Two events occur simultaneously: 1) An increase in consumer incomes shifts the demand curve for a normal good to the right (D↑). 2) An increase in input costs shifts the supply curve to the left (S↓).

The rightward shift in demand causes Price (P) to rise and Quantity (Q) to rise.

The leftward shift in supply causes Price (P) to rise and Quantity (Q) to fall.
Since both shifts cause the price to rise, the final equilibrium price will definitely be higher. However, the effect on quantity is ambiguous because the demand shift pushes it up while the supply shift pushes it down. The final outcome for quantity depends on the magnitude of the shifts.

6. The government imposes a price floor on an agricultural product, set above the equilibrium price. To make this policy effective and manage the resulting surplus, the government will likely have to:
a) Subsidize consumers to encourage them to buy more at the floor price.
b) Purchase the excess supply at the floor price and store or dispose of it.
c) Impose a tax on producers to discourage overproduction.
d) Ration the good among consumers.

Answer: b) Purchase the excess supply at the floor price and store or dispose of it.
Explanation: A price floor set above equilibrium creates a surplus (quantity supplied > quantity demanded) because producers are willing to sell more at the high price than consumers are willing to buy. If this surplus is left in the market, it would put downward pressure on the price, causing the floor to fail. To maintain the floor price, the government must eliminate the surplus, which is typically done by buying the excess supply itself.

7. The price elasticity of demand for luxury cars is -2.5. If the manufacturer wishes to maximize total revenue, they should:
a) Increase the price, as demand is elastic.
b) Keep the price constant, as total revenue is already maximized.
c) Decrease the price, as the percentage increase in quantity demanded will be greater than the percentage decrease in price.
d) Increase production regardless of price.

Answer: c) Decrease the price, as the percentage increase in quantity demanded will be greater than the percentage decrease in price.
Explanation: The absolute value of the PED is 2.5, which is greater than 1. This means demand is elastic. For elastic demand, there is an inverse relationship between price and total revenue. To increase total revenue, the firm must decrease the price. The resulting percentage increase in quantity demanded will be larger than the percentage decrease in price, leading to higher overall revenue (P↓ x Q↑↑ = TR↑).

8. The cross-price elasticity of demand (XED) between printers and ink cartridges is -1.8, and the income elasticity of demand (YED) for printers is +2.2. If the price of ink cartridges rises by 10%, this would most likely:
a) Be caused by a 22% increase in consumer income.
b) Cause a rightward shift in the demand curve for printers by 18%.
c) Cause a leftward shift in the demand curve for printers.
d) Make printers an inferior good.

Answer: c) Cause a leftward shift in the demand curve for printers.
Explanation: The negative XED (-1.8) indicates that printers and ink cartridges are complements. When the price of a complementary good (ink cartridges) increases, the demand for the original good (printers) decreases. A decrease in demand is represented graphically as a leftward shift of the demand curve. The YED information is a distractor for this question. The magnitude (-1.8 * 10% = -18%) suggests the size of the quantity change, not the shift itself.

9. Consider a market where a per-unit tax is imposed on producers. The deadweight loss from this tax will be largest when:
a) Both demand and supply are perfectly inelastic.
b) Both demand and supply are highly elastic.
c) Demand is highly elastic, and supply is highly inelastic.
d) Demand is highly inelastic, and supply is highly elastic.

Answer: b) Both demand and supply are highly elastic.
Explanation: Deadweight loss (DWL) from a tax is the loss of welfare from trades that no longer occur. When demand and supply are elastic, quantities are very responsive to price changes. A tax, which drives a wedge between the buyer's price and seller's price, will cause a very large reduction in the quantity traded. This large reduction in quantity creates a large DWL triangle. If they were inelastic, quantity would change very little, and the DWL would be small.

10. If the imposition of an excise tax on a product leaves the total revenue received by producers (net of tax) unchanged, what can be concluded about the price elasticity of demand (PED)?
a) PED = -1 (unit elastic).
b) PED > -1 (inelastic).
c) PED < -1 (elastic).
d) The PED must be perfectly inelastic.

Answer: a) PED = -1 (unit elastic).
Explanation: This is a very challenging question. Total revenue for producers (net of tax) is P_net * Q. Before the tax, consumer expenditure was P1 * Q1. After the tax, consumers pay P_c, the government gets the tax, and producers receive P_s. If P_s * Q2 = P1 * Q1, it implies that the curve linking the net price to producers and the quantity they sell has a total revenue that does not change with price, a characteristic of a unit elastic demand curve. Specifically, this relationship holds true if the demand curve is a rectangular hyperbola, which has a PED of -1 everywhere.

Chapter 3: Perfect Competition and Monopoly

11. In a perfectly competitive market in long-run equilibrium, which of the following conditions is NOT met?
a) P = MC (Allocative Efficiency)
b) P = minimum ATC (Productive Efficiency)
c) MR = MC (Profit Maximization)
d) P > MR

Answer: d) P > MR
Explanation: In perfect competition, a firm is a price taker and faces a perfectly elastic (horizontal) demand curve. For such a firm, the price it receives for each unit is constant. Therefore, the additional revenue from selling one more unit (Marginal Revenue) is equal to the price. The condition is P = AR = MR. The condition P > MR is the defining feature of a firm with market power facing a downward-sloping demand curve, like a monopolist.

13. The primary reason a natural monopoly is likely to exist is:
a) The government grants an exclusive patent to a single firm.
b) The firm controls a unique and essential natural resource.
c) Economies of scale are so significant that the LRAC curve is still declining over the entire range of market demand.
d) The firm engages in aggressive predatory pricing to drive out all competitors.

Answer: c) Economies of scale are so significant that the LRAC curve is still declining over the entire range of market demand.
Explanation: This is the technical definition of a natural monopoly. When the Long-Run Average Cost (LRAC) decreases as output increases across the entire range of quantities the market demands, it means a single large firm can produce at a lower cost per unit than any combination of smaller firms. It is therefore "natural" and more efficient for only one firm to serve the market.

14. Comparing the long-run equilibrium of a perfectly competitive firm and a profit-maximizing monopolist (assuming identical cost curves), the deadweight loss of monopoly represents:
a) The transfer of surplus from consumers to the monopolist's profit.
b) The total surplus lost to society due to the monopolist producing less than the socially optimal output.
c) The excess of price over marginal cost (P > MC).
d) The total cost of producing the monopolist's output.

Answer: b) The total surplus lost to society due to the monopolist producing less than the socially optimal output.
Explanation: Deadweight loss is the net loss of economic efficiency. It occurs because the monopolist maximizes profit by restricting output (to where MR=MC) to a level below the socially efficient point (where P=MC). This means some mutually beneficial trades that would occur in a competitive market do not happen. The value of these lost trades is the deadweight loss, a reduction in total surplus that is not transferred to anyone.

15. If a perfectly competitive industry is an "increasing-cost industry," what does its long-run industry supply curve look like?
a) Perfectly elastic (horizontal).
b) Upward sloping.
c) Perfectly inelastic (vertical).
d) Downward sloping.

Answer: b) Upward sloping.
Explanation: In an increasing-cost industry, when new firms enter (e.g., in response to profits), they bid up the price of some key inputs (like specialized labor or raw materials). This raises the cost curves for all firms in the industry. As a result, the new long-run equilibrium price will be higher than the original price. A curve connecting these long-run equilibrium points will slope upwards.

16. A government wishes to regulate a natural monopoly to achieve allocative efficiency. It would force the monopolist to set a price equal to:
a) Minimum Average Total Cost (min ATC).
b) Average Variable Cost (AVC).
c) Marginal Cost (MC).
d) Marginal Revenue (MR).

Answer: c) Marginal Cost (MC).
Explanation: Allocative efficiency is achieved when resources are allocated such that the value consumers place on the last unit of a product (the Price) is equal to the societal cost of producing that unit (the Marginal Cost). Setting P=MC forces the firm to produce the socially optimal quantity. (Note: For a natural monopoly where ATC is falling, MC is below ATC, so this pricing rule would force the firm to make a loss, likely requiring a government subsidy).

Chapter 4: Monopolistic Competition and Oligopoly

17. A key difference between the long-run equilibrium of a monopolistically competitive firm and a perfectly competitive firm is that the monopolistically competitive firm:
a) Earns supernormal profits.
b) Produces at the minimum point of its ATC curve.
c) Operates with excess capacity, where P > min ATC.
d) Faces a perfectly elastic demand curve.

Answer: c) Operates with excess capacity, where P > min ATC.
Explanation: Both firm types earn only normal profit (P=ATC) in the long run due to free entry. However, the monopolistically competitive firm faces a downward-sloping demand curve. The tangency point where P=ATC occurs on the falling portion of the ATC curve, not at its minimum. This means the firm is producing less than the productively efficient output, a situation known as "excess capacity."

18. In the Kinked Demand Curve model of oligopoly, the discontinuity in the marginal revenue curve is a direct result of:
a) The assumption that rivals will match price increases but not price decreases.
b) The firm's goal of maximizing market share rather than profit.
c) The assumption that rivals will match price decreases but not price increases.
d) The presence of a dominant firm engaged in price leadership.

Answer: c) The assumption that rivals will match price decreases but not price increases.
Explanation: The "kink" in the demand curve results from this asymmetric reaction assumption. Because the demand curve has two different slopes above and below the kink, the corresponding marginal revenue curve will have a vertical gap or discontinuity at that quantity. This gap helps explain price rigidity, as the MC curve can shift within this gap without changing the profit-maximizing price.

20. Which of the following best exemplifies a cartel's inherent instability problem, as described by the Prisoner's Dilemma?
a) Firms find it difficult to agree on the initial collusive price.
b) New firms, not part of the cartel, enter the market and undercut the cartel's price.
c) While the highest collective profit is at the high cartel price, each individual member has an incentive to secretly lower its price or exceed its quota.
d) Governments prosecute cartel members under anti-trust laws.

Answer: c) While the highest collective profit is at the high cartel price, each individual member has an incentive to secretly lower its price or exceed its quota.
Explanation: The core of the Prisoner's Dilemma in this context is the conflict between collective interest and individual self-interest. The cartel as a whole benefits most from cooperation (everyone sticking to high prices/low quotas). However, each individual member can make an even greater personal profit by cheating, assuming others continue to cooperate. This individual incentive to cheat is what makes cartels inherently unstable and hard to maintain.

21. The Cournot model and the Bertrand model of duopoly differ primarily in their core assumption about what firms choose. The Cournot model assumes firms compete on ________, while the Bertrand model assumes they compete on ________.
a) Advertising; Quality
b) Price; Quantity
c) Quantity; Price
d) Market Share; Profit Margins

Answer: c) Quantity; Price
Explanation: This is the definitional difference between these two foundational oligopoly models. In the Cournot model, each firm chooses its optimal output level, taking its rival's output as given. In the Bertrand model, each firm chooses its optimal price, taking its rival's price as given.

22. Non-price competition, such as heavy branding and advertising, is a hallmark of which market structures?
a) Perfect Competition and Monopoly
b) Monopoly and Monopolistic Competition
c) Monopolistic Competition and Oligopoly
d) Perfect Competition and Oligopoly

Answer: c) Monopolistic Competition and Oligopoly
Explanation: Firms engage in non-price competition to differentiate their products and build brand loyalty. This is the central feature of Monopolistic Competition (product differentiation). It is also extremely common in Oligopoly, as firms often prefer to compete on branding and features rather than engaging in potentially destructive price wars with their few powerful rivals.

Chapter 5: Consumer Theory

23. A consumer is at equilibrium, consuming Goods X and Y. If the price of Good X falls, the substitution effect, isolated by the Hicksian method, is represented by a movement:
a) To a new, higher indifference curve due to the increase in real income.
b) Along the new budget line to a point where MRS has changed.
c) To a different point on the original indifference curve, reflecting the new price ratio.
d) Parallel to the initial budget line until it is tangent to the new indifference curve.

Answer: c) To a different point on the original indifference curve, reflecting the new price ratio.
Explanation: The Hicksian method isolates the substitution effect by holding the consumer's utility (real income) constant. Graphically, this is done by finding the new combination of goods on the original indifference curve that the consumer would choose given the new, lower relative price of X. This means sliding along the original indifference curve to a point where it is tangent to a hypothetical budget line with the new price ratio's slope.

24. If for a consumer, the Marginal Rate of Substitution of X for Y (MRSxy) is 3, and the price ratio (Px/Py) is 2, the consumer can increase total utility by:
a) Consuming more of Good Y and less of Good X.
b) Consuming more of Good X and less of Good Y.
c) Maintaining their current consumption bundle.
d) Consuming more of both goods.

Answer: b) Consuming more of Good X and less of Good Y.
Explanation: The MRSxy=3 means the consumer is subjectively willing to give up 3 units of Y for 1 more unit of X. The price ratio Px/Py=2 means the market requires them to give up only 2 units of Y for 1 more unit of X. Since the consumer values X more highly (in terms of Y) than the market requires, they can gain utility by buying more X and selling (consuming less) Y.

25. A Giffen good must be:
a) A normal good for which the substitution effect outweighs the income effect.
b) An inferior good for which the substitution effect outweighs the income effect.
c) An inferior good for which the income effect outweighs the substitution effect.
d) A good with no substitutes.

Answer: c) An inferior good for which the income effect outweighs the substitution effect.
Explanation: A Giffen good is a good where a lower price leads to lower demand. This can only happen if two conditions hold: (1) The good must be an inferior good, so a price fall (which increases real income) has a negative income effect. (2) This negative income effect must be stronger than the always-positive substitution effect of the price fall.

26. The key theoretical difference between the Cardinal Utility approach and the Ordinal Utility approach is that:
a) Cardinal utility assumes utility can be ranked, while ordinal utility assumes it can be measured in "utils".
b) Ordinal utility assumes consumers can make consistent choices, whereas cardinal utility does not.
c) Ordinal utility uses indifference curves and does not require measurable utility, while cardinal utility assumes utility is numerically quantifiable.
d) Cardinal utility explains the law of demand, while ordinal utility does not.

Answer: c) Ordinal utility uses indifference curves and does not require measurable utility, while cardinal utility assumes utility is numerically quantifiable.
Explanation: This is the core distinction. Cardinal utility theory (Marshall) posits that satisfaction ("utility") can be measured in numerical units ("utils"). Ordinal utility theory (Hicks and Allen) makes the weaker and more realistic assumption that consumers can only rank their preferences (e.g., "I prefer A to B") without being able to say by how much. This is why it uses indifference curves.

27. An Engel curve for a luxury good is typically:
a) Upward sloping and becomes flatter at higher incomes.
b) Downward sloping.
c) A vertical line.
d) Upward sloping and becomes steeper at higher incomes.

Answer: d) Upward sloping and becomes steeper at higher incomes.
Explanation: An Engel curve plots quantity demanded against income. A luxury good is defined as a good with an income elasticity of demand (YED) greater than 1. This means that as income rises, the quantity demanded rises more than proportionally. This relationship is represented by a curve that is upward sloping and becomes steeper as income increases.

Chapter 6: Producer Theory

28. In the long run, if a firm doubles all its inputs and finds that its output more than doubles, this firm is experiencing:
a) The law of diminishing marginal returns.
b) Economies of scale (increasing returns to scale).
c) Constant returns to scale.
d) Diseconomies of scale.

Answer: b) Economies of scale (increasing returns to scale).
Explanation: Increasing returns to scale occurs when a proportional increase in all inputs results in a more-than-proportional increase in output. This is the definition of the phenomenon described. Economies of scale is the resulting fall in long-run average costs. The law of diminishing returns refers to the short run, where at least one factor is fixed.

29. The Long-Run Average Cost (LRAC) curve is best described as:
a) The sum of all short-run average total cost curves.
b) The locus of minimum points of all short-run average total cost curves.
c) An "envelope curve" tangent to the set of possible short-run average total cost curves.
d) A horizontal line representing the firm's minimum efficient scale.

Answer: c) An "envelope curve" tangent to the set of possible short-run average total cost curves.
Explanation: The LRAC curve shows the lowest possible average cost for producing any level of output when all factors are variable. Each SRATC corresponds to a fixed plant size. In the long run, the firm can choose the plant size (the SRATC) that produces a given output at the lowest cost. The LRAC is formed by the points of tangency with this family of SRATC curves, "enveloping" them from below.

30. A firm's Marginal Cost (MC) curve intersects its Average Total Cost (ATC) and Average Variable Cost (AVC) curves:
a) At their respective maximum points.
b) When ATC and AVC are still falling.
c) At their respective minimum points.
d) At the same output level.

Answer: c) At their respective minimum points.
Explanation: This is a fundamental geometric relationship. As long as the marginal cost of an additional unit is less than the average cost, the average will fall. When the marginal cost is greater than the average, the average will rise. Therefore, the MC curve must intersect the AVC and ATC curves at their lowest points.

31. A producer is at an optimal input combination using labor (L) and capital (K). Which condition must be met?
a) MP_L = MP_K
b) MRTS_LK > P_L/P_K
c) (MP_L / P_L) = (MP_K / P_K)
d) Total product is maximized.

Answer: c) (MP_L / P_L) = (MP_K / P_K)
Explanation: This is the equi-marginal principle applied to production. To achieve the least-cost combination of inputs for a given output, the firm must allocate its budget such that the last dollar spent on labor yields the same additional output as the last dollar spent on capital. If they were unequal, the firm could produce the same output for less cost by reallocating spending toward the more productive input per dollar.

32. If a Cobb-Douglas production function is given by Q = A * L^0.7 * K^0.4, this production process exhibits:
a) Increasing returns to scale.
b) Decreasing returns to scale.
c) Constant returns to scale.
d) Diminishing marginal returns to both labor and capital.

Answer: a) Increasing returns to scale.
Explanation: For a Cobb-Douglas function, returns to scale are determined by the sum of the exponents of the inputs. Here, α + β = 0.7 + 0.4 = 1.1. Since this sum is greater than 1, the production function exhibits increasing returns to scale (a 10% increase in both L and K would result in an 11% increase in Q).

Chapter 7: National Income Accounting

34. A country's GDP is greater than its GNP. This implies that:
a) The country's nationals are earning more from their investments abroad than foreigners are earning in the country.
b) Net Factor Income from Abroad (NFIA) is positive.
c) Income earned by foreign factors of production within the country is greater than the income earned by national factors abroad.
d) The country's depreciation is larger than its net exports.

Answer: c) Income earned by foreign factors of production within the country is greater than the income earned by national factors abroad.
Explanation: The relationship is GNP = GDP + NFIA (Net Factor Income from Abroad). If GDP > GNP, then NFIA must be negative. NFIA is calculated as [Income earned by nationals abroad] - [Income earned by foreigners in the country]. For NFIA to be negative, the second term must be larger than the first.

35. Which of the following transactions would be included in the calculation of a country's GDP?
a) The purchase of 100 shares of a publicly traded company.
b) The value of unpaid housework performed by a homeowner.
c) A government social security payment to a retiree.
d) A car produced in the country by a foreign-owned company.

Answer: d) A car produced in the country by a foreign-owned company.
Explanation: GDP measures all production that takes place within the geographical boundaries of a country, regardless of the nationality of the producer.
(a) is a financial transaction. (b) is a non-market transaction. (c) is a transfer payment. None of these are included in GDP.

36. To get from Net National Product (NNP) at market prices to National Income (NI), one must:
a) Add depreciation.
b) Subtract transfer payments and add corporate retained earnings.
c) Subtract indirect business taxes and add subsidies.
d) Add Net Factor Income from Abroad (NFIA).

Answer: c) Subtract indirect business taxes and add subsidies.
Explanation: The difference between market price measures and factor cost measures is indirect taxes and subsidies. Market prices include taxes levied on products (like VAT) and are reduced by subsidies. To get to the income actually earned by the factors of production (NI at factor cost), one must subtract the indirect taxes and add back the subsidies.

37. A key difference between the GDP deflator and the Consumer Price Index (CPI) is that:
a) The GDP deflator measures a fixed basket of goods, while the CPI measures a changing basket.
b) The GDP deflator includes the prices of imported consumer goods, whereas the CPI does not.
c) The CPI includes the prices of investment goods and exports, whereas the GDP deflator does not.
d) The GDP deflator measures the prices of all domestically produced goods and services, while the CPI measures prices of a basket of goods consumed by a typical household.

Answer: d) The GDP deflator measures the prices of all domestically produced goods and services, while the CPI measures prices of a basket of goods consumed by a typical household.
Explanation: This is the main conceptual difference. The GDP deflator's "basket" changes each year to reflect current production patterns (C+I+G+X) and only includes domestic goods. The CPI's "basket" is fixed for a period, reflects only household consumption (C), and includes the prices of imported consumer goods.

38. The Genuine Progress Indicator (GPI) is designed to be a better measure of welfare than GDP because it:
a) Only measures the output of the industrial sector.
b) Starts with personal consumption and subtracts the costs of environmental degradation and adds the value of non-market work.
c) Focuses solely on life expectancy and literacy rates.
d) Is calculated by summing the market value of all transactions, including intermediate goods.

Answer: b) Starts with personal consumption and subtracts the costs of environmental degradation and adds the value of non-market work.
Explanation: GPI attempts to create a more holistic measure of welfare by adjusting GDP. It starts with a base of personal consumption expenditure, adds positive components that GDP ignores (like volunteer and household work), and subtracts negative components that GDP ignores or counts as positive (like pollution, resource depletion, crime, and income inequality).

39. In a year, if nominal GDP grew by 5% and the GDP deflator rose from 110 to 113.3, what was the approximate growth rate of real GDP?
a) 8%
b) 5%
c) 3%
d) 2%

Answer: d) 2%
Explanation: First, calculate the inflation rate using the GDP deflator: Inflation Rate = [(New Index - Old Index) / Old Index] * 100 = [(113.3 - 110) / 110] * 100 = (3.3 / 110) * 100 = 3%.
Then, use the approximation: Real GDP Growth ≈ Nominal GDP Growth - Inflation Rate.
Real GDP Growth ≈ 5% - 3% = 2%.

Chapter 8: Money, Banking, and Fiscal Policy

40. If the central bank conducts an open market sale of government securities, the most direct and immediate effect is:
a) A decrease in the discount rate.
b) An increase in the required reserve ratio.
c) A decrease in commercial banks' reserves held at the central bank.
d) An increase in public debt.

Answer: c) A decrease in commercial banks' reserves held at the central bank.
Explanation: In an open market sale, the central bank sells government securities to commercial banks (or the public whose banks facilitate the transaction). The commercial banks pay for these securities by having their reserve accounts at the central bank debited. This directly reduces the amount of reserves in the banking system, tightening monetary policy.

41. The credit creation multiplier is 1 / RRR. However, the actual real-world money multiplier is smaller. This is primarily because the simple formula ignores:
a) The existence of time deposits.
b) The public's preference to hold some funds as cash (currency drain) and banks' holding of excess reserves.
c) The effect of open market operations.
d) The profits made by commercial banks.

Answer: b) The public's preference to hold some funds as cash (currency drain) and banks' holding of excess reserves.
Explanation: The simple formula assumes that 100% of every loan is re-deposited back into the banking system and that banks lend out all of their excess reserves. In reality, two "leakages" occur: (1) people hold a portion of their money as cash (the currency drain), and (2) banks may choose to hold more reserves than legally required (excess reserves). Both reduce the amount of money that is re-lent in each round, making the real-world multiplier smaller.

42. If a country's government has a primary budget deficit, it means that:
a) Total expenditure is less than total revenue.
b) Revenue expenditure is greater than revenue receipts.
c) Total expenditure, excluding interest payments, is greater than total revenue.
d) The government is only borrowing to pay interest on past debts.

Answer: c) Total expenditure, excluding interest payments, is greater than total revenue.
Explanation: Primary Deficit = (Total Expenditure - Interest Payments) - Total Revenue. A positive primary deficit means that even if you ignore the cost of servicing past debt, the government's current spending programs still exceed its revenues. This indicates that the current fiscal stance is adding to the overall debt stock.

43. Which of the following is an example of contractionary fiscal policy aimed at reducing aggregate demand?
a) The central bank sells government bonds.
b) The government decreases income taxes and increases its spending on infrastructure.
c) The government increases taxes and cancels a major public works project.
d) The central bank lowers the required reserve ratio.

Answer: c) The government increases taxes and cancels a major public works project.
Explanation: Fiscal policy involves government spending and taxation. Contractionary policy aims to cool down the economy, usually to fight inflation. Increasing taxes reduces households' disposable income and consumption. Cancelling a spending project directly reduces government expenditure (G). Both actions shift the aggregate demand curve to the left. (a) and (d) are monetary policy tools.

44. A rise in Non-Performing Assets (NPAs) on commercial banks' balance sheets directly impedes economic activity primarily by:
a) Reducing the central bank's control over the discount rate.
b) Reducing the banks' capacity to extend new loans and create credit.
c) Increasing the national debt.
d) Causing a depreciation of the currency.

Answer: b) Reducing the banks' capacity to extend new loans and create credit.
Explanation: When loans become non-performing, banks must set aside capital ("provisioning") to cover the potential losses. These provisions and the funds tied up in the bad loans reduce the bank's available capital base for making new, productive loans to businesses and consumers. This "credit crunch" slows down investment and consumption, impeding economic activity.

45. The M1 measure of the money supply is narrower than M2 because:
a) M1 includes only currency, while M2 includes demand deposits as well.
b) M2 includes less liquid assets like savings deposits and small time deposits, in addition to all of M1.
c) M1 is controlled by the government, while M2 is controlled by commercial banks.
d) M2 includes commercial bank reserves, while M1 does not.

Answer: b) M2 includes less liquid assets like savings deposits and small time deposits, in addition to all of M1.
Explanation: M1 typically consists of the most liquid assets used for transactions: currency in circulation and demand deposits (checking accounts). M2 is a broader measure that includes everything in M1 plus "near money" assets, which are slightly less liquid but easily converted to cash, such as savings accounts and small-denomination time deposits (certificates of deposit).

Chapter 9: Classical and Keynesian Models

46. According to Say's Law, as interpreted by classical economists, if savings (S) exceed planned investment (I), what mechanism restores equilibrium?
a) The price level will fall, increasing the real value of savings.
b) The interest rate will fall, discouraging saving and encouraging investment.
c) Government spending will automatically increase to fill the gap.
d) Aggregate output will fall until savings equal investment.

Answer: b) The interest rate will fall, discouraging saving and encouraging investment.
Explanation: The classical view held that the loanable funds market would equilibrate savings and investment through the interest rate. Savings represents the supply of loanable funds, and investment represents the demand. If supply (S) exceeds demand (I), there is a surplus of funds, which causes the price of funds (the interest rate) to fall. This fall encourages firms to invest more and discourages households from saving, restoring equilibrium.

47. The Classical Dichotomy implies that in the long run, a doubling of the money supply will lead to:
a) A doubling of real GDP.
b) A doubling of the general price level, with no change in real variables like output or employment.
c) A doubling of real wages.
d) A halving of the velocity of money.

Answer: b) A doubling of the general price level, with no change in real variables like output or employment.
Explanation: The classical dichotomy separates real and nominal variables. The Quantity Theory of Money (MV=PY) suggests that if money supply (M) doubles, and velocity (V) and real output (Y) are constant in the long run (as classicals assumed), then the price level (P) must also double. This is the concept of money neutrality—money affects nominal variables (prices) but not real variables (output, employment).

48. If the Marginal Propensity to Consume (MPC) is 0.75, and the government wishes to close a recessionary gap of $200 billion, what is the required increase in government spending, assuming no crowding out?
a) $200 billion
b) $150 billion
c) $50 billion
d) $40 billion

Answer: c) $50 billion
Explanation: First, calculate the expenditure multiplier (k):
k = 1 / (1 - MPC) = 1 / (1 - 0.75) = 1 / 0.25 = 4.
The change in GDP (ΔY) needed is $200 billion. The formula is ΔY = k * ΔG.
$200 billion = 4 * ΔG
ΔG = $200 billion / 4 = $50 billion.

49. The "crowding out" effect suggests that an increase in government borrowing to finance a budget deficit will likely:
a) Decrease interest rates and stimulate private investment.
b) Cause interest rates to rise, which reduces private investment and consumption spending.
c) Decrease the money supply.
d) Lead to a leftward shift of the LRAS curve.

Answer: b) Cause interest rates to rise, which reduces private investment and consumption spending.
Explanation: When the government borrows more, it increases the demand for loanable funds. This increased demand puts upward pressure on interest rates. Higher interest rates make it more expensive for private firms to borrow for investment projects (I) and for consumers to borrow for big-ticket items (C). This reduction in private spending partially offsets the initial expansionary effect of the government spending.

50. The SRAS curve is upward sloping primarily because:
a) Firms have an incentive to increase output as the price level rises, given that some input prices (like nominal wages) are sticky in the short run.
b) As the price level rises, the real interest rate falls, stimulating production.
c) Higher price levels increase the economy's stock of capital.
d) The labor force increases as the price level rises.

Answer: a) Firms have an incentive to increase output as the price level rises, given that some input prices (like nominal wages) are sticky in the short run.
Explanation: The sticky-wage theory is a primary explanation for the upward-sloping SRAS. When the overall price level (which determines firms' output prices) rises, but nominal wages are fixed by contract, firms' real labor costs fall. Production becomes more profitable, so they have an incentive to hire more labor and increase the quantity of goods and services supplied.

51. If an economy is in a short-run equilibrium where output is above its potential level (inflationary gap), the classical self-correction mechanism would involve:
a) A rightward shift of the AD curve.
b) An increase in potential output (rightward shift of LRAS).
c) A leftward shift of the SRAS curve, as nominal wages rise to catch up with higher prices.
d) Government intervention through expansionary fiscal policy.

Answer: c) A leftward shift of the SRAS curve, as nominal wages rise to catch up with higher prices.
Explanation: An inflationary gap means unemployment is below the natural rate, and the economy is "overheating." This creates a very tight labor market, giving workers the bargaining power to demand higher nominal wages. As wages rise, firms' production costs increase. This increase in input costs is represented by a leftward (upward) shift of the SRAS curve, which moves the economy back to the long-run equilibrium (at Y_p) but at a higher price level.

Chapter 10: Inflation, Unemployment, and Business Cycles

52. Stagflation, a period of rising prices and falling output, is best explained in the AD/AS model by:
a) A rightward shift of the AD curve.
b) A leftward shift of the SRAS curve.
c) A rightward shift of the LRAS curve.
d) A leftward shift of the AD curve.

Answer: b) A leftward shift of the SRAS curve.
Explanation: Stagflation combines economic stagnation (falling output, rising unemployment) with inflation (rising prices). This is precisely the outcome of an adverse supply shock, which is represented by a leftward shift of the SRAS curve. The new equilibrium point has a higher price level and a lower quantity of output.

53. The expectations-augmented Phillips Curve theory suggests that the long-run Phillips curve is vertical at the Natural Rate of Unemployment (NRU) because:
a) The trade-off between inflation and unemployment is permanent.
b) Any attempt to keep unemployment below the NRU through demand-side policies will be thwarted as inflation expectations adjust upwards, shifting the SRPC upwards.
c) The government is unable to implement effective fiscal policy.
d) Supply shocks constantly shift the SRPC.

Answer: b) Any attempt to keep unemployment below the NRU through demand-side policies will be thwarted as inflation expectations adjust upwards, shifting the SRPC upwards.
Explanation: The theory posits that the short-run trade-off only exists as long as expected inflation is constant. If policymakers try to exploit this trade-off to target lower unemployment, the resulting inflation will cause people to revise their expectations upwards. This increase in expected inflation shifts the entire Short-Run Phillips Curve (SRPC) up. In the long run, unemployment returns to the NRU, and the only result of the policy is a permanently higher rate of inflation.

54. A software engineer loses her job because her company's work is outsourced to another country where labor is cheaper. This engineer is experiencing:
a) Frictional unemployment.
b) Cyclical unemployment.
c) Structural unemployment.
d) Seasonal unemployment.

Answer: c) Structural unemployment.
Explanation: This job loss is not due to a recession (cyclical) or normal job transition (frictional). It's due to a fundamental change in the structure of the economy—the relocation of her industry/job function to another country. Her skills are no longer demanded in her location, creating a skills/location mismatch, which is the definition of structural unemployment.

55. Which of the following is considered an "automatic stabilizer" in an economy?
a) The central bank's decision to lower interest rates during a recession.
b) A government-legislated infrastructure spending project.
c) A progressive income tax system where tax revenues automatically fall as incomes decline during a recession.
d) An increase in stock market prices.

Answer: c) A progressive income tax system where tax revenues automatically fall as incomes decline during a recession.
Explanation: Automatic stabilizers are features of the fiscal system that work to dampen business cycles without any new discretionary government action. A progressive tax system is a key example. During a recession, as incomes fall, tax liabilities fall more than proportionally, which helps to cushion the fall in disposable income and consumption. The opposite happens in a boom.

56. The Natural Rate of Unemployment (NRU) is composed of:
a) Cyclical and Frictional unemployment only.
b) Cyclical and Structural unemployment only.
c) Frictional and Structural unemployment only.
d) Cyclical unemployment only.

Answer: c) Frictional and Structural unemployment only.
Explanation: The NRU is the rate of unemployment that exists when the economy is at its long-run potential (full employment). It represents the unavoidable baseline level of unemployment. By definition, this includes the short-term churn of frictional unemployment and the longer-term mismatches of structural unemployment. Cyclical unemployment, which is caused by downturns in the business cycle, is zero when the economy is at the NRU.

57. A leading economic indicator is a variable that:
a) Moves at the same time as the overall economy, confirming its current state.
b) Tends to change direction before the overall economy, providing a forecast.
c) Confirms a trend after the economy has already changed direction.
d) Is officially designated by the government to lead policy decisions.

Answer: b) Tends to change direction before the overall economy, providing a forecast.
Explanation: This is the definition of a leading indicator. Economists and policymakers watch these indicators (e.g., stock market indices, new building permits) closely because their movements often signal where the broader economy (as measured by GDP) is headed in the near future.

Chapter 11: International Trade Theory

58. Country X can produce 10 cars or 20 tonnes of wheat. Country Y can produce 8 cars or 18 tonnes of wheat. Based on this information:
a) Country Y has an absolute advantage in both goods.
b) Country X has a comparative advantage in wheat.
c) Country Y has a comparative advantage in wheat.
d) Neither country can gain from trade.

Answer: c) Country Y has a comparative advantage in wheat.
Explanation: We must calculate opportunity costs.

For Country X: Opp. cost of 1 wheat = 10 cars / 20 wheat = 0.5 cars. Opp. cost of 1 car = 20 wheat / 10 cars = 2 wheat.

For Country Y: Opp. cost of 1 wheat = 8 cars / 18 wheat = 0.44 cars. Opp. cost of 1 car = 18 wheat / 8 cars = 2.25 wheat.
Comparing opportunity costs:

For Wheat: Country Y gives up less (0.44 cars < 0.5 cars), so Y has the comparative advantage in wheat.

For Cars: Country X gives up less (2 wheat < 2.25 wheat), so X has the comparative advantage in cars.

59. The Heckscher-Ohlin model of trade posits that a country that is relatively capital-abundant will have a comparative advantage in and export:
a) Labor-intensive goods.
b) All goods.
c) Capital-intensive goods.
d) Goods requiring advanced technology, regardless of factor intensity.

Answer: c) Capital-intensive goods.
Explanation: This is the core prediction of the H-O model (also known as the Factor Proportions Theory). A country's comparative advantage is determined by its relative abundance of factors of production. A country with a lot of capital relative to labor will find it relatively cheaper to produce goods that use capital intensively.

60. An "improvement" in a country's terms of trade means that:
a) The volume of its exports has increased.
b) The ratio of its export prices to its import prices has increased.
c) It has achieved a balance of trade surplus.
d) It is importing more goods than it is exporting.

Answer: b) The ratio of its export prices to its import prices has increased.
Explanation: The Terms of Trade (ToT) is defined as (Index of Export Prices / Index of Import Prices) * 100. An improvement means this ratio has risen. This signifies that the country can purchase more units of imports for each unit of exports it sells, which is beneficial for its welfare.

61. The primary difference between a Free Trade Area (FTA) and a Customs Union is that:
a) An FTA has free movement of labor, while a customs union does not.
b) A customs union has a common external tariff, while members of an FTA maintain their own individual tariffs with non-members.
c) A customs union removes all trade barriers, while an FTA only reduces some tariffs.
d) A customs union has a common currency, while an FTA does not.

Answer: b) A customs union has a common external tariff, while members of an FTA maintain their own individual tariffs with non-members.
Explanation: This is the key distinguishing feature. Both eliminate internal trade barriers among members. However, a Customs Union goes a step further by requiring all members to adopt a single, uniform set of tariffs on goods imported from outside the union. FTA members have independent external trade policies.

62. When a country imposes a tariff on an imported good, the area of deadweight loss represents the:
a) Revenue collected by the government.
b) Increase in profit for domestic producers.
c) Reduction in consumer surplus that is not captured by either the government or domestic producers.
d) Total value of the reduction in imports.

Answer: c) Reduction in consumer surplus that is not captured by either the government or domestic producers.
Explanation: A tariff raises the price for consumers, causing a loss of consumer surplus. Part of this loss is transferred to domestic producers as higher prices/profits. Another part is transferred to the government as tariff revenue. The deadweight loss is the remaining part of the lost consumer surplus, which represents the net loss of welfare to society because the tariff has distorted consumption and production decisions away from the efficient free-trade outcome.

63. The "infant industry" argument for protectionism is most valid if:
a) The protected industry becomes permanently dependent on the tariff for survival.
b) The industry eventually "grows up" and becomes internationally competitive without protection.
c) The tariff revenue is used to subsidize consumers.
d) The protectionism leads to a trade war.

Answer: b) The industry eventually "grows up" and becomes internationally competitive without protection.
Explanation: The logic of the infant industry argument is that temporary protection is needed to allow a new domestic industry to mature, achieve economies of scale, and learn efficient production techniques. The argument is only economically justifiable if this period of protection is indeed temporary and leads to the creation of a globally competitive industry that no longer needs support.

Chapter 12: Balance of Payments and Foreign Exchange Markets

64. In the Balance of Payments, a Spanish tourist spending money on a holiday in Ghana would be recorded in Ghana's BoP as:
a) A debit in the current account.
b) A credit in the capital account.
c) A credit in the current account (export of services).
d) A credit in the financial account.

Answer: c) A credit in the current account (export of services).
Explanation: Tourism is considered a service. When a foreign tourist spends money in Ghana, Ghana is effectively "exporting" its tourism services. Exports lead to an inflow of foreign currency and are therefore recorded as a credit (+) entry. Since services are part of the current account, it is a credit in the current account.

65. A current account deficit in a country's Balance of Payments must be financed by:
a) An equal and offsetting current account surplus in the following year.
b) A surplus in the capital and/or financial accounts, or a decrease in official reserves.
c) An increase in official reserves.
d) An increase in net factor income from abroad.

Answer: b) A surplus in the capital and/or financial accounts, or a decrease in official reserves.
Explanation: A current account deficit means a country is spending more foreign currency than it earns. This net outflow must be "paid for." It can be financed by a net inflow on the other accounts, which means borrowing from abroad or selling assets to foreigners (a financial account surplus), or by running down the country's official holdings of foreign currency (a decrease in reserves).

66. A Japanese company builds a new car factory in the USA. This transaction would be recorded in the USA's Balance of Payments as:
a) A credit in the current account.
b) A debit in the financial account (direct investment outflow).
c) A debit in the current account.
d) A credit in the financial account (direct investment inflow).

Answer: d) A credit in the financial account (direct investment inflow).
Explanation: This is an example of Foreign Direct Investment (FDI), which is a financial account transaction. From the perspective of the USA (the host country), this is an inflow of foreign capital. Capital inflows are recorded as credits (+).

67. If a country with a fixed exchange rate system is facing persistent upward pressure on its currency's value (appreciation), its central bank must:
a) Sell domestic currency and buy foreign currency, leading to an increase in its foreign reserves.
b) Buy domestic currency and sell foreign currency, leading to a decrease in its foreign reserves.
c) Devalue its currency.
d) Raise domestic interest rates.

Answer: a) Sell domestic currency and buy foreign currency, leading to an increase in its foreign reserves.
Explanation: Upward pressure means demand for the domestic currency exceeds supply at the fixed rate. To prevent the price from rising (appreciating), the central bank must intervene to increase the supply of its currency. It does this by selling its own currency in the foreign exchange market and buying foreign currency in exchange. This action simultaneously increases its holdings of foreign exchange reserves.

68. According to the theory of Purchasing Power Parity (PPP), if the inflation rate in Mexico is 5% and the inflation rate in the USA is 2%, the Mexican Peso should:
a) Appreciate against the US Dollar by approximately 3%.
b) Depreciate against the US Dollar by approximately 3%.
c) Appreciate against the US Dollar by approximately 7%.
d) Depreciate against the US Dollar by approximately 7%.

Answer: b) Depreciate against the US Dollar by approximately 3%.
Explanation: Relative PPP states that the exchange rate will adjust to offset differences in inflation. The currency of the country with the higher inflation rate must depreciate to maintain constant purchasing power. The expected depreciation is approximately equal to the inflation differential: 5% - 2% = 3%.

69. The primary role of the International Monetary Fund (IMF) is to:
a) Provide long-term development loans for infrastructure projects in poor countries.
b) Regulate international trade in goods and services.
c) Promote global exchange rate stability and provide temporary financial assistance to countries with balance of payments problems.
d) Act as a global central bank with the power to issue a world currency.

Answer: c) Promote global exchange rate stability and provide temporary financial assistance to countries with balance of payments problems.
Explanation: This describes the core mandate of the IMF, established at Bretton Woods. It acts as a lender of last resort for countries facing BoP crises, aiming to ensure the stability of the international monetary system. Long-term development loans (a) are the role of the World Bank. Regulating trade (b) is the role of the WTO.

Chapter 13: Concepts of Development

70. A country experiences a 5% growth in real GDP, but its GINI coefficient also increases significantly, and the Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI) remains unchanged. This scenario is best described as:
a) Sustainable development.
b) Economic development but not economic growth.
c) Economic growth without equitable economic development.
d) A decrease in absolute poverty and a decrease in relative poverty.

Answer: c) Economic growth without equitable economic development.
Explanation: The country has achieved economic growth (rise in real GDP). However, the benefits are not being widely shared. Rising inequality (higher GINI) and no improvement in broad deprivations (stagnant MPI) indicate a lack of economic development. This is a classic case of growth that is not inclusive.

71. The Lorenz curve for a country lies very close to the 45-degree line of perfect equality. This indicates that:
a) The country has a very high per capita income.
b) The country has a relatively equal distribution of income.
c) The country's economy is growing rapidly.
d) The country has a low absolute poverty rate.

Answer: b) The country has a relatively equal distribution of income.
Explanation: The Lorenz curve measures the degree of income inequality. The 45-degree line represents perfect equality. A curve that is very close to this line signifies that the gap between perfect equality and the actual distribution is small, which means the GINI coefficient is low and income is distributed relatively equally. It says nothing directly about the level of income or poverty.

72. The "Resource Curse" or "Paradox of Plenty" refers to the observation that:
a) Countries with abundant natural resources always have the fastest economic growth.
b) Some countries rich in natural resources have experienced slower growth, more corruption, and less development than resource-poor countries.
c) Exporting natural resources leads to an improvement in a country's terms of trade.
d) The exploitation of natural resources is the only viable path to sustainable development.

Answer: b) Some countries rich in natural resources have experienced slower growth, more corruption, and less development than resource-poor countries.
Explanation: This paradox describes the counterintuitive phenomenon where natural resource wealth can hinder, rather than help, long-term development. This can be due to factors like Dutch disease, price volatility, neglect of other economic sectors, and governance problems associated with managing resource wealth.

73. Structural transformation in the process of economic development typically involves a shift in employment and output:
a) From services to industry, then to agriculture.
b) Directly from agriculture to services.
c) From agriculture to industry, then to services.
d) From industry to services, then to agriculture.

Answer: c) From agriculture to industry, then to services.
Explanation: This is the classic three-sector model of structural change. As a country develops, its economy first moves from being predominantly agrarian (primary sector) to being industrialized (secondary sector). As it becomes a mature, high-income economy, the services sector (tertiary sector) becomes dominant.

74. The Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI) is considered a more comprehensive measure of poverty than an income-based line because it:
a) Focuses solely on inequality.
b) Considers deprivations across health, education, and living standards simultaneously.
c) Only applies to developed countries.
d) Is easier to calculate.

Answer: b) Considers deprivations across health, education, and living standards simultaneously.
Explanation: The strength of the MPI lies in its "multidimensional" approach. It acknowledges that poverty is not just a lack of income but a complex set of overlapping deprivations. By measuring indicators in health, education, and living standards, it provides a fuller and more nuanced picture of poverty.

Chapter 14: Strategies for Economic Development

75. A key characteristic of the Import Substitution Industrialization (ISI) strategy is:
a) The use of low tariffs and promotion of free trade to encourage efficiency.
b) The development of industries primarily for export markets.
c) The use of high tariffs and quotas to protect domestic industries from foreign competition.
d) A focus on developing the agricultural sector before the industrial sector.

Answer: c) The use of high tariffs and quotas to protect domestic industries from foreign competition.
Explanation: ISI is an inward-looking strategy. The central policy mechanism is to make imported manufactured goods expensive or unavailable through trade barriers (tariffs, quotas) in order to create a protected domestic market for local "infant" industries to grow.

76. The East Asian "Tiger" economies are often cited as successful examples of which development strategy?
a) Import Substitution Industrialization (ISI)
b) Commodity-led growth
c) Export-Oriented Industrialization (EOI)
d) A command economy model

Answer: c) Export-Oriented Industrialization (EOI)
Explanation: The remarkable economic performance of South Korea, Taiwan, Hong Kong, and Singapore was largely based on an outward-looking strategy. They focused on developing internationally competitive manufacturing industries and driving growth through exports to the global market.

77. Which of the following is a potential major drawback of a heavy reliance on Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) for a host country?
a) A decrease in local tax revenue.
b) Repatriation of profits by multinational corporations, which can be a significant outflow on the financial account.
c) A mandatory transfer of all technology to local firms.
d) A reduction in local employment opportunities.

Answer: b) Repatriation of profits by multinational corporations, which can be a significant outflow on the financial account.
Explanation: While FDI brings an initial capital inflow, the profits generated belong to the foreign parent company. When these profits are sent back ("repatriated") to the home country, they represent an outflow of foreign exchange. This is recorded as a debit under investment income on the current account and can put pressure on the host country's balance of payments.

78. From a development perspective, investing in primary education and basic healthcare is crucial because:
a) They are the easiest services for the government to provide.
b) These investments in human capital are fundamental to improving labor productivity and creating a virtuous cycle of growth and development.
c) They are required by all foreign aid donors.

Answer: b) These investments in human capital are fundamental to improving labor productivity and creating a virtuous cycle of growth and development.
Explanation: Development economics strongly emphasizes the role of human capital. A healthier, more educated workforce is more productive, adaptable, and innovative. 79. The group lending model used by many microfinance institutions, such as the Grameen Bank, is primarily designed to:

86. A beekeeper's bees pollinate a nearby farmer's apple orchard. The farmer does not pay the beekeeper for this service. This is a classic example of:

a) Rivalrous and excludable.
b) Non-rivalrous and non-excludable.
c) Rivalrous but non-excludable.
d) Non-rivalrous but excludable.

Answer: b) Non-rivalrous and non-excludable.
Explanation: These are the two defining characteristics of a pure public good. c) Rivalrous but Non-Excludable (a common-pool resource).

Answer: c) Rivalrous but Non-Excludable (a common-pool resource).
Explanation: The tragedy arises from this specific combination. Non-excludability means anyone can access the resource, creating an incentive to use it. Rivalry means that one person's use depletes the resource for others. The combination leads to a race to exploit the resource before others do, resulting in its depletion.

In contrast, a cap-and-trade system provides quantity certainty (the cap) but the price of permits can fluctuate based on market demand and supply, creating price uncertainty for firms.

104. A person who was earning $60,000 per year quits their job to start a business. The opportunity cost of this entrepreneurial venture for the first year is at least:
a) $20,000
b) $60,000
c) $61,000
d) $81,000

Answer: c) $61,000

106. If the demand for a product is perfectly inelastic, and the supply is upward sloping, who bears the full burden of an excise tax imposed on producers?
a) The producers.

Answer: b) The consumers.
Explanation: Perfectly inelastic demand means consumers will buy the same quantity regardless of price (the demand curve is vertical). a) Equilibrium quantity to increase, while the effect on price is ambiguous.

Explanation: Two simultaneous shifts are occurring. 1) The drought reduces supply, shifting the supply curve to the left. 2) The medical report increases demand (due to a change in tastes/preferences), shifting the demand curve to the right.

Leftward supply shift: Price↑, Quantity↓.

Rightward demand shift: Price↑, Quantity↑.

Chapter 3: Perfect Competition and Monopoly

112. A perfectly competitive firm's short-run supply curve is:
a) Its entire marginal cost curve.
b) Its marginal cost curve above its average total cost curve.
c) Its marginal cost curve above its average variable cost curve.
d) Its average total cost curve.

Answer: c) Its marginal cost curve above its average variable cost curve.
Explanation: A firm will only produce in the short run if the price (P=MR) is high enough to cover its average variable costs. If P < AVC, it will shut down. Since the firm produces where P=MC, its supply curve (which shows the quantity it will supply at each price) corresponds to its MC curve, but only for the portion that lies above the minimum point of the AVC curve (the shutdown point).

113. Which of the following is a reason why a monopolist's marginal revenue is less than its price?
a) The monopolist is a price taker.
b) The law of diminishing marginal returns is in effect.
c) To sell an additional unit, the monopolist must lower the price on all units sold, not just the marginal unit.
d) The monopolist's costs are higher than a competitive firm's costs.

Answer: c) To sell an additional unit, the monopolist must lower the price on all units sold, not just the marginal unit.
Explanation: This is known as the "price effect". The monopolist faces the entire downward-sloping market demand curve. To induce one more customer to buy, it must lower the price. Assuming it cannot price discriminate, this lower price applies to all previous units as well, causing a loss of revenue on those units that partially offsets the revenue gained from the new unit. This makes MR < P.

114. In the long run, the entry of new firms into a perfectly competitive industry where firms are initially making supernormal profits will cause:
a) The individual firm's demand curve to shift downwards and the industry supply curve to shift rightwards.
b) The individual firm's demand curve to shift upwards and the industry supply curve to shift leftwards.
c) Both the industry demand curve and the industry supply curve to shift rightwards.
d) The individual firm's cost curves (MC and ATC) to shift upwards.

Answer: a) The individual firm's demand curve to shift downwards and the industry supply curve to shift rightwards.
Explanation: The supernormal profits attract new entrants. The entry of more firms increases the total industry supply, shifting the industry supply curve to the right. This causes the market price to fall. Since each individual firm is a price taker, a lower market price means the horizontal demand curve they face shifts downwards until P = min ATC, eliminating the supernormal profit.

115. A key social cost of monopoly, known as "X-inefficiency," refers to:

d) The inequitable distribution of income resulting from monopoly profits.

Answer: c) The tendency for a firm sheltered from competition to have higher-than-necessary costs due to organizational slack and complacency.
Explanation: X-inefficiency is a concept separate from allocative (P>MC) and productive (not at min ATC) inefficiency. It suggests that without the pressure of competition, a monopolist has less incentive to keep its costs as low as possible. This "organizational slack" means its entire cost curve structure may be higher than it would be in a competitive environment.

116. A monopolist is practicing perfect price discrimination. What is the relationship between its marginal revenue curve and its demand curve?
a) The marginal revenue curve is the same as the demand curve.
b) The marginal revenue curve lies below the demand curve with twice the slope.
c) The marginal revenue curve is a horizontal line.
d) There is no marginal revenue curve.

Answer: a) The marginal revenue curve is the same as the demand curve.
Explanation: Perfect price discrimination means the monopolist charges each customer the maximum price they are willing to pay. In this case, there is no "price effect"—lowering the price for a new customer does not require lowering it for existing ones. Therefore, the revenue from each additional unit sold (MR) is simply the price it is sold at, making the MR curve coincide with the demand curve. The monopolist would produce up to the point where P=MC.

Chapter 4: Monopolistic Competition and Oligopoly

118. The "excess capacity" theorem in monopolistic competition implies that in the long run, the firm:
a) Has unemployed resources within its factory.
b) Is productively inefficient because it could lower its average total cost by increasing its output.
c) Makes supernormal profits by restricting capacity.
d) Is allocatively inefficient because it produces too much output.

Answer: b) Is productively inefficient because it could lower its average total cost by increasing its output.
Explanation: "Excess capacity" means the firm is producing on the downward-sloping portion of its ATC curve, to the left of the minimum point. This means it is not achieving productive efficiency (production at minimum ATC). It could produce more and lower its cost per unit, but it chooses not to because doing so would require a price cut that would reduce its total profit (to below zero).

119. A key assumption of the Bertrand model of duopoly (with identical products) that leads to the P=MC outcome is that:
a) Each firm believes the other will maintain its current quantity.
b) Firms have limited production capacity.
c) Each firm has the capacity to supply the entire market and believes consumers will buy from the lowest-priced seller.
d) The firms successfully collude to act like a monopolist.

Answer: c) Each firm has the capacity to supply the entire market and believes consumers will buy from the lowest-priced seller.
Explanation: The Bertrand paradox (P=MC) arises because if one firm sets a price above marginal cost, the other firm has an incentive to slightly undercut it and capture the entire market. This undercutting process continues until the price is driven down to marginal cost. This logic relies on the assumption that firms are not capacity-constrained and can serve all the demand that comes their way.

120. In a repeated Prisoner's Dilemma game, which of the following strategies is most likely to sustain cooperation (e.g., maintaining a high collusive price)?
a) A dominant strategy of always defecting (setting a low price).
b) A strategy of always cooperating regardless of the rival's action.
c) A "Tit-for-Tat" strategy, where a firm cooperates in the first round and then mimics its rival's previous move in subsequent rounds.
d) A random strategy of choosing high or low prices.

Answer: c) A "Tit-for-Tat" strategy, where a firm cooperates in the first round and then mimics its rival's previous move in subsequent rounds.
Explanation: In a one-shot game, defection is the dominant strategy. But in a repeated game, the possibility of future retaliation or reward changes behavior. Tit-for-Tat is a robust strategy because it is initially cooperative, but it immediately punishes defection, and it is forgiving (it will cooperate again if the rival returns to cooperation). This creates a credible threat and reward structure that can sustain a collusive outcome.

121. From a societal welfare perspective, monopolistic competition is often considered a "trade-off" because:
a) It results in high prices for low-quality goods.
b) Society gains product variety and choice at the cost of some allocative and productive inefficiency.
c) It is the most efficient market structure but lacks innovation.
d) It maximizes producer surplus at the expense of all consumer surplus.

Answer: b) Society gains product variety and choice at the cost of some allocative and productive inefficiency.
Explanation: Compared to the theoretical ideal of perfect competition, monopolistic competition is inefficient (P>MC and P>minATC). This inefficiency is the "cost." However, the source of this inefficiency is product differentiation, which is also the source of consumer benefit—a wide variety of choices to suit different tastes and preferences. The structure represents a trade-off between efficiency and variety.

122. Which of these oligopolistic behaviors is typically not illegal under anti-trust laws?
a) A formal cartel agreement to fix prices and allocate market shares.
b) Predatory pricing designed to bankrupt a smaller competitor.
c) Tacit collusion where a dominant firm acts as a price leader and other firms follow its price changes without a formal agreement.
d) Bid rigging on government contracts.

Answer: c) Tacit collusion where a dominant firm acts as a price leader and other firms follow its price changes without a formal agreement.
Explanation: Tacit collusion is extremely difficult to prove in court because there is no explicit agreement or communication. While it results in coordinated behavior, it's not illegal simply to follow a competitor's price changes. Explicit collusion (a, d) and predatory pricing (b) are clear violations of anti-trust laws in most countries.

Chapter 5: Consumer Theory

123. If indifference curves were concave to the origin (bowed outwards) instead of convex, what would this imply?
a) The consumer has an increasing marginal rate of substitution and would specialize in consuming only one of the two goods.
b) The consumer views the two goods as perfect substitutes.
c) The consumer gets zero utility from the goods.
d) The law of diminishing marginal utility does not hold.

Answer: a) The consumer has an increasing marginal rate ofsubstitution and would specialize in consuming only one of the two goods.
Explanation: Concave indifference curves mean the MRS is increasing. As the consumer gets more of Good X, they are willing to give up more and more of Good Y to get another unit of X. This represents a preference for extremes. When faced with a budget line, such a consumer would never choose a combination of goods; their utility is maximized by moving to a "corner solution," spending all their income on either Good X or Good Y, whichever gives the highest utility.

124. The budget line for a consumer is PxQx + PyQy = M. What happens to the budget line if the government imposes an ad valorem tax (e.g., 10%) on Good X?
a) The budget line shifts outwards, parallel to the original.
b) The budget line shifts inwards, parallel to the original.
c) The budget line pivots inwards on the X-axis, becoming steeper.
d) The budget line pivots outwards on the Y-axis, becoming flatter.

Answer: c) The budget line pivots inwards on the X-axis, becoming steeper.
Explanation: An ad valorem tax on Good X increases its effective price to Px*(1+tax rate). A higher price for Good X means the consumer can afford less of it, so the x-intercept (M/Px) moves closer to the origin. The y-intercept (M/Py) is unchanged. The line pivots inwards around the y-intercept. This also makes the slope (-Px/Py) steeper (a larger negative number).

125. The Law of Diminishing Marginal Utility provides a cardinal utility-based explanation for:
a) Why the supply curve slopes upwards.
b) Why the demand curve slopes downwards.
c) Why firms aim to maximize profit.
d) The existence of Giffen goods.

Answer: b) Why the demand curve slopes downwards.
Explanation: According to this law, the additional satisfaction from consuming each successive unit of a good decreases. A rational consumer will only buy an additional unit if its price is low enough to be justified by its lower marginal utility. Therefore, to induce a consumer to buy more units, the price must fall, which explains the inverse relationship between price and quantity demanded.

126. If two goods are perfect complements (e.g., left shoes and right shoes), the consumer's indifference curves will be:
a) Downward sloping straight lines.
b) L-shaped.
c) Upward sloping.
d) Concave to the origin.

Answer: b) L-shaped.
Explanation: For perfect complements, the goods must be consumed in a fixed ratio (e.g., 1:1). Getting more of one good without more of the other yields no additional utility. This is represented by L-shaped indifference curves, where the corners of the "L"s lie on a ray from the origin representing the fixed consumption ratio.

127. The Income Consumption Curve (ICC) for a consumer buying two goods is backward bending (slopes downwards after a point). This indicates that:
a) Both goods are normal.
b) One of the goods is a Giffen good.
c) One of the goods is an inferior good.
d) The consumer's preferences are inconsistent.

Answer: c) One of the goods is an inferior good.
Explanation: The ICC traces the consumer's equilibrium points as income changes. If it is backward bending, it means that as income rises beyond a certain point, the quantity demanded of one of the goods starts to fall. A good for which demand falls as income rises is, by definition, an inferior good.

Chapter 6: Producer Theory

128. Diseconomies of scale are typically caused by:
a) The law of diminishing marginal returns in the short run.
b) Technological progress that makes existing capital obsolete.
c) A firm growing so large that managerial difficulties, coordination problems, and communication breakdowns lead to rising average costs.
d) The price of variable inputs falling as the firm buys in bulk.

Answer: c) A firm growing so large that managerial difficulties, coordination problems, and communication breakdowns lead to rising average costs.
Explanation: Diseconomies of scale is a long-run concept explaining why the LRAC curve eventually slopes upwards. It is not caused by short-run diminishing returns (which assumes a fixed factor). Instead, it arises from the complexities of managing and coordinating an extremely large-scale organization, which can lead to inefficiencies that outweigh the benefits of size.

129. If a firm's total revenue covers its total variable costs but not its total costs, the firm should:
a) Shut down immediately in the short run.
b) Continue to operate in the short run to minimize its losses.
c) Exit the industry immediately in the long run.
d) Increase its price.

Answer: b) Continue to operate in the short run to minimize its losses.
Explanation: In this situation (P > AVC but P < ATC), the firm is making a loss. However, each unit it sells generates revenue that is greater than the variable cost of producing it. This excess of revenue over variable cost contributes to paying off the fixed costs. If the firm were to shut down, its loss would be equal to its entire fixed cost. By operating, its loss is smaller. (It should, however, consider exiting in the long run if conditions do not improve).

130. An isoquant map describes ________, while an isocost line describes ________.
a) The firm's costs; the firm's revenues.
b) The firm's technological production possibilities; the firm's budget constraint for inputs.
c) Consumer preferences; consumer income.
d) Short-run costs; long-run costs.

Answer: b) The firm's technological production possibilities; the firm's budget constraint for inputs.
Explanation: An isoquant map shows all the different combinations of inputs (like labor and capital) that can produce various levels of output, representing the firm's production function or technology. An isocost line shows all the combinations of those same inputs that the firm can afford to buy for a given total cost, representing its budget. The tangency of the two determines the least-cost way to produce.

131. The Marginal Rate of Technical Substitution of Labor for Capital (MRTS_LK) is equal to:
a) The ratio of the price of labor to the price of capital (PL/PK).
b) The slope of the isocost line.
c) The ratio of the marginal product of labor to the marginal product of capital (MP_L / MP_K).
d) The additional output from hiring one more unit of labor.

Answer: c) The ratio of the marginal product of labor to the marginal product of capital (MP_L / MP_K).
Explanation: The MRTS_LK is the slope of the isoquant and represents the rate at which a firm can substitute labor for capital while keeping output constant. This trade-off is determined by the relative productivities of the two inputs. If labor is highly productive relative to capital, the firm can give up a lot of capital for a little labor. Hence, MRTS = MP_L / MP_K.

132. The vertical distance between a firm's Average Total Cost (ATC) curve and its Average Variable Cost (AVC) curve represents:
a) Marginal Cost (MC).
b) Total Fixed Cost (TFC).
c) Average Fixed Cost (AFC).
d) Economic profit per unit.

Answer: c) Average Fixed Cost (AFC).
Explanation: The total cost is the sum of fixed and variable costs (TC = TFC + TVC). Dividing by quantity (Q) gives ATC = AFC + AVC. Therefore, the difference between ATC and AVC is simply AFC (ATC - AVC = AFC). As output increases, this vertical distance shrinks because AFC (TFC/Q) continuously falls.

Chapter 7: National Income Accounting

133. Which of the following would cause a country's Gross National Product (GNP) to be significantly higher than its Gross Domestic Product (GDP)?
a) Many foreign-owned multinational companies operate within the country.
b) The country is a major recipient of foreign direct investment.
c) A large number of the country's citizens work abroad and send home significant remittances and earnings.
d) The country has a very high rate of depreciation on its capital stock.

Answer: c) A large number of the country's citizens work abroad and send home significant remittances and earnings.
Explanation: GNP = GDP + Net Factor Income from Abroad (NFIA). For GNP > GDP, NFIA must be positive. This occurs when the income earned by a country's own citizens and firms from abroad is greater than the income earned by foreigners within the country. A large expatriate workforce sending earnings home is a prime example of this.

134. "Double counting" in national income accounting is a problem that can be avoided by:
a) Summing the value added at each stage of production.
b) Including the value of all intermediate goods in the final calculation.
c) Using the income approach instead of the expenditure approach.
d) Counting the value of all sales, both final and intermediate.

Answer: a) Summing the value added at each stage of production.
Explanation: Double counting occurs if the value of intermediate goods is counted in addition to the value of the final good. For example, counting the value of the steel and the value of the car it's used in. The value-added approach avoids this by only summing the additional value created at each step (e.g., value of flour minus value of wheat, plus value of bread minus value of flour). Alternatively, one can simply count the market value of final goods and services only.

135. If Personal Income (PI) in a country is greater than National Income (NI), this could be explained by:
a) Very high corporate income taxes and retained earnings.
b) A very high level of personal direct taxes.
c) A very low savings rate among households.
d) Large government transfer payments to households (e.g., pensions, welfare) that exceed the deductions for corporate profits and taxes.

Answer: d) Large government transfer payments to households (e.g., pensions, welfare) that exceed the deductions for corporate profits and taxes.
Explanation: To get from NI to PI, we subtract income that is earned but not received by households (like undistributed corporate profits and taxes) and add income that is received but not earned from current production (like transfer payments). For PI > NI, the "add-backs" (transfer payments) must be larger than the "take-outs" (corporate profits/taxes, etc.).

136. The Human Development Index (HDI) is considered a broader measure of welfare than GDP per capita because it includes indicators for:
a) Environmental quality and income distribution.
b) Political freedom and national security.
c) A long and healthy life (life expectancy) and access to knowledge (education).
d) The size of the underground economy.

Answer: c) A long and healthy life (life expectancy) and access to knowledge (education).
Explanation: HDI combines three dimensions: a decent standard of living (measured by GNI per capita), health (measured by life expectancy at birth), and knowledge (measured by mean and expected years of schooling). This makes it a composite index that captures more than just income.

137. Which statement accurately describes the expenditure approach to calculating GDP?
a) GDP = Wages + Rent + Interest + Profit.
b) GDP = C + I + G + M - X.
c) It sums the spending on all final goods and services by households (C), firms (I), government (G), and the net foreign sector (X-M).
d) It measures the contribution of agriculture, industry, and services to total output.

Answer: c) It sums the spending on all final goods and services by households (C), firms (I), government (G), and the net foreign sector (X-M).
Explanation: This correctly describes the components and method of the expenditure approach, summarized by the famous identity Y = C + I + G + (X - M). (a) is the income approach. (b) has net exports reversed. (d) is the output/production approach.

Chapter 8: Money, Banking, and Fiscal Policy

138. If the central bank lowers the discount rate, but no commercial banks actually borrow from the discount window, the action might still be effective because:
a) It automatically reduces the required reserve ratio.
b) It serves as a powerful signal to the market of the central bank's intention to pursue a more expansionary monetary policy, potentially lowering other market interest rates.
c) It directly increases the reserves in the banking system.
d) It forces commercial banks to lower their lending rates.

Answer: b) It serves as a powerful signal to the market of the central bank's intention to pursue a more expansionary monetary policy, potentially lowering other market interest rates.
Explanation: Changes in the central bank's key policy rates (like the discount rate) have a significant "announcement" or "signaling" effect. Even if the direct channel (borrowing) isn't used, the signal of easier monetary conditions can influence expectations and lead other market participants to lower their interest rates.

139. A country's "public debt" is the stock of IOUs accumulated from past ________, while a "fiscal deficit" is a flow variable measuring the shortfall in ________.
a) current account deficits; trade
b) budget deficits; a single year
c) tax revenues; government spending
d) commercial bank loans; central bank reserves

Answer: b) budget deficits; a single year
Explanation: This question tests the stock vs. flow distinction. The fiscal deficit is the amount by which government spending exceeds revenue in a single time period (e.g., one year) - this is a flow. The public debt is the total accumulated amount of borrowing from all past years' deficits - this is a stock, measured at a point in time.

140. Which of the following is a key difference between money and "near money"?
a) Money is a store of value, whereas near money is not.
b) Money is a unit of account, whereas near money is not.
c) Money is directly usable as a medium of exchange, whereas near money must first be converted into money.
d) Money has intrinsic value, while near money does not.

Answer: c) Money is directly usable as a medium of exchange, whereas near money must first be converted into money.
Explanation: The defining feature of money (like cash or demand deposits in M1) is its perfect liquidity and immediate acceptability as a medium of exchange. Near money (like a savings account or a time deposit) serves the store of value function well but lacks this direct transaction capability. You must first withdraw cash from your savings account or wait for your time deposit to mature before you can spend it.

141. Fiat money has value primarily because:
a) It is backed by a physical commodity like gold.
b) Its supply is unlimited.
c) The government has declared it to be legal tender, and it is widely accepted in exchange.
d) It has high intrinsic value.

Answer: c) The government has declared it to be legal tender, and it is widely accepted in exchange.
Explanation: Fiat money, by definition, is not backed by a commodity. Its value derives from two sources: government decree ("fiat") making it legal tender, and the trust and widespread social convention of accepting it as a means of payment.

142. A government decides to reduce its budget deficit by cutting its spending on infrastructure projects. According to Keynesian analysis, this action is a form of:
a) Contractionary fiscal policy that will decrease aggregate demand.
b) Expansionary fiscal policy that will increase aggregate demand.
c) Contractionary monetary policy that will reduce the money supply.
d) Expansionary monetary policy that will increase the money supply.

Answer: a) Contractionary fiscal policy that will decrease aggregate demand.
Explanation: Fiscal policy deals with government spending (G) and taxes (T). Cutting spending directly reduces G, which is a component of aggregate demand (AD = C + I + G + X-M). This is a contractionary or restrictive policy intended to slow down the economy, which in this case is a side effect of trying to fix the budget.

Chapter 9: Classical and Keynesian Models

143. In the simplest Keynesian model, an increase in the marginal propensity to save (MPS) will:
a) Increase the size of the expenditure multiplier.
b) Decrease the size of the expenditure multiplier.
c) Increase the level of autonomous consumption.
d) Have no effect on the multiplier.

Answer: b) Decrease the size of the expenditure multiplier.
Explanation: The simple multiplier is calculated as k = 1 / MPS. There is an inverse relationship between the MPS and the multiplier. A higher MPS means a larger portion of each round of income "leaks out" into savings instead of being passed on as spending. This reduces the size of the subsequent rounds of spending and therefore makes the overall multiplier smaller.

144. John Maynard Keynes fundamentally disagreed with the classical school's assertion that:
a) Aggregate demand could influence the price level.
b) Flexible wages and prices would automatically and quickly restore the economy to full employment during a downturn.
c) Investment is inversely related to the interest rate.
d) The money supply affects the nominal price level in the long run.

Answer: b) Flexible wages and prices would automatically and quickly restore the economy to full employment during a downturn.
Explanation: This was the central point of departure. Keynes observed the Great Depression and argued that wages and prices are "sticky" downwards. Because of this stickiness, the economy's self-correction mechanism described by classicals (falling wages and prices boosting demand and clearing markets) does not work effectively. This allows an economy to get stuck in a prolonged "underemployment equilibrium," requiring government intervention to boost aggregate demand.

145. An increase in business confidence leads firms to increase investment spending. According to the AD/AS model, the short-run result would be:
a) A rightward shift of the AD curve, leading to higher output and a higher price level.
b) A leftward shift of the SRAS curve, leading to lower output and a higher price level.
c) A rightward shift of the LRAS curve.
d) A leftward shift of the AD curve, leading to lower output and a lower price level.

Answer: a) A rightward shift of the AD curve, leading to higher output and a higher price level.
Explanation: Investment (I) is a component of Aggregate Demand (AD). An increase in investment spending for any reason other than a change in the price level (like improved confidence) will shift the entire AD curve to the right. This leads to a new short-run equilibrium at a higher real GDP and a higher general price level.

146. Complete "crowding out" would imply that an increase in government spending financed by borrowing leads to:
a) An equal increase in private investment.
b) A rise in interest rates that causes a reduction in private investment exactly equal to the increase in government spending.
c) No change in interest rates.
d) A decrease in taxes of an equal amount.

Answer: b) A rise in interest rates that causes a reduction in private investment exactly equal to the increase in government spending.
Explanation: Complete crowding out is the extreme monetarist/classical view. It argues that the increase in interest rates caused by government borrowing is so significant that the resulting fall in private investment (and possibly consumption) perfectly offsets the initial increase in government spending, leading to no net change in aggregate demand.

147. The Keynesian consumption function is C = 200 + 0.8Yd. At an income level (Yd) of 1,000, what is the Average Propensity to Consume (APC)?
a) 0.8
b) 0.2
c) 1.0
d) 1,000

Answer: c) 1.0
Explanation: First, calculate total consumption at Yd = 1,000:
C = 200 + 0.8 * (1000) = 200 + 800 = 1,000.
Next, calculate the Average Propensity to Consume (APC), which is Total Consumption / Total Disposable Income (C/Yd).
APC = 1,000 / 1,000 = 1.0. At this specific income level (the break-even point), all income is consumed.

Chapter 10: Inflation, Unemployment, and Business Cycles

148. A major oil-exporting region experiences a war, disrupting global oil supply. For oil-importing countries, this is likely to cause:
a) Demand-pull inflation.
b) A leftward shift of their AD curves.
c) A recessionary gap without inflation.
d) Cost-push inflation and potentially stagflation.

Answer: d) Cost-push inflation and potentially stagflation.
Explanation: Oil is a key input for many industries and for transportation. A disruption to its supply causes a sharp increase in its price. For oil-importing countries, this is an adverse supply shock that increases the costs of production. This shifts the SRAS curve to the left, resulting in a higher price level (cost-push inflation) and lower output (stagnation).

149. According to the long-run Phillips curve, a government policy that successfully reduces the natural rate of unemployment (NRU) would be represented by:
a) A movement up along the vertical LRPC.
b) A rightward shift of the LRPC.
c) A leftward shift of the vertical LRPC.
d) A downward shift of the SRPC only.

Answer: c) A leftward shift of the vertical LRPC.
Explanation: The LRPC is vertical at the NRU. Policies that affect the NRU are supply-side policies (e.g., better job training, improved information flows, reducing labor market rigidities). If these policies are successful in reducing frictional and structural unemployment, the NRU itself decreases, which is shown as a leftward shift of the entire vertical LRPC.

150. Which of the following individuals would be harmed by an unexpected increase in the rate of inflation?
a) A homeowner with a fixed-rate mortgage.
b) A retired person living on a fixed pension payment.
c) The government, which has a large national debt.
d) A firm that has borrowed heavily from a bank at a fixed interest rate.

Answer: b) A retired person living on a fixed pension payment.
Explanation: Unexpected inflation harms those whose incomes do not rise with the price level (creditors) and benefits those whose payments are fixed (debtors). A person on a fixed pension has a nominal income that does not change, so as prices rise, the real purchasing power of their pension falls. A homeowner with a fixed-rate mortgage (a) benefits because they repay their loan with "cheaper" money. The government (c) and the firm (d) also benefit as debtors for the same reason.

151. During the trough phase of a business cycle, which of the following is most likely to be observed?
a) High consumer confidence and rising investment.
b) Low unemployment and rising inflation.
c) A high level of cyclical unemployment.
d) A large inflationary gap.

Answer: c) A high level of cyclical unemployment.
Explanation: The trough is the lowest point of economic activity in a cycle. It is characterized by low GDP, low business and consumer confidence, and a lack of demand for goods and services. This lack of demand leads firms to lay off workers, resulting in high levels of cyclical (demand-deficient) unemployment.

152. "Disinflation" refers to:
a) A decrease in the general price level (negative inflation).
b) A slowdown in the rate of price inflation.
c) Zero inflation.
d) A situation of falling output and rising prices.

Answer: b) A slowdown in the rate of price inflation.
Explanation: This is a key terminology distinction. Deflation is a fall in the price level (a). Disinflation is a reduction in the rate of inflation. For example, if inflation falls from 5% one year to 3% the next year, prices are still rising, but more slowly. This is disinflation.

Chapter 11: International Trade Theory

153. A "Voluntary Export Restraint" (VER) is a trade barrier where:
a) An importing country sets a strict legal quota on imports from a specific country.
b) An exporting country, often under pressure, agrees to limit the quantity of its exports to an importing country.
c) Both countries voluntarily agree to stop all trade with each other.
d) A group of countries voluntarily creates a free trade area.

Answer: b) An exporting country, often under pressure, agrees to limit the quantity of its exports to an importing country.
Explanation: VERs are a non-tariff barrier. They are "voluntary" only in name, as the exporting country typically agrees to them to avoid the imposition of a more damaging mandatory tariff or quota by the importing country.

154. Which argument for trade protectionism is considered most credible by mainstream economists, albeit under strict conditions?
a) The need to protect domestic jobs from all foreign competition.
b) The national security argument for key defense-related industries.
c) The infant industry argument, if protection is temporary and leads to future competitiveness.
d) The argument that trade deficits are always harmful and must be eliminated.

Answer: c) The infant industry argument, if protection is temporary and leads to future competitiveness.
Explanation: While most arguments for protectionism are rejected by economists as leading to inefficiency, the infant industry argument has some theoretical validity. The idea that a new industry needs temporary shelter to achieve economies of scale and "learn-by-doing" is plausible. However, its practical application is difficult, as protection often becomes permanent and shields inefficient firms. The national security argument (b) is also credible but can be overused to protect non-essential industries.

155. Trade diversion is a potential negative consequence of forming a regional trade bloc. It occurs when:
a) The bloc as a whole diverts its attention from social issues to trade issues.
b) The formation of the bloc causes trade to be diverted from a more efficient, lower-cost non-member country to a less efficient, higher-cost member country.
c) All international trade is diverted into trade among member countries only.
d) The gains from trade are diverted from consumers to producers.

Answer: b) The formation of the bloc causes trade to be diverted from a more efficient, lower-cost non-member country to a less efficient, higher-cost member country.
Explanation: Trade diversion happens because goods from member countries are given preferential tariff treatment (often zero tariffs). Before the bloc, a country might have imported a good from the most efficient global producer (a non-member). After the bloc, the good from a less-efficient member country may become cheaper due to the tariff advantage, causing the importing country to switch suppliers. This is a move away from global allocative efficiency.

156. The Leontief Paradox cast doubt on the Heckscher-Ohlin model by showing empirically that:
a) Countries do not gain from international trade.
b) The United States, a capital-abundant country, was exporting relatively labor-intensive goods.
c) The terms of trade for developing countries were deteriorating.
d) All international trade was intra-industry trade.

Answer: b) The United States, a capital-abundant country, was exporting relatively labor-intensive goods.
Explanation: The H-O model predicted that the US, being capital-abundant, should export capital-intensive goods and import labor-intensive goods. Wassily Leontief's 1953 study found the opposite pattern. This paradox spurred further research, with explanations including the importance of human capital (the US exports goods intensive in skilled labor) and technology.

157. An export subsidy is considered an unfair trade practice by the WTO because:
a) It harms consumers in the exporting country.
b) It generates no revenue for the government.
c) It artificially lowers the price of exports, allowing domestic producers to unfairly capture market share from more efficient foreign producers.
d) It violates the principle of comparative advantage.

Answer: c) It artificially lowers the price of exports, allowing domestic producers to unfairly capture market share from more efficient foreign producers.
Explanation: An export subsidy gives a country's producers an artificial advantage that is not based on their true efficiency or comparative advantage. It distorts international competition by allowing subsidized firms to sell at a price below their actual cost of production, potentially driving out non-subsidized, more efficient producers in other countries.

Chapter 12: Balance of Payments and Foreign Exchange Markets

158. If a country's central bank is selling its own currency in the foreign exchange market, what is its objective and what is the effect on its official reserves?
a) Objective: to cause a depreciation; Effect: reserves decrease.
b) Objective: to prevent a depreciation; Effect: reserves decrease.
c) Objective: to prevent an appreciation; Effect: reserves increase.
d) Objective: to cause an appreciation; Effect: reserves increase.

Answer: c) Objective: to prevent an appreciation; Effect: reserves increase.
Explanation: Selling its own currency increases its supply in the market, which puts downward pressure on its value. This is done to counteract upward pressure, i.e., to prevent an appreciation. To sell its own currency, the central bank must buy foreign currency in exchange. This action adds to its holdings of official foreign reserves.

159. The main component of a country's current account that often dictates whether it is in deficit or surplus is the:
a) Capital account balance.
b) Financial account balance.
c) Balance of trade (merchandise trade balance).
d) Change in official reserves.

Answer: c) Balance of trade (merchandise trade balance).
Explanation: While all components matter, for most large economies, the trade in goods (exports minus imports of merchandise) is the largest and most volatile component of the current account. A large trade deficit is the most common reason for a country to have a current account deficit.

160. According to Uncovered Interest Rate Parity (UIP), if the interest rate in Canada is 4% and in Japan it is 1%, what is the expected movement of the Canadian Dollar (CAD) relative to the Japanese Yen (JPY)?
a) The CAD is expected to appreciate by approximately 3%.
b) The CAD is expected to depreciate by approximately 3%.
c) The CAD is expected to depreciate by approximately 5%.
d) The JPY is expected to depreciate by approximately 3%.

Answer: b) The CAD is expected to depreciate by approximately 3%.
Explanation: UIP states that the higher interest rate currency must be expected to depreciate to equalize returns for investors. An investor could earn 4% in Canada or 1% in Japan. For the returns to be equal, the extra 3% interest earned in Canada must be offset by an expected 3% depreciation of the Canadian dollar over the investment period.

161. A country has a Currency Board arrangement, pegging its currency to the US dollar. If it experiences a large capital outflow, the Currency Board must:
a) Freely print more domestic currency to meet demand.
b) Allow the exchange rate to float downwards.
c) Sell US dollars from its reserves and contract the domestic money supply in direct proportion.
d) Ask the IMF for a long-term development loan.

Answer: c) Sell US dollars from its reserves and contract the domestic money supply in direct proportion.
Explanation: A currency board is an extreme fixed-rate system. The domestic money supply is rigidly tied to the stock of foreign reserves at a fixed rate. A capital outflow means people are selling the domestic currency to buy US dollars. The currency board must meet this demand by selling its US dollar reserves. As reserves fall, the domestic money supply must also be reduced (contracted) to maintain the 100% backing, which automatically tightens monetary conditions.

162. The World Bank Group's primary focus differs from the IMF's in that the World Bank:
a) Focuses on short-term balance of payments stabilization and exchange rate stability.
b) Is primarily a regulator of international financial markets.
c) Focuses on long-term development, poverty reduction, and financing for specific projects (like infrastructure, health, education) in developing countries.
d) Is funded primarily by grants from the UN.

Answer: c) Focuses on long-term development, poverty reduction, and financing for specific projects (like infrastructure, health, education) in developing countries.
Explanation: This is the core distinction. The IMF is like the "firefighter" of the international monetary system, dealing with short-term crises and macroeconomic stability. The World Bank is the "architect" or "builder," focused on long-term structural development and poverty reduction through project and program lending.

Chapter 13: Concepts of Development

163. Engel's Law, when applied to structural transformation, suggests that as per capita incomes rise across an economy:
a) The share of expenditure on all goods and services rises equally.
b) The share of national income derived from the agricultural sector will tend to fall.
c) The share of national income derived from the service sector will tend to fall.
d) All households will save a larger proportion of their income.

Answer: b) The share of national income derived from the agricultural sector will tend to fall.
Explanation: Engel's Law states that as income rises, the proportion of income spent on food declines. By extension, this means that as a country develops and its average income rises, the overall demand for agricultural products grows more slowly than the demand for manufactured goods and services. This demand-side factor contributes to the relative decline of the agricultural sector's share in GDP.

164. The "Vicious Circle of Poverty" is a concept that suggests:
a) Poverty is a temporary condition that market forces will always correct.
b) High inequality is a necessary precondition for breaking out of poverty.
c) Poverty tends to be self-perpetuating, as low income leads to low savings and investment, which in turn leads to low productivity and back to low income.
d) All poor countries are trapped in poverty forever.

Answer: c) Poverty tends to be self-perpetuating, as low income leads to low savings and investment, which in turn leads to low productivity and back to low income.
Explanation: This describes the core mechanism of the vicious circle concept. It's a feedback loop where the consequences of poverty (e.g., inability to save, invest, or acquire human capital) become the causes of continued poverty, making it difficult to escape without external intervention or a significant structural break.

165. Which of the following is an indicator of WEALTH inequality, not INCOME inequality?
a) The annual salaries of the top 1% versus the bottom 50%.
b) The GINI coefficient for annual earnings.
c) The distribution of ownership of property, stocks, and bonds across a population.
d) The share of total wages going to labor versus the share going to profit.

Answer: c) The distribution of ownership of property, stocks, and bonds across a population.
Explanation: Income is a flow variable (earnings per period). Wealth is a stock variable (the accumulated value of assets at a point in time). The ownership of assets like property and financial instruments represents wealth. Wealth inequality is typically much greater than income inequality.

166. A major limitation of using GDP per capita as a sole measure of a country's development is its failure to account for:
a) The total output of the economy.
b) The level of inflation.
c) The distribution of income among the population and non-market activities.
d) The country's population size.

Answer: c) The distribution of income among the population and non-market activities.
Explanation: GDP per capita is a simple average. It can be high even if income is highly concentrated in the hands of a few (high inequality). It also completely ignores valuable non-market production (like household work) and "bads" like pollution. HDI, GPI, and GINI are needed to supplement it.

167. A country heavily dependent on exporting a single non-renewable resource, like oil, faces a significant risk to its long-term sustainable development due to:
a) The Prebisch-Singer hypothesis.
b) The Heckscher-Ohlin theory.
c) Resource depletion and the volatility of global commodity prices.
d) A perpetually improving terms of trade.

Answer: c) Resource depletion and the volatility of global commodity prices.
Explanation: This captures two key risks. The resource is finite and will eventually run out (depletion), posing a sustainability problem. In the meantime, commodity prices are notoriously volatile on world markets, leading to boom-and-bust cycles that make macroeconomic management and long-term planning extremely difficult. This is a core part of the "resource curse."

Chapter 14: Strategies for Economic Development

168. One of the paradoxes of the Import Substitution Industrialization (ISI) strategy was that it often led to:
a) A strong bias in favor of the agricultural sector.
b) A decrease in urbanization.
c) A balance of payments crisis due to a continued need to import capital goods and intermediate inputs for the protected industries.
d) Highly efficient and competitive domestic firms.

Answer: c) A balance of payments crisis due to a continued need to import capital goods and intermediate inputs for the protected industries.
Explanation: While ISI aimed to save foreign exchange by replacing consumer good imports, the new domestic industries themselves were heavily reliant on importing machinery, technology, and components from developed countries. Because the strategy often discouraged exports (e.g., via an overvalued exchange rate), countries frequently struggled to earn the foreign exchange needed to pay for these essential capital imports, leading to balance of payments problems.

169. Official Development Assistance (ODA) or "foreign aid" is distinguished from other forms of international capital flow like FDI because ODA:
a) Always involves the private sector.
b) Has the primary objective of earning a commercial profit.
c) Is offered by governments or international organizations with a primary developmental or humanitarian objective, often on concessional (soft) terms.
d) Consists solely of migrant remittances.

Answer: c) Is offered by governments or international organizations with a primary developmental or humanitarian objective, often on concessional (soft) terms.
Explanation: This is the defining feature. FDI is a commercial investment seeking profit. ODA is an official transfer for the stated purpose of promoting development. Its "concessional" nature (containing a grant element of at least 25%) is what distinguishes it from official, but non-concessional, loans.

170. The concept of "human capital" refers to:
a) The amount of money a person has saved for retirement.
b) The stock of machinery and equipment available to the workforce.
c) The number of people in a country's labor force.
d) The skills, education, health, and knowledge embodied in the population that enhance productivity.

Answer: d) The skills, education, health, and knowledge embodied in the population that enhance productivity.
Explanation: Human capital theory treats education and health not just as consumption goods but as investments that create a stock of productive capacity within human beings, analogous to how physical capital (machinery) enhances productivity.

171. A major reason many microfinance programs target women is the empirical evidence suggesting that:
a) Women have more physical collateral to offer than men.
b) Women are less likely to start small businesses.
c) Loans given to women are more likely to lead to benefits for their children and families, and women often have higher repayment rates.
d) Formal banks prefer to lend to women over men.

Answer: c) Loans given to women are more likely to lead to benefits for their children and families, and women often have higher repayment rates.
Explanation: This reflects the widely observed "double dividend" of lending to women in developing contexts. Not only do women often prove to be more reliable borrowers (higher repayment rates), but they also tend to channel the proceeds of their economic activities more directly into family welfare, particularly the health and education of their children, creating a stronger developmental impact.

172. Compared to an ISI strategy, a successful EOI strategy generally requires:
a) Higher tariffs and more stringent import quotas.
b) A greater focus on maintaining a competitive or even undervalued exchange rate.
c) A domestic market large enough to absorb all industrial output.
d) Isolation from global technological trends.

Answer: b) A greater focus on maintaining a competitive or even undervalued exchange rate.
Explanation: For an EOI strategy to work, a country's exports must be price-competitive in global markets. A key policy tool for achieving this is managing the exchange rate to prevent it from appreciating and making exports too expensive. An overvalued exchange rate, sometimes used in ISI to cheapen capital imports, is detrimental to EOI.

173. The term "global value chain" refers to:
a) The increasing homogenization of cultural values worldwide.
b) The process where different stages of the production of a single good are located across different countries.
c) The total value of goods and services traded internationally.
d) A list of ethical principles for multinational corporations.

Answer: b) The process where different stages of the production of a single good are located across different countries.
Explanation: This is the definition of a global value chain or global supply chain. For example, a smartphone might have its R&D done in the US, key components manufactured in South Korea and Taiwan, be assembled in China, and marketed globally. 174. A potential negative consequence of the rise of the "gig economy," enabled by globalization and digital platforms, is:
a) A decrease in flexibility for businesses.
b) A reduction in the need for specialized skills.
c) An increase in worker benefits like health insurance and paid leave.

Answer: d) An erosion of job security and social safety nets for workers engaged in short-term, contract-based work.
Explanation: While the gig economy offers flexibility, a major concern is that gig workers are often classified as independent contractors rather than employees. This means they typically lack access to the benefits and protections associated with traditional employment, such as minimum wage guarantees, sick pay, pensions, and unemployment insurance, leading to greater economic insecurity.

175. The "race to the bottom" argument regarding globalization suggests that countries might:
a) Compete to raise their environmental and labor standards to attract ethical investors.
b) Compete by lowering their environmental and labor standards to attract foreign investment and reduce production costs.
c) Engage in competitive devaluations of their currencies.
d) Invest heavily in education to create the most skilled workforce.

Answer: b) Compete by lowering their environmental and labor standards to attract foreign investment and reduce production costs.
Explanation: This is a critical argument against certain aspects of globalization. The fear is that in a globally competitive environment, multinational corporations will choose to invest in countries with the weakest regulations (and thus lowest costs). This could create pressure on all countries to lower their standards to remain competitive, leading to a "race to the bottom" in terms of environmental protection and worker rights.

176. Which of the following is a primary driver of financial globalization?
a) The widespread adoption of fixed exchange rate systems.
b) The liberalization of capital controls by many countries, allowing for freer movement of investment funds.
c) An increase in global trade barriers.
d) The decline of multinational corporations.

Answer: b) The liberalization of capital controls by many countries, allowing for freer movement of investment funds.
Explanation: Financial globalization requires capital to be able to move across borders easily. For much of the mid-20th century, many countries had strict capital controls. The progressive removal or loosening of these controls, combined with new technology, is what enabled the massive increase in international financial flows (FDI, portfolio investment) that characterizes modern globalization.

177. A significant difference between "outsourcing" and "offshoring" is that:
a) Offshoring must involve a third-party firm, while outsourcing does not.
b) Outsourcing can be done domestically, while offshoring specifically means moving the business activity to another country.
c) Outsourcing is legal, while offshoring is not.
d) Offshoring always results in lower costs, while outsourcing always results in higher quality.

Answer: b) Outsourcing can be done domestically, while offshoring specifically means moving the business activity to another country.
Explanation: This is the key distinction. Outsourcing is about who does the work (the firm itself vs. a third party). Offshoring is about where the work is done (the home country vs. a foreign country). A firm can outsource to another company in the same city (not offshoring) or offshore by setting up its own factory in another country (not outsourcing).

Chapter 16: Environmental Economics and Resource Management

178. The primary market failure associated with public goods like clean air is the:
a) Tragedy of the Commons, because clean air is rivalrous.
b) Negative externality problem, where property rights are too strong.
c) Asymmetric information problem between consumers and producers.
d) Free-rider problem, stemming from the non-excludability of the good.

Answer: d) The free-rider problem, stemming from the non-excludability of the good.
Explanation: Clean air is non-excludable (you cannot stop someone from breathing it) and non-rivalrous. The market fails to provide/protect it because private firms cannot charge individuals for it. Individuals, in turn, have no incentive to pay for its upkeep (e.g., by paying to reduce pollution) if they believe others will do it, as they can "free ride" on the benefits.

179. A government trying to value the potential damage to a coral reef from a proposed coastal development asks residents and tourists in a survey how much they would be willing to pay annually to a conservation fund to prevent the damage. This valuation method is known as:
a) The Travel Cost Method.
b) Hedonic Pricing.
c) The Contingent Valuation Method.
d) Cost-Benefit Analysis.

Answer: c) The Contingent Valuation Method.
Explanation: Contingent Valuation is a stated preference method that uses surveys to create a hypothetical market. It directly asks people to state their willingness to pay (WTP) for a non-market good (like a protected reef) or willingness to accept (WTA) compensation for its loss.

180. A key difficulty in applying Cost-Benefit Analysis (CBA) to long-term environmental projects, such as climate change mitigation, is:
a) The inability to identify any of the costs.
b) The choice of an appropriate discount rate to value future costs and benefits.
c) The certainty of future technological breakthroughs.
d) The lack of any benefits from such projects.

Answer: b) The choice of an appropriate discount rate to value future costs and benefits.
Explanation: A discount rate is used to translate future values into present-day terms. A high discount rate makes future costs and benefits seem very small today. For climate change, the benefits of action are far in the future, while costs are immediate. Using a high discount rate can make even catastrophic future damages appear insignificant, thus justifying inaction. Choosing a low discount rate gives more weight to the welfare of future generations. This choice is highly contentious and has a massive impact on the CBA result.

181. Individual Transferable Quotas (ITQs) in a fishery are a policy tool designed to solve:
a) The problem of positive externalities.
b) The tragedy of the commons by creating a market-based solution.
c) The problem of unemployment among fishermen.
d) The need for government revenue.

Answer: b) The tragedy of the commons by creating a market-based solution.
Explanation: A fishery is a classic common-pool resource prone to overfishing (Tragedy of the Commons). ITQs address this by (1) setting a total allowable catch (a cap) and (2) creating property rights in the form of quotas (the right to catch a share of the total). Because these quotas are tradable, they create a market that encourages efficiency, as fishermen with lower costs can buy quotas from those with higher costs, and all have an interest in preserving the stock to maintain the value of their quotas.

182. According to the Coase Theorem, which of the following is NOT a necessary condition for private bargaining to solve an externality problem efficiently?
a) Clearly defined and enforceable property rights.
b) Low or zero transaction (bargaining) costs.
c) A small number of parties involved.
d) The government must assign the property right to the party that is being harmed.

Answer: d) The government must assign the property right to the party that is being harmed.
Explanation: The Coase Theorem's surprising insight is that the initial assignment of the property right does not matter for achieving the efficient outcome (though it does affect the distribution of wealth). As long as the right is clearly defined and transaction costs are low, the parties can bargain to the efficient solution regardless of whether the polluter has the "right to pollute" or the victim has the "right to a clean environment."

185. A profit-maximizing monopolist and a perfectly competitive firm both produce where MR=MC. Why does this lead to allocative efficiency (P=MC) in perfect competition but not in monopoly?
a) Because for the competitive firm P=MR, while for the monopolist P>MR.
b) Because monopolists face higher costs than competitive firms.
c) Because competitive firms earn zero economic profit in the long run.
d) Because the government regulates the competitive firm but not the monopolist.

Answer: a) Because for the competitive firm P=MR, while for the monopolist P>MR.
Explanation: Both firms follow the same profit-maximizing rule. However, their revenue structures differ. The perfectly competitive firm is a price taker, so P=MR. Thus, its profit-maximizing condition (MR=MC) becomes P=MC, which is also the condition for allocative efficiency. The monopolist faces a downward-sloping demand curve, so P>MR. Its profit-maximizing condition (MR=MC) therefore results in an outcome where P>MC, which is allocatively inefficient.

186. The concept of "Dutch Disease," a potential aspect of the Resource Curse, describes a situation where:
a) A country's reliance on agriculture leads to falling terms of trade.
b) A boom in a natural resource export sector causes the country's currency to appreciate, making its other export sectors (like manufacturing) uncompetitive.
c) A country's human capital is depleted by disease, reducing productivity.
d) High tariffs lead to inefficient domestic industries.

Answer: b) A boom in a natural resource export sector causes the country's currency to appreciate, making its other export sectors (like manufacturing) uncompetitive.
Explanation: Dutch Disease is a specific economic mechanism of the resource curse. A boom in one export sector (e.g., oil) leads to a large inflow of foreign currency. This increases the demand for the domestic currency, causing it to appreciate. The strong currency then makes the country's other tradable goods (e.g., from manufacturing or agriculture) more expensive and uncompetitive on world markets, causing those sectors to shrink.

c) A strengthening of monopolies and an increase in price floors.
d) An increase in structural unemployment and a decrease in frictional unemployment.

Answer: b) A rightward shift of the SRAS and LRAS curves, and made many markets more competitive.
Explanation: The internet represents a major technological advance and an improvement in efficiency. This increases productivity, shifting both the SRAS and LRAS curves to the right. Furthermore, by making it easier for consumers to compare prices and for new firms to enter markets (e-commerce), it has increased competition in many industries, pushing them closer to the perfectly competitive model.

188. A central bank can create money, but commercial banks can also create money through a different process. The central bank creates ________, while commercial banks create ________.
a) credit money; fiat money
b) near money; high-powered money
c) high-powered money (the monetary base); bank money (demand deposits) via the credit multiplier.
d) commodity money; fiat money

Answer: c) high-powered money (the monetary base); bank money (demand deposits) via the credit multiplier.
Explanation: The central bank has a monopoly on creating "high-powered money" or the monetary base (M0)—physical currency and the reserves held by commercial banks. Commercial banks, through the process of fractional-reserve banking, create "bank money" by making loans, which generate new demand deposits. This process is determined by the credit multiplier.

189. If an economy's government has a policy of consistently monetizing its large fiscal deficits, the most predictable long-run consequence according to the Quantity Theory of Money would be:
a) A stable and low rate of inflation.
b) A steadily appreciating currency.
c) High and potentially accelerating inflation.
d) A fall in the natural rate of unemployment.

Answer: c) High and potentially accelerating inflation.
Explanation: Monetizing the deficit means the central bank is effectively printing money to finance government spending. This leads to a sustained and rapid increase in the money supply (M). According to the Quantity Theory of Money (MV=PY), a rapid increase in M, with V and Y (real output) growing much more slowly, will lead directly to a rapid increase in the price level (P), i.e., high inflation. This is the root cause of hyperinflation.

190. How can the concept of opportunity cost be used to explain the law of supply?
a) The opportunity cost of leisure increases as wages rise, so people supply more labor.
b) The opportunity cost of producing one good increases as more of another is produced, limiting supply.
c) As a firm produces more of a good, its marginal cost rises due to diminishing returns. This rising marginal cost represents the opportunity cost of producing that extra unit, so the firm will only supply it at a higher price.
d) The opportunity cost of buying a good falls as its price falls, increasing demand.

Answer: c) As a firm produces more of a good, its marginal cost rises due to diminishing returns. This rising marginal cost represents the opportunity cost of producing that extra unit, so the firm will only supply it at a higher price.
Explanation: This provides a deeper explanation for the upward-sloping supply curve. A firm's marginal cost curve is its supply curve. Marginal cost itself reflects opportunity cost—the value of the resources (which could have been used for something else) needed to produce one more unit. Due to diminishing returns, the marginal cost of production rises. A firm will only be willing to supply an additional, more costly unit if the price it receives is high enough to cover this higher opportunity cost.

191. A government implements a policy to retrain coal miners for jobs in the renewable energy sector. This policy is primarily aimed at reducing:

Since structural unemployment is a component of the Natural Rate of Unemployment (NRU), this supply-side policy aims to lower the NRU.

193. The Environmental Kuznets Curve (EKC) hypothesis is most directly challenged by which global environmental problem?
a) Local river pollution from a specific factory.
b) Urban smog in developing country megacities.
c) Deforestation for subsistence agriculture.
d) The accumulation of greenhouse gases like CO2 in the atmosphere.

Answer: d) The accumulation of greenhouse gases like CO2 in the atmosphere.
Explanation: The EKC suggests an inverted-U relationship where environmental damage first rises and then falls as income grows. 194. The theory of comparative advantage assumes ________, while the Heckscher-Ohlin model attempts to explain comparative advantage based on differences in ________.
a) different technologies; factor endowments
b) perfect competition; monopoly power
c) fixed exchange rates; floating exchange rates
d) free trade; protectionism

Answer: a) different technologies; factor endowments
Explanation: The original Ricardian model of comparative advantage implicitly assumes that countries have different levels of labor productivity (i.e., different technologies) which creates the basis for trade. The Heckscher-Ohlin model takes the theory a step further by assuming similar technologies across countries and arguing that comparative advantage instead arises from differences in countries' endowments of factors of production (e.g., one country is rich in capital, another is rich in labor).

195. Consider a market for an inferior good. If a country experiences a simultaneous economic boom (rising incomes) and an improvement in the technology for producing the good, the equilibrium quantity will:
a) definitely increase.
b) definitely decrease.
c) remain the same.
d) be ambiguous.

Answer: d) be ambiguous.
Explanation: Two simultaneous shifts are occurring. 1) Rising incomes cause the demand for an inferior good to decrease, shifting the demand curve to the left. 2) Improved technology lowers production costs, shifting the supply curve to the right.

Leftward demand shift: Price↓, Quantity↓.

Rightward supply shift: Price↓, Quantity↑.

196. Which scenario represents a movement ALONG the aggregate demand curve, rather than a shift of the curve?

Explanation: The aggregate demand curve shows the relationship between the overall price level and the quantity of real GDP demanded. A movement along the curve is caused by a change in the price level itself. The real balance effect described in (b) is one of the three key reasons (along with the interest rate effect and exchange rate effect) why the AD curve has a negative slope. The other options describe non-price-level changes that would shift the entire AD curve.

197. A key weakness of the Import Substitution Industrialization (ISI) strategy that the Export-Oriented Industrialization (EOI) strategy aimed to overcome was:
a) The neglect of the agricultural sector.
b) The inherent inefficiency and lack of innovation in firms that are perpetually sheltered from international competition.

Explanation: While ISI could foster some industrialization, the lack of competitive pressure from global markets often meant that domestic firms had little incentive to innovate, improve quality, or control costs. They could survive and be profitable behind high tariff walls. EOI, by contrast, forces firms to compete on the world stage, making efficiency, quality, and innovation essential for survival and success.

199. A primary difference between fiscal policy and monetary policy is that:
a) Fiscal policy affects aggregate supply, while monetary policy affects aggregate demand.
b) Fiscal policy is conducted by the central bank, while monetary policy is conducted by the government's ministry of finance.
c) Fiscal policy involves the tools of government spending and taxation, while monetary policy involves tools that control the money supply and interest rates.
d) Fiscal policy is effective in the long run, while monetary policy is only effective in the short run.

Answer: c) Fiscal policy involves the tools of government spending and taxation, while monetary policy involves tools that control the money supply and interest rates.
Explanation: This is the fundamental definition and distinction between the two main levers of macroeconomic policy. The government controls the budget (spending and taxes - fiscal policy). The (typically independent) central bank controls the monetary base and influences interest rates (monetary policy).

200. A technological innovation makes capital significantly more productive. In the loanable funds market model, this would lead to:
a) A rightward shift of the supply of loanable funds curve, and a lower interest rate.
b) A leftward shift of the supply of loanable funds curve, and a higher interest rate.
c) A rightward shift of the demand for loanable funds curve, and a higher interest rate.
d) A leftward shift of the demand for loanable funds curve, and a lower interest rate.

Answer: c) A rightward shift of the demand for loanable funds curve, and a higher interest rate.
Explanation: The demand for loanable funds comes from investment. If capital becomes more productive, the expected return on investment projects increases. This will make firms more willing to borrow money at any given interest rate to finance new capital investment. This is represented as a rightward shift of the demand for loanable funds curve, which results in a higher equilibrium real interest rate.

Chapter 1: Introduction to Economics and Basic Concepts

201. Which of the following statements represents a normative economic statement, as opposed to a positive one?
a) An increase in the minimum wage leads to an increase in unemployment among teenagers.
b) The government should increase its spending on education to reduce inequality.
c) The central bank's reduction in interest rates caused an increase in investment.
d) The price elasticity of demand for gasoline is approximately -0.2.

Answer: b) The government should increase its spending on education to reduce inequality.
Explanation: Positive statements are objective, testable claims about "what is." Normative statements are subjective, value-based judgments about "what should be." Statement (b) expresses an opinion or policy recommendation based on a value judgment (that reducing inequality is a desirable goal), making it normative. The other statements are positive because they are, in principle, testable with data.

202. The factor of production "Enterprise" is rewarded with profit. This profit is primarily a compensation for:
a) Owning natural resources and renting them out.
b) Performing routine managerial tasks within a firm.
c) Undertaking the uninsurable risks of business and organizing other factors of production.
d) Providing the initial financial capital for a business venture.

Answer: c) Undertaking the uninsurable risks of business and organizing other factors of production.
Explanation: While managers earn salaries (labor) and capital earns interest, the unique role of the entrepreneur is to innovate and bear the uncertainty and risks of the business for which the outcome is unknown. Profit is the residual reward for successfully navigating these risks and effectively combining land, labor, and capital.

203. An economy experiences both a devastating war that destroys factories and a simultaneous outbreak of a disease that reduces its labor force. On its Production Possibility Curve (PPC), this would be represented as:
a) A movement from a point on the curve to a point inside the curve.
b) An unambiguous inward shift of the entire curve.
c) A pivoted shift of the curve along only one axis.
d) An outward shift of the curve due to post-war rebuilding efforts.

Answer: b) An unambiguous inward shift of the entire curve.
Explanation: The PPC's position is determined by the available factors of production and technology. The destruction of factories reduces the economy's capital stock. The reduction in the labor force reduces the labor input. Since both key factors of production have decreased, the entire production capacity of the economy has shrunk, leading to a parallel or non-parallel inward shift of the PPC.

Chapter 2: Demand, Supply, and Market Equilibrium

204. The government replaces a per-unit tax on producers of a good with a per-unit subsidy of the exact same amount. How will the new deadweight loss (DWL) compare to the old one?
a) The DWL will be the same, as the size of the government intervention is the same.
b) The DWL from the subsidy will be larger than the DWL from the tax.
c) There will be no DWL with the subsidy, as it benefits both consumers and producers.
d) A subsidy can also create a deadweight loss, as it encourages production and consumption beyond the socially efficient level.

Answer: d) A subsidy can also create a deadweight loss, as it encourages production and consumption beyond the socially efficient level.
Explanation: Both taxes and subsidies create deadweight loss by distorting the market from its efficient equilibrium. A tax reduces the quantity traded below the efficient level. A subsidy increases the quantity traded beyond the efficient level, to a point where the marginal social cost of production exceeds the marginal social benefit of consumption. This overproduction also results in a net welfare loss (DWL).

205. The demand for insulin is highly inelastic, while the supply is relatively elastic. If a specific tax is imposed on the production of insulin, the tax incidence will be such that:
a) Producers bear almost the entire burden of the tax.
b) The burden is shared equally between producers and consumers.
c) The government collects no tax revenue.
d) Consumers bear almost the entire burden of the tax.

Answer: d) Consumers bear almost the entire burden of the tax.
Explanation: The burden of a tax falls most heavily on the side of the market that is less elastic (less price-sensitive). Since demand is highly inelastic, consumers will continue to buy insulin even at a much higher price. Since supply is elastic, producers are very sensitive to the net price they receive. Therefore, producers will be able to pass on nearly the entire tax to consumers without losing many sales.

Chapter 3: Perfect Competition and Monopoly

207. A firm in a perfectly competitive market is producing at a quantity where P > MC. The firm can increase its profit by:
a) Increasing its output.
b) Decreasing its output.
c) Increasing its price.
d) Decreasing its fixed costs.

Answer: a) Increasing its output.
Explanation: The profit-maximization rule is to produce where P = MR = MC. If P > MC, it means that the price (and thus the marginal revenue) from selling one more unit is greater than the marginal cost of producing it. Therefore, producing and selling that additional unit will add more to total revenue than to total cost, increasing total profit. The firm should expand production.

208. A key difference between a monopoly that practices perfect price discrimination and a monopoly that charges a single price is that the perfect price discriminator:
a) Earns less profit.
b) Produces a smaller quantity of output.
c) Creates no consumer surplus and has no deadweight loss.
d) Faces a horizontal demand curve.

Answer: c) Creates no consumer surplus and has no deadweight loss.
Explanation: A perfect price discriminator charges each consumer the maximum price they are willing to pay. This means all consumer surplus is extracted and converted into monopolist profit. Because the monopolist has an incentive to sell to every customer whose willingness to pay is above the marginal cost (since MR=P), it will produce the same quantity as a perfectly competitive industry (where P=MC). By producing the allocatively efficient quantity, there is no deadweight loss.

209. If a government imposes a lump-sum tax (a fixed cost that does not vary with output) on a monopolist, how will this affect the monopolist's profit-maximizing price and quantity in the short run?
a) Both price and quantity will decrease.
b) Price will increase, and quantity will decrease.
c) Neither price nor quantity will change.
d) Price will decrease, and quantity will increase.

Answer: c) Neither price nor quantity will change.
Explanation: The profit-maximization rule is MR=MC. A lump-sum tax is a fixed cost. It increases Average Total Cost (ATC) but does not affect Marginal Cost (MC) or Marginal Revenue (MR). Since the MR and MC curves have not shifted, their intersection point remains the same, and the profit-maximizing quantity and the price charged for that quantity will not change. The monopolist's total profit will, however, decrease.

Chapter 4: Monopolistic Competition and Oligopoly

210. Which of the following is NOT a characteristic of monopolistic competition?
a) A large number of firms.
b) Differentiated products.
c) Significant barriers to entry.
d) The use of advertising and branding.

Answer: c) Significant barriers to entry.
Explanation: A key feature of monopolistic competition, similar to perfect competition, is the absence of significant barriers to entry. This free entry is what ensures that any short-run supernormal profits are competed away in the long run as new firms enter the market. Monopoly and Oligopoly are characterized by significant entry barriers.

211. In the dominant firm price leadership model of oligopoly:
a) The dominant firm sets its price to drive all smaller firms out of business.
b) The smaller "fringe" firms act as price makers, and the dominant firm is a price taker.
c) The dominant firm calculates the residual demand left after the fringe firms have supplied their profit-maximizing quantity, and then acts like a monopolist on that residual demand.
d) All firms in the industry formally meet to set the price.

Answer: c) The dominant firm calculates the residual demand left after the fringe firms have supplied their profit-maximizing quantity, and then acts like a monopolist on that residual demand.
Explanation: This describes the mechanics of the price leadership model. The dominant firm knows that the small fringe firms will act as price takers and supply whatever quantity is profitable for them at a given price. The dominant firm subtracts this fringe supply from the total market demand curve to derive its own "residual" demand curve. It then uses this residual curve to set its own profit-maximizing price and quantity (MR=MC), and the fringe firms follow that price.

212. In game theory, a "dominant strategy" for a player is a strategy that:
a) Leads to the best possible outcome for the player, regardless of what other players do.
b) Guarantees a win for the player.
c) Is the best response to the other players' dominant strategies.
d) Is chosen in a Nash Equilibrium.

Answer: a) Leads to the best possible outcome for the player, regardless of what other players do.
Explanation: A dominant strategy is powerful because a player should play it no matter what they believe their opponent will do. It is their best choice in every possible contingency. Not all games have dominant strategies for players, but when they do, they simplify the analysis. A Nash Equilibrium may or may not involve players using dominant strategies.

Chapter 5: Consumer Theory

213. A consumer's indifference map consists of a series of parallel, downward-sloping straight lines. This implies that the two goods are:
a) Perfect complements.
b) Perfect substitutes.
c) Inferior goods.
d) Unrelated goods.

Answer: b) Perfect substitutes.
Explanation: Straight-line indifference curves imply a constant Marginal Rate of Substitution (MRS). This means the consumer is always willing to trade one good for the other at the same fixed rate, which is the definition of perfect substitutes.

214. According to the Revealed Preference theory, if a consumer chooses bundle A over bundle B when both were affordable, we can infer that:
a) Bundle A has a higher marginal utility than bundle B.
b) The consumer is irrational.
c) Bundle A is on a higher indifference curve than bundle B.
d) The consumer prefers bundle A to bundle B (or is indifferent), and this preference has been "revealed" by their choice.

Answer: d) The consumer prefers bundle A to bundle B (or is indifferent), and this preference has been "revealed" by their choice.
Explanation: Revealed preference theory avoids making unobservable claims about utility or indifference curves. It relies solely on observed behavior. The act of choosing A when B was also in the affordable set is taken as direct evidence that A is preferred to B.

215. If a consumer's marginal utility for the last dollar spent on coffee is 12 utils, and their marginal utility for the last dollar spent on tea is 8 utils, they are:
a) At their consumer equilibrium.
b) Maximizing utility, as both are positive.
c) Not maximizing utility, and should consume more coffee and less tea.
d) Not maximizing utility, and should consume more tea and less coffee.

Answer: c) Not maximizing utility, and should consume more coffee and less tea.
Explanation: The rule for consumer equilibrium (the equi-marginal principle) is that the marginal utility per dollar spent should be equal for all goods (MUx/Px = MUy/Py). Since the MU per dollar for coffee (12) is greater than the MU per dollar for tea (8), the consumer can increase total utility by reallocating their spending away from tea and towards coffee.

Chapter 6: Producer Theory

216. A firm's Average Fixed Cost (AFC) curve is a rectangular hyperbola. This is because:
a) Fixed costs increase at a decreasing rate.
b) Total fixed cost is constant, so as quantity (Q) increases, the ratio TFC/Q decreases proportionately.
c) The law of diminishing returns causes fixed costs to fall.
d) Marginal cost is always below average fixed cost.

Answer: b) Total fixed cost is constant, so as quantity (Q) increases, the ratio TFC/Q decreases proportionately.
Explanation: A rectangular hyperbola is a curve where the product of the x and y coordinates is constant (in this case, Q * AFC = TFC). Since Total Fixed Cost (TFC) is, by definition, a constant amount, AFC must fall continuously as the fixed cost is "spread" over a larger and larger quantity of output.

217. In production theory, the long run is defined as a period of time in which:
a) The firm can vary all its inputs, including its plant size.
b) All costs are fixed.
c) The firm is making a profit.
d) At least one input is fixed, but some are variable.

Answer: a) The firm can vary all its inputs, including its plant size.
Explanation: This is the key definition distinguishing the short run from the long run in producer theory. The short run has at least one fixed factor (usually capital/plant size). The long run is a planning horizon long enough for the firm to change the quantities of all factors of production. There are no fixed costs in the long run.

218. If a firm is operating in the region of its LRAC curve that exhibits constant returns to scale, this implies that:
a) Doubling all inputs will lead to a more-than-proportional increase in output.
b) The firm has reached its maximum possible size.
c) The LRAC curve is flat, and doubling all inputs will lead to a doubling of output.
d) The firm should shut down in the long run.

Answer: c) The LRAC curve is flat, and doubling all inputs will lead to a doubling of output.
Explanation: Constant returns to scale means that a proportional change in all inputs leads to an exactly proportional change in output. This occurs on the flat, minimum portion of the U-shaped LRAC curve, where economies of scale have been exhausted but diseconomies have not yet begun.

Chapter 7: National Income Accounting

219. An increase in the imputed rental value of owner-occupied housing would:
a) Decrease GDP as it is a non-market transaction.
b) Increase GDP, as it is included in both the income (rental income) and expenditure (consumption of housing services) approaches.
c) Have no effect on GDP but would increase GNP.
d) Be recorded as an increase in gross investment.

Answer: b) Increase GDP, as it is included in both the income (rental income) and expenditure (consumption of housing services) approaches.
Explanation: To ensure GDP is comprehensive, national income accountants "impute" or estimate the rental value of homes occupied by their owners. This value is counted as if the homeowner is renting the house to themselves. It enters the expenditure approach as part of consumption (C) and the income approach as part of rental income, thus increasing the measure of GDP.

220. Why might the GDP deflator show a different inflation rate than the Consumer Price Index (CPI)?
a) The CPI includes the prices of capital goods and exports, while the GDP deflator does not.
b) The price of imported consumer goods, like electronics, might rise sharply, which would increase the CPI but not directly affect the GDP deflator.
c) The GDP deflator uses a fixed basket of goods, making it less accurate than the CPI.
d) The CPI is measured monthly, while the GDP deflator is measured annually.

Answer: b) The price of imported consumer goods, like electronics, might rise sharply, which would increase the CPI but not directly affect the GDP deflator.
Explanation: This highlights a key difference in scope. The CPI measures a basket of goods consumed by households, including imports. The GDP deflator measures the prices of goods produced domestically. Therefore, a rise in the price of an imported good will show up in the CPI but not in the GDP deflator.

221. A country's Disposable Personal Income (DI) can be greater than its Gross Domestic Product (GDP) if:
a) This is mathematically impossible.
b) The sum of transfer payments received by households and factor income from abroad is exceptionally large, while taxes and depreciation are very low.
c) The country has a very large trade surplus.
d) The country has a very high personal savings rate.

Answer: b) The sum of transfer payments received by households and factor income from abroad is exceptionally large, while taxes and depreciation are very low.
Explanation: While unusual, it's theoretically possible. To get from GDP to DI, you make many subtractions (depreciation, indirect taxes, corporate taxes, retained earnings, personal taxes) and additions (NFIA, transfer payments). If the additions (especially massive government transfers) are larger than all the subtractions, DI could exceed GDP. This might happen in a country with a large diaspora sending remittances (counted in transfers) and a government running huge welfare programs financed by past wealth or borrowing.

Chapter 8: Money, Banking, and Fiscal Policy

222. Which of the following describes the function of money as a "standard of deferred payment"?
a) Using cash to buy groceries at a store.
b) Quoting the price of a car in dollars.
c) Taking out a loan with the promise to repay the principal plus interest in the future.
d) Keeping cash under a mattress for a future emergency.

Answer: c) Taking out a loan with the promise to repay the principal plus interest in the future.
Explanation: This function relates to time. Money provides a standard unit for denominating future payments and obligations. Credit and debt contracts are specified in monetary terms because money serves this function.

223. The monetary base (M0) is often called "high-powered money" because:
a) It is the largest measure of the money supply.
b) It is directly spent by the government on high-powered projects.
c) Every dollar of the monetary base held as bank reserves can support a multiple-dollar expansion of the broader money supply through the credit creation process.
d) It is the only measure of money that is fully backed by gold.

Answer: c) Every dollar of the monetary base held as bank reserves can support a multiple-dollar expansion of the broader money supply through the credit creation process.
Explanation: The "high power" refers to its leveraged effect. The monetary base (currency + bank reserves) is the base upon which commercial banks create a much larger volume of bank money (demand deposits) via the money multiplier. Thus, a small change in the monetary base controlled by the central bank can have a large, "high-powered" effect on M1, M2, etc.

224. Which of the following is an example of a government's "capital expenditure"?
a) Paying the monthly salaries of public school teachers.
b) Making interest payments on outstanding government bonds.
c) Providing a subsidy to farmers.
d) Funding the construction of a new national highway.

Answer: d) Funding the construction of a new national highway.
Explanation: Capital expenditure is spending that creates a new physical asset or reduces a liability. The construction of a highway creates a long-lasting physical asset. The other options are examples of recurrent expenditure: (a) is a payment for ongoing services, (b) is a financing cost, and (c) is a transfer.

Chapter 9: Classical and Keynesian Models

225. According to the Keynesian "Paradox of Thrift," a widespread attempt by all households to increase their saving simultaneously will:
a) Lead to a large increase in national savings and investment.
b) Have no effect on the economy, as saving equals investment.
c) Lead to a decrease in aggregate demand, a fall in national income, and potentially no change or even a fall in total savings.
d) Cause a large increase in interest rates.

Answer: c) Lead to a decrease in aggregate demand, a fall in national income, and potentially no change or even a fall in total savings.
Explanation: In the Keynesian model, an increase in saving means a decrease in consumption. This fall in aggregate demand leads to a multiplied fall in national income. Since savings depend on income (S = -a + MPS*Y), the fall in income can be so large that the total amount of saving in the economy actually decreases, even though the marginal propensity to save has increased.

226. In the AD/AS framework, the "long run" is defined as the period in which:
a) All prices and nominal wages are fully flexible and have adjusted to their equilibrium levels.
b) The aggregate supply curve is upward sloping.
c) Cyclical unemployment is high.
d) Government fiscal policy is ineffective.

Answer: a) All prices and nominal wages are fully flexible and have adjusted to their equilibrium levels.
Explanation: The key difference between the SRAS and LRAS is the assumption about price flexibility. In the long run, all prices are assumed to have had enough time to adjust to any shocks. This means that output is determined by the economy's real productive capacity (its factors of production and technology), not by the price level, which is why the LRAS is vertical.

227. A key difference in the policy implications of the Classical and Keynesian models is that:
a) Classical economists advocate for active fiscal policy, while Keynesians advocate for a laissez-faire approach.
b) Keynesians believe monetary policy is always more effective than fiscal policy.
c) Keynesians argue for active government intervention to manage aggregate demand, while Classical economists believe markets are self-correcting and advocate for minimal intervention.
d) Both models conclude that government intervention is always necessary to achieve full employment.

Answer: c) Keynesians argue for active government intervention to manage aggregate demand, while Classical economists believe markets are self-correcting and advocate for minimal intervention.
Explanation: This is the central policy debate between the two schools. Classical theory, with Say's Law and flexible prices, implies the economy will fix itself. Keynesian theory, with its focus on sticky wages and aggregate demand failures, implies that government must step in with fiscal or monetary policy to restore full employment during a recession.

Chapter 10: Inflation, Unemployment, and Business Cycles

232. An import quota is often considered more detrimental to an importing country than a tariff that restricts imports by the same amount because:
a) The quota is more difficult to administer.
b) With a quota, the "quota rent" (extra profit from the artificially high price) may be captured by foreign exporters, whereas with a tariff, the government captures it as revenue.

Answer: b) With a quota, the "quota rent" (extra profit from the artificially high price) may be captured by foreign exporters, whereas with a tariff, the government captures it as revenue.

236. The "J-Curve" effect describes the phenomenon where, after a currency depreciation:

d) GDP first rises and then falls.

Answer: b) The current account balance first worsens for a period before it begins to improve.
Explanation: This effect occurs because in the short run, import and export quantities are often inelastic (contracts are in place, consumers are slow to change habits). c) Frictional unemployment involves a mismatch of skills, while structural involves the time taken for job searching.

Answer: d) Frictional unemployment relates to the normal churn of job searching, while structural unemployment relates to a fundamental mismatch between workers' skills and the jobs available.
Explanation: This correctly distinguishes the two. Frictional unemployment is about the time it takes to match an available worker with an available job. Structural unemployment is about the lack of a suitable match because the worker doesn't have the skills the employer needs, or is in the wrong location.

321. According to the original Short-Run Phillips Curve, a government could achieve a lower rate of unemployment by:
a) Accepting a higher rate of inflation.
b) Implementing supply-side policies to reduce the NRU.
c) Ensuring the money supply grows at a steady rate.
d) Balancing the government budget.

Answer: a) Accepting a higher rate of inflation.
Explanation: The original SRPC showed a stable, inverse trade-off between inflation and unemployment. The policy implication was that policymakers could "choose" a point on the curve, for example, by using expansionary demand-side policies to lower unemployment, with the known consequence of generating a higher rate of inflation.

322. At the peak of a business cycle, the economy is likely experiencing:
a) A high unemployment rate and falling GDP.
b) A large recessionary gap.
c) A low unemployment rate and potentially rising inflationary pressures.
d) A trough in economic activity.

Answer: c) A low unemployment rate and potentially rising inflationary pressures.
Explanation: The peak is the highest point of an economic expansion. At this point, real GDP is at its maximum for that cycle, the economy is operating at or near its full capacity, and firms are employing a large number of workers, leading to low unemployment. This high level of activity and demand often leads to demand-pull inflationary pressures.

Chapter 11: International Trade Theory

323. "Trade Creation" is a potential benefit of a regional trade bloc. It occurs when:
a) The bloc creates new jobs in all member countries.
b) High-cost domestic production in a member country is replaced by lower-cost imports from another member country.
c) Trade is diverted from an efficient non-member to an inefficient member.
d) The bloc as a whole increases its total exports to the rest of the world.

Answer: b) High-cost domestic production in a member country is replaced by lower-cost imports from another member country.
Explanation: Trade creation is the "good" side of a regional trade agreement. Before the bloc, a country might have protected its own inefficient domestic industry with a high tariff. After joining the bloc and removing tariffs on member goods, it now imports the good from a more efficient, lower-cost producer within the bloc. This represents a shift towards a more efficient allocation of resources.

324. A tariff and a quota that restrict imports to the same quantity will have different effects on government revenue. Specifically:
a) The tariff generates revenue, while the quota does not (unless licenses are auctioned).
b) The quota generates revenue, while the tariff does not.
c) Both generate the same amount of revenue.
d) Neither generates any revenue.

Answer: a) The tariff generates revenue, while the quota does not (unless licenses are auctioned).
Explanation: A tariff is a tax. The revenue from this tax (the per-unit tariff multiplied by the quantity of imports) is collected by the government. A quota simply limits the quantity. The higher price that results from this scarcity creates an extra profit (a "quota rent"), which is typically captured by the importers or foreign exporters, not the government.

325. Which of the following is an example of a transaction that would be recorded in a country's Capital Account (under the modern IMF definition)?

b) A domestic citizen sending a cash gift to a relative abroad.
c) The sale of a patent from a domestic firm to a foreign firm.
d) The purchase of foreign government bonds by a domestic citizen.

Answer: c) The sale of a patent from a domestic firm to a foreign firm.
Explanation: The Capital Account is now a very small component that records transfers of non-produced, non-financial assets. Patents, copyrights, and trademarks fit this definition.
(a) and (d) are financial account transactions (FDI and portfolio investment, respectively). (b) is a current account transaction (secondary income/current transfer).

326. An increase in a country's real interest rates, relative to the rest of the world, would likely lead to:
a) An outflow of financial capital and a depreciation of its currency.
b) An outflow of financial capital and an appreciation of its currency.
c) An inflow of financial capital and a depreciation of its currency.
d) An inflow of financial capital and an appreciation of its currency.

Answer: d) An inflow of financial capital and an appreciation of its currency.
Explanation: Higher real interest rates mean a higher return on financial assets in that country. This attracts "hot money" and other portfolio investments from global investors seeking the best return. This inflow of capital increases the demand for the country's currency in the foreign exchange market, causing its value to appreciate.

Chapter 13: Concepts of Development

327. Economic growth is a necessary but not sufficient condition for economic development. This statement means that:
a) A country must have economic growth to achieve development, but growth alone does not guarantee development.
b) A country can have development without any economic growth.
c) Economic development and economic growth are the same concept.
d) A country only needs to focus on growth, and development will follow automatically.

Answer: a) A country must have economic growth to achieve development, but growth alone does not guarantee development.
Explanation: Development (improvements in health, education, poverty reduction) requires resources. Economic growth is what generates these resources (higher incomes, more government tax revenue). In this sense, growth is necessary. However, if the fruits of that growth are not used to fund social programs or if growth is highly unequal, then development may not occur. Therefore, growth is not sufficient on its own.

Chapter 14: Strategies for Economic Development

328. "Tied aid" is a form of foreign aid that is often criticized because it:
a) Must be repaid with interest.
b) Bypasses the recipient government and goes directly to households.
c) Is not large enough to make a difference.
d) Requires the recipient country to spend the aid funds on goods and services from the donor country, which may not be the cheapest or most appropriate.

Answer: d) Requires the recipient country to spend the aid funds on goods and services from the donor country, which may not be the cheapest or most appropriate.
Explanation: Tied aid reduces the value and effectiveness of the aid. It essentially acts as a subsidy for the donor country's own exporters. The recipient country is not free to source the best-value or most suitable equipment and expertise from the global market, meaning the aid money doesn't go as far as it could.

Chapter 16: Environmental Economics

330. An electricity generating plant that burns coal creates SO2 emissions that lead to acid rain hundreds of miles away. This is an example of:
a) A transboundary negative externality.
b) The tragedy of the commons.
c) A positive externality in consumption.
d) A pure public good.

Answer: a) A transboundary negative externality.
Explanation: The pollution is a negative externality because it imposes costs on third parties. Because the pollution crosses national or regional borders, it is a "transboundary" externality, which makes it particularly difficult to solve as it requires cooperation between different political jurisdictions.

331. A key ethical debate in using Cost-Benefit Analysis for environmental issues is:
a) Whether it is appropriate to attempt to place a monetary value on things like biodiversity, a pristine ecosystem, or a human life.
b) Whether benefits should be measured before or after taxes.

Synthesis and Application Questions (Across Multiple Chapters)

332. If a country devalues its currency as part of a fixed exchange rate system, what is the most likely intended short-run effect on aggregate demand?
a) Aggregate demand will decrease due to higher import costs.
b) Aggregate demand will increase due to a rise in net exports.
c) Aggregate demand will remain unchanged.
d) Aggregate supply will shift to the right.

Answer: b) Aggregate demand will increase due to a rise in net exports.
Explanation: Devaluation makes the country's currency cheaper. This boosts its exports (as they become cheaper for foreigners) and reduces its imports (as they become more expensive domestically). The resulting increase in net exports (X-M) is a direct injection into aggregate demand (AD = C+I+G+(X-M)), causing the AD curve to shift right.

333. A perfectly competitive firm is productively efficient in the long run, and a monopolist is not. A key reason for this is:
a) The pressure of competition and free entry forces the competitive firm to operate at the minimum point of its LRAC to survive, a pressure the monopolist does not face.
b) Monopolists are inherently wasteful, while competitive firms are not.
c) The government regulates the competitive firm's costs.
d) The monopolist's demand curve is perfectly inelastic.

Answer: a) The pressure of competition and free entry forces the competitive firm to operate at the minimum point of its LRAC to survive, a pressure the monopolist does not face.
Explanation: Productive efficiency means producing at the lowest possible long-run average cost. In perfect competition, any firm that does not achieve this minimum cost will be undercut on price by more efficient rivals and driven out of the market by the process of entry and exit. A monopolist, protected by barriers to entry, can earn supernormal profits without necessarily being as cost-efficient as possible.

334. If the government wants to reduce cyclical unemployment, a Keynesian economist would most likely recommend ________, while a Classical economist would most likely recommend ________.
a) cutting government spending; raising government spending.
b) raising interest rates; lowering interest rates.
c) increasing government spending; policies to increase wage and price flexibility and non-intervention.
d) implementing a tariff; removing all tariffs.

Answer: c) increasing government spending; policies to increase wage and price flexibility and non-intervention.
Explanation: Cyclical unemployment is caused by deficient aggregate demand. The Keynesian prescription is active intervention to boost AD, such as increasing government spending (G). The Classical view is that markets are self-correcting. They would argue the problem is not a lack of demand, but wage/price rigidities that prevent the market from clearing. Their recommendation would be to remove these rigidities (e.g., reduce union power, eliminate minimum wages) and then let the market adjust on its own.

335. Which of the following is both a stock variable and a component of the financial account of the Balance of Payments?
a) The annual flow of Foreign Direct Investment.
b) The monthly trade deficit.
c) The stock of official foreign exchange reserves at a point in time.
d) The annual Gross Domestic Product.

Answer: c) The stock of official foreign exchange reserves at a point in time.
Explanation: A stock variable is measured at a point in time, while a flow is measured over a period. GDP, the trade deficit, and the flow of FDI are all flow variables. The stock of reserves is measured on a specific day (e.g., as of Dec 31). The change in reserves is a flow variable that is recorded in the financial account. This question is tricky, linking the definition of stock/flow to BoP components.

336. The infant industry argument is to ________ as the tragedy of the commons is to ________.
a) perfect competition; monopoly
b) trade protectionism; common-pool resources
c) fiscal policy; monetary policy
d) positive externalities; negative externalities

Answer: b) trade protectionism; common-pool resources
Explanation: This question tests conceptual pairings. The infant industry argument is a primary justification used for policies of trade protectionism. The tragedy of the commons is the primary problem associated with the management of common-pool resources (like open-ocean fisheries).

337. The discovery of a vast, easily extractable oil reserve in a developing country with a floating exchange rate is most likely to lead to:
a) Dutch Disease and an appreciation of the currency.
b) A depreciation of the currency and cost-push inflation.
c) A shift of the LRPC to the left.
d) The adoption of an ISI strategy.

Answer: a) Dutch Disease and an appreciation of the currency.
Explanation: The new oil exports will cause a massive inflow of foreign currency, leading to a sharp appreciation of the domestic currency. This strong currency will, in turn, make the country's other tradable sectors (like agriculture and manufacturing) uncompetitive on global markets, causing them to shrink. This phenomenon is known as Dutch Disease, a key component of the resource curse.

338. A policy that provides subsidies for primary education creates a ________, which is meant to correct for a ________.
a) positive externality; negative externality
b) Pigouvian subsidy; positive externality
c) negative externality; Pigouvian tax
d) public good; free-rider problem

Answer: b) Pigouvian subsidy; positive externality
Explanation: Primary education is a classic example of a good with positive externalities (it benefits not just the student but society as a whole). Because of this, the market under-provides it. A subsidy designed to correct for a positive externality by encouraging more consumption/production is called a Pigouvian subsidy.

339. A country that has a comparative advantage in the production of all goods it produces is:
a) A mathematical impossibility.
b) Likely to be a large, developed country, but can still gain from trade.
c) The only country that can gain from trade.
d) Not a meaningful concept, as comparative advantage is about relative, not absolute, production possibilities.

Answer: d) Not a meaningful concept, as comparative advantage is about relative, not absolute, production possibilities.
Explanation: This question is a trick. A country can have an absolute advantage in all goods. But comparative advantage is about opportunity cost. It is mathematically impossible for one country to have a lower opportunity cost in all goods. If it has a lower opportunity cost in Good A, the other country must, by definition, have a lower opportunity cost in Good B.

340. A government simultaneously cuts taxes and reduces its own spending by the exact same amount. In the simple Keynesian model, what is the effect on aggregate demand?
a) Aggregate demand will remain unchanged.
b) Aggregate demand will increase.
c) Aggregate demand will decrease.
d) The effect is ambiguous.

Answer: c) Aggregate demand will decrease.
Explanation: This is explained by the balanced budget multiplier. A decrease in government spending (G) reduces AD directly, dollar-for-dollar. A cut in taxes (T) increases AD indirectly, but only by the amount of the tax cut multiplied by the MPC (since some is saved). Since MPC is less than 1, the initial boost to AD from the tax cut is smaller than the initial reduction in AD from the spending cut. Therefore, the net effect is a decrease in aggregate demand. The balanced budget multiplier is exactly 1, so a $100B cut in G and T will reduce GDP by $100B.

341. An increase in income inequality, holding average income constant, would likely:
a) Not be captured by GDP, but would increase the GINI coefficient.
b) Be captured by a fall in GDP, and would decrease the GINI coefficient.
c) Increase the HDI significantly.
d) Be a sign of economic development.

Answer: a) Not be captured by GDP, but would increase the GINI coefficient.
Explanation: GDP per capita is an average and is insensitive to changes in distribution. Therefore, GDP would not change. The GINI coefficient, however, is a direct measure of income inequality. An increase in inequality would cause the GINI coefficient to rise (move closer to 1).

342. Which action by a central bank is a "qualitative" or "selective" credit control, as opposed to a "quantitative" one?
a) Selling government securities on the open market.
b) Raising the required reserve ratio.
c) Issuing a directive to commercial banks to limit their lending for real estate speculation.
d) Raising the discount rate.

Answer: c) Issuing a directive to commercial banks to limit their lending for real estate speculation.
Explanation: Quantitative tools (a, b, d) affect the overall quantity or cost of money and credit in the entire banking system. Qualitative or selective controls aim to influence the direction or allocation of credit to specific sectors of the economy, as in this case, trying to cool down the real estate market without tightening credit for all other sectors.

343. What is the fundamental difference between a commercial bank and a microfinance institution (MFI)?
a) Commercial banks are profit-making, while MFIs are not.
b) Commercial banks primarily serve large corporations and wealthy individuals, while MFIs target low-income households and micro-enterprises typically excluded from formal banking.
c) Commercial banks can create credit, while MFIs cannot.
d) Commercial banks are regulated, while MFIs are not.

Answer: b) Commercial banks primarily serve large corporations and wealthy individuals, while MFIs target low-income households and micro-enterprises typically excluded from formal banking.
Explanation: While other points may have some truth in certain contexts (many MFIs have a social mission, but many are also for-profit), the defining difference is their target market. MFIs were created specifically to fill the gap in financial services for the poor, who were deemed "unbankable" by traditional commercial banks due to a lack of collateral and the high transaction costs of small loans.

344. A major benefit of a floating exchange rate system that is absent in a currency board system is:
a) Price stability.
b) The ability to conduct independent monetary policy to respond to domestic shocks.
c) Certainty for international traders and investors.
d) Automatic control of fiscal deficits.

Answer: b) The ability to conduct independent monetary policy to respond to domestic shocks.
Explanation: A currency board is the most rigid form of fixed exchange rate, where the central bank gives up all monetary policy independence. The money supply is tied directly to reserves. In a floating system, the central bank is completely free to set interest rates and use its other tools to pursue domestic goals like fighting a recession or controlling inflation, without having to worry about maintaining a currency peg.

345. A short-run increase in aggregate demand will cause a larger increase in the price level and a smaller increase in output if:
a) The short-run aggregate supply (SRAS) curve is relatively flat.
b) The short-run aggregate supply (SRAS) curve is relatively steep.
c) The long-run aggregate supply (LRAS) curve is shifting to the right.
d) The economy has a large amount of unemployed resources.

Answer: b) The short-run aggregate supply (SRAS) curve is relatively steep.
Explanation: The slope of the SRAS curve reflects the economy's capacity constraints. If the SRAS is steep, it means the economy is already operating close to its full capacity. In this case, an increase in AD will mostly translate into higher prices (inflation) because firms cannot easily increase their output further.

346. An economy's break-even point in the Keynesian model occurs where:
a) Autonomous consumption is equal to saving.
b) The consumption function intersects the 45-degree line.
c) Marginal propensity to consume equals average propensity to consume.
d) Investment equals government spending.

Answer: b) The consumption function intersects the 45-degree line.
Explanation: The 45-degree line represents all points where Y = AD (or Y=C in a simple model without I and G). The break-even point is the level of income where all income is consumed (C=Y), meaning saving is zero. This occurs graphically where the consumption function, C = a + bY, crosses the 45-degree line.

347. From the perspective of economic efficiency, price discrimination is often seen as superior to a single-price monopoly because:
a) It always results in lower prices for all consumers.
b) It increases producer surplus at the expense of consumer surplus.
c) It typically leads to a higher output level, closer to the socially optimal quantity, thus reducing deadweight loss.
d) It is easier for the government to regulate.

Answer: c) It typically leads to a higher output level, closer to the socially optimal quantity, thus reducing deadweight loss.
Explanation: A single-price monopolist restricts output to raise the price for everyone. A price-discriminating monopolist has an incentive to sell to additional market segments at lower prices, as long as the price for that segment is above marginal cost. This results in a higher total quantity sold compared to the single-price monopolist. This increased output reduces the allocative inefficiency and the deadweight loss associated with monopoly.

348. Which of the following best explains why the income elasticity of demand for food is typically low and positive?
a) Food is an inferior good.
b) Food is a luxury good.
c) Food is a normal good but also a necessity, so as income rises, people spend more on it, but the percentage increase in food spending is smaller than the percentage increase in income.
d) Demand for food is perfectly elastic.

Answer: c) Food is a normal good but also a necessity, so as income rises, people spend more on it, but the percentage increase in food spending is smaller than the percentage increase in income.
Explanation: Food is a normal good (YED is positive) because people buy more/better food as income rises. But it is a necessity, so there is a limit to how much food people can consume. This means the increase in food spending is less than proportional to the increase in income, resulting in an income elasticity between 0 and 1.

349. In calculating Gross Domestic Product (GDP) using the expenditure approach, which of the following is subtracted from the sum of C + I + G + X?
a) Imports (M).
b) Depreciation.
c) Indirect Taxes.
d) Savings (S).

Answer: a) Imports (M).
Explanation: Consumption (C), Investment (I), and Government Spending (G) include expenditures on both domestically produced and imported goods. To get a measure of domestic production (GDP), we must subtract the value of spending on goods and services that were produced abroad, i.e., imports (M). Hence the formula ends in +(X-M).

350. An economy that relies on bartering instead of money would likely suffer from:
a) Hyperinflation.
b) The need for a "double coincidence of wants" for trade to occur.
c) Excessive saving and insufficient investment.
d) A lack of scarcity.

Answer: b) The need for a "double coincidence of wants" for trade to occur.
Explanation: This is the primary inefficiency of a barter system. For a trade to happen, person A, who has what person B wants, must also want what person B has. Finding such a match is difficult and makes trade highly inefficient. Money solves this problem by acting as a universally accepted medium of exchange.
