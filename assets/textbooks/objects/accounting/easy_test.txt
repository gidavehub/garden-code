Chapter 1: Introduction to Accounting and Its Objectives

Q: Which of the following best defines accounting?
A) The process of only recording financial transactions.
B) The systematic process of identifying, measuring, recording, classifying, summarizing, interpreting, and communicating financial information.
C) The process of preparing tax returns for a business.
D) The management of a company's financial resources.
A: B
E: Accounting is a comprehensive process that involves much more than just recording; it includes identification, measurement, classification, summarization, interpretation, and communication.

Q: The primary purpose of accounting is to:
A) Guarantee business profitability.
B) Provide information for decision-making.
C) Prevent business losses.
D) Ensure all employees are paid correctly.
A: B
E: While accounting information can help with other aspects, its main goal is to provide relevant and reliable financial information useful for making informed decisions.

Q: Which of the following is an internal user of financial information?
A) Tax authorities
B) Lenders
C) Management
D) Potential investors
A: C
E: Management uses accounting information for planning, controlling, and making decisions within the organization. Others are external users.

Q: The branch of accounting concerned with reporting to external users is:
A) Management Accounting
B) Cost Accounting
C) Financial Accounting
D) Tax Accounting
A: C
E: Financial accounting focuses on preparing general-purpose financial statements for external stakeholders like investors and creditors.

Q: Which accounting principle states that revenues are recognized when earned and expenses when incurred?
A) Consistency concept
B) Prudence concept
C) Materiality concept
D) Accrual concept
A: D
E: The accrual concept dictates that transactions are recorded in the period they occur, not necessarily when cash changes hands.

Q: The fundamental accounting equation is:
A) Assets = Liabilities - Equity
B) Assets = Liabilities + Equity
C) Assets + Liabilities = Equity
D) Equity = Assets + Liabilities
A: B
E: The accounting equation shows that a company's assets are financed by either liabilities (what it owes) or equity (the owners' stake).

Q: In the double-entry system, a debit entry will:
A) Always increase an account.
B) Always decrease an account.
C) Increase assets and expenses, and decrease liabilities, equity, and revenue.
D) Increase liabilities and revenue, and decrease assets and expenses.
A: C
E: Debits increase asset and expense accounts and decrease liability, equity (capital), and revenue accounts.

Q: The "language of business" is a common term for:
A) Economics
B) Management
C) Accounting
D) Marketing
A: C
E: Accounting translates business activities into financial terms, making it the universally understood language of business.

Q: Which users need financial information to assess a company's ability to repay loans?
A) Employees
B) Customers
C) Government
D) Lenders
A: D
E: Lenders (creditors, banks) use financial information to evaluate creditworthiness and the likelihood of loan repayment.

Q: Prudence concept implies that:
A) Profits should be anticipated, but losses should only be recognized when certain.
B) Assets and revenues should not be overstated, and liabilities and expenses should not be understated.
C) The same accounting methods should be used from period to period.
D) All financial information, however small, must be disclosed.
A: B
E: Prudence (or conservatism) guides accountants to exercise caution in judgments, ensuring financials are not overly optimistic.

Chapter 2: The Accounting Cycle and Books of First Entry

Q: The original record that provides evidence of a financial transaction is called a:
A) Journal
B) Ledger
C) Source document
D) Trial Balance
A: C
E: Source documents (e.g., invoices, receipts) are the initial proofs of transactions.

Q: Which of the following is NOT a book of prime entry?
A) Sales Journal
B) General Ledger
C) Cash Book
D) Purchases Journal
A: B
E: The General Ledger is the principal book where accounts are summarized; books of prime entry (journals) are where transactions are first recorded.

Q: The process of transferring entries from the journal to the ledger is called:
A) Journalizing
B) Balancing
C) Posting
D) Trialing
A: C
E: Posting involves systematically transferring debit and credit amounts from the journal to their respective ledger accounts.

Q: A trial balance is prepared to:
A) Determine the profit or loss of a business.
B) Check the arithmetical accuracy of the ledger accounts.
C) Show the financial position of a business.
D) Summarize cash inflows and outflows.
A: B
E: The primary purpose of a trial balance is to verify that total debits equal total credits in the ledger, indicating arithmetical accuracy.

Q: An error of commission is when:
A) A transaction is completely omitted from the books.
B) A transaction is recorded in the wrong account of the correct type.
C) An asset is treated as an expense.
D) Two errors cancel each other out.
A: B
E: An error of commission involves recording a transaction in the correct class of account but the wrong specific account (e.g., debiting Office Equipment instead of Motor Vehicles).

Q: A cash book with columns for cash, bank, and discount is known as a:
A) Single Column Cash Book
B) Double Column Cash Book
C) Triple Column Cash Book
D) Petty Cash Book
A: C
E: The Triple Column Cash Book facilitates recording cash transactions, bank transactions, and cash discounts (allowed and received) simultaneously.

Q: The imprest system is commonly used for managing:
A) Sales returns
B) Petty cash
C) Accounts receivable
D) Non-current assets
A: B
E: The imprest system involves maintaining a fixed float for small cash expenditures, with the petty cashier being reimbursed for amounts spent.

Q: If the debit side of a trial balance exceeds the credit side, the difference is placed on the:
A) Debit side of the suspense account.
B) Credit side of the suspense account.
C) Debit side of the capital account.
D) Credit side of the profit and loss account.
A: B
E: To make the trial balance agree temporarily, the difference is put in a suspense account on the shorter side. If debits exceed credits, the credit side is shorter.

Q: Adjusting entries are primarily made to:
A) Correct errors in the trial balance.
B) Record revenues when cash is received and expenses when cash is paid.
C) Apply the accrual basis of accounting.
D) Close temporary accounts at the end of the period.
A: C
E: Adjusting entries ensure that revenues are recognized when earned and expenses when incurred, aligning with the accrual basis and matching principle.

Q: An example of a source document for credit sales is a:
A) Purchase invoice
B) Sales invoice
C) Credit note issued to a supplier
D) Cheque counterfoil
A: B
E: A sales invoice is issued by the seller to the buyer as evidence of goods sold or services rendered on credit.

Chapter 3: Statement of Financial Position (Balance Sheet)

Q: Non-current assets are assets that:
A) Are expected to be converted into cash within one year.
B) Are held for long-term use in the business and not for resale.
C) Have no physical substance.
D) Represent amounts owed to the business by customers.
A: B
E: Non-current assets (like machinery, buildings) are acquired for long-term operational use, not for quick conversion to cash or resale.

Q: Which of the following is a current liability?
A) Debentures
B) Long-term bank loan
C) Trade Payables
D) Owner's Capital
A: C
E: Trade Payables (creditors) are amounts owed to suppliers for goods or services purchased on credit, typically due within one year.

Q: Owner's equity represents:
A) The total assets of the business.
B) The total liabilities of the business.
C) The owner's residual interest in the assets after deducting liabilities.
D) The profit earned by the business during the year.
A: C
E: Equity is the owner's claim on the business's net assets (Assets - Liabilities).

Q: Drawings are:
A) Additions to the owner's capital.
B) Withdrawals of cash or goods by the owner for personal use.
C) Expenses incurred in running the business.
D) Profits retained in the business.
A: B
E: Drawings represent a reduction in the owner's equity due to personal withdrawals.

Q: The systematic allocation of the cost of a tangible non-current asset over its useful life is known as:
A) Amortization
B) Appreciation
C) Depreciation
D) Depletion
A: C
E: Depreciation is charged for tangible non-current assets. Amortization is similar but for intangible assets, and depletion is for natural resources.

Q: An example of an intangible non-current asset is:
A) Inventory
B) Machinery
C) Goodwill
D) Accounts Receivable
A: C
E: Goodwill, patents, and trademarks are intangible assets as they lack physical substance but have value.

Q: The "Report Form" of a Balance Sheet presents:
A) Assets on the left, liabilities and equity on the right.
B) Assets, then liabilities, then equity in a downward sequence.
C) Only assets and liabilities.
D) Only equity components.
A: B
E: The report form (vertical format) lists assets first, followed by liabilities and equity sections underneath.

Q: According to IAS 1, if property, plant, and equipment are material, they must be shown:

B) Only in the notes to the financial statements.
C) As a separate line item on the face of the Statement of Financial Position.

Chapter 4: Statement of Comprehensive Income (Income Statement)

Q: Revenue is generally recognized when:
A) Cash is received from the customer.
B) An order is placed by the customer.
C) It is earned, and the performance obligation is satisfied.
D) The production of goods is completed.
A: C
E: The revenue recognition principle (e.g., IFRS 15) states revenue is recognized when control of goods/services is transferred to the customer.

Q: Trade discounts are:
A) Recorded as an expense in the seller's books.
B) Recorded as income in the buyer's books.
C) Deductions from the list price not recorded in the accounting books.
D) Deductions for prompt payment.
A: C
E: Trade discounts determine the actual invoice price and are not separately recorded; sales and purchases are recorded net of trade discounts.

Q: Sales Returns and Allowances account normally has a:
A) Credit balance
B) Debit balance
C) Nil balance
D) Balance equal to gross sales
A: B
E: Sales Returns and Allowances is a contra-revenue account, reducing gross sales, and thus has a debit balance.

Q: Cost of Goods Sold for a merchandising business is calculated as:
A) Opening Inventory + Purchases – Carriage Inwards – Closing Inventory
B) Opening Inventory + Purchases + Carriage Inwards – Closing Inventory
C) Net Sales – Gross Profit
D) Both B and C are correct.
A: D
E: COGS = Opening Inv. + Net Purchases + Carriage Inwards - Closing Inv. Also, Net Sales - COGS = Gross Profit, so Net Sales - Gross Profit = COGS.

Q: Under FIFO inventory valuation, it is assumed that:
A) The most recently purchased items are sold first.
B) The oldest items of inventory are sold first.
C) The average cost of items is used for goods sold.
D) Items are sold in random order.
A: B
E: FIFO (First-In, First-Out) assumes that the earliest acquired inventory items are the first ones to be sold.

Q: In periods of rising prices, which inventory valuation method generally results in the highest net profit?
A) LIFO
B) FIFO
C) Weighted Average Cost
D) Specific Identification
A: B
E: FIFO will match older, lower costs with current higher selling prices, leading to a higher gross and net profit.

Q: Selling and distribution expenses are examples of:
A) Cost of Goods Sold
B) Non-Operating Expenses
C) Operating Expenses
D) Administrative Expenses
A: C
E: Selling and distribution expenses (like advertising, sales salaries) are part of operating expenses incurred in the normal course of business.

Q: Interest income received from bank deposits is typically classified as:
A) Operating Revenue
B) Other Income (Non-Operating Income)
C) Sales Revenue
D) A reduction of interest expense
A: B
E: For most non-financial companies, interest income is considered non-operating income.

Q: The Multi-Step Income Statement is preferred because it:
A) Is simpler to prepare.
B) Shows important subtotals like Gross Profit and Operating Profit.
C) Only includes cash transactions.
D) Combines revenues and expenses in one step.
A: B
E: The multi-step format provides more detailed information by showing gross profit and operating profit before arriving at net profit, aiding analysis.

Q: "Other Comprehensive Income" (OCI) includes items like:
A) Sales revenue and cost of goods sold.
B) Operating expenses.
C) Revaluation gains on property, plant, and equipment.
D) Income tax expense.
A: C
E: OCI includes certain gains and losses not recognized in profit or loss but directly affecting equity, such as revaluation surpluses.

Chapter 5: Receivables and Payables Control Accounts

Q: A Sales Ledger Control Account summarizes all individual accounts in the:
A) Purchases Ledger
B) General Ledger
C) Sales Ledger
D) Cash Book
A: C
E: The Sales Ledger Control Account (or Accounts Receivable Control Account) balance should equal the sum of balances in the Sales Ledger (Debtors Ledger).

Q: The total of the Sales Returns Journal is posted to the:
A) Debit side of the Sales Ledger Control Account.
B) Credit side of the Sales Ledger Control Account.
C) Debit side of the Purchases Ledger Control Account.
D) Credit side of the Purchases Ledger Control Account.
A: B
E: Sales returns reduce the amount owed by debtors, so the total is credited to the Sales Ledger Control Account.

Q: If the total of the Schedule of Debtors does not agree with the Sales Ledger Control Account balance, it indicates:
A) The business is making a loss.
B) An error exists in either the control account or the subsidiary ledger (or both).
C) Too many credit sales were made.
D) The trial balance will not balance.
A: B
E: Disagreement signifies errors in posting or summarization that need investigation.

Q: A specific debt deemed uncollectible is written off by debiting Bad Debts Expense and crediting:
A) Allowance for Doubtful Debts
B) The individual Debtor's account (and Accounts Receivable Control)
C) Sales Account
D) Cash Account
A: B
E: When directly writing off a bad debt (without using an allowance), the specific debtor's account is credited to remove the uncollectible amount.

Q: An Allowance for Doubtful Debts is created to comply with the:
A) Consistency principle
B) Business entity concept
C) Matching principle and Prudence concept
D) Cost concept
A: C
E: The allowance method matches potential bad debt expense with the sales revenue of the period and conservatively recognizes potential losses.

Q: Which method of estimating doubtful debts focuses on the Balance Sheet's net realizable value of receivables?
A) Percentage of credit sales method
B) Percentage of receivables method
C) Direct write-off method
D) Specific invoice method
A: B
E: The percentage of receivables method (and aging method) aims to report receivables at their estimated collectible amount on the Balance Sheet.

Q: A cash discount given to a customer for prompt payment is termed:
A) Trade Discount
B) Discount Allowed
C) Discount Received
D) Sales Rebate
A: B
E: Discount Allowed is an expense for the seller, granted to encourage early payment by debtors.

Q: The primary purpose of a Bank Reconciliation Statement is to:
A) Determine the cash balance in hand.
B) Identify differences between the cash book bank balance and the bank statement balance.
C) Calculate bank charges for the period.
D) Correct errors made by the bank only.
A: B
E: It reconciles these two balances by identifying timing differences and errors.

Q: Unpresented cheques are cheques that have been:
A) Issued by the business but not yet cashed by the recipient.
B) Received by the business but not yet deposited.
C) Deposited by the business but not yet credited by the bank.
D) Returned by the bank due to insufficient funds.
A: A
E: Unpresented (or outstanding) cheques reduce the cash book bank balance but haven't yet reduced the bank statement balance.

Q: Bank charges shown on the bank statement but not yet in the cash book require an adjustment:
A) Adding to the bank statement balance.
B) Deducting from the bank statement balance.
C) Debiting bank charges expense and crediting bank in the cash book.
D) Debiting bank and crediting bank charges income in the cash book.
A: C
E: Before preparing the BRS, the cash book needs to be updated. Bank charges are an expense that reduces the cash book bank balance.

Chapter 6: Bills of Exchange and Promissory Notes

Q: A bill of exchange is primarily drawn by the:
A) Debtor
B) Creditor
C) Bank
D) Notary Public
A: B
E: The creditor (drawer) draws the bill, ordering the debtor (drawee) to pay.

Q: In a bill of exchange, the person on whom the bill is drawn is the:
A) Drawer
B) Payee
C) Drawee
D) Endorser
A: C
E: The drawee is the party ordered to make the payment. They become the acceptor upon signing the bill.

Q: When a creditor receives an accepted bill of exchange from a debtor, the creditor will debit:
A) Bills Payable Account
B) Debtor's Account
C) Bills Receivable Account
D) Sales Account
A: C
E: The accepted bill becomes a Bill Receivable, an asset, for the creditor. The debtor's personal account is credited.

Q: When a debtor accepts a bill drawn on them, they will credit:
A) Bills Receivable Account
B) Creditor's Account
C) Bills Payable Account
D) Purchases Account
A: C
E: Accepting a bill creates a Bill Payable, a liability, for the debtor. The creditor's personal account is debited.

Q: If a bill receivable is discounted with a bank, the Discount Charges account is:
A) Credited by the drawer.
B) Debited by the drawer.
C) Credited by the bank.
D) Debited by the drawee.
A: B
E: Discount charges represent an expense for the drawer (holder of the bill) for getting cash before the maturity date.

Q: The failure of an acceptor to pay a bill on its due date is known as:
A) Retirement of a bill
B) Discounting of a bill
C) Dishonour of a bill
D) Noting of a bill
A: C
E: Dishonour occurs when the acceptor does not meet their obligation to pay on maturity.

Q: Noting charges are expenses incurred:
A) By the drawee when accepting a bill.
B) By the drawer when drawing a bill.
C) By the holder when a bill is dishonoured, to get official evidence of dishonour.
D) By the bank for discounting a bill.
A: C
E: Noting charges are fees paid to a Notary Public to certify the dishonour of a bill. They are initially paid by the holder but are recoverable from the acceptor.

Q: When a bill previously discounted with a bank is dishonoured by the acceptor, the drawer will credit:
A) Bills Receivable Account
B) Debtor's Account
C) Bank Account
D) Discount Charges Account
A: C
E: The bank will recover the amount from the drawer by debiting their bank account; thus, the drawer credits Bank and debits the Debtor's account.

Q: If an acceptor pays a bill before its due date and receives a discount for early payment, this discount is known as:
A) Trade discount
B) Cash discount
C) Rebate on bill
D) Noting charge
A: C
E: A rebate is a reduction given by the holder for early payment of a bill. It's an income for the acceptor and an expense for the holder.

Q: Which of the following is a key difference between a bill of exchange and a promissory note?
A) A bill is an order to pay, while a note is a promise to pay.
B) A bill involves two parties, while a note involves three.
C) A bill is drawn by the debtor, while a note is drawn by the creditor.
D) A bill does not require acceptance, while a note does.
A: A
E: A bill of exchange is an order from a drawer to a drawee, whereas a promissory note is a direct promise from a maker to a payee.

Chapter 7: Partnership Formation and Operations

Q: A partnership deed is:
A) A mandatory document required by law to form any partnership.
B) A written agreement outlining the terms and conditions of the partnership.
C) A certificate issued by the Registrar of Companies.
D) An agreement solely about profit sharing.
A: B
E: While a partnership can exist orally, a written deed is highly advisable to clarify terms and avoid disputes.

Q: If a partnership deed is silent on profit sharing, profits and losses are usually shared:
A) In the ratio of capital contributions.
B) Equally among partners.
C) Based on the time devoted by each partner.
D) As decided by the managing partner.
A: B
E: Most Partnership Acts stipulate equal sharing of profits and losses if the agreement does not specify a ratio.

Q: Under the fixed capital system for partnerships, partners' salaries and interest on capital are recorded in the:
A) Partners' Capital Accounts
B) Partners' Current Accounts
C) Profit and Loss Account
D) Partners' Loan Accounts
A: B
E: In the fixed capital system, only permanent capital changes go to the Capital Account; all other appropriations and drawings are routed through the Current Account.

Q: The Profit and Loss Appropriation Account in a partnership shows:
A) How the net profit is calculated.
B) How the net profit is distributed among partners.
C) The firm's cash flows.
D) The valuation of partnership assets.
A: B
E: The P&L Appropriation Account starts with net profit and details its distribution (interest on capital, salaries, share of remaining profit).

Q: Interest on partners' drawings is treated in the Profit and Loss Appropriation Account as:
A) A debit (an appropriation expense)
B) A credit (an income or increase in distributable profit)
C) A direct deduction from partners' capital
D) An operating expense
A: B
E: Interest charged on drawings is effectively an income for the firm, increasing the profits available for appropriation.

Q: Interest on a loan from a partner to the firm is typically treated as:
A) An appropriation of profit.
B) A charge against profit (an expense in the main P&L Account).
C) A reduction in the partner's capital.
D) A direct addition to the partner's current account.
A: B
E: Interest on a partner's loan (beyond capital) is considered an expense of the business, similar to interest on an external loan.

Q: When a new partner is admitted, existing assets and liabilities are often revalued to:
A) Reduce the new partner's capital contribution.
B) Ensure the new partner does not unfairly benefit from or suffer due to past changes in asset/liability values.
C) Calculate goodwill automatically.
D) Comply with tax regulations.
A: B
E: Revaluation adjusts asset/liability values to fair market value at the admission date, with the gain/loss shared by old partners.

Q: Goodwill in a partnership represents:
A) The total physical assets of the firm.
B) The excess of liabilities over assets.
C) The value of the firm's reputation and earning capacity above normal.
D) The amount of cash contributed by partners.
A: C
E: Goodwill is an intangible asset arising from factors like reputation, customer base, and brand name that allow a firm to earn super-profits.

Q: The sacrificing ratio, calculated on the admission of a new partner, is used to:
A) Distribute revaluation profit.
B) Allocate the new partner's capital.
C) Distribute the premium for goodwill brought in by the new partner among old partners.
D) Determine the new profit-sharing ratio.
A: C
E: Old partners who sacrifice their share of profits in favor of the new partner are compensated for this sacrifice, often through the goodwill premium.

Q: On the retirement of a partner, the retiring partner is entitled to:
A) Only their original capital contribution.
B) Their share of goodwill and revaluation profit/loss, in addition to their capital and current account balances.
C) A fixed sum irrespective of profits or assets.
D) Future profits of the reconstituted firm.
A: B
E: The retiring partner's account is adjusted for their share of accumulated profits, revaluation changes, goodwill, interest, salary, etc., up to the date of retirement.

Chapter 8: Introduction to Limited Liability Companies

Q: The liability of shareholders in a limited liability company is generally limited to:
A) The total debts of the company.
B) The amount, if any, unpaid on their shares.
C) Their proportionate share of the company's annual losses.
D) A fixed sum determined by the directors.
A: B
E: Limited liability means shareholders are not personally liable for company debts beyond the nominal value of their shares (plus any premium agreed).

Q: The document that defines a company's objectives and its relationship with the outside world is the:
A) Articles of Association

C) Certificate of Incorporation
D) Memorandum of Association

Q: The internal rules and regulations for the management of a company's affairs are contained in the:

B) Memorandum of Association
C) Annual Report
D) Partnership Deed
A: A
E: The Articles of Association govern internal matters like share types, meetings, directors' powers, and dividend procedures.

B) Paid-up share capital
C) Authorized share capital (Nominal capital)
D) Called-up share capital

E: Authorized capital is the ceiling stated in the Memorandum of Association.

A: B
E: To maintain the capital base when preference shares are redeemed out of distributable profits, an equivalent amount is transferred to CRR.

Chapter 9: Cash Flow Statement and Funds Flow Statement

Q: The primary purpose of a Cash Flow Statement is to:
A) Show the company's profit or loss for a period.
B) List the company's assets and liabilities at a point in time.
C) Provide information about cash inflows and outflows during a period.
D) Determine the amount of working capital.
A: C
E: The Cash Flow Statement details the sources and uses of cash, categorized into operating, investing, and financing activities.

Q: In the Cash Flow Statement, cash flows from operating activities can be presented using:
A) Only the direct method.
B) Only the indirect method.
C) Either the direct or indirect method.
D) The working capital method.
A: C
E: IAS 7 allows both direct (showing gross cash receipts/payments) and indirect (reconciling net profit) methods for operating activities.

Q: Which of the following is an example of a cash flow from investing activities?
A) Proceeds from issuing shares.
B) Payment of dividends.
C) Cash received from customers.
D) Purchase of new machinery for cash.
A: D
E: Investing activities involve the acquisition and disposal of long-term assets and other investments.

Q: Which of the following is an example of a cash flow from financing activities?
A) Sale of goods for cash.
B) Repayment of a long-term bank loan.
C) Purchase of inventory.
D) Payment of salaries.
A: B
E: Financing activities relate to changes in the company's equity and borrowings.

Q: When using the indirect method for operating cash flows, depreciation expense is:
A) Deducted from net profit because it uses cash.
B) Added back to net profit because it is a non-cash expense.
C) Ignored as it doesn't affect cash.
D) Treated as a financing activity.
A: B
E: Net profit is an accrual figure. Depreciation, a non-cash expense, reduced this profit, so it's added back to arrive at cash flow.

Q: An increase in trade receivables during a period, using the indirect method, implies:
A) Cash collected from customers was more than credit sales; add to net profit.
B) Cash collected from customers was less than credit sales; deduct from net profit.
C) It's a non-cash transaction and ignored.
D) It's an investing activity.
A: B
E: An increase in receivables means not all credit sales were converted to cash, so it's a deduction when reconciling profit to cash.

Q: Interest paid by a non-financial company can be classified in the Cash Flow Statement as:
A) Only an operating activity.
B) Only a financing activity.
C) Either an operating or an investing activity.
D) Either an operating or a financing activity, applied consistently.
A: D
E: IAS 7 allows flexibility for interest paid to be classified under operating or financing activities.

Q: A Funds Flow Statement primarily focuses on changes in:
A) Total assets
B) Net profit
C) Working capital
D) Cash and cash equivalents
A: C
E: The Funds Flow Statement (Statement of Changes in Working Capital) explains how net working capital (Current Assets - Current Liabilities) changed during a period.

Q: In a Funds Flow Statement, "funds from operations" is calculated by:
A) Net profit plus cash expenses.
B) Net profit adjusted for non-fund items (e.g., adding back depreciation).
C) Total cash inflows from customers.
D) Sales revenue less operating expenses.
A: B
E: Funds from operations starts with net profit and adjusts for items that affected profit but not working capital (like depreciation) and items that affected working capital but are not in typical operating profit (like profit/loss on sale of fixed assets).

Q: Purchase of a non-current asset by issuing shares is an example of:
A) A cash flow from investing activity.
B) A cash flow from financing activity.
C) Both an investing and a financing cash flow.
D) A significant non-cash investing and financing activity.
A: D
E: Since no cash is exchanged, it's a non-cash transaction disclosed in the notes to the Cash Flow Statement.

Chapter 10: Ratio Analysis and Financial Performance

Q: Liquidity ratios measure a company's:
A) Ability to generate profits.
B) Ability to meet its long-term obligations.
C) Ability to meet its short-term obligations.
D) Efficiency in using its assets.
A: C
E: Liquidity refers to how quickly a company can convert assets to cash to pay its current debts.

Q: The Current Ratio is calculated as:
A) Current Assets / Non-Current Liabilities
B) Current Liabilities / Current Assets
C) Current Assets / Current Liabilities
D) (Current Assets - Inventory) / Current Liabilities
A: C
E: Current Ratio = Current Assets divided by Current Liabilities.

Q: Which ratio is a more stringent test of liquidity than the Current Ratio?
A) Gross Profit Margin
B) Debt-Equity Ratio
C) Quick Ratio (Acid-Test Ratio)
D) Return on Capital Employed
A: C
E: The Quick Ratio excludes less liquid current assets like inventory and prepayments.

Q: Gross Profit Margin is calculated as:
A) (Net Profit / Net Sales) × 100%
B) (Gross Profit / Cost of Goods Sold) × 100%
C) (Gross Profit / Net Sales) × 100%
D) (Operating Profit / Net Sales) × 100%
A: C
E: Gross Profit Margin = (Gross Profit / Net Sales) × 100%.

Q: Return on Capital Employed (ROCE) measures:
A) The return generated for ordinary shareholders.
B) The company's ability to pay short-term debts.
C) How efficiently a company uses its total long-term funds to generate profits.
D) The percentage of sales remaining after all expenses.
A: C
E: ROCE (Operating Profit / Capital Employed) shows the profitability relative to the capital invested in the business.

Q: A high Debt-Equity ratio suggests:
A) Lower financial risk.
B) Higher financial risk due to greater reliance on borrowed funds.
C) Strong liquidity.
D) Efficient use of assets.
A: B
E: A higher ratio indicates that the company is more leveraged (higher gearing), which increases risk for shareholders and can strain ability to meet debt obligations.

Q: The Interest Coverage Ratio indicates:
A) How quickly interest income is earned.
B) How many times operating profit can cover interest expense.
C) The proportion of debt in the capital structure.
D) The return on interest-bearing investments.
A: B
E: Interest Coverage Ratio (EBIT / Interest Expense) measures the margin of safety for paying interest.

Q: Inventory Turnover Ratio measures:
A) The average time inventory is held.
B) How many times inventory is sold and replaced during a period.
C) The profitability of inventory.
D) The amount of cash tied up in inventory.
A: B
E: A higher inventory turnover (COGS / Average Inventory) generally indicates efficient inventory management.

Q: Debtors Collection Period (Days Sales Outstanding) indicates:
A) The average time taken to pay suppliers.
B) The average time taken to collect cash from credit customers.
C) The profitability of credit sales.
D) The proportion of bad debts.
A: B
E: A shorter collection period is generally better for cash flow. (Average Debtors / Credit Sales) * 365.

Q: One limitation of ratio analysis is that:
A) It provides too much detail.
B) It is based on historical data and may not predict future performance.
C) It is only useful for internal management.
D) It guarantees accurate financial assessment.
A: B
E: Ratios are calculated from past data, and while they help analyze trends, future performance can be affected by many other factors.

Chapter 11: Introduction to Accounting Software Systems

Q: A key feature of most accounting software packages is:
A) Manual posting to ledgers.
B) Automatic posting of transactions to relevant ledgers.
C) The requirement for physical journals.
D) Generation of only the Balance Sheet.
A: B
E: Computerized systems automate the double-entry and posting processes, improving speed and accuracy.

Q: In computerized accounting, the chart of accounts provides:
A) A list of all physical assets.
B) The coded list of all ledger accounts used by the business.
C) The software's user manual.
D) A summary of customer balances.
A: B
E: The chart of accounts is the structured list of general ledger accounts that forms the basis for recording and classifying transactions.

Q: Batch processing in an accounting system means:
A) Transactions are processed individually and immediately.
B) Transactions are collected over a period and processed together.
C) Only one transaction can be processed at a time.
D) Reports are generated only once a month.
A: B
E: Batch processing involves accumulating transactions and processing them in groups, which can create a time lag.

Q: An audit trail in a computerized system helps to:
A) Speed up data entry.
B) Automatically correct errors.
C) Track all transactions and system activities, including who made changes and when.
D) Prevent unauthorized access.
A: C
E: Audit trails provide a record of activities, crucial for investigating errors, fraud, or unauthorized changes.

Q: Which of the following is an advantage of computerized accounting?
A) High initial cost of software and hardware.
B) Reduced speed and efficiency.
C) Increased arithmetical accuracy and automatic report generation.
D) Need for extensive manual record-keeping.
A: C
E: Computer systems significantly enhance accuracy by minimizing calculation errors and automate the production of various financial reports.

Q: Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) in accounting software aims to:
A) Ensure all users have access to all data.
B) Restrict user access to data and functions based on their job responsibilities.
C) Speed up the backup process.
D) Encrypt all financial data.
A: B
E: RBAC is a security measure that limits users to only the parts of the system necessary for their specific roles, enhancing internal control.

Q: The "Garbage In, Garbage Out" (GIGO) principle in computerized accounting implies:
A) The software can automatically fix incorrect input data.
B) The accuracy of output reports depends on the accuracy of the data input.
C) All outputs from the system are inherently reliable.
D) The system is resistant to data entry errors.
A: B
E: If incorrect data is fed into the system, the resulting information and reports will also be incorrect, despite the software's processing accuracy.

Q: Regular off-site backup of accounting data is crucial to:
A) Improve the speed of data processing.
B) Reduce the need for strong passwords.
C) Recover data in case of local disasters like fire or system failure.
D) Prevent unauthorized access to the live system.
A: C
E: Off-site backups safeguard data against physical disasters at the primary location, enabling data recovery.

Q: An integrated accounting software package often includes modules for:
A) Only sales and purchases.
B) Sales, purchases, inventory, payroll, and general ledger.
C) Only financial statement generation.
D) Only tax calculation.
A: B
E: Modern systems are often modular, covering various accounting functions in an integrated manner.

Q: One limitation of computerized accounting systems is:
A) Inability to handle large volumes of transactions.
B) Vulnerability to system failures and data loss if not properly managed.
C) Slow generation of reports.
D) Lack of accuracy in calculations.
A: B
E: Despite many advantages, computerized systems are susceptible to technical issues, power outages, or cyber threats, making backups and security vital.

Chapter 12: Professional Ethics and Corporate Governance in Accounting

Q: The ethical principle requiring accountants to be straightforward and honest is:
A) Objectivity
B) Confidentiality
C) Integrity
D) Professional Competence and Due Care
A: C
E: Integrity means upholding honesty and fair dealing in all professional and business relationships.

Q: Avoiding bias, conflict of interest, or undue influence of others in professional judgment relates to the principle of:
A) Integrity
B) Objectivity
C) Professional Behavior
D) Confidentiality
A: B
E: Objectivity requires accountants to make impartial decisions based on facts, without letting personal feelings or external pressures sway their judgment.

Q: An accountant not disclosing a client's confidential information without proper authority adheres to the principle of:
A) Professional Competence and Due Care
B) Objectivity
C) Integrity
D) Confidentiality
A: D
E: Confidentiality mandates protecting client information acquired during professional service unless there's a legal or professional duty/right to disclose.

Q: Corporate governance primarily concerns:
A) The marketing strategies of a company.
B) The system by which a company is directed and controlled.
C) The daily operational management of a company.
D) The human resource policies of a company.
A: B
E: Corporate governance involves the structures and processes for directing and overseeing a company to ensure accountability, fairness, and transparency.

Q: The sub-committee of the Board of Directors responsible for overseeing financial reporting and audit is the:
A) Remuneration Committee
B) Nomination Committee
C) Audit Committee
D) Risk Management Committee
A: C
E: The Audit Committee plays a key role in ensuring the integrity of financial reporting, overseeing internal controls, and liaising with external auditors.

Q: An example of a control activity within an internal control system is:
A) Setting the company's strategic objectives.
B) Segregation of duties.
C) Identifying potential business risks.
D) Conducting external audits.
A: B
E: Segregation of duties (e.g., separating authorization, record-keeping, and asset custody) is a fundamental control activity to prevent errors and fraud.

Q: The "Fraud Triangle" includes which three factors often present in fraudulent activity?
A) Greed, Opportunity, Deception
B) Pressure, Opportunity, Rationalization
C) Intent, Action, Concealment
D) Weak Controls, Poor Management, Lack of Ethics
A: B
E: The fraud triangle highlights that fraud typically occurs when there's a perceived pressure (motivation), a perceived opportunity, and a way for the individual to rationalize their dishonest act.

Q: Internal audit is an activity designed to:
A) Primarily prepare the company's tax returns.
B) Express an opinion on the fairness of financial statements for external users.
C) Evaluate and improve the effectiveness of an organization's risk management, control, and governance processes.
D) Set the company's overall strategic direction.
A: C
E: Internal audit provides independent assurance and consulting to management and the board on internal controls, efficiency, and compliance.

Q: Which of the following bodies is responsible for developing International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS)?
A) Financial Accounting Standards Board (FASB)
B) International Federation of Accountants (IFAC)
C) International Accounting Standards Board (IASB)
D) Local institutes like ICAG or ICAN directly.
A: C
E: The IASB is the independent standard-setting body that develops and issues IFRS. IFAC is a global organization for the accountancy profession.

Q: Requiring accountants to undertake Continuing Professional Development (CPD) aims to ensure:
A) Higher salaries for accountants.
B) Adherence to the principle of confidentiality.
C) Compliance with the principle of Professional Competence and Due Care.
D) Increased membership in professional bodies.
A: C
E: CPD helps accountants maintain and update their knowledge and skills, ensuring they can provide competent professional services.

Miscellaneous and Applied Concept Questions (Drawing from multiple chapters or broader understanding typical of WAEC)

Q: If closing inventory is overstated, what is the effect on Gross Profit and Net Profit for the period?
A) Gross Profit Overstated, Net Profit Overstated
B) Gross Profit Understated, Net Profit Understated
C) Gross Profit Overstated, Net Profit Understated
D) Gross Profit Understated, Net Profit Overstated
A: A
E: Overstating closing inventory understates Cost of Goods Sold (COGS = Op Inv + Purch - Cl Inv). If COGS is understated, Gross Profit (Sales - COGS) is overstated, and consequently, Net Profit is overstated.

Q: Carriage inwards is treated as:
A) A selling and distribution expense.
B) Part of the cost of purchases, added to calculate Cost of Goods Sold.
C) An administrative expense.
D) A deduction from sales.
A: B
E: Carriage inwards represents the transport cost of bringing goods purchased into the business, thus forming part of the cost of those goods.

Q: If an asset is purchased on credit, the double entry is:
A) Debit Asset, Credit Cash
B) Debit Asset, Credit Creditor (Accounts Payable)
C) Debit Creditor (Accounts Payable), Credit Asset
D) Debit Purchases, Credit Creditor (Accounts Payable)
A: B
E: The asset account increases (debit), and a liability to the supplier (creditor/accounts payable) increases (credit).

Q: The main difference between capital expenditure and revenue expenditure is that:
A) Capital expenditure is for small amounts, revenue expenditure for large amounts.
B) Capital expenditure provides benefits for more than one accounting period, while revenue expenditure benefits the current period.
C) Capital expenditure is recorded in the Income Statement, revenue expenditure in the Balance Sheet.
D) Capital expenditure is always paid in cash, revenue expenditure can be on credit.
A: B
E: Capital expenditure (e.g., buying machinery) results in acquiring a non-current asset or improving its earning capacity over several years. Revenue expenditure (e.g., repairs, rent) relates to day-to-day running costs.

Q: Accrued expenses are:
A) Expenses paid in advance.
B) Expenses incurred but not yet paid.
C) Revenue earned but not yet received.
D) Non-cash expenses like depreciation.
A: B
E: Accrued expenses represent liabilities for services or goods consumed during the period but for which payment has not yet been made (e.g., salaries owed at period end).

Q: Which of the following transactions would result in a contra entry in a three-column cash book?
A) Paying a creditor by cheque.
B) Receiving cash from a debtor.
C) Withdrawing cash from the bank for office use.
D) Purchasing goods for cash.
A: C
E: A contra entry affects both the cash and bank columns on opposite sides of the cash book, such as when cash is moved between the office (cash) and the bank account.

Q: A credit note issued to a customer for goods returned by them would be recorded in the seller's:
A) Sales Journal
B) Purchases Returns Journal
C) Sales Returns Journal (Returns Inwards Journal)
D) General Journal
A: C
E: The Sales Returns Journal is used to record merchandise returned by customers to whom credit sales were made.

Q: Which account is LEAST likely to appear in the trial balance of a sole trader?
A) Bank Overdraft
B) Accumulated Depreciation
C) Share Premium
D) Drawings
A: C
E: Share Premium arises from the issue of shares by a limited company, not a sole trader.

Q: When calculating Return on Capital Employed (ROCE) for a company, "Capital Employed" can be defined as:
A) Total Assets
B) Current Assets - Current Liabilities
C) Shareholders' Equity + Non-Current Liabilities
D) Non-Current Assets only
A: C
E: Capital Employed represents the total long-term funds invested in the business, which can be calculated as Equity + Non-Current Liabilities OR Total Assets - Current Liabilities.

Q: An item is considered material in accounting if:
A) It is a non-current asset.
B) It is recorded in monetary terms.
C) Its omission or misstatement could influence the economic decisions of users.
D) It is related to cash transactions.
A: C
E: Materiality is a concept of relative significance. An item is material if it's significant enough to affect a user's judgment.

Q: Under the reducing balance method of depreciation, the annual depreciation charge:
A) Remains constant each year.
B) Increases each year.
C) Decreases each year.
D) Is calculated on the original cost of the asset.
A: C
E: The reducing balance method applies a fixed percentage to the Net Book Value (cost less accumulated depreciation) of the asset, resulting in a higher charge in early years and a lower charge in later years.

Q: When shares are reissued at a discount, the discount amount on reissue:
A) Must not exceed the amount previously forfeited on those shares.
B) Is debited to Share Premium account.
C) Is unlimited.
D) Is debited to Profit and Loss account.
A: A
E: The discount on reissue of forfeited shares is debited to the Forfeited Shares Account, and this debit cannot exceed the credit balance (amount previously paid) in that account for the shares being reissued.

Q: If a company's Gross Profit Margin increases while its Net Profit Margin decreases, a possible reason is:
A) A decrease in cost of goods sold.
B) An increase in operating expenses relative to sales.
C) An increase in sales price without a corresponding increase in costs.
D) A decrease in interest expense.
A: B
E: An increasing Gross Profit Margin indicates better control over COGS or higher selling prices. If Net Profit Margin decreases despite this, it suggests that operating expenses (or other expenses below gross profit) have risen disproportionately.

Q: A business takes goods costing $200 for the owner's private use. The double entry is:
A) Debit Drawings $200, Credit Sales $200
B) Debit Drawings $200, Credit Purchases $200
C) Debit Purchases $200, Credit Drawings $200
D) Debit Sales $200, Credit Drawings $200
A: B
E: Drawings increase (Debit Drawings, which reduces equity). The goods taken reduce the goods available for sale, so Purchases account (or Inventory, depending on system) is credited to reflect this reduction in goods for business purposes.

Q: The 'going concern' concept assumes that a business:
A) Will make a profit in the coming year.
B) Will not be able C) Will continue in operational existence for the foreseeable future.
D) Can be sold for its net asset value.
A: C
E: The going concern assumption means that the business is expected to continue its operations without any intention or necessity of liquidation or ceasing trade in the near future.

Q: In partnership accounts, if interest on capital is allowed, it is:
A) An expense debited to the Profit and Loss Account.
B) An appropriation of profit debited to the Profit and Loss Appropriation Account.
C) A direct payment to partners from the bank account.
D) Added to the firm's liabilities.
A: B
E: Interest on capital is a way of distributing profit to partners as a return on their investment, not an operating expense of the firm.

Q: What is the effect of paying a creditor on the accounting equation?
A) Assets increase, Liabilities increase.
B) Assets decrease, Liabilities decrease.
C) Assets decrease, Equity decreases.
D) Liabilities decrease, Equity increases.
A: B
E: Paying a creditor involves a decrease in an asset (Cash/Bank) and a decrease in a liability (Accounts Payable).

Q: An example of a nominal account is:
A) Cash Account
B) Machinery Account
C) Rent Expense Account
D) Debtors Account
A: C
E: Nominal accounts are accounts for incomes, expenses, gains, and losses, which are closed to the Profit and Loss Account at year-end. Real accounts (assets) and personal accounts (debtors, creditors) are balanced and carried forward.

Q: Which of the following financial statements reports financial performance over a period of time?
A) Balance Sheet
B) Income Statement
C) Cash Flow Statement (also reports over a period, but primarily cash performance)
D) Statement of Changes in Equity
A: B
E: The Income Statement (or Statement of Comprehensive Income) summarizes revenues earned and expenses incurred over a specific accounting period to arrive at net profit or loss. The Cash Flow Statement also reports over a period but focuses on cash movements. Given typical WAEC emphasis, "financial performance" usually points to Income Statement.

Q: A debit balance in a partner's Current Account indicates that:
A) The partner has introduced extra capital.
B) The firm owes money to the partner.
C) The partner owes money to the firm (e.g., drawings/losses exceed profit share/interest).
D) The partner has a loan with the firm.
A: C
E: Under a fixed capital system, a debit balance in the Current Account means the partner has taken out more (through drawings, interest on drawings, share of loss) than their entitlements (salary, interest on capital, share of profit).

Q: Authorized share capital of a company refers to the:
A) Amount of capital actually paid by shareholders.
B) Total nominal value of shares the company has offered to the public.
C) Maximum nominal value of shares the company is legally permitted to issue.
D) Market value of all issued shares.
A: C
E: Authorized (or nominal) capital is stated in the company's Memorandum of Association as the upper limit of shares it can issue.

Q: Which of these items would appear on the credit side of a Purchases Ledger Control Account?
A) Cash paid to suppliers
B) Credit purchases
C) Discounts received from suppliers
D) Returns outwards
A: B
E: The Purchases Ledger Control Account (Accounts Payable) is a liability account. It is credited with credit purchases (increasing the liability) and debited with payments, discounts received, and returns outwards.

Q: Inventory is valued at the lower of cost and net realizable value (NRV) due to the:
A) Consistency concept
B) Going concern concept
C) Prudence (Conservatism) concept
D) Business entity concept
A: C
E: The prudence concept dictates that assets should not be overstated. If NRV is lower than cost, inventory must be written down to NRV to recognize a potential loss.

Q: Which activity in the Cash Flow Statement would include proceeds from the sale of old machinery?
A) Operating activities
B) Investing activities
C) Financing activities
D) Non-cash activities
A: B
E: The sale (disposal) of non-current assets like machinery is classified under investing activities.

Q: If a company's Current Ratio is 2.5:1 and its Quick Ratio is 0.8:1, it may indicate:
A) A high level of cash and bank balances.
B) A significant proportion of current assets is tied up in inventory and/or prepayments.
C) Very low levels of current liabilities.
D) Efficient collection of accounts receivable.
A: B
E: The Quick Ratio excludes inventory and prepayments. A significant drop from the Current Ratio to the Quick Ratio implies that inventory and/or prepayments make up a large part of current assets.

Q: The "double entry" principle means that for every transaction:
A) Two accounts must be debited.
B) Two accounts must be credited.
C) One account is debited and another account is credited with an equal amount.
D) The transaction is recorded twice in the journal.
A: C
E: Every transaction has a dual aspect, requiring equal debit and credit entries to maintain the balance of the accounting equation.

Q: If a business overpays a creditor, this may result in:
A) A credit balance in the creditor's account in the purchases ledger.
B) A debit balance in the creditor's account in the purchases ledger.
C) A credit balance in the cash book.
D) A debit balance in the bank statement.
A: B
E: Normally, a creditor's account (liability) has a credit balance. If overpaid, the business has effectively made an advance or is due a refund, creating a debit balance (an asset) in that specific creditor's account.

Q: "Window dressing" in financial statements refers to:
A) Using clear and understandable formats for presentation.
B) Illegally falsifying accounting records.
C) Manipulating financial figures legally (within accounting rules) to make results appear more favorable than they are.
D) Providing excessive detail in the notes to the accounts.
A: C
E: Window dressing involves making transactions or choosing accounting methods (often at year-end) to present a better picture of financial performance or position, without necessarily breaking laws or standards but potentially violating the spirit of faithful representation.

Q: Which of these is NOT an appropriation of partnership profit?
A) Interest on partners' capital

A) To indicate the amount of the transaction.
B) To provide a brief explanation of the transaction.
C) To cross-reference the journal entry to the ledger account number where it has been posted.
D) To show the date of the transaction.
A: C
E: The ledger folio number in the journal shows which ledger account the debit or credit entry has been posted to, aiding in tracing and verifying.

Q: If an error of $100 understating an expense is discovered after the trial balance is prepared (and it balances due to a compensating error not yet found), how would correcting this error affect net profit?
A) Net profit would increase by $100.
B) Net profit would decrease by $100.
C) Net profit would not change.
D) Net profit would decrease by $200.
A: B
E: Correcting the understatement of an expense means recognizing an additional $100 expense, which will reduce the net profit by $100.

Q: "Balancing c/d" (carried down) in a ledger account represents:
A) The opening balance for the period.
B) The total debits of the account.
C) The closing balance of the account that will become the opening balance for the next period.
D) An error in the account.
A: C
E: Balance c/d is the figure needed to make both sides of the account total equally; it is the closing balance.

Q: An internal document prepared to authorize payment for an expense is typically a:
A) Sales invoice
B) Payment voucher
C) Goods Received Note
D) Credit note
A: B
E: A payment voucher is often used internally to provide authorization and details for making a payment, especially for expenses.

Q: A petty cash system operating on an imprest basis means that:
A) The petty cashier can spend an unlimited amount.
B) At any time, cash in hand plus vouched expenses should equal the fixed float.
C) Reimbursement is made weekly regardless of spending.
D) Only specific types of expenses can be paid from petty cash.
A: B
E: The core principle of the imprest system is that the petty cash float is restored to its original fixed amount, meaning cash + vouchers = float.

Q: An error where $250 cash paid for motor expenses was correctly credited to cash but debited to motor vehicles account (an asset) is an:
A) Error of omission
B) Error of commission
C) Error of principle
D) Compensating error
A: C
E: This is an error of principle because an expense (revenue expenditure) has been incorrectly treated as capital expenditure (an asset).

Q: Which of these is NOT a purpose of the trial balance?
A) To list all ledger account balances.
B) To verify the arithmetical equality of debits and credits.
C) To provide a basis for preparing financial statements.
D) To confirm that all transactions have been recorded correctly and in the correct accounts.
A: D
E: A trial balance that balances does not guarantee freedom from all errors (e.g., omission, principle, commission). It only checks arithmetic.

Q: Adjusting entries are typically recorded:
A) Throughout the accounting period as transactions occur.
B) At the end of the accounting period, before financial statements are prepared.
C) Only when errors are discovered.
D) At the beginning of the accounting period.
A: B
E: Adjusting entries are made at period-end to bring accounts up-to-date on an accrual basis (e.g., for accrued expenses, prepaid expenses, depreciation).

Q: The discount columns in a three-column cash book are:
A) Part of the double entry system like cash and bank columns.
B) Memorandum columns, and their totals are posted to Discount Allowed/Received accounts in the general ledger.
C) Used to record trade discounts.
D) Balanced like cash and bank columns.
A: B
E: The discount columns function as a form of journal; their totals are posted as single entries to the respective discount accounts. They themselves are not ledger accounts.

Chapter 3: Statement of Financial Position (Balance Sheet) (Continued)

Q: Assets are classified into current and non-current based on:
A) Their physical size.
B) Their expected period of use or conversion into cash (typically one year or operating cycle).
C) Whether they were purchased for cash or on credit.
D) Their importance to the business.
A: B
E: The key distinction is the timeframe: current assets are expected to be used/converted to cash within a year or operating cycle, while non-current assets are held for longer-term use.

Q: Accumulated Depreciation account is an example of:
A) An asset account.
B) A liability account.
C) An equity account.
D) A contra-asset account.
A: D
E: It has a credit balance and is deducted from the related non-current asset's cost on the Balance Sheet to arrive at Net Book Value.

Q: The capital of a sole trader is affected by:
A) Only profits or losses.
B) Profits, losses, drawings, and additional capital introduced.
C) Only cash transactions.
D) Purchases and sales of goods.
A: B
E: All these items directly impact the owner's stake (equity/capital) in the business.

Q: A building owned by a business and used for its operations is a:
A) Current asset
B) Tangible non-current asset
C) Intangible non-current asset
D) Current liability
A: B
E: A building has physical substance and is held for long-term use, making it a tangible non-current asset.

Q: If a company uses the reducing balance method for depreciation, the amount of depreciation charged each year:
A) Is highest in the early years of the asset's life.
B) Is lowest in the early years of the asset's life.
C) Remains the same each year.
D) Is always equal to the straight-line depreciation amount.
A: A
E: The reducing balance method applies a fixed rate to a declining net book value, resulting in larger depreciation amounts in earlier years.

Q: An example of an item included in "Owner's Equity" on a sole trader's Balance Sheet is:
A) Bank Loan
B) Accounts Payable
C) Closing Capital (after adjustments for profit/loss and drawings)
D) Prepaid Expenses
A: C
E: Closing Capital represents the owner's net investment or stake in the business at the end of the period.

Q: Which of the following is NOT a reason for charging depreciation?
A) To allocate the cost of the asset over its useful life.
B) To reflect the decrease in the market value of the asset each year.
C) To match the cost of using the asset with the revenue it helps generate.
D) To show the asset at a more realistic value in the Balance Sheet (Net Book Value).
A: B
E: Depreciation is a cost allocation process, not a valuation process to determine market value. Market value can fluctuate independently of depreciation.

Q: What does "Working Capital" represent?
A) Total Assets - Total Liabilities
B) Current Assets - Current Liabilities
C) Non-Current Assets - Non-Current Liabilities
D) Owner's Capital + Net Profit
A: B
E: Working capital (or net current assets) is the excess of current assets over current liabilities, representing funds available for day-to-day operations.

Q: If total assets are $50,000 and total liabilities are $20,000, owner's equity is:
A) $70,000
B) $30,000
C) $20,000
D) Cannot be determined.
A: B
E: Equity = Assets - Liabilities = $50,000 - $20,000 = $30,000.

Q: An example of a current asset resulting from the accrual concept is:
A) Cash at bank
B) Accrued revenue (e.g., interest receivable)
C) Machinery
D) Accounts payable
A: B
E: Accrued revenue represents income earned but not yet received in cash, creating an asset (a receivable).

Chapter 4: Statement of Comprehensive Income (Income Statement) (Continued)

Q: Gross Profit is the difference between:
A) Net Sales and Operating Expenses.
B) Net Sales and Cost of Goods Sold.
C) Total Revenue and Total Expenses.
D) Operating Profit and Interest Expense.
A: B
E: Gross Profit = Net Sales - Cost of Goods Sold, representing the profit from buying/producing and selling goods before other operating expenses.

Q: Carriage outwards represents:
A) Cost of transporting goods purchased into the business.
B) Cost of transporting goods sold to customers.
C) An administrative expense.
D) A reduction in purchases.
A: B
E: Carriage outwards is a selling and distribution expense related to delivering goods to customers.

Q: Under the periodic inventory system, Cost of Goods Sold is calculated:
A) Continuously after each sale.
B) At the end of the accounting period using Opening Inventory + Purchases - Closing Inventory.
C) As Total Sales less Net Profit.
D) By counting the physical inventory sold daily.
A: B
E: In a periodic system, COGS is determined by a calculation at period-end after a physical stock count determines closing inventory.

Q: If opening inventory is $5,000, purchases are $20,000, and closing inventory is $3,000, Cost of Goods Sold is:
A) $18,000
B) $22,000
C) $28,000
D) $23,000
A: B
E: COGS = $5,000 (Opening) + $20,000 (Purchases) - $3,000 (Closing) = $22,000.

Q: Which inventory valuation method is NOT permitted under IFRS?
A) FIFO (First-In, First-Out)
B) LIFO (Last-In, First-Out)
C) Weighted Average Cost (AVCO)
D) Specific Identification
A: B
E: IFRS prohibits the LIFO method because it often does not reflect the actual physical flow of goods and can be used to distort profit.

Q: "Bad Debts Expense" is classified in the Income Statement as:
A) Part of Cost of Goods Sold.
B) An administrative expense or a selling expense (Operating Expense).
C) A non-operating expense.
D) A direct deduction from Sales Revenue.
A: B
E: Bad debts arise from credit sales and are typically considered an operating expense, often under selling or administrative categories.

Q: The Single-Step Income Statement format:
A) Provides detailed subtotals like Gross Profit.
B) Groups all revenues together and deducts total expenses in one step to arrive at Net Profit.
C) Is generally preferred by IFRS for its detailed analysis.
D) Separates operating and non-operating items clearly.
A: B
E: The single-step format is simpler, presenting total revenues less total expenses to reach net profit without intermediate subtotals like gross profit.

Q: A loss on the sale of a non-current asset would typically be shown in the Income Statement as:
A) Part of Cost of Goods Sold.
B) An operating expense.
C) Other Expense (or part of calculation of Profit Before Tax).
D) A reduction of sales revenue.
A: C
E: Gains or losses on the disposal of non-current assets are usually non-operating items and are included in arriving at Profit Before Tax, after Operating Profit.

Q: What is the impact of an increase in the "Allowance for Doubtful Debts" on the Income Statement?
A) It increases net profit.
B) It decreases net profit (by increasing Bad Debts Expense).
C) It has no effect on net profit, only the Balance Sheet.
D) It is treated as Other Comprehensive Income.
A: B
E: When the allowance is increased, an expense (Bad Debts Expense or Increase in Allowance for Doubtful Debts) is recognized in the Income Statement, reducing profit.

Q: Total Comprehensive Income includes:
A) Only Net Profit for the period.
B) Only Other Comprehensive Income items.
C) Net Profit for the period plus or minus Other Comprehensive Income items.
D) Gross Profit less Operating Expenses.
A: C
E: Total Comprehensive Income is a broader measure of performance, encompassing net profit (from P&L) and other gains/losses recognized directly in equity (OCI).

Chapter 5: Receivables and Payables Control Accounts (Continued)

Q: The main purpose of preparing a Purchases Ledger Control Account is to:
A) Ascertain the total cash paid to suppliers.
B) Provide a summary of all individual supplier account balances.
C) Calculate purchase discounts received.
D) Replace the need for a Purchases Day Book.
A: B
E: The Purchases Ledger Control Account (Accounts Payable Control) acts as a summary, and its balance should match the total of individual balances in the Purchases Ledger.

Q: A debit balance in the Purchases Ledger Control Account could arise from:
A) Overpayment to a supplier or a large return of goods after payment.
B) Normal credit purchases exceeding payments.
C) Failure to record supplier invoices.
D) Taking maximum purchase discounts.
A: A
E: Typically, PLCA has a credit balance (liability). A debit balance indicates the business is owed money by a supplier, perhaps due to an overpayment or a credit note received exceeding amounts owed.

Q: When an allowance for doubtful debts is initially created, the double entry is:
A) Debit Allowance for Doubtful Debts, Credit Bad Debts Expense
B) Debit Bad Debts Expense, Credit Allowance for Doubtful Debts
C) Debit Accounts Receivable, Credit Allowance for Doubtful Debts
D) Debit Allowance for Doubtful Debts, Credit Accounts Receivable
A: B
E: Creating the allowance involves recognizing an expense (Bad Debts Expense) and crediting the contra-asset account (Allowance for Doubtful Debts).

Q: Which items appear on the credit side of the Sales Ledger Control Account?
A) Credit sales, Dishonoured cheques from customers.
B) Cash received from customers, Sales returns, Discounts allowed, Bad debts written off.
C) Opening debit balance, Interest charged to customers on overdue accounts.
D) Cash sales, Carriage inwards.
A: B
E: Transactions that reduce the total amount owed by debtors are credited to the SLCA. These include cash receipts, returns, discounts, and write-offs.

Q: A Bank Reconciliation Statement is prepared using the Cash Book (bank column) and the:
A) Petty Cash Book
B) Sales Day Book
C) Bank Statement
D) General Ledger
A: C
E: The BRS reconciles the balance as per the company's cash book (bank records) with the balance as per the bank's record (bank statement).

Q: If the cash book shows a bank overdraft and the bank statement shows a positive balance, unpresented cheques would be a reason to:
A) Increase the cash book balance.
B) Decrease the bank statement balance to reconcile.
C) Increase the bank statement balance.
D) Decrease the cash book balance.
A: B
E: Unpresented cheques have been deducted in the cash book (making it more overdrawn or less positive) but not yet by the bank. So, to reconcile starting from the bank statement positive balance, you'd deduct them.

Q: Direct debits appearing on the bank statement but not in the cash book should be:
A) Added to the bank statement balance during reconciliation.
B) Recorded in the cash book as a debit to bank and credit to the relevant expense/liability.
C) Recorded in the cash book as a credit to bank and debit to the relevant expense/liability.
D) Ignored if the amount is small.
A: C
E: The cash book needs updating. A direct debit is a payment from the bank account, so credit Bank in the cash book and debit the appropriate expense or liability.

Q: A "dishonoured cheque" from a customer means:
A) The customer paid with a post-dated cheque.
B) The bank made an error in processing the cheque.
C) The cheque deposited by the business has been returned unpaid by the customer's bank.
D) The business issued a cheque that was refused by the supplier.
A: C
E: A dishonoured cheque means the customer's bank refused payment (e.g., due to insufficient funds), and the amount is debited back to the business's bank account.

Q: What is the effect of discount received on the Purchases Ledger Control Account?
A) It increases the credit balance.
B) It increases the debit balance.
C) It is recorded on the debit side, reducing the total liability to suppliers.
D) It is recorded on the credit side, increasing the total liability.
A: C
E: Discount received reduces the amount owed to suppliers, so it's a debit to the PLCA (and to individual supplier accounts).

Q: If the Accounts Receivable Control account balance is $10,000 and the Allowance for Doubtful Debts is $500, the net realizable value of receivables is:
A) $10,500
B) $10,000
C) $9,500
D) $500
A: C
E: Net Realizable Value = Gross Receivables - Allowance for Doubtful Debts = $10,000 - $500 = $9,500.

Chapter 6: Bills of Exchange and Promissory Notes (Continued)

Q: The act by which the drawee of a bill of exchange signifies their assent to the order of the drawer is called:
A) Endorsement
B) Noting
C) Acceptance
D) Discounting
A: C
E: Acceptance (usually by signing across the bill) makes the drawee liable to pay the bill.

Q: If a business issues a promissory note to a supplier, it will debit Accounts Payable and credit:
A) Bills Receivable
B) Cash
C) Bills Payable
D) Bank
A: C
E: A promissory note issued by the business represents a formal promise to pay, creating a liability termed Bills Payable.

Q: When the holder of a bill receivable endorses it to one of their creditors:
A) Bills Receivable is debited, and the Creditor's account is credited.
B) Bills Receivable is credited, and the Creditor's account is debited.
C) Cash is debited, and Bills Receivable is credited.
D) Creditor's account is debited, and Bank is credited.
A: B
E: The Bill Receivable (asset) is given away (Credit Bills Receivable). The liability to the Creditor is settled (Debit Creditor's Account/Accounts Payable).

Q: If an accepted bill of exchange is paid by the acceptor on the due date, the acceptor will debit:
A) Bills Receivable Account
B) Bills Payable Account
C) Drawer's Account
D) Cash/Bank Account
A: B
E: The acceptor is settling their liability (Bills Payable), so Bills Payable is debited, and Cash/Bank is credited.

Q: The difference between the face value of a bill and the cash received when it is discounted is known as:
A) Rebate
B) Interest
C) Discount charges (or Discounting Fee)
D) Noting charges
A: C
E: The bank charges a fee (discount charges) for providing cash before the bill's maturity date.

Q: What happens in the drawer's books if a bill they drew, and subsequently discounted with the bank, is dishonoured by the acceptor?
A) Debit Bills Receivable, Credit Bank
B) Debit Acceptor's (Debtor's) Account, Credit Bank
C) Debit Bank, Credit Acceptor's (Debtor's) Account
D) Debit Bad Debts, Credit Bills Receivable
A: B
E: The bank will recover the amount from the drawer (Credit Bank in drawer's books). The drawer will then re-establish the debt owed by the original acceptor (Debit Acceptor's/Debtor's Account).

Q: The maturity date of a bill drawn "3 months after date" on 1st March (ignoring days of grace for simplicity here if not specified in Q):
A) 1st May
B) 30th May
C) 1st June
D) 30th June
A: C
E: March to April (1), April to May (2), May to June (3). So, 1st June. (WAEC might specify if days of grace apply).

Q: Retiring a bill under rebate means:
A) The bill is dishonoured.
B) The acceptor pays the bill before its due date and gets a small discount.
C) The bill is renewed for a longer period.
D) The bill is paid by an endorser.
A: B
E: The acceptor benefits from early payment with a rebate (discount), and the holder agrees to this.

Q: A Bills Payable Journal is used by a business to record:
A) All bills of exchange it receives from its customers.
B) All bills of exchange it accepts (drawn by its suppliers) or promissory notes it issues.
C) All dishonoured bills.
D) All cash payments related to bills.
A: B
E: The Bills Payable Journal records the details of new Bills Payable liabilities incurred by the business.

Q: From the perspective of the maker, a Promissory Note is a:
A) Bill Receivable
B) Bill Payable
C) Contingent Liability
D) Non-current Asset
A: B
E: The maker is the one promising to pay, so for them, it is a Bill Payable (liability).

Chapter 7: Partnership Formation and Operations (Continued)

Q: Which of the following is LEAST likely to be included in a Partnership Deed?
A) Profit-sharing ratio.
B) Method of calculating annual depreciation on firm's assets.
C) Interest on capital and drawings.
D) Procedure for admission of a new partner.
A: B
E: While accounting policies might be agreed upon, specific detailed calculation methods for things like depreciation are more operational and might not be in the deed, unlike fundamental partner agreements (profit share, interest, admission).

Q: If a partner contributes a personal asset (e.g., a van) to the partnership as capital, the van should be recorded in the firm's books at:
A) The original cost of the van to the partner.
B) The agreed fair market value of the van at the time of contribution.
C) The partner's desired value.
D) Zero value, as it's not purchased by the firm.
A: B
E: Assets contributed by partners are recorded at their fair market value at the date they are brought into the partnership.

Q: The primary reason for preparing a Profit and Loss Appropriation Account in a partnership is to:
A) Calculate the net profit of the firm before any partner entitlements.
B) Show how the distributable profit (after interest on loans, etc.) is allocated among partners.
C) Record cash drawings made by partners.
D) List the assets and liabilities of the partnership.
A: B
E: It takes the net profit and then shows appropriations like interest on capital, salaries, and the final division of remaining profit/loss.

Q: Partners' salaries, if agreed in the deed, are treated as:
A) A charge against profit, debited to the main Profit and Loss Account.
B) An appropriation of profit, debited to the Profit and Loss Appropriation Account.
C) A reduction of the partners' capital contributions.
D) An expense that reduces the gross profit.
A: B
E: Similar to interest on capital, partners' salaries are considered a way of distributing profits, not an operating expense of the business itself.

Q: When a new partner is admitted and brings in cash for goodwill (premium method), the goodwill amount is typically credited to:
A) The Revaluation Account.
B) The old partners' capital/current accounts in their sacrificing ratio.
C) The new partner's capital account.
D) The firm's Profit and Loss Account.
A: B
E: The premium paid by the new partner for goodwill compensates the existing partners for the share of future profits they are giving up (sacrificing).

Q: A debit balance on a partner's Current Account implies that:
A) The partner is owed money by the firm.
B) The partner has made excess drawings or their share of losses/interest on drawings exceeds their entitlements (profit share, interest on capital, salary).
C) The partner has contributed additional capital.
D) The firm is highly profitable.
A: B
E: A debit balance indicates the partner has withdrawn more value than has been credited to them through appropriations, effectively owing the firm.

Q: If a Revaluation Account shows a credit balance upon the admission of a new partner, it indicates:
A) A net loss on revaluation of assets and liabilities.
B) A net profit on revaluation of assets and liabilities.
C) The new partner has contributed extra capital.
D) Goodwill has been written off.
A: B
E: A credit balance in the Revaluation Account means increases in asset values and/or decreases in liability values exceeded decreases in asset values and/or increases in liability values, resulting in a net profit on revaluation.

Q: Which type of capital account system in a partnership keeps the partners' original capital investments largely unchanged?
A) Fluctuating Capital System
B) Fixed Capital System
C) Nominal Capital System
D) Working Capital System
A: B
E: In the Fixed Capital System, the Capital Accounts are generally kept static, with most adjustments (profit share, drawings, interest, salary) going through separate Current Accounts.

Q: When goodwill is raised in the books of a partnership (though less common under IFRS for internally generated goodwill), it is often credited to old partners in:
A) Their new profit-sharing ratio.
B) Their old profit-sharing ratio.
C) Their sacrificing ratio.
D) Proportion to their loan accounts.
A: B
E: If internally generated goodwill is valued and brought into the books before a change in partnership, it reflects the value built up by the old partners and is shared among them in their existing old PSR.

Q: Upon the death of a partner, the amount due to the deceased partner's estate typically includes their share of:
A) Only future profits of the firm.
B) Accumulated reserves, revaluation profit, goodwill, and their capital balance up to date of death.
C) The total assets of the firm.
D) The sales revenue for the current year.
A: B
E: The deceased partner's estate is entitled to their full share in the firm up to the date of death, including profits, reserves, asset revaluations, and goodwill.

B) $60
C) $40
D) $160

Q: Which type of shares carries the highest risk and potentially the highest reward for investors?
A) Cumulative Preference Shares
B) Redeemable Preference Shares
C) Ordinary Shares (Equity Shares)

Chapter 9: Cash Flow Statement and Funds Flow Statement (Continued)

Q: Cash Equivalents, for the purpose of a Cash Flow Statement, are:
A) All current assets.
B) Short-term, highly liquid investments readily convertible to known cash amounts with insignificant risk of value change.
C) Inventory and accounts receivable.
D) Undeposited cash receipts.
A: B
E: Cash equivalents (e.g., treasury bills with short maturities) are very close to cash in terms of liquidity and risk.

Q: Using the direct method for operating cash flows, "cash paid to suppliers" is calculated by adjusting:

B) Cost of Goods Sold for changes in inventory and payables.
C) Operating expenses for non-cash items.
D) Net profit for changes in working capital.

Q: Which of these is generally NOT a cash flow from investing activities?
A) Sale of a building.
B) Purchase of shares in another company as a long-term investment.
C) Cash received from issuing debentures.
D) Acquisition of a patent.
A: C
E: Issuing debentures (borrowing long-term) is a financing activity as it relates to how the company is funded. Others are acquisitions/disposals of long-term assets/investments.

Q: If a company repays the principal amount of a bank loan, it is classified as:
A) Cash outflow from operating activities.
B) Cash outflow from investing activities.
C) Cash outflow from financing activities.
D) A non-cash transaction.
A: C
E: Repayment of loan principal is a financing activity, as it reduces the company's borrowings. (Interest paid could be operating or financing).

Let's rethink C more carefully. Paying CL (Trade Creditors) with another CL (Overdraft) within the category of current liabilities can be seen as no change in Net WC if one simply replaces another equally.

Q: The assumption that a business will continue to operate for an indefinitely long period in the future is the:
A) Accrual concept
B) Monetary unit assumption
C) Going concern concept
D) Business entity concept
A: C

Q: The ultimate purpose of interpreting financial information is to:

C) Ensure all transactions are recorded.

A: C
E: The General Journal is for miscellaneous, infrequent, or adjusting/correcting entries not covered by special journals like sales, purchases, or cash books.

D) Dr Stationery Expense $50, Cr Cash $50 (Original entry error not the cash side usually).

D) Only if the initial trial balance does not balance.

D) Net Profit

Q: An increase in the value of land owned by a business due to market appreciation, if recognized in the accounts, would typically lead to a credit in:
A) Retained Earnings
B) Revaluation Reserve (or Revaluation Surplus under Equity)
C) Sales Revenue
D) Cash Account

Q: "Amortization" is a term similar to depreciation but usually applied to:

C) Intangible non-current assets (e.g., patents, copyrights).
D) Current liabilities.
A: C

E: Net Book Value = Cost - Accumulated Depreciation = $20,000 - $8,000 = $12,000. Market value is different.

Chapter 4: Statement of Comprehensive Income (Income Statement) (Continued Set 3)

Q: If sales are $100,000, sales returns are $5,000, and cost of goods sold is $60,000, the gross profit is:
A) $35,000
B) $40,000
C) $45,000
D) $95,000
A: A
E: Net Sales = $100,000 - $5,000 = $95,000. Gross Profit = Net Sales - COGS = $95,000 - $60,000 = $35,000.

Q: An example of a "Selling and Distribution Expense" is:
A) Office rent
B) Depreciation of factory machinery
C) Advertising costs
D) Interest paid on a loan
A: C
E: Advertising costs are incurred to promote sales and distribute products. Office rent is admin, factory depreciation is COGS, loan interest is finance/other expense.

Q: In a period of falling prices (deflation), which inventory valuation method generally results in the highest Cost of Goods Sold?
A) FIFO
B) LIFO (if permitted)
C) Weighted Average Cost
D) Specific Identification (depends on which specific items sold)
A: A
E: Under FIFO, the first (oldest) items purchased are assumed sold first. In deflation, these older items would have higher costs than more recent purchases, leading to a higher COGS.

Q: The figure representing "Profit from Operations" is also known as:
A) Gross Profit
B) Net Profit After Tax
C) Earnings Before Interest and Tax (EBIT)
D) Retained Earnings
A: C
E: Operating Profit (Profit from Operations) is calculated before deducting interest expense and income tax expense.

Q: If Gross Profit is $40,000, Operating Expenses are $25,000, Other Income is $5,000, and Interest Expense is $3,000, Profit Before Tax is:
A) $10,000
B) $12,000
C) $17,000
D) $23,000
A: C
E: Operating Profit = $40,000 - $25,000 = $15,000. Profit Before Tax = $15,000 + $5,000 (Other Inc) - $3,000 (Interest Exp) = $17,000.

Q: "Carriage on purchases" (Carriage Inwards) is treated as:
A) An addition to sales revenue.
B) An addition to the cost of purchases (and thus COGS).
C) A selling expense.
D) An administrative expense.
A: B
E: Carriage inwards is the cost of transporting goods purchased into the business and is considered part of the total cost of those purchases.

Q: Which accounting concept is most directly related to the "lower of cost and net realizable value" rule for inventory?
A) Consistency
B) Prudence (Conservatism)
C) Business Entity
D) Going Concern
A: B
E: The prudence concept advises caution and means that potential losses (like a fall in inventory value below cost) should be recognized, but potential gains should not be anticipated.

Q: What is "Net Sales"?
A) Total cash sales only.
B) Gross sales less cost of goods sold.
C) Gross sales less sales returns and allowances (and less sales discounts).
D) Sales revenue after deducting all operating expenses.
A: C
E: Net sales is the revenue from sales after accounting for returns, allowances given to customers, and any cash discounts taken by customers for prompt payment.

Q: If a business overestimates its bad debts expense for the year, the effect on its reported net profit for that year will be:
A) Net profit will be overstated.
B) Net profit will be understated.
C) Net profit will not be affected.
D) Only gross profit will be affected.
A: B
E: Overestimating bad debts expense means recognizing a higher expense than necessary, which will reduce the reported net profit.

Q: When an Income Statement is prepared using the "function of expense" method (as is common), expenses are classified according to:
A) Whether they are fixed or variable.
B) Their business function, e.g., cost of sales, selling expenses, administrative expenses.
C) Their nature, e.g., salaries, rent, depreciation, regardless of function.
D) Whether they are cash or non-cash expenses.
A: B
E: Classifying by function groups expenses according to the activity they relate to (e.g., selling, administration). Classifying by nature lists individual expense types. IFRS allows either.

Q: The main source of information for entries in the Sales Ledger Control Account, related to total credit sales, is the:
A) Total of the Cash Book (receipts side).
B) Total of the Sales Day Book (Sales Journal).
C) Schedule of Debtors.
D) General Journal.
A: B
E: The total credit sales for the period, recorded in the Sales Day Book, is posted as a debit to the Sales Ledger Control Account.

Q: When a business pays its supplier and receives a cash discount, the double entry in the supplier's individual account in the Purchases Ledger is:
A) Debit Cash, Credit Discount Received.
B) Debit the full invoice amount (to clear the liability).
C) Credit Cash Paid, Credit Discount Received.
D) Debit Cash Paid, Debit Discount Received.
A: B
E: The supplier's account (a liability) is debited with the full invoice amount being settled. The corresponding credits are to Cash/Bank (for amount paid) and Discount Received (for discount taken) which are reflected in the cash book and general ledger discount account.

Q: An increase in the Allowance for Doubtful Debts from one year to the next will:
A) Be credited to the Bad Debts Expense account.
B) Be debited to the Bad Debts Expense account.
C) Be debited to the Accounts Receivable account.
D) Be credited to the Sales Revenue account.
A: B
E: An increase in the required allowance means an additional expense must be recognized. Debit Bad Debts Expense, Credit Allowance for Doubtful Debts.

Q: "Deposits in Transit" or "Outstanding Lodgements" in a bank reconciliation are:
A) Cheques issued by the business but not yet cashed by payees.
B) Deposits recorded in the cash book but not yet credited by the bank on the bank statement.
C) Bank charges not yet recorded in the cash book.
D) Direct credits made into the bank account by customers.
A: B
E: These are amounts the business has deposited and recorded, but they haven't yet appeared on the bank statement.

Q: If a business receives a cheque from a customer which is later returned by the bank as "NSF" (Not Sufficient Funds), the business should:
A) Debit Bank, Credit Customer's Account (Debtor).
B) Debit Customer's Account (Debtor), Credit Bank.
C) Debit Bad Debts Expense, Credit Bank.
D) Make no entry until cash is received.
A: B
E: The bank will reduce the business's bank balance (Credit Bank in cash book). The customer still owes the money, so their account (Debtor) is debited to re-establish the receivable.

Q: What is a contra entry relating to control accounts? (This might be an ambiguous Q in WAEC context. "Contra entries" are mainly for Cash Book). Let's rephrase for SLCA/PLCA.
Q: A set-off or contra between a customer's balance in the sales ledger and their balance as a supplier in the purchases ledger is recorded by:
A) Debiting Sales Ledger Control A/c and Crediting Purchases Ledger Control A/c.
B) Crediting Sales Ledger Control A/c and Debiting Purchases Ledger Control A/c.
C) Only adjusting the individual subsidiary ledger accounts.
D) Debiting both control accounts.
A: B
E: If a person is both a customer and a supplier, and a set-off is agreed, the amount receivable (SLCA) is reduced (Credit SLCA), and the amount payable (PLCA) is reduced (Debit PLCA).

Q: Trade discounts are granted by sellers to buyers primarily to:
A) Encourage prompt payment.
B) Arrive at the actual transaction price, often for bulk purchases or trade status.
C) Compensate for faulty goods.
D) Reduce the seller's tax liability.
A: B
E: Trade discounts are price reductions from list prices before the invoice price is determined. Prompt payment is encouraged by cash discounts.

Q: If the Sales Ledger Control Account has a credit balance, it could mean:
A) The business has overpaid its debtors.
B) Debtors have paid in advance, or there's a large credit note exceeding amounts owed by some debtors.
C) The business has very high sales.
D) Too many bad debts have been written off.
A: B
E: Normally SLCA has a debit balance (asset). A credit balance for the control account (or an individual debtor) suggests the business owes that specific debtor, perhaps due to an overpayment or an advance payment for goods.

Q: After updating the Cash Book for items on the Bank Statement (like bank charges, direct credits), the next step in bank reconciliation is to:
A) Prepare the Income Statement.
B) Prepare the Bank Reconciliation Statement itself by comparing the adjusted Cash Book balance with the Bank Statement balance and identifying outstanding items.
C) Close all nominal accounts.
D) Calculate the Quick Ratio.
A: B
E: Once the Cash Book is up-to-date with items already processed by the bank, the formal BRS is prepared to account for timing differences (unpresented cheques, deposits in transit) and bank errors.

Q: The direct write-off method for bad debts:
A) Is preferred under IFRS for better matching.
B) Recognizes bad debt expense only when a specific account is deemed uncollectible.
C) Involves creating an allowance for doubtful debts at year-end.
D) Results in a more conservative Balance Sheet valuation of receivables.
A: B
E: The direct write-off method doesn't anticipate bad debts; expense is recorded only at the point a specific debt is identified as bad. The allowance method is preferred for matching.

Chapter 7: Partnership Formation and Operations (Continued Set 3)

Q: The legal concept meaning that partners are individually and collectively liable for all debts of the partnership is:
A) Limited liability
B) Separate legal entity
C) Unlimited liability (often joint and several)
D) Mutual agency
A: C
E: In a general partnership, partners typically have unlimited liability, meaning their personal assets can be used to pay firm debts if firm assets are insufficient.

Q: If a partnership agreement provides for interest on drawings, this interest:

B) The partner's Current Account.
C) The Profit and Loss Account.
D) The Drawings Expense Account.
A: B

B) Interest on drawings

Chapter 9: Cash Flow Statement and Funds Flow Statement (Continued Set 3)

Q: The main difference between the Direct and Indirect methods for preparing the operating activities section of the Cash Flow Statement lies in:
A) The final net cash flow from operating activities figure.
B) The classification of investing and financing activities.
C) How the cash flows from core revenue-generating activities are presented (gross flows vs. reconciliation from net profit).
D) The treatment of non-cash transactions.
A: C
E: The direct method shows actual cash receipts (e.g., from customers) and payments (e.g., to suppliers). The indirect method starts with net profit and adjusts. The net result is the same.

Q: Purchase of short-term government bonds (maturing in 60 days) with surplus cash by a company would be:
A) An operating cash outflow.
B) An investing cash outflow.
C) A financing cash outflow.
D) Usually part of "cash and cash equivalents" management, so a shift within that category (not a flow from op/inv/fin).
A: D
E: Such short-term, highly liquid investments are often treated as cash equivalents. Movements between cash and cash equivalents are not reported as operating, investing, or financing flows but are part of managing the total cash equivalent pool.

Q: If a company has a net loss for the year, its cash flow from operating activities (using indirect method):
A) Must also be negative.
B) Could be positive if non-cash expenses (like depreciation) and positive working capital changes exceed the net loss.
C) Is always equal to the net loss.
D) Is reported under financing activities.
A: B
E: A net loss is the starting point. Adding back large non-cash charges (like depreciation) or favorable working capital movements (e.g., decrease in receivables, increase in payables) can result in positive operating cash flow.

Q: An "application of funds" in a Funds Flow Statement (working capital basis) is a transaction that:
A) Increases working capital.
B) Decreases working capital or uses existing working capital.
C) Has no effect on working capital.
D) Is related only to non-current assets.
A: B
E: Applications (uses) of funds involve activities like purchasing non-current assets, paying dividends, or redeeming long-term debt, which reduce working capital or use funds generated from operations or other sources.

Q: If a company sells an old machine for cash at a price equal to its Net Book Value, the effect on the Cash Flow Statement is:
A) Cash inflow under operating activities.
B) Cash inflow under investing activities; no gain/loss to adjust in operating (indirect method).
C) Cash inflow under financing activities.
D) No cash flow effect, only an accounting adjustment.
A: B
E: The cash proceeds from selling a non-current asset (machine) are an investing inflow. If sold at NBV, there's no gain or loss, so no adjustment related to this would be needed from net profit in the operating section (indirect method).

Q: An increase in trade creditors (Accounts Payable) over a period means that, for operating cash flows (indirect method):
A) Less cash was paid to suppliers than expenses/purchases incurred, so add back to net profit.
B) More cash was paid to suppliers, so deduct from net profit.
C) It represents an investing activity.
D) It reduces net profit directly.
A: A
E: If payables increase, the company has effectively received more credit from suppliers, or paid less than it incurred in costs/purchases, thus conserving cash relative to the recognized expense/cost.

Q: "Free Cash Flow" (FCF) is often calculated as Operating Cash Flow minus:
A) Dividends Paid
B) Capital Expenditures (for maintaining/expanding assets)
C) Interest Paid
D) Net increase in working capital
A: B
E: FCF represents the cash available to all investors (debt and equity holders) after the company has made investments in fixed assets needed to maintain or expand its operations.

Q: A primary use of the Cash Flow Statement by investors is to assess:
A) The market value of the company's shares.
B) The company's ability to generate future cash flows to pay dividends and service debt.
C) The total assets owned by the company.
D) The historical cost of non-current assets.
A: B
E: Investors use the CFS to evaluate the company's cash-generating ability, its liquidity, solvency, and its capacity to fund operations, investments, and returns to providers of capital.

Q: If a company issues shares to acquire another business (a non-cash transaction), this should be:
A) Shown as both a cash inflow from financing and a cash outflow for investing in the Cash Flow Statement.
B) Ignored completely as no cash changed hands.
C) Disclosed in the notes to the Cash Flow Statement as a significant non-cash investing and financing activity.
D) Added to net profit under operating activities.
A: C
E: Significant non-cash transactions like acquiring assets by issuing equity are not part of the cash flow reconciliation itself but must be disclosed in the notes for completeness.

Q: Which of these indicates a use of working capital in a Funds Flow Statement?
A) Issue of shares for cash.
B) Depreciation of fixed assets.
C) Purchase of a non-current asset for cash.
D) Decrease in current liabilities.
A: C
E: Purchasing a non-current asset (fixed asset) for cash uses up current assets (cash), thus reducing working capital, making it an application (use) of funds. Depreciation is a non-fund item. Share issue is source. Decrease in CL increases WC.

Q: If current assets increase by $10,000 and current liabilities decrease by $5,000, what is the effect on working capital?
A) Increase by $5,000
B) Increase by $15,000
C) Decrease by $5,000
D) Decrease by $15,000
A: B
E: Working Capital = CA - CL. New WC = (CA + 10k) - (CL - 5k) = CA + 10k - CL + 5k = (CA - CL) + 15k. So, increases by $15,000.

Q: An amount set aside out of profits to meet a known liability whose timing or exact amount is uncertain is a:
A) Reserve
B) Provision
C) Contingent Asset
D) Accumulated Depreciation
A: B
E: A provision (e.g., provision for warranty claims, provision for doubtful debts before it becomes an allowance specific to A/R) is a liability of uncertain timing or amount, recognized when an obligation exists and a reliable estimate can be made. Reserves are appropriations of profit for general or specific purposes but not to meet known liabilities.

Chapter 1: Introduction to Accounting and Its Objectives (Continued Set 4)

Q: Which qualitative characteristic of financial information enables users to identify similarities and differences between sets of information (e.g., between two companies or two periods)?
A) Relevance
B) Faithful Representation
C) Comparability
D) Verifiability
A: C

Q: The accounting process involves all the following steps EXCEPT:
A) Identifying economic events.
B) Recording financial transactions.
C) Guaranteeing future profits for the business.
D) Communicating financial information to users.
A: C
E: While accounting provides information that can help in profit generation, it does not guarantee profits.

Q: An owner withdraws goods costing $100 from the business for personal use. The effect on the accounting equation is:
A) Assets (Inventory) decrease $100, Equity (Drawings increase, so Equity decreases) $100.
B) Assets (Inventory) decrease $100, Liabilities decrease $100.
C) Equity (Drawings increase) $100, Liabilities increase $100.
D) Assets (Cash) decrease $100, Equity (Drawings increase) $100.
A: A
E: Inventory (an asset) reduces. Drawings increase, which is a reduction in the owner's equity.

Q: Which branch of accounting involves the independent examination of an entity's financial statements to express an opinion on their truth and fairness?
A) Cost Accounting
B) Management Accounting
C) Tax Accounting
D) Auditing
A: D
E: Auditing provides an independent verification of financial statements, enhancing their credibility.

Q: The realization aspect of the revenue recognition principle primarily focuses on:
A) When cash is collected from the customer.
B) The probability that the economic benefits (cash) associated with the transaction will flow to the entity.
C) The cost of producing the goods sold.
D) The need to match expenses with revenues.
A: B
E: Realization means that it is probable that the seller will collect the consideration to which it is entitled in exchange for the goods or services transferred.

Q: In the expanded accounting equation (Assets = Liabilities + Capital - Drawings + Revenues - Expenses), an increase in expenses will:
A) Increase assets.
B) Decrease liabilities.
C) Decrease equity.
D) Increase revenue.
A: C
E: Expenses reduce profits, and reduced profits lead to a decrease in owner's equity.

Q: The "monetary unit assumption" in accounting means that:
A) Only cash transactions are recorded.
B) All business transactions can be expressed and recorded in terms of a stable unit of money.
C) Businesses should only operate in one currency.
D) The value of money is always constant.
A: B
E: This assumption allows for the aggregation and reporting of diverse economic events using a common denominator (money), although it ignores the effects of inflation/deflation unless specific standards address it.

Q: Which of these accounts has a normal debit balance?

A: B

Q: The process of determining the difference between the total debits and total credits in a ledger account to find its closing balance is called:

Chapter 3: Statement of Financial Position (Balance Sheet) (Continued Set 4)

Q: Which of the following statements best describes "Equity" in a business?
A) The total amount of cash held by the business.
B) The net worth of the business, representing the owners' claim on the net assets.
C) The total value of all non-current assets.
D) The profit earned by the business over its entire lifetime.
A: B
E: Equity (Assets - Liabilities) represents the residual interest of the owners in the business's assets after all liabilities are settled.

Q: An example of a financial instrument that is often a "Cash Equivalent" on the Balance Sheet is:
A) Shares held in a subsidiary company.
B) A 5-year government bond.
C) A 30-day Treasury Bill purchased with surplus cash.
D) Trade receivables due in 60 days.
A: C

Q: "Net Book Value" or "Carrying Amount" of a depreciable non-current asset will decrease each year by:
A) The amount of depreciation charged for that year.
B) The market value fluctuation.
C) Any repairs and maintenance costs incurred.
D) Interest charges related to its purchase.
A: A
E: As depreciation is charged (expense) and accumulated depreciation increases, the NBV (Cost - Accumulated Depreciation) decreases.

A) An asset.
B) A liability.
C) A deduction from Capital in the Equity section.
D) An operating expense.
A: C
E: Drawings represent a reduction in the owner's equity (capital) and are shown as such when calculating closing capital.

Q: If a business takes out a long-term loan repayable over 10 years, the portion of that loan due for repayment within the next 12 months should be classified as:
A) A non-current asset.
B) A current liability (Current Portion of Long-Term Debt).
C) Entirely a non-current liability.
D) Part of owner's equity.
A: B
E: The part of a long-term debt maturing within the next operating cycle or year is reclassified as a current liability.

Q: What is the purpose of recording "Accumulated Depreciation"?

B) To maintain a record of the total depreciation charged against an asset since it was acquired.
C) To create a cash fund for replacing the asset.
D) To directly reduce the cost of the asset in its ledger account.
A: B
E: Accumulated Depreciation is a contra-asset account that totals all depreciation expensed to date for a particular asset or class of assets.

Q: The straight-line method of depreciation results in:
A) A higher depreciation charge in later years.
B) An equal amount of depreciation expense being charged each year over the asset's useful life.
C) A depreciation charge based on the asset's usage or output.
D) The asset's net book value remaining constant.
A: B
E: This method spreads the depreciable amount evenly across each period of the asset's estimated useful life.

C) A current asset.
D) Inventory.
A: B
E: A patent provides exclusive rights, has value, but lacks physical substance, making it an intangible non-current asset.

Q: If an asset is depreciated more rapidly in its early years, such as with the reducing balance method, this may be justified because:

E: For some assets, their efficiency and contribution to revenue generation are higher initially, justifying a higher depreciation charge then, especially if repair costs are expected to rise later.

Q: When a sole trader contributes their private car to the business, the car should be recorded in the business books at:

E: Assets brought into a business by the owner are valued at their fair market value to accurately reflect the capital contributed.

Q: "Cost of Goods Available for Sale" is calculated as:

E: Gross Profit = $200,000 - $120,000 = $80,000. Operating Profit = Gross Profit - Operating Expenses = $80,000 - $50,000 = $30,000.

A: C

A) Original purchase cost.
B) Gross profit margin.
C) Estimated costs of completion and estimated costs necessary to make the sale.
D) Accumulated depreciation.
A: C
E: NRV is the net amount a company expects to realize from selling inventory in the ordinary course of business.

Q: An increase in sales revenue, all other factors remaining constant, will generally lead to:
A) A decrease in gross profit.
B) An increase in net profit.
C) An increase in cost of goods sold proportionally.
D) No change in operating expenses.
A: B
E: Higher sales (assuming costs per unit and operating expenses don't rise more than proportionally or are fixed) will usually result in higher gross and net profit. C is possible but not always exact proportion. D is often true for fixed operating expenses but not variable ones. B is the most direct general outcome.

A) Operating Profit
B) Net Profit Before Tax
C) Gross Profit
D) Net Profit After Tax
A: C
E: This is the definition of Gross Profit.

A) An operating expense.
B) Other Income or a reduction in Cost of Goods Sold/Purchases.
C) Sales revenue.
D) A selling expense.
A: B
E: Discount received for prompt payment to suppliers is either an income or reduces the cost of purchases (ultimately COGS).

Q: Under IFRS, when can revenue from a contract with a customer generally be recognized?

C) When the company satisfies a performance obligation by transferring control of a promised good or service to the customer.
D) When the goods are manufactured and ready for dispatch.

E: IFRS 15 bases revenue recognition on the satisfaction of performance obligations (transfer of control).

Q: If a company writes off bad debts using the "allowance method," the entry to write off a specific uncollectible account receivable:

B) There are errors in the recording or posting of credit purchase transactions or related items.
C) The company made too many cash purchases.

A: B

Q: The entry to record "cash discounts allowed" to customers will involve a debit to Discount Allowed account and a credit to:

C) The Bad Debts Expense account.
D) The Bank Account.

E: When a customer takes a discount, Cash/Bank is debited (amount received), Discount Allowed (expense) is debited, and the customer's account (part of Accounts Receivable) is credited with the full invoice amount settled. Individual customer account in SL is credited.

Q: An "unpresented cheque" in a bank reconciliation will:
A) Be added to the bank statement balance.
B) Be deducted from the bank statement balance.
C) Be added to the cash book balance.
D) Be deducted from the cash book balance.
A: B
E: These cheques have been recorded as payments (reducing balance) in the cash book but not yet processed by the bank, so the bank statement balance is higher than it should be and needs to be reduced for reconciliation.

Q: If the cash book shows a balance of $500 (Dr) and the bank statement shows a balance of $600 (Cr), and the only difference is a cheque of $100 issued by the business but not yet presented, then:
A) The cash book is incorrect.
B) The bank statement is incorrect.
C) The records are reconciled (Bank statement $600 - Unpresented cheque $100 = $500, matching adjusted Cash Book if Cash Book is the base or bank statement reconciles TO the cash book if no CB adj).
D) A suspense account is needed.
A: C
E: Reconciling from Bank Statement: $600 (Cr) - $100 (Unpresented) = $500. This matches the Cash Book balance. So they reconcile.

Q: Bank interest charged on an overdraft, appearing on the bank statement but not yet in the cash book, should be recorded in the cash book by:
A) Debiting Bank, Crediting Interest Income.
B) Debiting Interest Expense, Crediting Bank.
C) Debiting Bank, Crediting Interest Expense.
D) Debiting Interest Income, Crediting Bank.
A: B
E: Interest charged is an expense (Debit Interest Expense). It reduces the bank balance (Credit Bank in cash book, as Bank is an asset, reduction is Cr, or overdraft increases which is also a Cr).

Q: The primary reason for maintaining control accounts is to:
A) Provide detailed information about each individual debtor and creditor.
B) Help locate errors more easily by providing a summary check against subsidiary ledger totals.
C) Replace the need for preparing a trial balance.
D) Calculate cash discounts automatically.
A: B
E: Control accounts act as a summary and an arithmetical check on the accuracy of the postings made to the numerous individual accounts in the subsidiary ledgers.

Q: When using the percentage of receivables method to estimate the allowance for doubtful debts, the calculated figure represents:
A) The bad debts expense for the period.
B) The required ending balance in the Allowance for Doubtful Debts account.
C) The total actual bad debts written off during the period.
D) The cash to be collected from debtors.
A: B
E: This method aims to determine what the closing balance of the Allowance account should be to reflect receivables at their net realizable value. The expense is then the adjustment needed to reach this balance.

Q: If a company overpays a supplier by $200, its Purchase Ledger Control Account would (if only one PLCA exists), temporarily or by way of an item becoming a net debit, or individual account would have a debit, contributing to PLCA sum:
A) Have its overall credit balance increased by $200.
B) Show that a specific supplier's account (or the overall balance if a net position) is a debit of $200 or overall reduced credit if total creditors are high.
C) Have its overall debit balance increased by $200.
D) Not be affected, only the cash book.
A: B
E: Typically, the PLCA shows a total credit balance. An overpayment would mean for that specific supplier, they owe the business, creating a debit balance for that supplier within the purchases ledger. This might result in a net reduction of the overall credit balance of the PLCA or could lead to a rare net debit for the control account if such items are significant enough. "Show that a specific supplier's account... is a debit" reflects what's going on.

Q: The "due date" of a bill of exchange is the date on which:
A) The bill is drawn.
B) The bill is accepted by the drawee.
C) The bill becomes legally payable by the acceptor.
D) The bill is discounted with a bank.
A: C
E: The due date (or maturity date, often including days of grace) is the date the acceptor is obliged to make payment.

Q: "Noting" a dishonoured bill of exchange provides:
A) A guarantee of future payment.
B) An extension of the payment period.
C) Legal, formal evidence of the fact that the bill was presented for payment and was dishonoured.
D) A discount to the holder.
A: C
E: Noting by a Notary Public is a formal step to authenticate the dishonour, which can be useful if legal action is pursued.

Q: If the holder of a Bill Receivable chooses to "retire" it before maturity, allowing the acceptor a rebate, the rebate is an expense for the holder and:
A) An expense for the acceptor.
B) An income for the acceptor.
C) A liability for the acceptor.
D) An asset for the acceptor.
A: B
E: The acceptor benefits from paying early by getting a discount (rebate), which is treated as income (or reduction of an expense previously related to the bill if relevant) for them.

Q: The journal entry in the books of the acceptor (drawee) when they honour (pay) a Bill Payable of $2,000 by cheque is:
A) Dr Bills Payable $2,000; Cr Bank $2,000
B) Dr Bank $2,000; Cr Bills Payable $2,000
C) Dr Drawer's Account $2,000; Cr Bank $2,000
D) Dr Bills Payable $2,000; Cr Cash $2,000
A: A
E: The liability (Bills Payable) is extinguished (Debit). Payment is made from Bank (asset decreases, Credit).

Q: Which document typically has only two main parties: the maker and the payee?
A) Bill of Exchange
B) Cheque
C) Promissory Note
D) Letter of Credit
A: C
E: A promissory note involves the maker (who promises to pay) and the payee (to whom payment is promised). A bill of exchange involves three: drawer, drawee, payee.

Q: The process of calculating the period for which a bill is drawn, including any days of grace if applicable, is to determine its:
A) Discounting period
B) Acceptance date
C) Tenor and maturity date
D) Endorsement date
A: C
E: The tenor is the nominal duration of the bill. Adding days of grace (if any) to the nominal due date (calculated from date of bill/acceptance and tenor) gives the legal maturity date.

Chapter 7: Partnership Formation and Operations (Continued Set 4)

Q: One of the key advantages of forming a partnership over a sole proprietorship is:
A) Limited liability for all partners.
B) Access to more capital and wider range of skills/expertise.
C) Complete control by a single owner.
D) Simpler legal formalities and less regulation.
A: B
E: Partnerships can pool resources (capital) and talents from multiple individuals. A and D are generally disadvantages compared to companies/sole traders respectively. C is a feature of sole tradership.

Q: If the Partnership Deed specifies that Partner A is to receive a salary of $5,000 per annum, this salary is treated as:
A) An operating expense of the partnership, reducing net profit before appropriation.
B) An appropriation of partnership profit, shown in the Profit and Loss Appropriation Account.
C) A direct withdrawal from Partner A's capital account.
D) A loan from the firm to Partner A.
A: B
E: Partners' salaries are distributions of profit, not expenses against profit in the same way as employee salaries.

Q: A fluctuating capital account for a partner will typically be credited with all the following EXCEPT:
A) Partner's share of profit.
B) Interest on the partner's capital.
C) Drawings made by the partner.
D) Additional capital introduced by the partner.
A: C
E: Drawings are debited to the partner's capital account (or current account if fixed system) as they represent a withdrawal of equity.

Q: Upon the admission of a new partner into an existing partnership, why is it common to revalue existing assets and liabilities?
A) To determine the initial capital contribution of the new partner.
B) To ensure that the existing partners are fairly credited or debited for unrealized changes in asset/liability values before the new partner shares in future values.
C) To comply with requirements for converting to a limited company.
D) To reduce the firm's overall tax liability.
A: B
E: Revaluation ensures the incoming partner does not unfairly benefit from past gains or suffer from past losses that belong to the old partners.

Q: If Partner X and Partner Y share profits in the ratio 3:2, and the divisible profit for the year is $50,000, Partner X's share of profit is:
A) $20,000
B) $25,000
C) $30,000
D) $33,333
A: C
E: Total parts = 3 + 2 = 5. Partner X's share = (3/5) * $50,000 = $30,000.

Q: When an outgoing partner's share of goodwill is adjusted by debiting the remaining partners' capital accounts, it is usually done in their:
A) Old profit-sharing ratio.
B) New profit-sharing ratio after retirement.
C) Sacrificing ratio.
D) Gaining ratio (the ratio in which they acquire the outgoing partner's share).
A: D
E: The remaining partners who gain the share of the retiring partner typically compensate for the retiring partner's goodwill share in their gaining ratio.

Q: The rule in "Garner v Murray" addresses how to deal with:
A) The calculation of interest on partners' capital.
B) The distribution of profit in a partnership without a deed.
C) The sharing of a capital deficiency of an insolvent partner by the solvent partners upon dissolution.
D) The valuation of goodwill on the admission of a partner.
A: C
E: This legal precedent relates to the bearing of losses due to an insolvent partner's capital shortfall during partnership dissolution.

Q: Interest on partners' drawings, if charged, has what effect on the Profit and Loss Appropriation Account?
A) It is debited, reducing divisible profit.
B) It is credited, increasing divisible profit.
C) It is debited directly to partners' Capital Accounts.
D) It has no effect on this account.
A: B
E: Interest charged on drawings is treated as an income or addition to profits available for appropriation.

Q: The main purpose of a Partner's Current Account (under a fixed capital system) is to:
A) Record only the partner's initial and additional permanent capital.
B) Track all day-to-day cash transactions of the partnership.
C) Record all appropriations of profit (like interest, salary, profit share) and drawings for each partner, keeping their main capital account fixed.
D) Act as a loan account between the partner and the firm.
A: C
E: It allows the capital account to remain stable while current accounts reflect the periodic adjustments for profit, drawings, interest, etc.

Q: If a partnership makes a net loss for the year, this loss is typically:
A) Debited to the partners' loan accounts.
B) Credited to the partners' capital or current accounts in their profit-sharing ratio.
C) Debited to the partners' capital or current accounts in their profit-sharing ratio.
D) Carried forward as an asset.
A: C
E: A share of loss reduces each partner's equity and is thus debited to their respective capital or current account.

Q: A Public Limited Company (PLC) differs from a Private Limited Company (Ltd) because a PLC:
A) Has fewer regulatory requirements.

C) Can generally offer its shares to the public and its shares are often freely transferable (and may be listed on a stock exchange).

A: C

Q: The internal rules governing the conduct of a company's affairs, such as procedures for meetings and appointment of directors, are found in its:
A) Certificate of Incorporation
B) Memorandum of Association
C) Prospectus
D) Articles of Association (or Bylaws)
A: D
E: The Articles of Association detail the internal operational and governance rules of the company.

Q: Preference shares are described as "cumulative" if:
A) They give holders the right to vote at all company meetings.
B) Unpaid dividends from previous years accumulate and must be paid before ordinary shareholders receive dividends.
C) They can be converted into ordinary shares at the holder's option.
D) They participate in surplus profits with ordinary shareholders.
A: B
E: This feature protects preference shareholders by ensuring missed dividends are carried forward and paid when profits allow.

Q: When a company issues shares at a price above their nominal (par) value, the excess amount is recorded in:
A) The Share Capital account.
B) The Retained Earnings account.
C) The Share Premium account.
D) The General Reserve account.
A: C
E: Share Premium is a capital reserve that records the premium received over the nominal value of shares issued.

Q: A "Bonus Issue" (or Scrip Issue) of shares involves:
A) The company raising new cash by selling shares to existing shareholders.
B) The company converting its accumulated profits (reserves) into new fully paid shares issued free to existing shareholders.
C) The company buying back its own shares from the market.
D) Existing shareholders selling their shares to new investors.
A: B
E: A bonus issue capitalizes reserves, increasing share capital but not changing total equity or bringing in new cash.

Q: If a company forfeits shares for non-payment of a call, and then reissues these shares at a discount, the maximum discount allowed on reissue is generally limited to:
A) The nominal value of the shares.
B) The amount previously paid (forfeited) on those shares by the original shareholder.
C) 10% of the nominal value.
D) There is no limit on the discount.
A: B
E: The discount on reissue cannot exceed the credit balance in the Forfeited Shares Account for those particular shares, ensuring the company ultimately receives at least the nominal value over the two transactions.

Q: A "Final Dividend" for a company is usually recommended by the directors and then formally approved by:
A) The company's bankers.
B) The government tax authorities.
C) The shareholders at the Annual General Meeting (AGM).
D) The external auditors.
A: C
E: While proposed by directors, the final dividend must be declared (approved) by the shareholders.

Q: "Redeemable Preference Shares" are shares that:

D) Have voting rights equal to ordinary shares.
A: B
E: These shares have a provision for repayment of capital by the company, subject to legal conditions and terms of issue.

Q: When redeemable preference shares are redeemed "out of profits," the company must typically transfer an amount equal to the nominal value of shares redeemed from distributable profits to a:
A) General Reserve
B) Capital Redemption Reserve (CRR)
C) Share Premium Account
D) Sinking Fund Investment Account
A: B
E: This transfer to CRR maintains the company's capital base and protects creditors.

Chapter 9: Cash Flow Statement and Funds Flow Statement (Continued Set 4)

Q: The "Direct Method" of presenting cash flows from operating activities in a Cash Flow Statement shows:
A) Net profit adjusted for non-cash items.
B) Major classes of gross cash receipts (e.g., from customers) and gross cash payments (e.g., to suppliers, employees).
C) Changes in working capital accounts.
D) Only cash flows from financing activities.
A: B
E: The direct method essentially reconstructs the cash basis of the operating part of the income statement.

Q: Which of these transactions is an example of a cash flow from "Financing Activities"?
A) Purchase of a new machine for cash.
B) Payment of salaries to employees.
C) Issuance of new ordinary shares for cash.
D) Cash sales of inventory.
A: C
E: Issuing shares raises equity capital for the company and is thus a financing activity. A=Investing, B&D=Operating.

Q: When preparing the operating activities section of a Cash Flow Statement using the "Indirect Method," an increase in inventory during the period is:
A) Added to net profit.
B) Deducted from net profit.
C) Ignored as it is an investing activity.
D) Treated as a non-cash gain.
A: B
E: An increase in inventory means cash was used to purchase more inventory than was sold (or purchases were higher), so this use of cash is deducted from net profit.

Q: Payment of cash dividends to shareholders is classified in the Cash Flow Statement as a cash outflow from:
A) Operating Activities
B) Investing Activities
C) Financing Activities
D) Extraordinary Activities
A: C
E: Dividends are returns to equity providers, so their payment is a financing activity.

Q: If a company sells a piece of equipment for cash less than its Net Book Value, the Cash Flow Statement (indirect method, operating section) would show:
A) The cash proceeds added to net profit.
B) The loss on sale added back to net profit.
C) The loss on sale deducted from net profit.
D) The entire cash proceeds deducted from net profit.
A: B
E: The loss on sale reduced the accrual net profit but was a non-cash operating charge related to an investing activity. The actual cash proceeds are shown under investing activities. So, the loss is added back in operating section.

Q: The "Funds Flow Statement" (Statement of Changes in Working Capital) aims to show:
A) The detailed cash inflows and outflows during a period.
B) The sources from which working capital was generated and how it was applied or used during a period.
C) The profitability of the company compared to the previous year.
D) The market value of the company's shares.
A: B
E: It explains the change in net working capital by detailing its sources (e.g., from operations, share issues) and applications (e.g., asset purchases, dividend payments).

Q: Depreciation expense is added back to net profit when calculating cash flow from operations (indirect method) because:
A) It represents a cash inflow.
B) It is a non-cash expense that reduced net profit but did not involve a cash outflow in the current period.
C) It is a financing activity.
D) It increases working capital.
A: B
E: Depreciation is an allocation of past cash outflow (asset purchase) and does not use cash in the period it's expensed. It's added back to reverse its P&L effect for cash flow purposes.

Q: A decrease in Trade Payables (Accounts Payable) during a period, using the indirect method for operating cash flows, is:
A) Added to net profit.
B) Deducted from net profit.
C) An investing cash inflow.
D) A financing cash inflow.
A: B
E: A decrease in payables means the company paid off more of its supplier debts than new credit purchases incurred (or paid for current expenses in cash more rapidly), resulting in a cash outflow relative to expenses/purchases recognized, so it's deducted.

Q: A "Schedule of Changes in Working Capital" is prepared as part of a Funds Flow Statement to:
A) Calculate the net profit for the year.
B) Determine the net increase or decrease in working capital between two balance sheet dates.
C) List all non-current assets and non-current liabilities.
D) Show the company's cash budget for the next period.
A: B
E: This schedule compares current assets and current liabilities at the start and end of the period to find the overall change in net working capital.

Q: Interest received from a bank deposit would usually be classified by a non-financial company in its Cash Flow Statement as a cash inflow from:
A) Operating activities or Investing activities (applied consistently).
B) Only Financing activities.
C) A reduction of interest paid.
D) Cash equivalents.
A: A
E: IAS 7 allows flexibility for interest received to be classified as either operating (if part of main revenue-producing or incidental) or investing (as a return on an investment).

Chapter 10: Ratio Analysis and Financial Performance (Continued Set 4)

Q: Working Capital is defined as:
A) Total Assets minus Total Liabilities.
B) Current Assets minus Current Liabilities.
C) Non-Current Assets minus Non-Current Liabilities.
D) Cash plus Bank Balances.
A: B
E: Working Capital (or Net Current Assets) represents the funds available for short-term operational needs.

Q: If a company has a Gross Profit Margin of 30%, it means that:
A) For every $100 of sales, its net profit is $30.
B) For every $100 of sales, its gross profit is $30 (and cost of goods sold is $70).
C) Its operating expenses are 30% of sales.
D) Its cost of goods sold is 30% of sales.
A: B
E: Gross Profit Margin = (Gross Profit / Net Sales) * 100%. So, 30% implies $30 gross profit for $100 sales.

Q: "Capital Employed" for the purpose of calculating ROCE typically includes:
A) Only Total Assets.
B) Only Shareholders' Equity.
C) Shareholders' Equity plus Non-Current Liabilities (or Total Assets minus Current Liabilities).
D) Only Non-Current Assets.
A: C
E: Capital Employed represents the total long-term funds invested in the business operations.

Q: A company with a high Debtors Collection Period (e.g., 90 days when terms are 30 days) is likely to experience:
A) Increased cash flow and improved liquidity.
B) Reduced risk of bad debts.
C) Potential cash flow problems due to cash being tied up in receivables, and increased risk of bad debts.
D) Higher inventory turnover.
A: C
E: A long collection period means cash is not coming in quickly from credit sales, which can strain liquidity and increases the chance that some debts will become uncollectible.

Q: The ratio that measures how many times a company's operating profit can cover its fixed interest payments is the:
A) Current Ratio
B) Net Profit Margin
C) Interest Coverage Ratio (Times Interest Earned)
D) Debt to Equity Ratio
A: C
E: Interest Coverage Ratio (EBIT / Interest Expense) indicates the margin of safety for meeting interest obligations.

Q: An Inventory Holding Period of 45 days means that, on average:
A) The company takes 45 days to pay its suppliers.
B) The company holds its inventory for 45 days before selling it.
C) The company collects cash from its debtors in 45 days.
D) The company's gross profit margin is 45%.
A: B
E: This efficiency ratio shows the average duration for which inventory is kept in stock.

Q: Comparing a company's financial ratios with industry averages is known as:
A) Trend analysis.
B) Vertical analysis.
C) Benchmarking (or Inter-company comparison).
D) Horizontal analysis.
A: C
E: Benchmarking involves comparing performance against competitors or industry norms to assess relative standing. Trend analysis is comparing over time.

Q: Which ratio provides insight into a company's reliance on external debt versus owner's funding?
A) Quick Ratio
B) Return on Equity (ROE)
C) Debt to Equity Ratio (or Gearing Ratio)
D) Inventory Turnover Ratio
A: C
E: The Debt to Equity Ratio directly measures the proportion of debt to equity in the capital structure, indicating financial leverage.

Q: A declining Net Profit Margin, despite a stable Gross Profit Margin, could suggest:
A) Improving control over cost of goods sold.
B) Increasing efficiency in managing operating expenses.
C) Rising operating expenses relative to sales, or increasing interest/tax burden.
D) Falling sales prices.
A: C
E: If gross profit is stable, a falling net profit margin indicates that costs below the gross profit line (operating expenses, interest, taxes) are consuming a larger portion of the gross profit.

Q: Financial ratios are most useful when:
A) Viewed in isolation without any comparison.
B) Used to predict exact future share prices.
C) Analyzed over time (trends) and compared with industry benchmarks, alongside qualitative factors.
D) They are all showing improvement each year.
A: C
E: Meaningful interpretation requires context through historical trends, industry comparisons, and an understanding of underlying business and economic factors.

Chapter 11: Introduction to Accounting Software Systems (Continued Set 4)

Q: A key security feature in accounting software that restricts users' access to specific functions based on their job is called:
A) Data encryption
B) Audit trail
C) Role-Based Access Control (RBAC)
D) Automatic backup
A: C
E: RBAC helps enforce segregation of duties and protects sensitive data by limiting what each user can see and do.

Q: In computerized accounting, how are subsidiary ledgers (e.g., for individual debtors) typically linked to the general ledger?

Q: "Cloud-based" accounting software means that:

Q: One primary advantage of real-time processing in accounting systems is that:

B) Financial information is always up-to-date, reflecting transactions as they occur.
C) Data entry can be done offline and uploaded later.
D) It simplifies the backup process.
A: B
E: Real-time (or OLTP) systems update records instantaneously, providing management with current information for decision-making.

Q: Which of these is a potential limitation or risk associated with using computerized accounting systems?
A) Reduced accuracy of calculations.
B) Slower generation of financial reports.
C) Vulnerability to data loss from system crashes or cyber-attacks if not properly secured and backed up.
D) Inability to handle large volumes of transactions.

E: While offering many benefits, dependency on technology also brings risks like system failures, viruses, and hacking, making security and backups crucial.

Q: An "audit trail" facility in accounting software is important because it:

C) Guarantees that no fraud will occur.
D) Automatically generates the financial statements.
A: B
E: The audit trail allows for transactions to be traced from their origin through the system to the final reports, assisting in verification and investigation.

Q: Regular testing of data backup and restoration procedures is important to:

E: Backups are useless if they cannot be reliably restored. Regular testing verifies the integrity of the backup process.

Q: Many accounting software packages offer 'modular design'. This means:

E: Electronic import and matching features can significantly speed up the bank reconciliation process and improve its accuracy.

Q: Which is a critical control in a computerized environment to prevent unauthorized changes to financial data once entered?

E: Strong access controls (limiting who can change what) and audit trails (logging all changes) are vital for data integrity.

Miscellaneous and Applied Concept Questions (Drawing from multiple chapters) - Set 4

Q: An increase in accumulated depreciation on an asset results in:
A) An increase in the asset's net book value.
B) A decrease in the asset's net book value.
C) No change in the asset's net book value.
D) An increase in the asset's original cost.
A: B
E: Net Book Value = Cost - Accumulated Depreciation. As accumulated depreciation grows, NBV falls.

Q: The account used to summarize all revenues and expenses to determine net profit or loss is the:
A) Balance Sheet
B) Cash Flow Statement
C) Trial Balance
D) Income Statement (or Profit and Loss Account)
A: D
E: The Income Statement matches revenues against expenses for a period to calculate profit or loss.

Q: If opening inventory is $8,000, purchases are $45,000, and cost of goods sold is $42,000, closing inventory is:
A) $11,000
B) $5,000
C) $1,000
D) $3,000
A: A
E: COGS = Op Inv + Purch - Cl Inv. $42,000 = $8,000 + $45,000 - Cl Inv. $42,000 = $53,000 - Cl Inv. Cl Inv = $53,000 - $42,000 = $11,000.

Q: Which is an example of "Capital Expenditure"?
A) Paying monthly rent for an office.
B) Purchasing a new delivery van for the business.
C) Paying for routine repairs to existing machinery.
D) Salaries paid to administrative staff.
A: B
E: Purchasing a delivery van is acquiring a non-current asset that will provide benefits over multiple accounting periods. Others are revenue expenditures.

E: Funds from operations (Net Profit adjusted for non-fund items like depreciation) is a primary internal source of working capital.

Q: "Net Realizable Value" (NRV) of inventory is defined as:
A) Original cost less expected profit margin.
B) Estimated selling price in the ordinary course of business.
C) Estimated selling price less estimated costs of completion and estimated costs necessary to make the sale.
D) Current replacement cost of the inventory.
A: C
E: This is the net amount expected to be realized from selling the inventory, used for the "lower of cost or NRV" valuation rule.

Q: What is the effect on a company's Gross Profit Margin if the cost of goods sold increases while sales revenue remains constant?
A) Gross Profit Margin will increase.
B) Gross Profit Margin will decrease.
C) Gross Profit Margin will remain unchanged.
D) Net Profit Margin will increase.
A: B
E: If COGS increases with constant sales, Gross Profit (Sales - COGS) decreases, and therefore the Gross Profit Margin (Gross Profit / Sales) will also decrease.

A: C
E: This allows diverse business activities to be quantified and aggregated in financial reports using a single currency.

Q: A trial balance helps to detect which of the following errors?
A) Complete omission of a transaction from the books.
B) An error of principle (e.g., treating an asset as an expense, but amounts are equal).
C) A transaction entered with the correct total debit and credit but in the wrong accounts of the same type (error of commission).
D) Unequal debit and credit entries made for a single transaction (e.g., Dr $100, Cr $10).
A: D
E: The trial balance's primary function is to check if total debits equal total credits. An error like in option D would cause an imbalance. A, B, C are errors it would NOT detect.
