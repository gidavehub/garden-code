Chapter 1: Nature and Scope of Commerce

Q1. Commerce is best defined as
A. the production of goods and services.
B. the act of buying and selling only.
C. all activities involved in the exchange and distribution of goods and services.
D. the management of business organizations.
Answer: C
Explanation: Commerce encompasses not just trade (buying and selling) but also all the aids to trade (like transport, banking, insurance) that facilitate distribution.

Q2. Trade can be specifically described as the
A. storage and transportation of goods.
B. actual buying and selling of goods and services to make a profit.
C. financing and insurance of business activities.
D. communication and advertising of products.
Answer: B
Explanation: Trade is the core component of commerce and refers directly to the exchange of ownership of goods and services for a price.

Q3. The aid to trade that helps to overcome the hindrance of place is
A. Banking.
B. Insurance.
C. Transport.
D. Warehousing.
Answer: C
Explanation: Transport moves goods from where they are produced to where they are needed, bridging the geographical gap or hindrance of place.

Q4. A manufacturer stores finished goods until they are demanded by consumers. This action overcomes the hindrance of
A. Risk.
B. Time.
C. Persons.
D. Finance.
Answer: B
Explanation: Warehousing (storage) allows goods to be held from the time of production until they are needed for consumption, overcoming the time gap.

Q5. An insurance company provides cover against theft and fire, thereby removing the hindrance of
A. Finance.
B. Risk.
C. Place.
D. Information.
Answer: B
Explanation: Insurance is the primary aid to trade that manages the risk of loss or damage to goods.

Q6. A bank providing an overdraft facility to a trader helps to overcome the hindrance of
A. Persons.
B. Risk.
C. Time.
D. Finance.
Answer: D
Explanation: Banking and financial institutions provide the necessary funds (like loans and overdrafts) to finance trade activities.

Q7. Which of the following is an example of an intangible service traded in commerce?
A. A sack of rice
B. A manufactured car
C. A legal consultation
D. A bar of soap
Answer: C
Explanation: A legal consultation is a service; it is an intangible activity offered for sale, unlike the other options which are tangible goods.

Q8. Buying goods in large quantities from producers and selling in smaller lots to retailers is the primary function of a
A. Wholesaler.
B. Consumer.
C. Producer.
D. Marketing Agent.
Answer: A
Explanation: This process, known as "bulk breaking," is a key function of a wholesaler who acts as an intermediary between producers and retailers.

Q9. The final link in the traditional distribution chain is the
A. Manufacturer.
B. Agent.
C. Retailer.
D. Wholesaler.
Answer: C
Explanation: The retailer sells goods in very small quantities directly to the final consumer.

Q10. Trade that takes place within the geographical boundaries of a single country is known as
A. External trade.
B. Entrepot trade.
C. Foreign trade.
D. Internal trade.
Answer: D
Explanation: Internal trade (also known as domestic or home trade) is confined to one country.

Q11. The buying of goods from another country for use within your own country is called
A. Import trade.
B. Export trade.
C. Re-export trade.
D. Retail trade.
Answer: A
Explanation: Importing is the process of bringing goods and services into a country from abroad.

Q12. Singapore buying raw oil from the Middle East and re-exporting refined petroleum to other countries is an example of
A. Wholesale trade.
B. Import trade.
C. Entrepot trade.
D. Bilateral trade.
Answer: C
Explanation: Entrepot trade involves importing goods with the specific intention of re-exporting them, often after some processing or repackaging.

Q13. Which of the following is the broadest term?
A. Trade
B. Business
C. Commerce
D. Industry
Answer: B
Explanation: Business is the most encompassing term, including all economic activities like industry (production) and commerce (distribution).

Q14. In the hierarchy of economic activities, commerce is a branch of
A. Trade.
B. Industry.
C. Business.
D. Production.
Answer: C
Explanation: Business includes both industry (production) and commerce (distribution). Therefore, commerce is a branch of business.

Q15. The core focus of 'Trade' is on the creation of
A. Form utility.
B. Place utility.
C. Time utility.
D. Possession utility.
Answer: D
Explanation: Trade specifically deals with the transfer of ownership of goods, which is the creation of possession utility. Place and time utility are created by transport and warehousing respectively.

Q16. Advertising and communication primarily overcome the hindrance of
A. Risk.
B. Finance.
C. Information.
D. Place.
Answer: C
Explanation: Advertising informs potential customers about the availability, features, and benefits of products, bridging the information gap.

Q17. The main objective of most commercial activities is to
A. provide free services.
B. earn a profit.
C. conduct research only.
D. obey government laws.
Answer: B
Explanation: While other objectives exist, earning profit is the primary motive and reward for undertaking commercial risks.

Q18. The creation of employment opportunities and improvement in the standard of living are examples of which objective of commerce?
A. Profit Maximization
B. Customer Satisfaction
C. Economic Growth and Development
D. Market Creation
Answer: C
Explanation: These are national or macro-level objectives that well-functioning commerce contributes to, leading to overall economic growth.

Chapter 2: Forms of Business Organizations

Q19. A key feature of a sole proprietorship is
A. Limited liability.
B. Separate legal entity.
C. Perpetual succession.
D. Unlimited liability.
Answer: D
Explanation: In a sole proprietorship, the owner is personally responsible for all business debts, a concept known as unlimited liability.

Q20. The formal agreement that outlines the terms of a partnership is called a
A. Partnership Manifesto.
B. Partnership Deed.
C. Memorandum of Association.
D. Certificate of Incorporation.
Answer: B
Explanation: A Partnership Deed is the written agreement detailing the rights, duties, and profit/loss sharing ratios of partners.

Q.21. A business organization that is legally a separate entity from its owners is a
A. Sole proprietorship.
B. General partnership.
C. Limited liability company.
D. Cooperative society.
Answer: C
Explanation: A limited liability company is incorporated as an "artificial legal person," distinct from its shareholders. Cooperatives are also separate entities, but LLC is the primary example here.

Q22. A partner who provides capital but does not take an active part in management is known as a
A. Managing partner.
B. Secret partner.
C. Sleeping partner.
D. Nominal partner.
Answer: C
Explanation: A sleeping or dormant partner contributes capital but is not involved in the day-to-day management of the business.

Q23. Which document defines a limited company's relationship with the outside world and its scope of activities?
A. Articles of Association
B. Prospectus
C. Memorandum of Association
D. Partnership Deed
Answer: C
Explanation: The Memorandum of Association (MOA) is the charter document that outlines the company's name, objects, capital, and liability.

Q24. A business owned and democratically controlled by its members, operating on a "one member, one vote" principle, is a
A. Franchise.
B. Joint Venture.
C. Limited Partnership.
D. Cooperative Society.
Answer: D
Explanation: The principle of democratic control ("one member, one vote") is a defining characteristic of a cooperative.

Q25. McDonald's granting an individual the right to operate a restaurant using its brand and systems is an example of
A. A trust.
B. A joint venture.
C. A franchise.
D. A public company.
Answer: C
Explanation: A franchise is a business arrangement where a franchisor grants a franchisee the right to use its business model and brand.

Q26. Which of the following is a major advantage of a sole proprietorship?
A. Unlimited liability
B. Access to large capital
C. Quick decision-making
D. Perpetual succession
Answer: C
Explanation: Since the owner has full control and does not need to consult anyone, they can make decisions promptly.

Q27. The concept of "mutual agency" is a key feature of a
A. Sole proprietorship.
B. Public limited company.
C. General partnership.
D. Trust.
Answer: C
Explanation: In a general partnership, each partner is an agent of the firm and their actions can bind all other partners.

Q28. The liability of shareholders in a public limited company is
A. Limited to their personal assets.
B. Unlimited.
C. Limited to the amount unpaid on their shares.
D. Joint and several.
Answer: C
Explanation: Limited liability means a shareholder's liability is restricted to the nominal value of the shares they hold.

Q29. A business arrangement where two or more parties pool resources for a specific project, like building a new bridge, is called a
A. Partnership.
B. Cooperative.
C. Franchise.
D. Joint Venture.
Answer: D
Explanation: A joint venture is a collaboration between two or more parties for a specific, often time-limited, project.

Q30. A minor can be admitted into a partnership only for its
A. Management.
B. Losses.
C. Benefits.
D. Liabilities.
Answer: C
Explanation: A minor cannot be a full partner but can be admitted to share in the profits (benefits) of a partnership, with their liability limited to their share in the firm's assets.

Q31. Which of these documents governs the internal management and rules of a limited company?
A. Memorandum of Association
B. Certificate of Trading
C. Partnership Deed
D. Articles of Association
Answer: D
Explanation: The Articles of Association (AOA) serves as the internal rulebook for a company, dealing with matters like director's powers and meetings.

Q32. A "partner by estoppel" or "holding out" is one who
A. Manages the business actively.
B. Is not known to the public.
C. Lends their name and reputation to the firm, becoming liable to third parties.
D. Has limited liability.
Answer: C
Explanation: A nominal partner (by estoppel) represents themselves as a partner, and third parties can hold them liable if they extended credit based on that representation.

Q33. A major disadvantage of a partnership is the
A. Ease of formation.
B. Sharing of risks.
C. Potential for disputes among partners.
D. Access to more skills.
Answer: C
Explanation: Differences in opinion and management styles among partners can easily lead to conflicts, a significant drawback.

Q34. The term "perpetual succession" means that
A. The company must make profits forever.
B. The directors can never be removed.
C. The company's existence is independent of its members' lives.
D. All shares must be passed down to family members.
Answer: C
Explanation: Perpetual succession is a key feature of a company, meaning it continues to exist legally even if its shareholders or directors change.

Q35. A consumer cooperative is formed primarily to
A. Provide credit to members.
B. Provide housing to members.
C. Maximize profit for investors.
D. Provide consumer goods to members at reasonable prices.
Answer: D
Explanation: The primary goal of a consumer cooperative is to buy goods in bulk and sell them to its members, eliminating retailer profit margins.

Chapter 3: Transportation and Warehousing

Q36. Which mode of transport is best described as providing flexible, door-to-door service?
A. Rail transport
B. Road transport
C. Air transport
D. Water transport
Answer: B
Explanation: Road transport is highly flexible and can deliver goods directly from the consignor's premises to the consignee's premises.

Q37. Which of the following is most suitable for transporting very heavy, bulky goods like coal over long terrestrial distances?
A. Aeroplane
B. Van
C. Train
D. Pipeline
Answer: C
Explanation: Rail transport is economical and has a high carrying capacity, making it ideal for bulky goods over long land distances.

Q38. The fastest mode of transport, suitable for high-value and perishable goods, is
A. Road.
B. Rail.
C. Water.
D. Air.
Answer: D
Explanation: Air transport is the quickest mode, making it essential for urgent, perishable, or high-value items despite its high cost.

Q39. A warehouse that is licensed by customs authorities to store imported goods before payment of duty is known as a
A. Public warehouse.
B. Private warehouse.
C. Bonded warehouse.
D. Cold storage warehouse.
Answer: C
Explanation: A bonded warehouse is specifically for storing imported goods on which customs duty has not yet been paid, facilitating deferment of tax or re-export.

Q40. The primary function of warehousing in commerce is to
A. Create demand for goods.
B. Process raw materials into finished goods.
C. Overcome the time gap between production and consumption.
D. Provide short-term loans to traders.
Answer: C
Explanation: Warehousing's main purpose is storage, which bridges the time gap (hindrance of time) between when a product is made and when it's needed.

Q41. A cold storage warehouse is a specialized type of warehouse used for storing
A. Bulky goods.
B. Perishable goods.
C. Industrial chemicals.
D. Imported goods.
Answer: B
Explanation: Cold storage maintains low temperatures to preserve perishable items like fruits, vegetables, and pharmaceuticals.

Q42. Which of the following is a major disadvantage of road transport?
A. High flexibility
B. Door-to-door service
C. Limited carrying capacity per vehicle
D. Feeder to other modes
Answer: C
Explanation: Individual road vehicles like trucks have a lower carrying capacity compared to a train or a ship, making them less efficient for massive bulk transport.

Q43. Unit Load Devices (ULDs) such as pallets and containers shaped to fit an aircraft's hold are primarily associated with
A. Rail cargo.
B. Sea cargo.
C. Air cargo.
D. Road cargo.
Answer: C
Explanation: ULDs are specifically designed for consolidating and efficiently loading cargo onto aircraft.

Q44. Liners and Tramps are two main types of vessels used in
A. Inland water transport.
B. Pipeline transport.
C. Ocean transport.
D. Rail transport.
Answer: C
Explanation: Liners (fixed routes) and Tramps (hired for specific voyages) are classifications of ships used in maritime/ocean transport.

Q.45. Using a warehouse receipt as collateral to obtain a bank loan is an example of which function of warehousing?
A. Storage
B. Price Stabilization
C. Risk Bearing
D. Financing
Answer: D
Explanation: Warehouse receipts serve as proof of goods in storage and can be used as security to secure loans, thus acting as a financing function.

Q46. The division of large quantities of goods into smaller lots suitable for retailers is a warehousing function known as
A. Consolidation.
B. Bulk breaking.
C. Price stabilization.
D. Grading.
Answer: B
Explanation: Bulk breaking is the process of breaking down large incoming shipments into smaller quantities for onward distribution.

Q47. A major disadvantage of water transport is that it is
A. The most expensive mode.
B. The slowest mode.
C. Limited in carrying capacity.
D. Inflexible for bulky goods.
Answer: B
Explanation: While economical for bulk goods, water transport is significantly slower than other modes like road, rail, or air.

Q48. A pipeline is a specialized mode of transport best suited for
A. High-value goods.
B. Live animals.
C. Liquids and gases.
D. Perishable foods.
Answer: C
Explanation: Pipelines are specifically designed for the continuous flow of substances like oil, natural gas, and water.

Q49. A public warehouse is one that is
A. Owned and operated by the government for its own use.
B. Owned by a manufacturer for their own goods.
C. Available for any trader or business to rent storage space.
D. Exclusively for storing goods before customs duty is paid.
Answer: C
Explanation: Public warehouses are commercial businesses that offer storage and related services to the general public for a fee.

Q50. The process of managing the flow of goods into and out of a warehouse efficiently is known as
A. Logistics.
B. Marketing.
C. Inventory Control.
D. Merchandising.
Answer: C
Explanation: Inventory control, or stock control, is the systematic management of goods held in stock within a warehouse or business.

Chapter 4: Insurance and Risk Management

Q51. The fee paid by the insured to the insurer for risk cover is called a
A. Dividend.
B. Claim.
C. Premium.
D. Policy.
Answer: C
Explanation: The premium is the periodic payment made by the policyholder to the insurance company in exchange for insurance coverage.

Q52. The principle of insurance that requires both the insurer and insured to disclose all material facts is
A. Indemnity.
B. Insurable Interest.
C. Utmost Good Faith.
D. Proximate Cause.
Answer: C
Explanation: Utmost Good Faith (Uberrimae Fidei) mandates complete and honest disclosure from both parties in an insurance contract.

Q53. A person can insure their own property because they would suffer a financial loss if it were damaged. This is based on the principle of
A. Contribution.
B. Insurable Interest.
C. Subrogation.
D. Indemnity.
Answer: B
Explanation: Insurable interest means the insured must have a legally recognized financial stake in the subject matter being insured.

Q54. Which principle states that an insurer, after compensating the insured, acquires the right to sue a third party responsible for the loss?
A. Contribution
B. Subrogation
C. Proximate Cause
D. Utmost Good Faith
Answer: B
Explanation: Subrogation allows the insurer to "step into the shoes" of the insured to recover the loss from the party at fault, preventing the insured from being paid twice.

Q55. Which of the following is NOT a contract of indemnity?
A. Fire Insurance
B. Life Insurance
C. Marine Insurance
D. Motor Insurance
Answer: B
Explanation: Life insurance is not a contract of indemnity because the value of a human life cannot be financially quantified; a pre-agreed sum (sum assured) is paid.

Q56. When a property is insured with two different insurers, and a loss occurs, both insurers will share the loss based on the principle of
A. Indemnity.
B. Contribution.
C. Subrogation.
D. Insurable Interest.
Answer: B
Explanation: The principle of contribution ensures that if an asset is insured with multiple insurers, they will each contribute proportionally to any claim.

Q57. A ship's cargo is damaged by seawater entering through a hole caused by a storm. The storm is the __________ cause of the loss.
A. Remote
B. Secondary
C. Insignificant
D. Proximate
Answer: D
Explanation: Proximate Cause (Causa Proxima) refers to the direct, dominant, and effective cause of the loss, which in this case is the storm (a peril of the sea).

Q58. A policy that covers loss or damage to the ship itself is known as
A. Cargo Insurance.
B. Freight Insurance.
C. Hull Insurance.
D. Liability Insurance.
Answer: C
Explanation: Hull insurance specifically covers the vessel (the hull, machinery, and fittings).

Q59. The type of motor insurance that is often compulsory by law is
A. Comprehensive insurance.
B. Third-party liability insurance.
C. Third-party, fire, and theft insurance.
D. All risks insurance.
Answer: B
Explanation: Third-party liability insurance is typically the minimum legal requirement, covering injury or damage caused to others.

Q60. Insurance that covers loss of profits and ongoing expenses after a fire has disrupted business operations is called
A. Fire Insurance.
B. Fidelity Guarantee Insurance.
C. Public Liability Insurance.
D. Business Interruption Insurance.
Answer: D
Explanation: Business Interruption (or Consequential Loss) Insurance specifically covers the financial losses resulting from the disruption of business.

Q61. In the risk management process, taking steps like installing fire sprinklers is an example of
A. Risk Avoidance.
B. Risk Transfer.
C. Risk Reduction.
D. Risk Acceptance.
Answer: C
Explanation: This is a mitigation strategy aimed at reducing the likelihood or severity of a loss, which is risk reduction (or loss prevention).

Q62. An amount the insured must pay out-of-pocket on a claim before the insurer pays is called a
A. Premium.
B. Surcharge.
C. Deductible.
D. Rebate.
Answer: C
Explanation: A deductible (or excess) is a pre-agreed amount that the policyholder is responsible for in the event of a claim.

Q63. Insuring against financial loss due to employee dishonesty is known as
A. Fidelity Guarantee Insurance.
B. Employers' Liability Insurance.
C. Product Liability Insurance.
D. Public Liability Insurance.
Answer: A
Explanation: Fidelity guarantee insurance protects an employer against losses caused by the fraudulent or dishonest acts of its employees.

Q64. If a property worth GHS 100,000 is insured for only GHS 60,000, and a loss of GHS 10,000 occurs, the insurer may only pay a proportionate amount due to the
A. Contribution Clause.
B. Subrogation Clause.
C. Average Clause.
D. Arbitration Clause.
Answer: C
Explanation: The average clause applies in cases of underinsurance, limiting the insurer's liability to the proportion of the value insured.

Q65. In the risk management process, deciding to purchase insurance is an example of
A. Risk Retention.
B. Risk Avoidance.
C. Risk Transfer.
D. Risk Reduction.
Answer: C
Explanation: Insurance is the primary mechanism for transferring the financial consequences of a risk from the insured to the insurer.

Chapter 5: Banking System and Operations

Q66. The type of bank that is responsible for overseeing the entire monetary system of a country is the
A. Commercial Bank.
B. Merchant Bank.
C. Central Bank.
D. Microfinance Bank.
Answer: C
Explanation: The Central Bank is the apex financial institution responsible for monetary policy, regulation, and currency issuance.

Q67. A financial institution that specializes in advising large corporations on mergers, acquisitions, and underwriting is a
A. Central Bank.
B. Commercial Bank.
C. Microfinance Bank.
D. Merchant Bank.
Answer: D
Explanation: Merchant Banks (or Investment Banks) provide specialized wholesale banking and corporate finance advisory services.

Q68. The primary function of commercial banks includes accepting deposits and
A. printing currency.
B. setting interest rates for the economy.
C. granting loans and advances.
D. managing government debt.
Answer: C
Explanation: The two core functions of commercial banks are mobilizing funds by accepting deposits and lending those funds out as loans and advances.

Q69. Which type of bank account is best suited for a business that needs to make frequent payments by cheque?
A. Fixed Deposit Account
B. Savings Account
C. Current Account
D. Recurring Deposit Account
Answer: C
Explanation: A current account (or chequing account) is designed for high-frequency transactions and allows for cheque payments and overdrafts.

Q70. A bank facility that allows a customer to withdraw more money than is in their account up to an agreed limit is an
A. Overdraft.
B. Term loan.
C. Endorsement.
D. Annuity.
Answer: A
Explanation: An overdraft is a flexible, short-term borrowing facility linked to a current account.

Q71. When a bank makes payments like insurance premiums on behalf of a customer, it is performing an
A. Agency Function.
B. Primary Function.
C. Issuing Function.
D. General Utility Function.
Answer: A
Explanation: Acting on behalf of a customer, such as making regular payments, is classified as an agency function for which a fee may be charged.

Q72. The service that allows customers to conduct banking transactions via the internet using a web browser is called
A. Mobile Banking.
B. Internet Banking.
C. Point of Sale (POS).
D. Automated Teller Machine (ATM).
Answer: B
Explanation: Internet Banking (or Online Banking) provides access to banking services through the bank's website.

Q73. SWIFT is a secure network used by banks primarily for
A. processing domestic retail payments.
B. managing internal human resources.
C. communicating for international fund transfers.
D. withdrawing cash from machines.
Answer: C
Explanation: SWIFT (Society for Worldwide Interbank Financial Telecommunication) is the global messaging system used for cross-border payment instructions.

Q74. A device used in a retail shop to process a customer's card payment is known as a/an
A. ATM.
B. EFT.
C. POS terminal.
D. Bank Draft.
Answer: C
Explanation: A Point of Sale (POS) terminal is the hardware used to electronically process debit and credit card transactions at a merchant location.

Q75. The role of the Central Bank as the "lender of last resort" means it provides emergency funds to
A. the government.
B. individuals who are in debt.
C. commercial banks facing temporary liquidity shortages.
D. foreign countries.
Answer: C
Explanation: The Central Bank provides liquidity to commercial banks in times of crisis to prevent financial instability.

Q76. The main tool used by a Central Bank to control the money supply by buying and selling government securities is
A. Moral Suasion.
B. Open Market Operations.
C. Bank Rate adjustments.
D. Reserve Requirements.
Answer: B
Explanation: Open Market Operations (OMO) is the direct buying and selling of securities in the open market to inject or withdraw liquidity from the banking system.

Q77. When a bank provides a safe deposit locker for a customer's valuables, it is performing a
A. Primary Function.
B. General Utility Function.
C. Agency Function.
D. Discounting Function.
Answer: B
Explanation: Providing services for the convenience of customers, such as safe custody, falls under general utility functions.

Q78. The interest rate at which the Central Bank lends money to commercial banks is the
A. Prime lending rate.
B. Mortgage rate.
C. Bank rate.
D. Inflation rate.
Answer: C
Explanation: The Bank Rate (or Discount Rate) is a key monetary policy tool used by the central bank to influence the cost of borrowing for commercial banks.

Q79. Which e-banking service allows for immediate, individual settlement of high-value interbank transactions?
A. NEFT
B. RTGS
C. ATM
D. ACH
Answer: B
Explanation: Real Time Gross Settlement (RTGS) processes high-value transactions in real-time on a one-to-one basis.

Q80. A financial institution that provides small loans, known as microcredit, to low-income individuals is a
A. Merchant Bank.
B. Development Bank.
C. Central Bank.
D. Microfinance Bank.
Answer: D
Explanation: Microfinance institutions specialize in providing small-scale financial services to those typically excluded from formal banking.

Chapter 6: Other Financial Intermediaries and Services

Q81. A non-bank financial institution that pools money from many investors to purchase a diversified portfolio of stocks and bonds is a
A. Pension Fund.
B. Insurance Company.
C. Mutual Fund.
D. Finance Company.
Answer: C
Explanation: A mutual fund (or unit trust) is a professionally managed collective investment scheme that offers diversification to small investors.

Q82. Which of the following is considered a contractual long-term savings institution?
A. Commercial Bank
B. Stock Exchange
C. Pension Fund
D. Credit Union
Answer: C
Explanation: Pension funds collect regular contributions over an employee's working life, representing a form of long-term contractual savings for retirement.

Q83. The market where new securities are issued by companies for the first time to raise capital is the
A. Primary Market.
B. Secondary Market.
C. Money Market.
D. Commodity Market.
Answer: A
Explanation: The primary market (or new issues market) is where companies sell new stocks and bonds directly to investors.

Q84. The first time a private company offers its shares to the general public is called a/an
A. Rights Issue.
B. Initial Public Offering (IPO).
C. Bonus Issue.
D. Private Placement.
Answer: B
Explanation: An IPO is the process by which a private company becomes a public company by selling its shares to the public for the first time.

Q85. Existing securities are bought and sold among investors on the
A. Money Market.
B. Foreign Exchange Market.
C. Primary Market.
D. Secondary Market.
Answer: D
Explanation: The secondary market (of which a stock exchange is a key part) is where investors trade previously issued securities with each other.

Q86. A key function of a stock exchange is to
A. Issue new shares for companies.
B. Print government currency.
C. Provide liquidity for existing securities.
D. Provide short-term loans to businesses.
Answer: C
Explanation: By facilitating the easy buying and selling of shares, a stock exchange provides liquidity, making these investments more attractive.

Q87. A short-term debt instrument issued by the government at a discount to its face value is a
A. Treasury Bill.
B. Commercial Paper.
C. Certificate of Deposit.
D. Corporate Bond.
Answer: A
Explanation: Treasury Bills (T-Bills) are issued by governments to fund short-term needs and are a cornerstone of the money market.

Q88. Unsecured short-term promissory notes issued by large, creditworthy companies are known as
A. Debentures.
B. Shares.
C. Commercial Papers.
D. Treasury Bills.
Answer: C
Explanation: Commercial Paper (CP) is an unsecured form of short-term borrowing used by corporations with high credit ratings.

Q89. In e-commerce, the online equivalent of a physical point-of-sale terminal is a
A. Shopping Cart.
B. Payment Gateway.
C. Digital Wallet.
D. Cryptocurrency.
Answer: B
Explanation: A payment gateway is the service that securely processes online payments, similar to how a POS machine processes in-person payments.

Q90. A digital currency secured by cryptography and based on blockchain technology is known as a
A. Bank Draft.
B. E-Wallet.
C. Certificate of Deposit.
D. Cryptocurrency.
Answer: D
Explanation: This is the definition of a cryptocurrency like Bitcoin or Ethereum.

Q91. When an existing public company offers new shares to its current shareholders, it is called a
A. Bonus Issue.
B. Private Placement.
C. Rights Issue.
D. Initial Public Offering.
Answer: C
Explanation: A rights issue gives existing shareholders the right to buy new shares, usually at a discount.

Q92. The market for short-term funds, with instruments maturing in less than one year, is the
A. Capital Market.
B. Stock Market.
C. Money Market.
D. Bond Market.
Answer: C
Explanation: The money market deals with short-term borrowing and lending.

Q93. A digital wallet like Apple Pay or Google Pay primarily functions to
A. create new money.
B. act as a bank account.
C. securely store payment information for easy use.
D. process interbank settlements.
