Chapter 1: Introduction to Literary Genres

1. Which of the following best exemplifies the concept of "variability" in oral tradition as described in the text?
a) A printed version of Homer's Iliad being translated into multiple languages.
b) A storyteller from one village telling a folktale with a different ending than a storyteller from a neighboring village.
c) A playwright providing detailed stage directions to ensure every performance is identical.
d) An author releasing a "director's cut" of their novel with an extra chapter.

Answer: b)
Explanation: Variability in oral tradition refers to how works change as they are passed down through different performers and generations. A tale told differently in two villages is a perfect example of this. The other options relate to the written tradition, where the text is generally fixed.

2. A postmodern novel that incorporates a traditional West African riddle into its narrative, uses non-linear timelines, and is written in the form of a dramatic script is best described as an example of:
a) A work that rigidly adheres to genre classifications.
b) A failure to understand the principles of the written tradition.
c) A text that demonstrates how genre classifications are often fluid and can blend elements.
d) A work primarily belonging to the oral tradition due to the inclusion of a riddle.

Answer: c)
Explanation: The textbook states, "These genre classifications are not always rigid; works can blend elements of different genres." A work combining elements of Postmodernism, Oral Tradition (riddle), Drama (script form), and Prose (novel) is a prime example of this fluidity.

3. According to the text, the key distinction between Realism and its intensified form, Naturalism, is that Naturalism places a greater emphasis on:
a) The inner psychological state of the individual, free from external influence.
b) Determinism, where characters are shaped by forces like heredity and environment.
c) The accurate depiction of middle-class life and manners.
d) The use of experimental forms like stream of consciousness.

Answer: b)
Explanation: The text explicitly states, "Naturalism was an intensified form of realism, emphasizing determinism (characters shaped by heredity and environment)." This is the key distinguishing factor.

4. An analyst examining a novel asks, "How does the author's choice of long, complex sentences in descriptive passages contrast with the short, clipped dialogue, and what effect does this have on the narrative's pacing?" This analyst is primarily focused on which element of literature?
a) Theme
b) Plot
c) Character
d) Style

Answer: d)
Explanation: The question is about the author's distinctive way of using language, which is the definition of Style. Specifically, it addresses syntax (sentence structure) and diction (word choice) and their effect on pacing, all components of style.

5. The text suggests that the shift from oral to written traditions facilitated the development of more intricate plots and complex linguistic structures. Why is this MOST likely the case?
a) Oral storytellers were less creative than written authors.
b) Written form allows an author to revise and layer complexity without the constraints of real-time performance and audience memory.
c) The invention of the printing press made literature more accessible to the common person.
d) Mnemonic devices used in orature, such as repetition, are inherently simple.

Answer: b)
Explanation: The written word allows for permanence ("fixity of text"). An author can draft, revise, edit, and build layers of complexity over time. An oral storyteller must rely on memory and the flow of performance, which makes sustaining very intricate plots more difficult.

Chapter 2: Literary Analysis Tools

6. A character in a novel says, "This tiny little cabin is a veritable palace!" after being rescued from a shipwreck. The textbook would classify this statement primarily as an example of:
a) Paradox
b) Metaphor
c) Dramatic Irony
d) Verbal Irony

Answer: d)
Explanation: Verbal irony occurs when what is said is the opposite of what is meant. Calling a tiny cabin a "palace" is a clear example of saying the opposite for effect.

7. Which of the following scenarios best illustrates the narrative technique of in medias res?
a) A novel beginning with a detailed family history of the protagonist.
b) A story that opens with the protagonist waking from a dream that foreshadows the story's ending.
c) A play starting with two characters in the middle of a heated argument about a betrayal that the audience does not yet understand.
d) A biography that follows the subject's life chronologically from birth to death.

Answer: c)
Explanation: In medias res means "in the midst of things." Starting a story with a heated argument about a past event plunges the reader directly into the action and creates immediate suspense, with the background to be filled in later.

8. A critic argues that Nora Helmer's decision to leave her home in A Doll's House is not just a personal choice but a rejection of the oppressive economic and social structures that confine women to the domestic sphere, rendering them commodities in a bourgeois marriage. This critic is most clearly applying which critical approach?
a) Formalist Criticism
b) Postcolonial Criticism
c) Marxist Criticism
d) Historical Criticism

Answer: c)
Explanation: Marxist criticism focuses on social class, economic systems, and ideology. The critic's focus on Nora's role within "oppressive economic and social structures" and as a "commodity in a bourgeois marriage" directly applies a Marxist and feminist-Marxist lens.

9. Which of the following is the LEAST likely annotation to be made during a Formalist "close reading" of a poem?
a) "Note the alliteration in line 3; the harsh 'k' sound enhances the theme of conflict."
b) "This metaphor in stanza 2 directly alludes to a similar image in the poet's earlier work, suggesting a continued preoccupation with mortality."
c) "The shift from iambic pentameter to trochaic meter in the final line creates a jarring effect, emphasizing the speaker's shock."
d) "The ambiguity of the word 'fall' in the title could refer to both the season and the concept of sin."

Answer: b)
Explanation: A Formalist reading focuses only on the text itself. Comparing an image to the poet's other works or preoccupations involves bringing in external, biographical information (the author's life/career), which Formalism and New Criticism explicitly reject (this is related to the "Intentional Fallacy"). The other options all focus on internal textual features.

10. What is the key difference between Synecdoche and Metonymy as described in the text?
a) Synecdoche uses a part to represent the whole, while metonymy uses a closely associated concept to represent the thing itself.
b) Metonymy is a form of metaphor, while synecdoche is a form of simile.
c) Synecdoche always refers to people, while metonymy refers to objects or concepts.
d) Metonymy involves a direct comparison, whereas synecdoche is an implied comparison.

Answer: a)
Explanation: This is the core distinction. Synecdoche is a part-for-whole relationship (e.g., "wheels" for a car). Metonymy is substitution by association (e.g., "The Crown" for the monarchy).

Chapter 3: Elements of Drama and Play Analysis

11. A character on stage, believing he is alone, delivers a long speech revealing his secret plan to overthrow the king. Unbeknownst to him, another character is hiding behind a curtain and hears everything. This is a classic example of a soliloquy being used to create:
a) Situational Irony
b) An Aside
c) Dramatic Irony
d) A Prologue

Answer: c)
Explanation: The character is delivering a soliloquy (revealing inner thoughts alone on stage). The audience knows another character is overhearing this secret information, but the speaker does not. This gap in knowledge between the audience and a character is the definition of dramatic irony.

12. The text describes subtext as "the underlying, unspoken meaning." In which of the following lines of dialogue is the subtext most potent?
a) A character shouting, "I am furious with you!"
b) A detective asking a suspect, "Where were you on the night of the crime?"
c) After being passed over for a promotion, a character says to her successful rival with a thin smile, "I'm so very... happy for you."
d) A stage direction reads: (He weeps openly).

Answer: c)
Explanation: The spoken words ("I'm so very... happy for you") directly contradict the unspoken feeling (jealousy, anger, disappointment). This gap between text and unspoken meaning is the essence of potent subtext.

13. According to the text's definitions, a play like Wole Soyinka's The Lion and the Jewel, which uses humor and wit to critique societal attitudes towards tradition and modernity, would best fit into the subgenre of:
a) Farce
b) Tragedy
c) Satirical Comedy

Explanation: The play uses humor and wit not just for laughs (like a farce) but to critique and ridicule societal attitudes. This is the definition of satirical comedy.

14. How does the concept of "catharsis" in classical tragedy differ from the intended effect of Theatre of the Absurd?
a) Catharsis aims to evoke pity and fear leading to emotional release, while Absurdism aims to evoke a sense of meaninglessness and anxiety.
b) Catharsis is the feeling of joy at a happy ending, while Absurdism always ends in the protagonist's death.
c) Catharsis is achieved through logical plot resolution, while Absurdism uses nonsensical dialogue to confuse the audience.
d) Catharsis purges the audience of negative emotions, while Absurdism aims to make the audience feel superior to the foolish characters.

Answer: a)
Explanation: The text defines catharsis as an emotional purging of pity and fear. Absurdist theatre, in contrast, aims to reflect a sense of meaninglessness, futility, and anxiety, deliberately unsettling the audience rather than providing emotional release.

15. When reading a play's script, stage directions are crucial for understanding the playwright's intentions. Which element of a performance is MOST directly shaped by stage directions?

18. The tragic irony in Death and the King's Horseman is most profoundly located in the fact that:

Chapter 5: Understanding Poetic Forms and Techniques

21. A 14-line poem has the rhyme scheme ABBA CDDC EFFE GG. According to the structural definitions in the text, this poem is:
a) A perfect Shakespearean Sonnet.
b) A perfect Petrarchan Sonnet.
c) A hybrid sonnet form that deviates from both major conventions.
d) Not a sonnet, because the octave's rhyme scheme is not ABBAABBA.

Answer: c)
Explanation: A Shakespearean sonnet is ABAB CDCD EFEF GG. A Petrarchan is typically ABBAABBA for the octave. The rhyme scheme ABBA CDDC EFFE GG has the 14 lines and final couplet of a sonnet but uses a different, non-standard rhyme scheme in the quatrains, making it a hybrid or variant form.

22. "The wo<u>r</u>m tu<u>r</u>ns in the da<u>r</u>k, fi<u>r</u>m ea<u>r</u>th." This line demonstrates a strong use of:
a) Assonance
b) Alliteration
c) Cacophony
d) Consonance

Answer: d)
Explanation: The line has a strong repetition of the 'r' consonant sound within and at the end of the words ("wo<u>r</u>m," "tu<u>r</u>ns," "da<u>r</u>k," "fi<u>r</u>m," "ea<u>r</u>th"). This is the definition of consonance.

a) Ode

24. The primary advantage of Free Verse (Vers Libre) over a strict form like a Sonnet is that it allows the poet to:
a) Ignore the use of sound devices like alliteration and assonance.
b) Craft poetic rhythms and line breaks based on natural speech patterns and thematic emphasis rather than a preconceived metrical pattern.
c) Write a poem more quickly and with less skill.
d) Avoid the use of imagery and figurative language.

Answer: b)
Explanation: The core freedom of free verse is the liberation from a fixed meter (like iambic pentameter). This allows the poet to use line length, line breaks, and natural speech rhythms to create meaning and emphasis.

26. In David Diop's "Africa," the progression from "Your beautiful black blood, that irrigates the fields" to the image of the tree growing "patiently, obstinately" with "its fruit gradually acquiring the bitter taste of liberty" primarily symbolizes:

Explanation: The "black blood" represents the suffering and sacrifice of the people under colonialism, which "irrigates" or lays the foundation for a new, independent Africa (the tree). 32. In a short story, the protagonist is a brilliant but arrogant scientist who must overcome her own pride and learn to collaborate with her team to solve a crisis. Her primary conflict is:
a) Character vs. Nature
b) Internal (Character vs. Self)
c) Character vs. Society
d) External (Character vs. Character)

Answer: b)

33. Which of the following best distinguishes a "round" character from a "dynamic" character?
a) A round character is complex, while a dynamic character is simple.
b) A dynamic character changes over the course of the story, whereas a round character is complex but does not necessarily have to change.
c) Round characters are always protagonists, while dynamic characters are always antagonists.
d) Dynamic characters are revealed through direct characterization, while round characters are revealed through indirect characterization.

Answer: b)
Explanation: This is a crucial distinction. "Round" refers to complexity (multi-faceted). "Dynamic" refers to change. A character can be very complex (round) but remain the same throughout the story (static). Conversely, a simple character (flat) could theoretically undergo a major change (dynamic).

34. A short story ends at the exact moment the protagonist makes a life-altering choice, without showing the consequences of that choice. This type of ending would be described as:
a) A dénouement
b) An open ending
c) A falling action
d) An exposition

Answer: b)
Explanation: An open ending deliberately leaves the outcome unresolved, forcing the reader to speculate on the consequences. This is the opposite of a dénouement, which ties up loose ends.

35. The narrative perspective that creates the most objective and camera-like view, without access to any character's inner thoughts, is:
a) First Person
b) Third Person Omniscient
c) Third Person Limited
d) Third Person Objective

Answer: d)
Explanation: The Third Person Objective perspective is defined as reporting events like a neutral camera, with no access to any character's thoughts or feelings, creating the most detached view.

Chapter 8: Selected Short Stories and Critical Approaches

36. In Chinua Achebe's "The Voter," Roof's act of tearing his ballot paper in half is deeply ironic because:
a) It is an illegal act that gets him arrested.
b) He believes he has cleverly satisfied both his bribe-induced oath to the iyi and his loyalty to Marcus Ibe, but in reality, he has invalidated his vote and subverted the entire process.
c) It is the first time he has ever voted in an election.
d) The ballot paper was designed to be tear-proof.

Answer: b)
Explanation: The irony is situational. Roof thinks he has found a clever compromise to appease both sides and fulfill his conflicting obligations. The reality is that his action is meaningless within the democratic system (it's a spoiled ballot) and represents a complete moral failure, not a clever success.

37. The central theme of Richard Wright's "To Hell with Dying" is best articulated as:
a) The finality and terror of death.
b) The power of innocent love and human connection to affirm life and defy despair, even in the face of inevitable mortality.
c) A critique of the inadequate healthcare available to African Americans in the rural South.
d) The superiority of folk remedies over modern medicine.

Answer: b)
Explanation: While the story deals with death and hardship, its core message is about the life-affirming ritual. The children's love, expressed through the tickling, literally brings Mr. Sweet back from his despair, showing that human connection is a powerful force against the desire to give up.

38. The "masterpiece" that Behrman paints in O. Henry's "The Last Leaf" is thematically significant primarily because:
a) It is a hyper-realistic painting that fools everyone.
b) Its true value lies not in its artistic technique but in its act of selfless sacrifice and its life-saving power.
c) It finally earns him the fame and fortune he always desired.
d) It demonstrates the superiority of painting over other art forms.

Answer: b)
Explanation: The artistic merit of the painted leaf is secondary. Its true significance, and what makes it Behrman's "masterpiece," is the selfless love and sacrifice that motivated its creation and the fact that it achieved the ultimate purpose of art: to have a profound impact on a human life.

39. A Marxist analysis of "The Voter" would most likely focus on:
a) The spiritual significance of the iyi.
b) How the political system is a superstructure where economic power (Marcus Ibe's wealth) is used to manipulate the populace and maintain class dominance.
c) Roof's internal psychological conflict.
d) Achebe's use of simple, direct prose.

Answer: b)
Explanation: A Marxist analysis would view the story through the lens of class and economics. Chief Marcus Ibe uses his economic power (bribes) to control the political superstructure (the election) and maintain his position in the ruling class. Roof is an agent within this system of economic manipulation.

40. Comparing Johnsy from "The Last Leaf" and Mr. Sweet from "To Hell with Dying," one could argue that both characters:
a) Are artists who sacrifice their lives for others.
b) Represent the bleakness of poverty in urban environments.
c) Initially succumb to despair and a will to die, but are revived by an external act of love or hope.
d) Are antagonists who cause trouble for the main characters.

Answer: c)
Explanation: Both characters reach a point where they lose the will to live. Johnsy ties her life to the falling leaves and gives in to her illness. Mr. Sweet repeatedly has "dying spells" where he is ready to give up. Both are brought back from this brink by an external act: Johnsy by the hope inspired by Behrman's leaf, and Mr. Sweet by the love expressed in the children's tickling.

Chapter 9: Novel‐Study and In‐Depth Analysis

41. Okonkwo's character in Things Fall Apart embodies a tragic paradox, in that:
a) His greatest strengths—his ambition, work ethic, and adherence to tradition—are the very qualities that, in their extreme form, lead to his inflexibility and downfall.
b) He secretly wishes he had been more like his father, Unoka.
c) He is a great warrior but a poor farmer.
d) He loves his son Nwoye but is unable to show it.

Answer: a)
Explanation: This is the essence of a classic tragic hero. Okonkwo's fear of being weak like his father drives him to be strong, hardworking, and traditional. But these same traits, taken to the extreme, make him violent, intolerant of others (like Nwoye), and unable to adapt to change, which ultimately destroys him.

42. The title of Buchi Emecheta's The Joys of Motherhood is deeply ironic because the novel demonstrates that for Nnu Ego:
a) Motherhood brings her immense wealth and social power.
b) The societal ideal of motherhood is a source of immense sacrifice, hardship, and ultimate disillusionment, rather than simple joy.
c) Her daughters bring her more joy than her sons.
d) She finds joy only after she abandons her children and moves to the city.

Answer: b)
Explanation: The title is ironic because Nnu Ego's life is a catalogue of suffering in the name of motherhood. She sacrifices everything for her children based on the traditional promise that they will be her security, but societal changes leave her isolated and unrewarded, questioning the very "joys" she was taught to value.

43. The central conflict for Obi Okonkwo in No Longer at Ease is best described as the struggle to reconcile:
a) His Christian faith with traditional Igbo religion.
b) The demands of his urban job with his desire to live in his village.
c) His idealistic, Western-influenced moral code with the pervasive corruption and communal financial pressures of pre-independence Nigerian society.
d) His love for Clara with his love for his mother.

Answer: c)
Explanation: This is the novel's central conflict. Obi returns with Western ideals about meritocracy and romance but is immediately confronted with the realities of a system rife with corruption, and the immense financial and social pressures from his community (the UPU) and family (the osu taboo).

44. Comparing Okonkwo (Things Fall Apart) and his grandson Obi (No Longer at Ease), Achebe illustrates a generational shift in the nature of tragedy for the Nigerian man. Okonkwo's tragedy is one of..., while Obi's is one of...
a) violence; peace
b) resisting cultural collision; succumbing to internal moral decay
c) poverty; wealth
d) ignorance; education

Answer: b)
Explanation: Okonkwo's tragedy is epic, stemming from his active, violent resistance to the external force of colonialism. Obi's tragedy is modern and internal; he is a product of both systems and is destroyed not by fighting the system, but by his own internal moral weakness and his inability to withstand its corrupting pressures.

45. A key difference between the missionaries Mr. Brown and Reverend Smith in Things Fall Apart is that:
a) Mr. Brown represents a more tolerant, accommodating approach to conversion, while Reverend Smith represents an uncompromising, confrontational fundamentalism that exacerbates conflict.
b) Mr. Brown is British, while Reverend Smith is American.
c) Mr. Brown builds a church, while Reverend Smith builds only a school.
d) Mr. Brown is successful, while Reverend Smith fails completely.

Answer: a)
Explanation: The text draws a clear contrast between the two missionaries. Mr. Brown engages in dialogue and respects certain aspects of Igbo culture, winning converts through discussion. Reverend Smith is a zealot who sees things in black and white, condemns traditional beliefs, and encourages conflict.

Chapter 10: Selected WAEC/Cambridge Novellas and Essays

46. Hemingway's "Iceberg Theory," as exemplified in The Old Man and the Sea, suggests that:
a) The story's meaning is explicitly stated in the dialogue.
b) The true meaning and emotional power of the story lie in the omitted parts and underlying implications, not just the surface details.
c) The setting of the cold Gulf Stream is the most important symbolic element.
d) The plot should be complex and multi-layered, like the submerged part of an iceberg.

Answer: b)
Explanation: The "Iceberg Theory" posits that the words on the page are merely the "tip of the iceberg." The real story, with its symbolic weight and emotional resonance, lies beneath the surface in what is implied, omitted, and suggested by the sparse, direct prose.

47. In Conrad's Heart of Darkness, Kurtz's final words, "The horror! The horror!", are famously ambiguous. A postcolonial interpretation would LEAST likely argue that they refer to:
a) The horror of the brutal colonial exploitation he witnessed and perpetrated.
b) The horror of the darkness he discovered within his own soul when removed from European societal constraints.
c) The horror of the African jungle and its "savage" inhabitants.
d) The horror of recognizing the hypocrisy of the "civilizing mission."

Answer: c)
Explanation: A modern postcolonial reading of Conrad sees the "darkness" not as an inherent quality of Africa or its people, but as something within the colonizers themselves, or a result of the colonial enterprise.
