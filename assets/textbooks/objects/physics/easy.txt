Of course! Here are 250 WAEC standard Physics multiple-choice questions, complete with answers and explanations. The questions cover the entire WAEC syllabus, including Mechanics, Heat, Waves, Optics, Electricity & Magnetism, and Modern Physics.

Section 1: Mechanics (Questions 1-65)

1. Which of the following is a fundamental quantity?
A. Velocity
B. Acceleration
C. Force
D. Mass
Answer: D
Explanation: Mass is a fundamental S.I. quantity. Velocity, acceleration, and force are derived quantities (derived from mass, length, and time).

2. The dimension of pressure is...
A. ML⁻¹T⁻²
B. MLT⁻²
C. ML²T⁻³
D. ML⁻³
Answer: A
Explanation: Pressure = Force / Area. The dimension of Force is MLT⁻² and Area is L². Therefore, Pressure = MLT⁻² / L² = ML⁻¹T⁻².

3. Which of the following instruments is most suitable for measuring the diameter of a thin wire?
A. Metre rule
B. Vernier calipers
C. Micrometer screw gauge
D. Measuring tape
Answer: C
Explanation: A micrometer screw gauge is designed for high-precision measurements of very small diameters, typically up to 0.01mm or better.

4. A car accelerates uniformly from rest at 3 m/s². What is its velocity after 5 seconds?
A. 8 m/s
B. 15 m/s
C. 1.67 m/s
D. 0.6 m/s
Answer: B
Explanation: Using the first equation of motion, v = u + at. Here, u (initial velocity) = 0, a (acceleration) = 3 m/s², and t (time) = 5 s. So, v = 0 + (3 * 5) = 15 m/s.

5. The slope of a velocity-time graph represents...
A. Distance
B. Displacement
C. Acceleration
D. Speed
Answer: C
Explanation: Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity. On a velocity-time graph, the slope (change in y-axis / change in x-axis) is (change in velocity / change in time), which is the definition of acceleration.

6. A stone is thrown vertically upwards. At its maximum height, its velocity is...
A. Maximum
B. Zero
C. Equal to g
D. Negative
Answer: B
Explanation: At the maximum height of its trajectory, the stone momentarily stops moving upwards before it begins to fall. Therefore, its instantaneous velocity is zero. However, its acceleration is still g (9.8 m/s²) downwards.

7. Which of Newton's laws is also known as the law of inertia?
A. First Law
B. Second Law
C. Third Law
D. Law of Gravitation
Answer: A
Explanation: Newton's First Law of Motion states that an object will remain at rest or in uniform motion in a straight line unless acted upon by an external force. This property of an object to resist changes in its state of motion is called inertia.

8. A force of 20 N acts on a body of mass 5 kg. What is the acceleration produced?
A. 100 m/s²
B. 25 m/s²
C. 4 m/s²
D. 0.25 m/s²
Answer: C
Explanation: According to Newton's Second Law, Force (F) = mass (m) × acceleration (a). Therefore, a = F / m = 20 N / 5 kg = 4 m/s².

9. The product of mass and velocity is known as...
A. Impulse
B. Momentum
C. Force
D. Work
Answer: B
Explanation: Momentum is defined as the product of an object's mass and its velocity (p = mv).

10. A body of mass 2 kg moving at 10 m/s collides with a stationary body of mass 3 kg. If they stick together after the collision, what is their common velocity?
A. 4 m/s
B. 5 m/s
C. 6.67 m/s
D. 2 m/s
Answer: A
Explanation: By the principle of conservation of momentum, total momentum before collision = total momentum after collision.
(m₁u₁ + m₂u₂) = (m₁ + m₂)v
(2 * 10 + 3 * 0) = (2 + 3)v
20 = 5v
v = 20 / 5 = 4 m/s.

11. Which of the following is a scalar quantity?
A. Force
B. Velocity
C. Energy
D. Momentum
Answer: C
Explanation: Energy is a scalar quantity as it has only magnitude and no direction. Force, velocity, and momentum are vector quantities because they have both magnitude and direction.

12. The turning effect of a force about a pivot is called...
A. Power
B. Work
C. Pressure
D. Moment
Answer: D
Explanation: A moment (or torque) is the measure of the tendency of a force to cause a body to rotate about a specific point or axis.

13. For a body to be in equilibrium, which of the following conditions must be met?
I. The resultant force must be zero.
II. The resultant moment about any point must be zero.
III. The body must be at rest.
A. I only
B. I and II only
C. II and III only
D. I, II and III
Answer: B
Explanation: A body is in equilibrium if the vector sum of all forces acting on it is zero (no translational acceleration) and the sum of all moments about any point is zero (no angular acceleration). It can be at rest or moving with a constant velocity.

14. A uniform metre rule is balanced at the 30 cm mark when a load of 80 g is hung at the 0 cm mark. What is the mass of the rule?
A. 100 g
B. 120 g
C. 150 g
D. 80 g
Answer: B
Explanation: The weight of the uniform rule acts at its center, the 50 cm mark. The pivot is at the 30 cm mark.
Anticlockwise moment = Clockwise moment
Load × distance from pivot = Mass of rule × distance from pivot
80 g × (30 - 0) cm = M × (50 - 30) cm
80 × 30 = M × 20
2400 = 20M
M = 2400 / 20 = 120 g.

15. The energy possessed by a body due to its position is known as...
A. Kinetic energy
B. Potential energy
C. Chemical energy
D. Elastic energy
Answer: B
Explanation: Potential energy is the stored energy an object has due to its position or state. For example, gravitational potential energy is due to its height above a reference point.

16. A stone of mass 0.5 kg is dropped from a height of 10 m. Calculate its kinetic energy just before it hits the ground. [g = 10 m/s²]
A. 5 J
B. 25 J
C. 50 J
D. 100 J
Answer: C
Explanation: By the principle of conservation of energy, the potential energy at the top is converted into kinetic energy at the bottom.
P.E. = mgh = 0.5 kg × 10 m/s² × 10 m = 50 J.
Therefore, the K.E. just before impact is 50 J.

17. The rate of doing work is called...
A. Energy
B. Power
C. Force
D. Momentum
Answer: B
Explanation: Power is defined as the work done per unit time (P = W/t).

18. A machine has a velocity ratio of 5 and an efficiency of 80%. What is its mechanical advantage?
A. 4.0
B. 6.25
C. 0.16
D. 5.0
Answer: A
Explanation: Efficiency (η) = (Mechanical Advantage / Velocity Ratio) × 100%.
80% = (MA / 5) × 100%
0.8 = MA / 5
MA = 0.8 × 5 = 4.0.

19. Which of the following is an example of a first-class lever?
A. Wheelbarrow
B. A pair of scissors
C. Human forearm
D. Bottle opener
Answer: B
Explanation: In a first-class lever, the pivot (fulcrum) is between the effort and the load. A pair of scissors has the pivot where the two blades meet, the effort on the handles, and the load on the cutting edges.

20. Hooke's law states that...
A. Stress is directly proportional to strain, within the elastic limit.
B. Stress is inversely proportional to strain.
C. For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction.
D. The extension of a spring is proportional to the radius.
Answer: A
Explanation: Hooke's law (F = -kx) states that the force needed to extend or compress a spring by some distance is directly proportional to that distance, as long as the elastic limit is not exceeded. This is equivalent to saying stress is proportional to strain.

21. The ability of a material to return to its original shape and size after the deforming force has been removed is called...
A. Plasticity
B. Ductility
C. Malleability
D. Elasticity
Answer: D
Explanation: Elasticity is the property that allows a material to regain its original form after being deformed.

22. Young's Modulus is the ratio of...
A. Tensile stress to tensile strain
B. Force to extension
C. Mass to volume
D. Work to time
Answer: A
Explanation: Young's Modulus is a measure of a material's stiffness and is defined as the ratio of tensile stress (force per unit area) to tensile strain (proportional deformation).

23. Pressure in a liquid is independent of the...
A. Depth of the liquid
B. Density of the liquid
C. Shape of the container
D. Acceleration due to gravity
Answer: C
Explanation: The pressure at a point in a liquid is given by P = hρg, where h is depth, ρ is density, and g is acceleration due to gravity. The shape of the container does not affect the pressure at a given depth.

24. The upthrust experienced by a body immersed in a fluid is equal to the...
A. Mass of the fluid displaced
B. Volume of the fluid displaced
C. Weight of the fluid displaced
D. Density of the fluid displaced
Answer: C
Explanation: This is Archimedes' Principle, which states that the buoyant force (upthrust) on a submerged object is equal to the weight of the fluid that the object displaces.

25. A block of wood floats in water with two-thirds of its volume submerged. The density of the wood is... [Density of water = 1000 kg/m ³]
A. 333.3 kg/m ³
B. 666.7 kg/m ³
C. 1000 kg/m ³
D. 1500 kg/m ³
Answer: B
Explanation: For a floating object, Weight of object = Weight of fluid displaced.
(Volume of object × density of object × g) = (Volume of fluid displaced × density of fluid × g)
V_obj × ρ_obj = V_disp × ρ_fluid
Since V_disp = (2/3)V_obj,
V_obj × ρ_obj = (2/3)V_obj × 1000 kg/m ³
ρ_obj = (2/3) × 1000 = 666.7 kg/m ³.

26. The property of a liquid that makes its surface behave like a stretched elastic skin is called...
A. Viscosity
B. Surface tension
C. Capillarity
D. Osmosis
Answer: B
Explanation: Surface tension is the tendency of liquid surfaces to shrink into the minimum surface area possible, caused by cohesive forces between liquid molecules.

27. The meniscus of water in a clean glass tube is...
A. Concave
B. Convex
C. Flat
D. Irregular
Answer: A
Explanation: The adhesive forces between water molecules and glass are stronger than the cohesive forces between water molecules, causing the water to "climb" the glass walls, forming a concave meniscus.

28. The terminal velocity of a falling object is reached when...
A. The upthrust equals the weight of the object
B. The viscous drag equals the weight of the object
C. The upthrust plus the viscous drag equals the weight of the object
D. The weight of the object is zero
Answer: C
Explanation: Terminal velocity is the constant speed that a freely falling object eventually reaches when the resistance of the medium (like air resistance or viscous drag) through which it is falling equals the force of gravity. The total upward force (upthrust + drag) balances the downward force (weight).

29. A satellite in a circular orbit around the Earth is maintained by...
A. Its kinetic energy
B. The Earth's magnetic field
C. Centripetal force provided by gravity
D. The vacuum of space
Answer: C
Explanation: The gravitational force between the Earth and the satellite provides the necessary centripetal force to keep the satellite in its circular path.

30. A car of mass 1200 kg moves around a circular track of radius 50 m with a speed of 10 m/s. The centripetal force on the car is...
A. 240 N
B. 1200 N
C. 2400 N
D. 6000 N
Answer: C
Explanation: Centripetal force F = mv²/r.
F = (1200 kg × (10 m/s)²) / 50 m
F = (1200 × 100) / 50 = 120000 / 50 = 2400 N.

31. The period of a simple pendulum is directly proportional to...
A. Its mass
B. The square root of its length
C. The amplitude of swing
D. The acceleration due to gravity
Answer: B
Explanation: The formula for the period of a simple pendulum is T = 2π√(L/g), where L is the length and g is the acceleration due to gravity. Thus, T is directly proportional to √L.

32. Work done is zero when the force is...
A. In the same direction as displacement
B. In the opposite direction to displacement
C. Perpendicular to the displacement
D. At an angle of 45° to the displacement
Answer: C
Explanation: Work done W = Fd cos(θ). If the force is perpendicular to the displacement, the angle θ is 90°, and cos(90°) = 0. Therefore, the work done is zero.

33. Which of the following is NOT a vector quantity?
A. Weight
B. Electric field intensity
C. Temperature
D. Magnetic flux density
Answer: C
Explanation: Temperature is a scalar quantity; it has magnitude but no direction. The others are vector quantities.

34. The impulse of a force is equal to the change in...
A. Velocity
B. Acceleration
C. Momentum
D. Kinetic energy
Answer: C
Explanation: Impulse is defined as the product of force and the time for which it acts (FΔt). According to the impulse-momentum theorem, impulse is equal to the change in momentum (Δp).

36. The time rate of change of momentum is...
A. Impulse
B. Force
C. Work
D. Power
Answer: B
Explanation: This is the definition of force according to Newton's Second Law (F = Δp / Δt).

37. A projectile is fired at an angle θ to the horizontal. Its horizontal range is maximum when θ is...
A. 30°
B. 45°
C. 60°
D. 90°
Answer: B
Explanation: The formula for the range of a projectile is R = (u² sin(2θ)) / g. The sine function has a maximum value of 1, which occurs when its angle is 90°. Therefore, 2θ = 90°, which means θ = 45°.

38. The unit of work, the Joule, is equivalent to...
A. Kg m s⁻²
B. Kg m² s⁻²
C. Kg m² s⁻¹
D. N m⁻¹
Answer: B
Explanation: Work = Force × Distance. The unit of force is N (or kg m s⁻²) and distance is m. So, Work = (kg m s⁻²) × m = kg m² s⁻².

39. A simple machine with an efficiency of 75% requires an effort of 100 N to lift a load of 300 N. The velocity ratio of the machine is...
A. 3
B. 4
C. 2.25
D. 0.25
Answer: B
Explanation: First, find the Mechanical Advantage (MA) = Load / Effort = 300 N / 100 N = 3.
Efficiency = (MA / VR) × 100%.
75% = (3 / VR) × 100%
0.75 = 3 / VR
VR = 3 / 0.75 = 4.

40. A constant force of 50 N is used to pull a box 5 m along a horizontal floor. The work done is...
A. 10 J
B. 250 J
C. 55 J
D. 0.1 J
Answer: B
Explanation: Work Done = Force × Distance = 50 N × 5 m = 250 J.

Section 2: Thermal Physics (Heat) (Questions 66-100)

66. The temperature at which water boils is known as the...
A. Upper fixed point
B. Lower fixed point
C. Triple point
D. Ice point
Answer: A
Explanation: On the Celsius scale, the upper fixed point is the boiling point of pure water at standard atmospheric pressure (100°C), while the lower fixed point is the melting point of ice (0°C).

67. Convert 25°C to Kelvin.
A. 298 K
B. 248 K
C. -248 K
D. 300 K
Answer: A
Explanation: To convert from Celsius to Kelvin, add 273.15 (or 273 for most WAEC calculations).
K = °C + 273 = 25 + 273 = 298 K.

68. The instrument used to measure temperature is a...
A. Barometer
B. Manometer
C. Thermometer
D. Hydrometer
Answer: C
Explanation: A thermometer is specifically designed to measure temperature. A barometer measures atmospheric pressure.

69. The transfer of heat through a vacuum is primarily by...
A. Conduction
B. Convection
C. Radiation
D. Evaporation
Answer: C
Explanation: Conduction and convection require a medium to transfer heat. Radiation involves the transfer of heat via electromagnetic waves, which can travel through a vacuum, like the heat from the sun reaching Earth.

70. A shiny, silvery surface is a...
A. Poor absorber and poor radiator of heat
B. Good absorber and good radiator of heat
C. Good absorber and poor radiator of heat
D. Poor absorber and good radiator of heat
Answer: A
Explanation: Shiny, polished surfaces are poor absorbers and poor emitters (radiators) of thermal radiation. They are good reflectors of heat. Black, dull surfaces are good absorbers and good radiators.

71. The phenomenon where a liquid turns into gas at a temperature below its boiling point is called...
A. Condensation
B. Sublimation
C. Evaporation
D. Freezing
Answer: C
Explanation: Evaporation is a surface phenomenon where a liquid changes to a gas at any temperature below its boiling point.

72. The specific heat capacity of a substance is the quantity of heat required to...
A. Raise the temperature of the substance by 1 K
B. Raise the temperature of a unit mass of the substance by 1 K
C. Melt a unit mass of the substance at a constant temperature
D. Boil a unit mass of the substance at a constant temperature
Answer: B
Explanation: Specific heat capacity (c) is defined as the heat energy required to raise the temperature of 1 kg of a substance by 1 Kelvin (or 1°C). The unit is J/kg·K.

74. The heat required to change a substance from a solid to a liquid state without a change in temperature is called...
A. Specific heat capacity
B. Latent heat of fusion
C. Latent heat of vaporization
D. Heat of combustion
Answer: B
Explanation: Latent heat of fusion is the "hidden" heat absorbed during melting (solid to liquid) or released during freezing (liquid to solid) at a constant temperature.

75. Which gas law states that the volume of a fixed mass of gas is directly proportional to its absolute temperature, provided the pressure is constant?
A. Boyle's Law
B. Charles's Law
C. Pressure Law (Gay-Lussac's Law)
D. Avogadro's Law
Answer: B
Explanation: Charles's Law (V ∝ T at constant P) describes the relationship between volume and absolute temperature for a gas at constant pressure.

76. A gas at a pressure of 100 kPa has a volume of 2 m³. If the pressure is increased to 200 kPa at a constant temperature, the new volume will be...
A. 0.5 m³
B. 1 m³
C. 2 m³
D. 4 m³
Answer: B
Explanation: According to Boyle's Law (P₁V₁ = P₂V₂), since temperature is constant.
(100 kPa) × (2 m³) = (200 kPa) × V₂
200 = 200 × V₂
V₂ = 200 / 200 = 1 m³.

77. The transfer of heat in fluids by the bulk movement of the fluid itself is called...
A. Conduction
B. Convection
C. Radiation
D. Diffusion
Answer: B
Explanation: Convection involves the movement of hotter, less dense parts of a fluid upwards, and cooler, denser parts downwards, creating convection currents that distribute heat.

78. Land and sea breezes are a result of...
A. Conduction
B. Radiation
C. Convection
D. Refraction
Answer: C
Explanation: They are caused by the different rates at which land and sea heat up and cool down, leading to pressure differences and the formation of convection currents (wind).

79. The ideal gas equation is given by...
A. PV = nT
B. P/V = nRT
C. PV = nRT
D. PT = nRV
Answer: C
Explanation: The ideal gas law combines Boyle's, Charles's, and Avogadro's laws into the equation PV = nRT, where P is pressure, V is volume, n is the number of moles, R is the ideal gas constant, and T is the absolute temperature.

80. What happens to the average kinetic energy of the molecules of a gas when its temperature is increased?
A. It decreases
B. It remains the same
C. It increases
D. It becomes zero
Answer: C
Explanation: The absolute temperature (in Kelvin) of a gas is directly proportional to the average kinetic energy of its molecules. Increasing the temperature increases the molecular motion.

100. A bimetallic strip is used in which of the following devices?
A. A simple pendulum
B. A fire alarm/thermostat

Explanation: A bimetallic strip consists of two different metals bonded together. Since they have different coefficients of thermal expansion, the strip bends when heated or cooled. This bending action can be used to make or break an electrical contact in a thermostat or fire alarm.

Section 3: Waves & Sound (Questions 101-135)

101. Which of the following is a longitudinal wave?
A. Light wave
B. Water wave
C. Sound wave
D. Wave on a rope
Answer: C
Explanation: In a longitudinal wave, the particles of the medium oscillate parallel to the direction of wave propagation. Sound is a classic example. Light is a transverse wave.

102. The distance between two successive crests of a wave is called the...
A. Amplitude
B. Frequency
C. Wavelength
D. Period
Answer: C
Explanation: Wavelength (λ) is the spatial period of a periodic wave – the distance over which the wave's shape repeats. It is the distance between consecutive corresponding points of the same phase, such as two adjacent crests or troughs.

103. The relationship between wave speed (v), frequency (f), and wavelength (λ) is...
A. v = f / λ
B. v = fλ
C. f = vλ
D. λ = vf
Answer: B
Explanation: The wave equation states that the speed of a wave is the product of its frequency and its wavelength.

104. A wave has a frequency of 50 Hz and a wavelength of 2 m. What is its speed?
A. 25 m/s
B. 0.04 m/s
C. 52 m/s
D. 100 m/s
Answer: D
Explanation: Using the wave equation v = fλ.
v = 50 Hz × 2 m = 100 m/s.

105. The phenomenon of a wave bouncing off a surface is known as...
A. Refraction
B. Diffraction
C. Reflection
D. Interference
Answer: C
Explanation: Reflection is the change in direction of a wavefront at an interface between two different media so that the wavefront returns into the medium from which it originated.

106. The bending of waves as they pass around an obstacle or through an opening is called...
A. Refraction
B. Diffraction
C. Reflection
D. Polarization
Answer: B
Explanation: Diffraction is the characteristic bending of waves as they pass by the edge of an object or through a narrow slit.

107. When two waves meet and their crests coincide, the result is...
A. Destructive interference
B. Constructive interference
C. A standing wave
D. Diffraction
Answer: B
Explanation: Constructive interference occurs when the crests of two waves align, resulting in a new wave with a larger amplitude (the sum of the individual amplitudes).

108. The pitch of a sound is primarily determined by its...
A. Amplitude
B. Speed
C. Wavelength
D. Frequency
Answer: D
Explanation: Pitch is the perceptual property of sounds that allows their ordering on a frequency-related scale. A high frequency corresponds to a high pitch, and a low frequency corresponds to a low pitch.

109. The loudness of a sound is primarily determined by its...
A. Amplitude
B. Frequency
C. Speed
D. Phase
Answer: A
Explanation: Loudness is related to the intensity of the sound wave, which in turn is proportional to the square of the amplitude. A larger amplitude means a louder sound.

110. An echo is produced by the...
A. Refraction of sound waves
B. Reflection of sound waves
C. Diffraction of sound waves
D. Interference of sound waves
Answer: B
Explanation: An echo is a reflection of sound that arrives at the listener with a delay after the direct sound.

111. A man stands 170 m from a tall wall and claps his hands. How long does it take for him to hear the echo? [Speed of sound in air = 340 m/s]
A. 0.5 s
B. 1.0 s
C. 2.0 s
D. 0.25 s
Answer: B
Explanation: The sound travels to the wall and back, covering a total distance of 170 m + 170 m = 340 m.
Time = Distance / Speed = 340 m / 340 m/s = 1.0 s.

112. A stationary wave is produced when...
A. Two waves of the same frequency travel in the same direction
B. Two waves of different frequencies travel in opposite directions
C. Two waves of the same frequency and amplitude travel in opposite directions
D. A single wave is reflected multiple times
Answer: C
Explanation: A standing (or stationary) wave is formed by the superposition (interference) of two waves of identical frequency and amplitude travelling in opposite directions.

113. Points of zero displacement on a stationary wave are called...
A. Antinodes
B. Nodes
C. Crests
D. Troughs
Answer: B
Explanation: Nodes are points along a standing wave where the wave has minimum amplitude (zero displacement). Antinodes are points of maximum amplitude.

172. The force between two point charges is described by...
A. Newton's Law
B. Ohm's Law
C. Faraday's Law

B. Volt
C. Coulomb

A. Electric field
B. Magnetic field
C. Gravitational field
D. Electric circuit
Answer: A
Explanation: An electric field is a vector field that surrounds electric charges and exerts a force on other charges that are placed in it.

175. The work done in moving a unit positive charge from one point to another in an electric field is the...
A. Electric current

B. Decreasing the area of the plates

Answer: C
Explanation: The capacitance of a parallel-plate capacitor is given by C = (εA)/d, where ε is the permittivity of the dielectric, A is the area of the plates, and d is the distance between them. Therefore, increasing A increases C.

A. 11 μF
B. 1 μF
C. 0.9 μF
D. 5 μF
Answer: B
Explanation: For capacitors in series, the reciprocal of the equivalent capacitance is the sum of the reciprocals of the individual capacitances.
1/C_eq = 1/C₁ + 1/C₂ + 1/C₃ = 1/2 + 1/3 + 1/6
1/C_eq = (3 + 2 + 1)/6 = 6/6 = 1
C_eq = 1 μF.

A. V = IR, provided temperature and other physical conditions remain constant
B. I = VR
C. R = V/I²
D. V is always proportional to I
Answer: A
Explanation: Ohm's Law states that the current (I) through a conductor between two points is directly proportional to the voltage (V) across the two points, provided the temperature and other physical conditions are unchanged. This relationship is V = IR.

A. 9 Ω
B. 0.92 Ω
C. 1.08 Ω
D. 0.11 Ω
Answer: B
Explanation: For resistors in parallel:
1/R_eq = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + 1/R₃ = 1/2 + 1/3 + 1/4
1/R_eq = (6 + 4 + 3)/12 = 13/12
R_eq = 12/13 ≈ 0.92 Ω.

A. Its length
B. Its cross-sectional area
C. The material of the wire
D. The current flowing through it
Answer: C
Explanation: Resistivity (ρ) is an intrinsic property of a material that quantifies how strongly it resists electric current. Resistance (R) depends on length and area (R = ρL/A), but resistivity (ρ) is a constant for a given material at a specific temperature.

Answer: C
Explanation: E.m.f. is the energy provided by a source (like a battery) to each unit of charge passing through it. It is the potential difference across the terminals of the cell when no current is flowing (open circuit).

B. 0.50 A
C. 3.00 A
D. 0.75 A
Answer: B
Explanation: The total resistance in the circuit is the sum of the external and internal resistances: R_total = R_ext + r = 2.5 Ω + 0.5 Ω = 3.0 Ω.

I = 1.5 V / 3.0 Ω = 0.5 A.

A. P = I²R
B. P = V/R

Explanation: Faraday's Law of Electromagnetic Induction describes how a changing magnetic flux through a circuit induces an electromotive force (e.m.f.). This is the principle behind generators.

190. A transformer is a device used to...
A. Change AC voltage to DC voltage

Explanation: According to Newton's Second Law, F = ma. Rearranging for acceleration, a = F / m = 15 N / 3 kg = 5.0 m/s².

7. A book resting on a table is an example of...
A. Newton's First Law
B. Newton's Second Law
C. Newton's Third Law
D. All of the above

Answer: D
Explanation: It demonstrates the First Law (it's at rest). It demonstrates the Second Law (net force is zero, so acceleration is zero). It demonstrates the Third Law (the book exerts a downward force on the table, and the table exerts an equal and opposite upward force on the book).

8. The product of mass and velocity is known as...
A. Impulse
B. Force
C. Momentum
D. Work

Answer: C
Explanation: Momentum (p) is defined as the product of an object's mass (m) and velocity (v), i.e., p = mv.

A. 10 kg m/s
B. 25 kg m/s
C. 100 kg m/s
D. 25000 kg m/s

Answer: A
Explanation: Momentum p = mv = 0.02 kg × 500 m/s = 10 kg m/s.

10. What is the S.I. unit of impulse?
A. Ns
B. N/s
C. kg/s
D. J/s

Answer: A
Explanation: Impulse is the product of force (N) and the time (s) for which it acts. So, the unit is Newton-second (Ns). It is also equal to the change in momentum (kg m/s).

11. A body is thrown vertically upwards. At its maximum height, its velocity is...
A. Maximum
B. Zero
C. Minimum but not zero
D. Equal to the acceleration due to gravity

Answer: B
Explanation: At the highest point of its trajectory, the body momentarily stops moving upwards before it starts to fall. Hence, its instantaneous velocity is zero.

12. Which of the following is a vector quantity?
A. Mass
B. Time
C. Temperature
D. Weight

Answer: D
Explanation: A vector quantity has both magnitude and direction. Weight is the force of gravity acting on a mass (magnitude) and it always acts downwards (direction). Mass, time, and temperature are scalar quantities.

13. A man pushes a wall with a force of 100 N for 10 s, but the wall does not move. The work done by the man is...
A. 1000 J
B. 100 J
C. 10 J
D. 0 J

Answer: D
Explanation: Work done (W) = Force (F) × distance (d). Since the wall does not move, the distance moved is zero. Therefore, W = 100 N × 0 m = 0 J.

14. The energy possessed by a body due to its motion is called...
A. Potential energy
B. Kinetic energy
C. Chemical energy
D. Elastic energy

Answer: B
Explanation: Kinetic energy is the energy of motion.

15. Calculate the kinetic energy of a 4 kg object moving at 5 m/s.
A. 20 J
B. 40 J
C. 50 J
D. 100 J

Answer: C
Explanation: Kinetic Energy (KE) = ½mv². KE = ½ × 4 kg × (5 m/s)² = 2 × 25 = 50 J.

16. A stone of mass 2 kg is lifted 3 m vertically. The potential energy gained is... (g = 10 m/s²)
A. 6 J
B. 20 J
C. 30 J
D. 60 J

Answer: D
Explanation: Potential Energy (PE) = mgh. PE = 2 kg × 10 m/s² × 3 m = 60 J.

17. Power is defined as the rate of...
A. Doing work
B. Change of velocity
C. Change of momentum
D. Change of position

Answer: A
Explanation: Power is the rate at which work is done or energy is transferred. Power = Work / Time.

18. A crane lifts a load of 2000 N through a vertical height of 5 m in 4 s. What is the power of the crane?
A. 1600 W
B. 2500 W
C. 10000 W
D. 40000 W

Answer: B
Explanation: Work done = Force × distance = 2000 N × 5 m = 10000 J.
Power = Work / Time = 10000 J / 4 s = 2500 W.

19. Which of the following is a non-renewable source of energy?
A. Solar
B. Wind
C. Coal
D. Geothermal

Answer: C
Explanation: Coal is a fossil fuel, which is a non-renewable energy source. The others are considered renewable.

20. The law of conservation of energy states that energy can...
A. Be created but not destroyed

Answer: C
Explanation: The law of conservation of energy states that energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transformed from one form to another.

Answer: A
Explanation: Efficiency = (Mechanical Advantage / Velocity Ratio) × 100%.
80% = (MA / 5) × 100%.
0.8 = MA / 5.

22. The ratio of the distance moved by the effort to the distance moved by the load is the...

Sub-topic: Circular Motion and Gravitation

26. The force that keeps an object moving in a circular path is called...
A. Centrifugal force
B. Centripetal force
C. Gravitational force
D. Frictional force

Answer: B
Explanation: Centripetal force is the net force that is directed towards the center of the circular path, causing the object to continuously change direction and stay in orbit.

A. F = mv/r
B. F = mr²/v
C. F = mv²/r
D. F = m/vr²

Answer: C
Explanation: The magnitude of the centripetal force is given by F = mv²/r, where m is the mass, v is the speed, and r is the radius of the circular path.

28. An object of mass 2 kg moves in a circle of radius 0.5 m with a constant speed of 10 m/s. The centripetal force is...
A. 40 N
B. 100 N
C. 200 N
D. 400 N

Answer: D
Explanation: F = mv²/r = (2 kg × (10 m/s)²) / 0.5 m = (2 × 100) / 0.5 = 200 / 0.5 = 400 N.

29. The time taken to complete one full circle is called the...
A. Frequency
B. Period

Explanation: The period (T) is the time required for one complete cycle or revolution.

30. The value of the acceleration of free fall (g) on Earth...

Answer: B
Explanation: The value of 'g' is slightly greater at the poles than at the equator due to the Earth's rotation and its slightly flattened shape. It also decreases with altitude.

31. A satellite in a stable circular orbit around the Earth has its centripetal force provided by the...
A. Sun's gravity
B. Earth's gravity
C. Rocket thrusters
D. Solar wind

Answer: B
Explanation: The gravitational attraction between the Earth and the satellite provides the necessary centripetal force to keep the satellite in orbit.

Sub-topic: Simple Harmonic Motion (SHM)

32. Which of the following is a condition for Simple Harmonic Motion?

B. The acceleration is directly proportional to the displacement and directed towards the equilibrium position.
C. The velocity is directly proportional to the displacement.
D. The restoring force is proportional to the velocity.

Answer: B
Explanation: The defining characteristic of SHM is that the restoring force (and thus acceleration) is directly proportional to the displacement from the mean position and is always directed towards that position. (F = -kx).

33. The period of a simple pendulum depends on its...
A. Mass
B. Amplitude of swing
C. Length
D. Shape of the bob

Answer: C
Explanation: The period (T) of a simple pendulum is given by the formula T = 2π√(L/g), where L is the length and g is the acceleration due to gravity. It does not depend on the mass or amplitude (for small angles).

34. What happens to the period of a simple pendulum if its length is quadrupled?
A. It is halved
B. It is doubled
C. It is quadrupled
D. It remains the same

Answer: B
Explanation: Since T is proportional to √L, if L becomes 4L, the new period T' will be proportional to √(4L) = 2√L. Therefore, the period is doubled.

35. A simple pendulum makes 20 oscillations in 40 seconds. What is its period?
A. 0.5 s
B. 2.0 s
C. 20 s
D. 800 s

Answer: B
Explanation: Period (T) = Total time / Number of oscillations = 40 s / 20 = 2.0 s.

36. The maximum displacement of an oscillating body from its equilibrium position is its...
A. Period

D. Amplitude

Answer: D
Explanation: Amplitude is the maximum extent of a vibration or oscillation, measured from the position of equilibrium.

Sub-topic: Statics, Equilibrium, and Moments

37. The turning effect of a force about a pivot is called...
A. Power
B. Work
C. Moment
D. Impulse

Answer: C
Explanation: The moment of a force (or torque) is a measure of its tendency to cause a body to rotate about a specific point or axis.

38. For a body to be in rotational equilibrium...
A. The net upward force must equal the net downward force.
B. The sum of clockwise moments must equal the sum of anticlockwise moments.
C. The body must be at rest.
D. The body must be moving with constant velocity.

Answer: B
Explanation: This is the principle of moments. For rotational equilibrium, the net torque (or moment) about any pivot must be zero.

39. A uniform meter rule is balanced at the 30 cm mark when a load of 0.5 N is hung at the 0 cm mark. What is the weight of the rule?
A. 0.50 N
B. 0.75 N
C. 1.00 N
D. 1.50 N

Answer: B
Explanation: The weight of a uniform rule acts at its center (50 cm mark). The pivot is at 30 cm.
Anticlockwise moment = Load × distance from pivot = 0.5 N × (30 - 0) cm = 15 Ncm.
Clockwise moment = Weight (W) × distance from pivot = W × (50 - 30) cm = 20W Ncm.
For balance, Clockwise moment = Anticlockwise moment.
20W = 15 => W = 15 / 20 = 0.75 N.

40. The point through which the entire weight of a body appears to act is called the...
A. Center of mass
B. Center of gravity
C. Fulcrum
D. Pivot

Answer: B
Explanation: The center of gravity is the specific point where the resultant gravitational force on the body acts. For uniform objects in a uniform gravitational field, this coincides with the center of mass.

41. Which of the following objects is in neutral equilibrium?
A. A cone resting on its base
B. A cone balanced on its tip
C. A cone lying on its side
D. A book standing on its edge

Answer: C
Explanation: An object is in neutral equilibrium if, when slightly displaced, it remains in the new position. A cone on its side can be rolled to a new position and will stay there. A cone on its base is stable, and on its tip is unstable.

Sub-topic: Properties of Matter (Elasticity, Pressure, Surface Tension, etc.)

42. Hooke's Law states that the force applied is proportional to the...
A. Stress
B. Strain
C. Extension
D. Young's Modulus

Answer: C
Explanation: Hooke's Law (F = ke) states that, within the elastic limit, the force (F) required to stretch or compress a spring by some distance (e) is directly proportional to that distance.

43. The ratio of tensile stress to tensile strain is known as...
A. Elastic limit
B. Breaking point
C. Young's Modulus
D. Rigidity Modulus

Answer: C
Explanation: Young's Modulus (E) is a measure of the stiffness of an elastic material and is a quantity used to characterize materials. E = Stress / Strain.

44. A wire of length 2 m and cross-sectional area 1 x 10⁻⁶ m² is stretched by 1 mm by a force of 100 N. Calculate the Young's Modulus of the wire.
A. 2 x 10¹¹ N/m²
B. 2 x 10⁹ N/m²
C. 5 x 10¹⁰ N/m²
D. 5 x 10⁸ N/m²

Answer: A
Explanation:
Stress = Force / Area = 100 N / 1 x 10⁻⁶ m² = 1 x 10⁸ N/m².
Strain = Extension / Original Length = (1 x 10⁻³ m) / 2 m = 5 x 10⁻⁴.
Young's Modulus = Stress / Strain = (1 x 10⁸) / (5 x 10⁻⁴) = 0.2 x 10¹² = 2 x 10¹¹ N/m².

45. Pressure in a liquid is independent of the...
A. Depth of the liquid
B. Density of the liquid
C. Shape of the container
D. Acceleration due to gravity

Answer: C
Explanation: The pressure at a certain depth in a liquid is given by P = hρg, where h is depth, ρ is density, and g is acceleration due to gravity. It does not depend on the width or shape of the container.

46. A block of wood floats in water because its density is...
A. Greater than the density of water
B. Equal to the density of water
C. Less than the density of water
D. Zero

Answer: C
Explanation: According to Archimedes' principle, an object will float if its average density is less than the density of the fluid it is placed in.

47. The upward force exerted by a fluid on an object immersed in it is called...
A. Upthrust
B. Surface tension
C. Viscosity
D. Pressure

Answer: A
Explanation: Upthrust, or buoyant force, is the upward force that opposes the weight of an immersed object.

48. The tendency of a liquid surface to shrink into the minimum possible surface area is known as...
A. Viscosity
B. Capillarity
C. Surface tension
D. Adhesion

Answer: C
Explanation: Surface tension is caused by the cohesive forces between liquid molecules, which makes the surface behave like a stretched elastic membrane.

49. The rise or fall of a liquid in a narrow tube is called...
A. Diffusion
B. Osmosis
C. Capillarity
D. Viscosity

Answer: C
Explanation: Capillarity or capillary action is the ability of a liquid to flow in narrow spaces without the assistance of, or even in opposition to, external forces like gravity.

50. The internal friction between adjacent layers of a fluid in motion is called...
A. Surface tension
B. Turbulence
C. Viscosity
D. Adhesion

Answer: C
Explanation: Viscosity is a measure of a fluid's resistance to flow. It's often thought of as the "thickness" of a fluid.

51. Which principle explains the operation of a hydraulic press?
A. Pascal's Principle
B. Archimedes' Principle
C. Bernoulli's Principle
D. Hooke's Law

Answer: A
Explanation: Pascal's Principle states that a pressure change at any point in a confined incompressible fluid is transmitted throughout the fluid such that the same change occurs everywhere. This is the basis for hydraulic systems.

52. The pressure exerted by the atmosphere at sea level is approximately...

Explanation: Standard atmospheric pressure is about 101,325 Pascals (Pa), or 1.013 x 10⁵ Pa.

53. A spring with a constant of 200 N/m is stretched by 10 cm. The energy stored in the spring is...
A. 1 J
B. 2 J
C. 10 J
D. 20 J

Answer: A
Explanation: Energy stored (E) = ½ke². First, convert extension to meters: 10 cm = 0.1 m.
E = ½ × 200 N/m × (0.1 m)² = 100 × 0.01 = 1 J.

54. A stone is dropped from a height of 80 m. How long does it take to reach the ground? (g = 10 m/s²)
A. 2 s
B. 4 s
C. 8 s
D. 16 s

Answer: B
Explanation: Use the equation s = ut + ½at². Here, s=80, u=0, a=g=10.
80 = 0(t) + ½(10)t² => 80 = 5t² => t² = 16 => t = 4 s.

55. A car travels at a constant velocity of 50 km/h for 20 minutes. What is the distance covered?

B. 25.0 km
C. 1000 km
D. 1.5 km

Answer: A
Explanation: First, convert time to hours: 20 minutes = 20/60 = 1/3 hour.
Distance = Velocity × Time = 50 km/h × (1/3) h ≈ 16.7 km.

56. A couple consists of...
A. A single force causing rotation.
B. Two equal and opposite forces acting along the same line.
C. Two equal and opposite forces acting along parallel lines.

Answer: C
Explanation: A couple is a pair of equal, parallel forces that are opposite in direction. It produces a pure turning effect (torque) without any linear acceleration.

57. The efficiency of a machine can never be 100% due to...
A. The load being too heavy
B. The effort being too small
C. Friction
D. Poor design

Answer: C
Explanation: In any real machine, some of the work input is converted into heat due to friction between moving parts. This means the work output is always less than the work input, making the efficiency less than 100%.

58. What is the terminal velocity?

B. The final velocity of an accelerating object.
C. The constant velocity of a falling object when air resistance equals its weight.
D. The minimum velocity required to escape Earth's gravity.

Answer: C
Explanation: As an object falls through a fluid (like air), the drag force increases with speed. Terminal velocity is reached when this drag force becomes equal in magnitude to the gravitational force, resulting in zero net force and thus zero acceleration.

59. If two forces of 3 N and 4 N act at right angles to each other, the magnitude of the resultant force is...
A. 1 N
B. 5 N
C. 7 N
D. 12 N

Answer: B
Explanation: When forces are at right angles, the resultant can be found using the Pythagorean theorem: R² = 3² + 4² = 9 + 16 = 25. Therefore, R = √25 = 5 N.

Explanation: Diffusion is the net movement of particles from a region of higher concentration to a region of lower concentration.

61. A barometer is used to measure...

Explanation: A barometer is a scientific instrument used to measure atmospheric pressure.

62. A body of mass 'm' is moving with velocity 'v'. If its velocity is doubled, its kinetic energy becomes...

Explanation: A wedge is a simple machine that is a modification of an inclined plane. Scissors (Class 1), a wheelbarrow (Class 2), and a fishing rod (Class 3) are all examples of levers.

74. Heat is a form of...

Answer: B

76. A 2 kg block of copper requires 780 J of heat to raise its temperature by 1 K. What is the specific heat capacity of copper?

B. 390 J/kg·K
C. 780 J/kg·K
D. 1560 J/kg·K

Answer: B
Explanation: Using the formula Q = mcΔT, we can rearrange for c: c = Q / (mΔT).

78. A black surface is a good...
A. Reflector of heat
B. Emitter of heat only

Explanation: Good absorbers of radiation are also good emitters of radiation. A dull black surface is the best example of this.

A. Conduction
B. Convection
C. Radiation
D. Transmission

Answer: B
Explanation: Convection involves the movement of the heated fluid itself. Hotter, less dense fluid rises, and cooler, denser fluid sinks, creating a convection current that transfers heat.

80. Which method of heat transfer does not require a medium?
A. Conduction
B. Convection
C. Radiation
D. All require a medium.

Answer: C
Explanation: Radiation is the transfer of heat through electromagnetic waves (like infrared radiation) and can travel through a vacuum, which is how the Earth receives heat from the Sun.

81. Land and sea breezes are a result of...
A. Conduction
B. Convection
C. Radiation
D. Advection

Answer: B
Explanation: They are caused by the differential heating of land and sea. During the day, land heats up faster, air above it rises, and cooler air from the sea moves in (sea breeze). The reverse happens at night (land breeze). This is a convection process.

84. How much heat is required to melt 0.5 kg of ice at 0°C? (Specific latent heat of fusion of ice = 3.34 x 10⁵ J/kg)

Explanation: A liquid boils when its vapor pressure equals the external pressure. If the external pressure is higher, the liquid needs to be heated to a higher temperature to boil. This is why food cooks faster in a pressure cooker.

86. A Thermos flask minimizes heat loss by...

Explanation: The vacuum between the double walls prevents heat transfer by conduction and convection. The silvered surfaces minimize heat transfer by radiation. The stopper prevents heat loss by convection.

Explanation: Charles' Law states that for a fixed mass of gas at constant pressure, the volume is directly proportional to its absolute temperature (V ∝ T).

90. A gas has a volume of 30 cm³ at 27°C. What is its volume at 127°C, if the pressure is constant?
A. 22.5 cm³
B. 40.0 cm³
C. 60.0 cm³
D. 141.1 cm³

Answer: B
Explanation: Use Charles' Law (V₁/T₁ = V₂/T₂). Temperatures must be in Kelvin.
T₁ = 27°C + 273 = 300 K.
T₂ = 127°C + 273 = 400 K.
V₁ = 30 cm³.
V₂ = V₁(T₂/T₁) = 30 × (400 / 300) = 30 × (4/3) = 40.0 cm³.

A. 1.69 x 10⁵ Pa
B. 2.33 x 10⁵ Pa
C. 5.70 x 10⁵ Pa
D. 2.00 x 10⁵ Pa

Answer: B
Explanation: Use the Pressure Law (P₁/T₁ = P₂/T₂). Temperatures must be in Kelvin.
T₁ = 27°C + 273 = 300 K.
T₂ = 77°C + 273 = 350 K.
P₁ = 2.0 x 10⁵ Pa.
P₂ = P₁(T₂/T₁) = 2.0 x 10⁵ × (350 / 300) ≈ 2.33 x 10⁵ Pa.

92. The general gas equation is given by...
A. P₁V₁T₁ = P₂V₂T₂
B. P₁T₁/V₁ = P₂T₂/V₂
C. P₁V₁/T₁ = P₂V₂/T₂
D. PV/T = R

Answer: C
Explanation: The combined gas law, or general gas equation, relates pressure, volume, and absolute temperature for a fixed mass of gas: PV/T = constant.

93. The absolute zero of temperature is...
A. 0°C
B. -273°C
C. 0°F
D. 273 K

Answer: B
Explanation: Absolute zero is 0 Kelvin, which is equivalent to -273.15°C (often approximated as -273°C). It is the theoretical temperature at which all molecular motion ceases.

94. The kinetic theory of gases assumes that the collisions between gas molecules are...
A. Perfectly inelastic
B. Perfectly elastic
C. Sometimes elastic
D. Never occur

Answer: B
Explanation: One of the main assumptions of the ideal gas model is that collisions between molecules (and with the container walls) are perfectly elastic, meaning kinetic energy is conserved during collisions.

95. An increase in the temperature of a gas in a fixed volume container leads to an increase in pressure because the molecules...
A. Collide with the walls more frequently and with greater force.

C. Stick to the walls of the container.

Explanation: Higher temperature means higher average kinetic energy of the molecules. They move faster, so they hit the container walls more often and with greater momentum, resulting in a higher pressure.

97. Thermal expansion is generally greatest in...

Explanation: Gases expand the most for a given change in temperature, followed by liquids, and then solids.

Explanation: A bimetallic strip consists of two different metals bonded together. Since they have different coefficients of thermal expansion, the strip bends when heated or cooled. This bending action can be used to switch an electrical circuit on or off, which is the principle of a thermostat.

A. Real, inverted, and same size
B. Virtual, erect, and magnified

Explanation: A plane mirror produces a virtual image that is upright (erect), laterally inverted, and the same size as the object, located at the same distance behind the mirror as the object is in front.

B. Only virtual images
C. Both real and virtual images
D. No images

Answer: C
Explanation: A concave mirror forms a real, inverted image if the object is placed beyond the focal point. It forms a virtual, erect, and magnified image if the object is placed between the focal point and the mirror.

131. The focal length of a concave mirror is 10 cm. An object is placed at 30 cm from the mirror. The image formed is...
A. Real and at 15 cm
B. Virtual and at 15 cm
C. Real and at 20 cm
D. Virtual and at 20 cm

Answer: A
Explanation: Using the mirror formula: 1/f = 1/u + 1/v.
For a concave mirror, f is positive. 1/10 = 1/30 + 1/v.
1/v = 1/10 - 1/30 = (3 - 1)/30 = 2/30 = 1/15.
So, v = 15 cm. Since v is positive, the image is real.

B. Convex mirror

Explanation: Refractive index (n) = c/v, where c is the speed of light in a vacuum and v is the speed of light in the medium.

145. An optical fibre works on the principle of...

Explanation: Convection is the mode of heat transfer that occurs in fluids (liquids and gases) where heat is carried from one place to another by the actual movement of the fluid itself.

Question 76: Calculate the heat required to melt 2 kg of ice at 0°C. (Specific latent heat of fusion of ice = 3.34 x 10⁵ J/kg)

Answer: C
Explanation: The formula for heat absorbed during a phase change is Q = mL, where m is the mass and L is the specific latent heat.
Q = 2 kg × (3.34 × 10⁵ J/kg) = 6.68 × 10⁵ J.

Question 77: Boyle's law states that the pressure of a fixed mass of gas is...

D. inversely proportional to its absolute temperature, if the volume is constant.

Answer: C
Explanation: Boyle's Law states that for a fixed mass of an ideal gas kept at a constant temperature, the pressure (P) is inversely proportional to the volume (V). Mathematically, P ∝ 1/V or P₁V₁ = P₂V₂.

Question 80: Which of the following instruments is NOT used for measuring temperature?

Question 95: The triple point of water is a unique temperature and pressure at which...

Question 105: In a pressure cooker, the boiling point of water is increased because...

Question 174: Three resistors of 2Ω, 3Ω, and 4Ω are connected in series. The total resistance is...
A. 0.92 Ω
B. 1.08 Ω
C. 9.0 Ω

Answer: C
Explanation: For resistors in series, the total resistance (R_T) is the sum of the individual resistances.
R_T = R₁ + R₂ + R₃ = 2 + 3 + 4 = 9.0 Ω.

Question 175: If the same three resistors (2Ω, 3Ω, 4Ω) are connected in parallel, their total resistance is...
A. 0.92 Ω
B. 1.08 Ω
C. 9.0 Ω
D. 24.0 Ω

Answer: A
Explanation: For resistors in parallel, the reciprocal of the total resistance is the sum of the reciprocals of the individual resistances.
1/R_T = 1/2 + 1/3 + 1/4 = (6 + 4 + 3) / 12 = 13/12.
R_T = 12 / 13 ≈ 0.92 Ω.

Explanation: Ohm's Law specifically states that the current flowing through a conductor is directly proportional to the potential difference across its ends, provided that the temperature and other physical conditions remain unchanged. V=IR is the mathematical representation of this.

Explanation: A transformer is a device that transfers electrical energy from one circuit to another through electromagnetic induction, and is used to increase (step-up) or decrease (step-down) AC voltages.

Question 179: According to Faraday's law of electromagnetic induction, an e.m.f. is induced in a conductor when it...

Explanation: Faraday's Law states that an electromotive force (e.m.f.) is induced in a conductor whenever the magnetic flux linked with the circuit changes. "Cutting magnetic flux" is the practical way this happens. Option D is also correct, but C is the more general and commonly tested answer.

Question 200: What is the cost of running a 1.5 kW electric fire for 6 hours if the electricity costs 10 Naira per kWh?

Question 225: The purpose of a commutator in a D.C. motor is to...

After 1 half-life (10 days), 1/2 remains.
After 2 half-lives (20 days), 1/2 of 1/2 = 1/4 remains.
After 3 half-lives (30 days), 1/2 of 1/4 = 1/8 remains.

Section A: Mechanics (Questions 1 - 70)

1. Which of the following is a fundamental unit?
A. Newton
B. Joule
C. Watt
D. Kilogram

Answer: D. Kilogram
Explanation: The kilogram (mass) is one of the seven SI fundamental units. Newton (force), Joule (energy), and Watt (power) are all derived units.

2. The dimension of pressure is...
A. MLT⁻²
B. ML⁻¹T⁻²
C. ML²T⁻³
D. ML⁻³

Answer: B. ML⁻¹T⁻²
Explanation: Pressure = Force / Area. Force has dimensions [M][L][T]⁻². Area has dimensions [L]². Therefore, Pressure = [M][L][T]⁻² / [L]² = [M][L]⁻¹[T]⁻².

3. A car accelerates uniformly from rest at 3 m/s². What is its velocity after 5 seconds?
A. 5 m/s
B. 10 m/s
C. 15 m/s
D. 25 m/s

Answer: C. 15 m/s
Explanation: Using the equation of motion v = u + at. Here, u (initial velocity) = 0, a (acceleration) = 3 m/s², and t (time) = 5 s. So, v = 0 + (3 * 5) = 15 m/s.

4. The area under a velocity-time graph represents...
A. Acceleration
B. Displacement
C. Speed
D. Time

Answer: B. Displacement
Explanation: The area under a velocity-time graph is the product of velocity and time, which gives displacement. The gradient of the graph represents acceleration.

5. A body of mass 2 kg moving at 5 m/s collides with a stationary body of mass 3 kg. If they stick together after the collision, what is their common velocity?
A. 1 m/s
B. 2 m/s
C. 2.5 m/s
D. 3 m/s

Answer: B. 2 m/s
Explanation: By the principle of conservation of linear momentum, m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = (m₁ + m₂)v.
(2 * 5) + (3 * 0) = (2 + 3)v
10 = 5v
v = 10 / 5 = 2 m/s.

6. Which of Newton's laws is also known as the law of inertia?
A. First Law
B. Second Law
C. Third Law
D. Law of Gravitation

Answer: A. First Law
Explanation: Newton's First Law of Motion states that an object will remain at rest or in uniform motion in a straight line unless acted upon by an external force. This property of resisting a change in the state of motion is called inertia.

7. A force of 20 N acts on a body of mass 4 kg. Calculate the acceleration produced.
A. 0.2 m/s²
B. 5.0 m/s²
C. 16.0 m/s²
D. 80.0 m/s²

Answer: B. 5.0 m/s²
Explanation: From Newton's Second Law, F = ma. Therefore, a = F/m = 20 N / 4 kg = 5 m/s².

8. An object is projected with a velocity of 20 m/s at an angle of 30° to the horizontal. What is the time of flight? (g = 10 m/s²)
A. 1 s
B. 2 s
C. 3 s
D. 4 s

Answer: B. 2 s
Explanation: The time of flight T is given by the formula T = (2u sinθ) / g.
T = (2 * 20 * sin 30°) / 10
T = (40 * 0.5) / 10 = 20 / 10 = 2 s.

9. The point at which the entire weight of a body appears to act is called the...
A. Centre of mass
B. Centre of gravity
C. Pivot
D. Fulcrum

Answer: B. Centre of gravity
Explanation: The centre of gravity is the specific point where the resultant gravitational force (weight) on the body acts.

10. A uniform metre rule is balanced at the 35 cm mark when a mass of 100 g is hung at the 10 cm mark. What is the mass of the rule?
A. 100 g
B. 125 g
C. 150 g
D. 166.7 g

Answer: D. 166.7 g
Explanation: The weight of the uniform metre rule (W) acts at its centre, the 50 cm mark. The pivot is at the 35 cm mark.
By the principle of moments, Clockwise moments = Anticlockwise moments.
Anticlockwise moment = 100 g * (35 - 10) cm = 100 * 25 = 2500 gcm.
Clockwise moment = W * (50 - 35) cm = W * 15.
W * 15 = 2500 => W = 2500 / 15 = 166.7 g.

11. Which of the following is a scalar quantity?
A. Velocity
B. Acceleration
C. Force
D. Work

Answer: D. Work
Explanation: Work is a scalar quantity as it has only magnitude and no direction. Velocity, acceleration, and force are all vector quantities because they have both magnitude and direction.

12. The energy possessed by a body by virtue of its position is called...
A. Kinetic energy
B. Potential energy
C. Chemical energy
D. Electrical energy

Answer: B. Potential energy
Explanation: Potential energy (specifically gravitational potential energy) is the stored energy an object has due to its position in a gravitational field.

A. 4.0
B. 5.0
C. 6.25
D. 8.0

Answer: A. 4.0
Explanation: Efficiency = (Mechanical Advantage / Velocity Ratio) * 100%.
80 = (MA / 5) * 100
MA = (80 * 5) / 100 = 400 / 100 = 4.0.

14. A man exerts a force of 200 N to push a car a distance of 10 m. Calculate the work done.
A. 20 J
B. 200 J
C. 2000 J
D. 20000 J

Answer: C. 2000 J
Explanation: Work Done = Force × Distance.
Work Done = 200 N × 10 m = 2000 J.

15. The tendency of a liquid to rise or fall in a narrow tube is known as...

18. What is the S.I. unit of momentum?

19. An object of mass 5 kg falls from a height of 10 m. What is its kinetic energy just before it hits the ground? (g = 10 m/s²)

B. 500 J

D. 1000 J

Answer: B. 500 J
Explanation: By the principle of conservation of energy, the potential energy at the top is converted to kinetic energy at the bottom.

B. Centripetal force

Explanation: Centripetal force is the net force that acts on an object to keep it moving along a circular path. It is always directed towards the center of the circle.

A. As high as possible
B. As low as possible

Explanation: In any real machine, some of the work input is used to overcome friction and is converted into heat. This lost energy means the useful work output is always less than the work input, making the efficiency less than 100%.

28. Young's modulus is a measure of a material's...

Explanation: Young's modulus (or elastic modulus) is the ratio of stress to strain. A higher value indicates that a material is stiffer and deforms less under a given load.

Explanation: At the highest point of its trajectory, the stone momentarily stops moving upwards before it begins to fall back down. Its instantaneous vertical velocity is zero.

Answer: B. Mass
Explanation: The formula for the period of a simple pendulum is T = 2π√(L/g). The period (T) depends on the length (L) and the acceleration due to gravity (g), but not on the mass of the bob.

31. Power is defined as the rate of...
A. Doing work
B. Change of momentum
C. Change of velocity
D. Change of position

Answer: A. Doing work
Explanation: Power is the rate at which energy is transferred or converted, or the rate at which work is done. Power = Work / Time.

Answer: C. Watt
Explanation: The SI unit of power is the Watt (W), which is equivalent to one joule per second (J/s).

33. Two forces of 6 N and 8 N act on a body at right angles to each other. The magnitude of the resultant force is...

B. 10 N
C. 14 N
D. 48 N

Answer: B. 10 N
Explanation: Since the forces are at right angles, the resultant can be found using the Pythagorean theorem. R² = 6² + 8² = 36 + 64 = 100. So, R = √100 = 10 N.

34. The frictional force between two surfaces in contact depends on...

Explanation: The force of friction is given by F = μR, where μ is the coefficient of friction (which depends on the nature of the surfaces) and R is the normal reaction force.

B. Newton's Second Law
C. Newton's Third Law
D. The Law of Conservation of Energy

Answer: C. Newton's Third Law
Explanation: Newton's Third Law states that for every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction. The rocket exerts a downward force on the gas (action), and the gas exerts an equal upward force on the rocket (reaction), propelling it upwards.

37. Impulse can be measured as...

B. Change in momentum
C. Mass × acceleration
D. Work done / time

Answer: B. Change in momentum
Explanation: The impulse-momentum theorem states that the impulse applied to an object is equal to the change in its momentum. Impulse = FΔt = Δp.

After 1 half-life, 1/2 remains.
After 2 half-lives, (1/2) * (1/2) = 1/4 remains.
After 3 half-lives, (1/4) * (1/2) = 1/8 remains.
