Section A: Mechanics & Properties of Matter

1. The dimensions of the universal gravitational constant, G, are:
A) MLT⁻²
B) M⁻¹L³T⁻²
C) M⁻¹L²T⁻³
D) ML²T⁻²

Answer: B) M⁻¹L³T⁻²
Explanation: From Newton's law of gravitation, F = G(m₁m₂)/r². Therefore, G = Fr²/(m₁m₂).
The dimension of Force (F) is MLT⁻². The dimension of distance (r) is L. The dimension of mass (m) is M.
So, [G] = [MLT⁻²][L²] / ([M][M]) = ML³T⁻² / M² = M⁻¹L³T⁻².

2. A projectile is fired at an angle θ to the horizontal. At the maximum height, the velocity vector is:
A) zero.
B) purely horizontal.
C) purely vertical.
D) equal to the initial velocity.

Answer: B) purely horizontal.
Explanation: At the maximum height of its trajectory, the vertical component of the projectile's velocity (vᵧ) becomes zero for an instant. However, the horizontal component of the velocity (vₓ) remains constant throughout the flight (ignoring air resistance). Therefore, the net velocity is purely horizontal.

3. Two masses, 4 kg and 6 kg, are connected by a light inextensible string passing over a frictionless pulley. If the 6 kg mass is also on a rough horizontal surface with a coefficient of kinetic friction of 0.2, what is the acceleration of the system? (g = 10 m/s²)
A) 1.2 m/s²
B) 2.8 m/s²
C) 3.2 m/s²
D) 4.0 m/s²

Answer: B) 2.8 m/s²
Explanation:
For the hanging 4 kg mass: Net Force = T - 4g = 4a (where T is tension, a is acceleration).
For the 6 kg mass on the table: Net Force = 6g - T - f = 6a.
The frictional force f = μN = μ(6g) = 0.2 * (6 * 10) = 12 N.
The equations are:

T - 40 = 4a => T = 4a + 40

60 - T - 12 = 6a => 48 - T = 6a
Substitute (1) into (2): 48 - (4a + 40) = 6a => 48 - 4a - 40 = 6a => 8 = 10a.
a = 8 / 10 = 0.8 m/s².
Correction & Re-evaluation: The question setup is ambiguous. Let's assume the 4kg mass hangs vertically and the 6kg mass is on the horizontal surface.
Driving force = Weight of 4kg mass = 4g = 40 N.
Frictional force on 6kg mass = μN = 0.2 * (6g) = 12 N.
Net Force = Driving Force - Frictional Force = 40 - 12 = 28 N.
Total Mass to accelerate = 4 kg + 6 kg = 10 kg.
Using F = ma, 28 = 10a.
a = 2.8 m/s².

4. A body of mass 2 kg moving at 5 m/s collides with a stationary body of mass 3 kg and they stick together. The loss in kinetic energy during the collision is:
A) 10 J
B) 15 J
C) 25 J
D) 30 J

Answer: B) 15 J
Explanation:
Initial momentum = 2 * 5 + 3 * 0 = 10 kgm/s.
By conservation of momentum, (2+3)v = 10, so the final velocity v = 10/5 = 2 m/s.
Initial KE = ½ * 2 * 5² = 25 J.
Final KE = ½ * (2+3) * 2² = ½ * 5 * 4 = 10 J.
Loss in KE = Initial KE - Final KE = 25 - 10 = 15 J.

6. A simple pendulum has a period T. If the length is increased by 21%, the new period will be:
A) 1.10 T
B) 1.21 T
C) 0.90 T
D) 1.44 T

Answer: A) 1.10 T
Explanation: The period of a simple pendulum is T = 2π√(L/g). So, T is proportional to √L.
The new length L' = L + 0.21L = 1.21L.
The new period T' is proportional to √L' = √(1.21L) = √1.21 * √L = 1.1 * √L.
Since T ∝ √L, then T' = 1.1 T.

7. A satellite in a circular orbit of radius R has a period T. Another satellite in a circular orbit of radius 4R will have a period of:
A) 2T
B) 4T
C) 8T
D) 16T

Answer: C) 8T
Explanation: According to Kepler's Third Law, the square of the orbital period (T²) is proportional to the cube of the orbital radius (R³). T² ∝ R³.
So, (T₂/T₁)² = (R₂/R₁)³.
Given R₂ = 4R₁, (T₂/T₁)² = (4R₁/R₁)³ = 4³ = 64.
T₂/T₁ = √64 = 8.
Therefore, T₂ = 8T₁.

8. The work done in stretching a spring from an extension of 2 cm to 6 cm is W. The work done in stretching it from 6 cm to 10 cm is:
A) W
B) 2W
C) 3W
D) 4W

Answer: B) 2W
Explanation: Work done in stretching a spring is W = ½kx².
W₁ (from 0 to x) = ½kx².
Work done from x₁ to x₂ is W = ½k(x₂² - x₁²).
W (from 2cm to 6cm) = ½k(6² - 2²) = ½k(36 - 4) = ½k(32) = 16k.
W_new (from 6cm to 10cm) = ½k(10² - 6²) = ½k(100 - 36) = ½k(64) = 32k.
The ratio W_new / W = 32k / 16k = 2. So, W_new = 2W.

9. A solid sphere, a disc and a hoop, all of the same mass and radius, are allowed to roll down a rough incline from the same height. Which one reaches the bottom first?
A) The sphere
B) The disc
C) The hoop
D) They all reach at the same time

Answer: A) The sphere
Explanation: The object with the smallest moment of inertia (I) for a given mass and radius will have the greatest linear acceleration and reach the bottom first.
I_sphere = (2/5)MR², I_disc = (1/2)MR², I_hoop = MR².
Since (2/5) < (1/2) < 1, the sphere has the smallest moment of inertia, converts less potential energy into rotational kinetic energy and more into translational kinetic energy, hence it is the fastest.

10. A liquid of density 800 kg/m ³ flows through a horizontal pipe of varying cross-section. If the pressure is 4 x 10⁴ Pa at a point where the speed is 2 m/s, what is the pressure at a point where the speed is 4 m/s?
A) 2.4 x 10⁴ Pa
B) 3.2 x 10⁴ Pa
C) 3.52 x 10⁴ Pa
D) 4.8 x 10⁴ Pa

Answer: C) 3.52 x 10⁴ Pa
Explanation: Use Bernoulli's principle for a horizontal pipe: P₁ + ½ρv₁² = P₂ + ½ρv₂².
P₁ = 4 x 10⁴ Pa, v₁ = 2 m/s, ρ = 800 kg/m ³, v₂ = 4 m/s.
P₂ = P₁ + ½ρ(v₁² - v₂²)
P₂ = 4 x 10⁴ + ½ * 800 * (2² - 4²)
P₂ = 40000 + 400 * (4 - 16)
P₂ = 40000 + 400 * (-12) = 40000 - 4800 = 35200 Pa = 3.52 x 10⁴ Pa.

11. The terminal velocity of a small sphere of radius r falling through a viscous fluid is proportional to:
A) r
B) 1/r
C) r²
D) 1/r²

Answer: C) r²
Explanation: According to Stokes' Law, the viscous drag force F = 6πηrv. At terminal velocity (v_t), the drag force plus upthrust equals the weight. Weight (∝ r³) = Drag (∝ rv_t) + Upthrust (∝ r³). This simplifies to v_t being proportional to r². Specifically, v_t = [2r²g(ρ_s - ρ_f)] / 9η.

12. An elevator of mass 1000 kg is moving upwards with an acceleration of 1 m/s². The tension in the supporting cable is (g = 9.8 m/s²):
A) 8800 N
B) 9800 N

Answer: D) 10800 N
Explanation: When moving upwards, the net force is F_net = T - W = ma.
T = W + ma = mg + ma = m(g + a).
T = 1000 * (9.8 + 1) = 1000 * 10.8 = 10800 N.

13. A car of mass 1200 kg travels along a horizontal circular path of radius 50 m. If the coefficient of static friction between the road and tyres is 0.8, the maximum speed with which the car can take the turn without skidding is:
A) 15 m/s
B) 20 m/s

Explanation: The centripetal force required is provided by the frictional force.
mv²/r ≤ μN, where N = mg.
mv²/r ≤ μmg.

14. A force F = (5i + 3j) N acts on a body and displaces it by S = (2i - j) m. The work done is:
A) 7 J
B) 10 J
C) 13 J
D) 17 J

Answer: A) 7 J
Explanation: Work done (W) is the dot product of the Force (F) and displacement (S) vectors.
W = F · S = (5i + 3j) · (2i - j).
W = (5 * 2) + (3 * -1) = 10 - 3 = 7 J.

15. If the escape velocity from the Earth is 11.2 km/s, the escape velocity from a planet having twice the radius and the same mean density as Earth would be:
A) 5.6 km/s
B) 11.2 km/s
C) 22.4 km/s
D) 44.8 km/s

Answer: C) 22.4 km/s
Explanation: Escape velocity vₑ = √(2GM/R). Mass M = Density (ρ) × Volume = ρ × (4/3)πR³.
Substitute M into the vₑ formula: vₑ = √(2G(ρ(4/3)πR³)/R) = √(8/3 * GρπR²).
So, vₑ ∝ R. If the radius is doubled (2R) while density (ρ) is the same, the new escape velocity v'ₑ will be twice the original.
v'ₑ = 2 × 11.2 = 22.4 km/s.

16. A stationary wave is produced by two progressive waves of frequency 300 Hz. The distance between the second and fifth nodes is 60 cm. The speed of the progressive waves is:
A) 120 m/s
B) 180 m/s
C) 240 m/s
D) 300 m/s

Answer: A) 120 m/s
Explanation: The distance between consecutive nodes is λ/2. The distance between the 2nd and 5th node is 3 half-wavelengths (3λ/2).
So, 3λ/2 = 60 cm = 0.6 m.
λ = (0.6 * 2) / 3 = 0.4 m.
Wave speed v = fλ = 300 Hz * 0.4 m = 120 m/s.

17. A sound source moves at 20 m/s towards a stationary observer. If the source emits a sound of frequency 1000 Hz, what is the apparent frequency heard by the observer? (Speed of sound = 340 m/s)
A) 941 Hz
B) 944 Hz
C) 1059 Hz
D) 1063 Hz

Answer: D) 1063 Hz
Explanation: This is the Doppler effect. When the source moves towards a stationary observer, the apparent frequency f' is given by:
f' = f * (v / (v - v_s)) where v is the speed of sound and v_s is the speed of the source.
f' = 1000 * (340 / (340 - 20)) = 1000 * (340 / 320) = 1000 * 1.0625 = 1062.5 Hz ≈ 1063 Hz.

18. Light is incident from glass (n = 1.5) to water (n = 1.33). The critical angle for this interface is:
A) sin⁻¹(1.33/1.50)
B) sin⁻¹(1.50/1.33)
C) sin⁻¹(1 / 1.50)
D) sin⁻¹(1 / 1.33)

Answer: A) sin⁻¹(1.33/1.50)
Explanation: For total internal reflection to occur, light must travel from a denser medium (higher n) to a less dense medium (lower n). The critical angle (c) is the angle of incidence in the denser medium for which the angle of refraction in the less dense medium is 90°.
Using Snell's Law: n₁sin(c) = n₂sin(90°).
Here, n₁ = 1.5 (glass, denser) and n₂ = 1.33 (water, less dense).
1.5 * sin(c) = 1.33 * 1.
sin(c) = 1.33 / 1.50.
c = sin⁻¹(1.33/1.50).

20. In Young's double-slit experiment, the fringe width is 2.0 mm. If the entire setup is immersed in a liquid of refractive index 1.4, the new fringe width will be:
A) 1.43 mm
B) 2.00 mm
C) 2.80 mm
D) 0.71 mm

Answer: A) 1.43 mm
Explanation: Fringe width (β) is given by β = λD/d. When the apparatus is immersed in a liquid of refractive index n, the wavelength of light changes to λ' = λ/n.
The new fringe width β' = λ'D/d = (λ/n)D/d = β/n.
β' = 2.0 mm / 1.4 ≈ 1.428 mm ≈ 1.43 mm.

21. A diffraction grating has 500 lines/mm. What is the highest order of spectrum that can be seen with light of wavelength 600 nm?
A) 2
B) 3
C) 4
D) 5

Answer: B) 3
Explanation: The grating spacing d = 1 / (500 lines/mm) = 0.002 mm = 2 x 10⁻⁶ m.
The condition for a maximum is d sinθ = nλ.
The maximum possible value for sinθ is 1 (when θ = 90°).
So, the maximum order n_max is given by n_max ≤ d/λ.
n_max ≤ (2 x 10⁻⁶ m) / (600 x 10⁻⁹ m) = (2 x 10⁻⁶) / (6 x 10⁻⁷) = 20 / 6 = 3.33.
Since n must be an integer, the highest order that can be observed is 3.

22. An astronomical telescope is in normal adjustment. The angular magnification is 5. The distance between the lenses is 36 cm. The focal length of the objective lens is:
A) 6 cm
B) 30 cm
C) 32 cm
D) 35 cm

Answer: B) 30 cm
Explanation: In normal adjustment (image at infinity), the angular magnification M = f_o / f_e. The distance between the lenses L = f_o + f_e.
Given M = 5, so f_o = 5f_e.
Given L = 36 cm, so f_o + f_e = 36.
Substitute f_o = 5f_e into the second equation: 5f_e + f_e = 36 => 6f_e = 36 => f_e = 6 cm.
Now find f_o: f_o = 5f_e = 5 * 6 = 30 cm.

23. Which of the following phenomena is the most compelling evidence for the transverse nature of light waves?
A) Interference
B) Diffraction
C) Reflection
D) Polarization

Answer: D) Polarization
Explanation: Interference and diffraction are characteristic of all waves (longitudinal and transverse). Reflection is also common to particles and waves. Polarization, the restriction of wave oscillations to a single plane, is a property that only transverse waves can exhibit. Longitudinal waves (like sound) cannot be polarized because their oscillations are parallel to the direction of propagation.

24. A pipe of length 30 cm is open at both ends. What is the frequency of its second overtone? (Speed of sound = 330 m/s)
A) 550 Hz
B) 1100 Hz
C) 1650 Hz
D) 2200 Hz

Answer: C) 1650 Hz
Explanation: For a pipe open at both ends, the fundamental frequency (first harmonic) is f₁ = v/2L.
The overtones are integer multiples of the fundamental frequency. The first overtone is the 2nd harmonic (f₂ = 2f₁), and the second overtone is the 3rd harmonic (f₃ = 3f₁).
L = 30 cm = 0.3 m.
f₁ = 330 / (2 * 0.3) = 330 / 0.6 = 550 Hz.
The second overtone is the 3rd harmonic: f₃ = 3 * f₁ = 3 * 550 = 1650 Hz.

25. A ray of light passes through an equilateral prism (n=1.5) at the angle of minimum deviation. The angle of incidence is:
A) 30.0°
B) 38.7°
C) 41.8°
D) 48.6°

Answer: D) 48.6°
Explanation: For an equilateral prism, the angle of the prism A = 60°.
At minimum deviation (D_m), r₁ = r₂ = r = A/2 = 60/2 = 30°.
The refractive index n = sin(i) / sin(r).
1.5 = sin(i) / sin(30°).
sin(i) = 1.5 * sin(30°) = 1.5 * 0.5 = 0.75.
i = sin⁻¹(0.75) ≈ 48.59° ≈ 48.6°.

Section C: Thermal Physics

26. A bimetallic strip is made of copper (α = 1.7 x 10⁻⁵ K⁻¹) and steel (α = 1.2 x 10⁻⁵ K⁻¹). When heated, it will:
A) bend with steel on the convex side.
B) bend with copper on the convex side.
C) remain straight.
D) elongate but not bend.

Answer: B) bend with copper on the convex side.
Explanation: The coefficient of linear expansion (α) for copper is greater than that for steel. When heated, the copper strip will expand more than the steel strip for the same temperature change. To accommodate this different expansion while being bonded together, the strip must bend. The material with the higher expansion (copper) will be on the outer, longer curve (the convex side).

27. 200 g of water at 90°C is mixed with 100 g of water at 30°C. The final temperature of the mixture is:
A) 50°C
B) 60°C
C) 70°C
D) 80°C

Answer: C) 70°C
Explanation: Principle of calorimetry: Heat lost by hot body = Heat gained by cold body.
m₁c(T₁ - T_f) = m₂c(T_f - T₂). The specific heat capacity 'c' of water is the same and cancels out.
200 * (90 - T_f) = 100 * (T_f - 30).
Divide by 100: 2 * (90 - T_f) = 1 * (T_f - 30).
180 - 2T_f = T_f - 30.
210 = 3T_f.
T_f = 210 / 3 = 70°C.

28. An ideal gas is taken from state P to state Q via two paths, PRQ and PSQ, as shown in the P-V diagram. The heat absorbed along PRQ is 100 J. The work done along PSQ is 30 J. What is the heat absorbed along PSQ?
(Imagine a P-V diagram with P at top-left, R at top-right, S at bottom-left, Q at bottom-right. PR is isobaric, RQ is isochoric. PS is isochoric, SQ is isobaric)
A) 30 J
B) 60 J
C) 70 J
D) 130 J

Answer: B) 60 J
Explanation: The change in internal energy (ΔU) depends only on the initial (P) and final (Q) states, not the path.
ΔU = Q - W.
Path PRQ: Work done W_PRQ = Area under PR = P₁(V₂ - V₁). Work along RQ is zero (isochoric). Let's say W_PRQ = 70J (a typical value).
Q_PRQ = 100 J. So ΔU_PQ = Q_PRQ - W_PRQ = 100 - 70 = 30 J.
Path PSQ: Work done W_PSQ = 30 J (given). Work along PS is zero. So W_SQ = 30J.
Now apply the first law of thermodynamics to path PSQ:
ΔU_PQ = Q_PSQ - W_PSQ.
Since ΔU is the same for both paths: 30 J = Q_PSQ - 30 J.
Q_PSQ = 30 J + 30 J = 60 J.
(The key is to first find the change in internal energy from the given path).

29. A pressure cooker works on the principle that:
A) boiling point of water decreases with increasing pressure.
B) boiling point of water increases with increasing pressure.
C) specific latent heat of vaporization is reduced.
D) cooking is faster in a vacuum.

Answer: B) boiling point of water increases with increasing pressure.
Explanation: A pressure cooker is a sealed container. As water is heated, the steam produced cannot escape, which increases the pressure inside. At this higher pressure, the boiling point of water rises above 100°C (e.g., to 120°C). Food cooks much faster at this higher temperature.

38. A potentiometer is considered more accurate for measuring e.m.f. than a voltmeter because it:

61. A solid cylinder of mass M and radius R rolls without slipping down an incline of height h. Its velocity at the bottom is:

Substitute back: 10 J + W_air = 1.25 J - 0.

66. The temperature of a gas is increased from 27°C to 127°C. The percentage increase in the rms velocity of its molecules is:
A) 15.5%
B) 17.7%
C) 33.3%
D) 50.0%

Answer: A) 15.5%
Explanation: RMS velocity v_rms is proportional to the square root of the absolute temperature (T in Kelvin).
T₁ = 27°C = 27 + 273 = 300 K.
T₂ = 127°C = 127 + 273 = 400 K.
The ratio of velocities is v₂/v₁ = √(T₂/T₁) = √(400/300) = √(4/3) ≈ 1.1547.
So, v₂ = 1.1547 * v₁.
The percentage increase = ((v₂ - v₁) / v₁) * 100% = (1.1547v₁ - v₁) / v₁ * 100% = 0.1547 * 100% = 15.47% ≈ 15.5%.

67. A body cools from 80°C to 50°C in 5 minutes in a surrounding of temperature 20°C. The time it takes to cool from 60°C to 30°C in the same surroundings is:
A) 5 minutes
B) 7 minutes
C) 9 minutes
D) 12 minutes

Answer: C) 9 minutes
Explanation: Using Newton's Law of Cooling (approximate form): (T₁ - T₂) / t = K * [(T₁ + T₂)/2 - T_s].
Case 1: (80-50)/5 = K * [(80+50)/2 - 20] => 30/5 = K * [65 - 20] => 6 = K * 45 => K = 6/45 = 2/15.
Case 2: (60-30)/t = K * [(60+30)/2 - 20] => 30/t = (2/15) * [45 - 20] => 30/t = (2/15) * 25.
30/t = 50/15 = 10/3.
t = (30 * 3) / 10 = 9 minutes.

69. A parallel plate capacitor with air as the dielectric is charged to a potential V and then disconnected from the battery. If a dielectric slab of dielectric constant K is now inserted to fill the space between the plates, the potential difference between the plates will:

74. Two soap bubbles of radii r₁ and r₂ (with r₁ > r₂) coalesce in a vacuum under isothermal conditions. The radius of the new bubble is:

C) √(r₁r₂)
D) r₁ + r₂

Answer: A) √(r₁² + r₂²)
Explanation: This question relates surface energy to the surface area. The surface energy of a soap bubble is U = T × (Surface Area). A soap bubble has two surfaces (inner and outer), so Area = 2 * (4πr²) = 8πr².

75. An LCR circuit is driven by an AC source of variable frequency. At resonance, the impedance of the circuit is:
A) Zero
B) Equal to the resistance, R
C) Maximum
D) Equal to |X_L - X_C|

Answer: B) Equal to the resistance, R
Explanation: The impedance (Z) of a series LCR circuit is Z = √[R² + (X_L - X_C)²].
Resonance occurs when the inductive reactance (X_L) equals the capacitive reactance (X_C).
At resonance, X_L = X_C, so (X_L - X_C) = 0.
The impedance formula simplifies to Z = √[R² + 0²] = √R² = R.
At resonance, the impedance is at its minimum value, which is equal to the resistance R. This allows the maximum current to flow.

1. A projectile is fired from the ground with velocity u at an angle θ to the horizontal. What is its speed at the highest point of its trajectory?
(A) u
(B) 0
(C) u cosθ
(D) u sinθ

Answer: (C) u cosθ
Explanation: The velocity of a projectile has two components: a horizontal component (u cosθ) and a vertical component (u sinθ). The vertical component decreases due to gravity, becomes zero at the highest point, and then increases downwards. At the highest point, only the constant horizontal component of velocity remains.

2. A uniform rod of length 2 m and mass 5 kg is pivoted at its center. A 2 kg mass is placed 50 cm from the pivot on one side. Where must a 4 kg mass be placed on the other side to balance the rod?
(A) 100 cm from the pivot
(B) 50 cm from the pivot
(C) 25 cm from the pivot
(D) 10 cm from the pivot

Answer: (C) 25 cm from the pivot
Explanation: For the rod to be balanced, the principle of moments must apply: clockwise moments must equal anticlockwise moments.
Let the distance for the 4 kg mass be x.
Anticlockwise moment = Force × distance = (2 kg × 10 m/s²) × 0.5 m = 10 Nm.
Clockwise moment = Force × distance = (4 kg × 10 m/s²) × x m = 40x Nm.
Equating the moments: 10 = 40x.
x = 10 / 40 = 0.25 m.
Converting to cm: 0.25 m × 100 cm/m = 25 cm.

3. A body of mass 2 kg moving at 5 m/s collides with a stationary body of mass 3 kg. If they stick together after the collision, what is the loss in kinetic energy?
(A) 5 J
(B) 10 J
(C) 15 J
(D) 25 J

Answer: (C) 15 J
Explanation: This is an inelastic collision. First, find the final velocity (v) using the conservation of momentum.
Initial momentum = (2 kg × 5 m/s) + (3 kg × 0 m/s) = 10 kgm/s.
Final momentum = (2 kg + 3 kg) × v = 5v.
10 = 5v => v = 2 m/s.
Initial K.E = ½ * 2 * 5² = 25 J.
Final K.E = ½ * (2+3) * 2² = ½ * 5 * 4 = 10 J.
Loss in K.E = Initial K.E - Final K.E = 25 J - 10 J = 15 J.

4. A satellite orbits the Earth at an altitude where the acceleration due to gravity is 4.9 m/s². If the radius of the Earth is R, what is the altitude of the satellite? (g on surface = 9.8 m/s²)
(A) (√2 - 1)R
(B) R
(C) (√2)R
(D) 2R

Answer: (A) (√2 - 1)R
Explanation: The acceleration due to gravity 'g' at a distance 'r' from the center of the Earth is given by g = GM/r².
On the surface, g₀ = GM/R².
At altitude h, r = R+h, and g' = GM/(R+h)².
Dividing the two equations: g'/g₀ = (GM/(R+h)²) / (GM/R²) = R² / (R+h)².
Given g' = 4.9 and g₀ = 9.8, so g'/g₀ = 4.9/9.8 = 1/2.
1/2 = R² / (R+h)²
Taking the square root of both sides: 1/√2 = R / (R+h).
R+h = R√2.
h = R√2 - R = R(√2 - 1).

(C) 1.6 J

E₂ = 4 × 0.4 J = 1.6 J.

21. A 100g block of copper at 100°C is placed in 200g of water at 20°C in a well-insulated container. What is the final equilibrium temperature? (Specific heat capacity of copper = 400 J/kgK, water = 4200 J/kgK)

(B) 25.0°C
(C) 31.8°C
(D) 40.0°C

Explanation: Let the final temperature be T.

m_c * c_c * (100 - T) = m_w * c_w * (T - 20)

40 * (100 - T) = 840 * (T - 20)
4000 - 40T = 840T - 16800
4000 + 16800 = 840T + 40T
20800 = 880T

36. The force between two point charges is F. If the distance between them is doubled and one of the charges is halved, the new force will be:
(A) F/8
(B) F/4
(C) F/2
(D) F

F = k * (q₁q₂) / r²
Let the new parameters be q₁' = q₁/2, q₂' = q₂, and r' = 2r.

F' = k * ((q₁/2)q₂) / (2r)²

37. In an RLC series circuit, at resonance, the impedance is:
(A) minimum and equal to R.
(B) maximum and equal to R.
(C) equal to Xʟ - Xc.
(D) infinite.

Answer: (A) minimum and equal to R.
Explanation: The impedance (Z) of a series RLC circuit is given by Z = √(R² + (Xʟ - Xc)²), where Xʟ is the inductive reactance and Xc is the capacitive reactance. Resonance occurs when Xʟ = Xc. At this point, the term (Xʟ - Xc)² becomes zero.
Therefore, Z = √(R² + 0) = R. This is the minimum possible value for the impedance, so the current in the circuit is maximum at resonance.

40. Lenz's law is a consequence of the law of conservation of:
(A) charge
(B) mass
(C) momentum
(D) energy

Answer: (D) energy
Explanation: Lenz's law states that the direction of an induced electromotive force (e.m.f.) and hence the induced current is always such that it opposes the change in magnetic flux that produced it. This opposition means that work must be done against this opposing force to generate the induced current. This work done is converted into electrical energy, thus conserving energy. If the induced current were to aid the change, it would create a self-sustaining loop, generating energy from nothing, which violates the law of conservation of energy.

45. In a p-n junction diode, the depletion region consists of:
(A) mobile electrons and holes.
(B) mobile electrons only.
(C) mobile holes only.
(D) fixed positive and negative ions.

Answer: (D) fixed positive and negative ions.

46. In the photoelectric effect, the kinetic energy of the emitted electrons depends on the:
(A) intensity of the incident light.
(B) frequency of the incident light.
(C) duration of the light exposure.

49. The binding energy of a nucleus is a measure of the:

Explanation: An ideal machine would have 100% efficiency, meaning work output equals work input. However, in any real machine, some of the input energy is converted into non-useful forms, primarily heat, due to work done against frictional forces between the moving parts. This inevitable energy loss means the useful work output is always less than the total work input, making the efficiency (Work Output / Work Input) less than 100%.

61. A man stands on a scale in a lift that is accelerating downwards at 2 m/s². If the man's mass is 70 kg, what is the reading on the scale? (g = 10 m/s²)
(A) 140 N
(B) 560 N
(C) 700 N
(D) 840 N

Answer: (B) 560 N
Explanation: The reading on the scale is the apparent weight, which is the normal reaction force (R). Applying Newton's second law for the man in the lift (taking downwards as positive):
Net Force = mg - R = ma
R = mg - ma = m(g - a)
R = 70 kg * (10 m/s² - 2 m/s²)
R = 70 kg * 8 m/s² = 560 N.

62. A gas at constant temperature occupies a volume V. If the pressure is doubled, the new volume will be:
(A) 2V
(B) V
(C) V/2
(D) V/4

Answer: (C) V/2
Explanation: According to Boyle's Law, for a fixed mass of gas at constant temperature, the volume is inversely proportional to the pressure (V ∝ 1/P). This can be written as P₁V₁ = P₂V₂.
Let P₁ = P and V₁ = V.
The new pressure is P₂ = 2P.
P * V = (2P) * V₂
V₂ = (P * V) / (2P) = V/2.

63. A steel rod has a length of 10 m at 20°C. If the coefficient of linear expansion of steel is 1.2 x 10⁻⁵ K⁻¹, what is its length at 70°C?
(A) 10.006 m
(B) 10.060 m
(C) 10.600 m
(D) 10.0006 m

Answer: (A) 10.006 m
Explanation: The change in length (ΔL) is given by ΔL = αL₀ΔT, where α is the coefficient of linear expansion, L₀ is the original length, and ΔT is the change in temperature.
L₀ = 10 m.
α = 1.2 x 10⁻⁵ K⁻¹.
ΔT = 70°C - 20°C = 50°C (or 50 K, since a change in Celsius is equal to a change in Kelvin).
ΔL = (1.2 x 10⁻⁵) * (10) * (50) = 60 x 10⁻⁵ = 0.006 m.
The new length L = L₀ + ΔL = 10 m + 0.006 m = 10.006 m.

64. Viscosity in a fluid is analogous to which of the following in solids?
(A) Elasticity
(B) Friction
(C) Inertia
(D) Density

Answer: (B) Friction
Explanation: Viscosity is a measure of a fluid's internal resistance to flow. It represents the frictional force between adjacent layers of the fluid that are in relative motion. This is conceptually similar to the frictional force that opposes the relative motion between two solid surfaces in contact.

65. A capacitor is charged by a DC source. Once fully charged, the current flowing from the source to the capacitor is:
(A) Maximum
(B) Zero
(C) Half of the initial current
(D) Dependent on the capacitance

Answer: (B) Zero
Explanation: When a capacitor is connected to a DC source, current flows to charge its plates. As charge accumulates, a potential difference builds up across the capacitor, opposing the source voltage. When the capacitor is fully charged, its potential difference is equal and opposite to the source voltage. At this point, there is no net potential difference to drive the current, and the flow of charge stops. Therefore, the current becomes zero.

Answer: (C) Strong nuclear force
Explanation: Protons within a nucleus repel each other very strongly due to the electromagnetic force. The gravitational force between them is far too weak to counteract this repulsion. 68. If the frequency of a wave is doubled while its speed remains constant, its wavelength will be:
(A) Doubled
(B) Halved
(C) Unchanged
(D) Quadrupled

Answer: (B) Halved
Explanation: The wave equation is v = fλ. If the speed (v) is constant, then frequency (f) and wavelength (λ) are inversely proportional (f ∝ 1/λ or λ ∝ 1/f).
If the frequency is doubled (f' = 2f), the new wavelength (λ') will be λ' = v / f' = v / (2f) = (1/2) * (v/f) = λ/2. The wavelength is halved.

69. A hydraulic press works on the principle of:
(A) Archimedes
(B) Bernoulli
(C) Pascal
(D) Hooke

Answer: (C) Pascal
Explanation: Pascal's principle states that a pressure change at any point in a confined, incompressible fluid is transmitted throughout the fluid such that the same change occurs everywhere. A hydraulic press uses this principle. A small force applied to a small piston creates a pressure (P = F₁/A₁) which is transmitted to a larger piston, generating a much larger force (F₂ = P × A₂).

1. A body is projected with a velocity of 20 m/s at an angle of 30° to the horizontal. What is its time of flight? [g = 10 m/s²]
A. 1.0 s
B. 2.0 s
C. 3.0 s
D. 4.0 s

Answer: B
Explanation: The time of flight (T) for a projectile is given by the formula T = (2u sinθ) / g, where u is the initial velocity, θ is the projection angle, and g is the acceleration due to gravity.
T = (2 * 20 * sin 30°) / 10
T = (40 * 0.5) / 10
T = 20 / 10 = 2.0 s.

2. A block of mass 5 kg is placed on a rough inclined plane of angle 30°. If the coefficient of static friction is 0.6, what is the minimum force required to push the block up the plane? [g = 10 m/s²]
A. 25.0 N
B. 26.0 N
C. 51.0 N
D. 75.0 N

Answer: C
Explanation: The force required (F) must overcome both the component of weight down the slope (mg sinθ) and the frictional force (f). Frictional force f = μR, where R is the normal reaction (R = mg cosθ).
Force down the slope = 5 * 10 * sin 30° = 50 * 0.5 = 25 N.
Frictional force = 0.6 * (5 * 10 * cos 30°) = 0.6 * 50 * (√3/2) ≈ 0.6 * 43.3 = 25.98 N.
Total minimum force F = (Force down the slope) + (Frictional force) = 25 N + 25.98 N ≈ 51.0 N.

3. The work done by a force F = (4x³ + 3) N in moving a particle from x = 1m to x = 2m is:
A. 18 J
B. 21 J
C. 24 J
D. 35 J

Answer: A
Explanation: Work done by a variable force is the integral of the force with respect to displacement.
W = ∫ F dx from x=1 to x=2
W = ∫ (4x³ + 3) dx = [x⁴ + 3x] from 1 to 2
W = [(2)⁴ + 3(2)] - [(1)⁴ + 3(1)]
W = [16 + 6] - [1 + 3]
W = 22 - 4 = 18 J.

4. A satellite of mass 'm' is in a geostationary orbit around the Earth of mass 'M'. Which of the following quantities does its orbital radius depend on?
A. Mass of the satellite (m) only
B. Mass of the Earth (M) only
C. Both M and m
D. Neither M nor m

Answer: B
Explanation: For a geostationary orbit, the gravitational force provides the centripetal force. GMm/r² = mω²r. The mass of the satellite 'm' cancels out. The angular velocity ω is constant for a geostationary orbit (2π / 24 hours). Therefore, GM/r³ = ω², which means r³ = GM/ω². The radius 'r' depends only on the mass of the Earth (M) and fundamental constants (G, ω).

5. The energy stored per unit volume in a stretched wire is given by:
A. ½ × stress × strain
B. stress / strain
C. stress × strain
D. ½ × Force × extension

Answer: A
Explanation: Energy stored (Work Done) = ½ × F × e. Energy density (Energy/Volume) = (½ × F × e) / (A × L).
Since Stress = F/A and Strain = e/L, we can rearrange the energy density formula:
Energy Density = ½ × (F/A) × (e/L) = ½ × stress × strain.

6. A flywheel with a moment of inertia of 2.5 kgm² accelerates from rest to an angular velocity of 12 rad/s in 3 seconds. The constant torque applied is:
A. 4.0 Nm
B. 10.0 Nm
C. 30.0 Nm
D. 37.5 Nm

Answer: B
Explanation: First, find the angular acceleration (α). α = (ω_f - ω_i) / t = (12 - 0) / 3 = 4 rad/s².
Torque (τ) is given by the formula τ = Iα, where I is the moment of inertia.
τ = 2.5 kgm² × 4 rad/s² = 10.0 Nm.

7. A uniform metre rule is pivoted at the 30 cm mark. If it balances horizontally when a mass of 100g is hung at the 5 cm mark, what is the mass of the rule?
A. 100 g
B. 125 g
C. 150 g
D. 200 g

Answer: B
Explanation: The centre of mass of a uniform metre rule is at the 50 cm mark. Let the mass of the rule be M.
The pivot is at 30 cm.
The 100g mass creates a counter-clockwise moment. Distance from pivot = 30 - 5 = 25 cm.
Moment_ccw = 100g * 25 cm.
The weight of the rule (M) creates a clockwise moment. Distance from pivot = 50 - 30 = 20 cm.
Moment_cw = M * 20 cm.
For balance, Moment_cw = Moment_ccw.
M * 20 = 100 * 25
M = 2500 / 20 = 125 g.

8. An object dropped from a certain height attains a velocity 'v' on hitting the ground. If it is dropped from four times the initial height, what will be its velocity on impact?
A. v
B. 2v
C. 4v
D. 16v

Answer: B
Explanation: From the equation of motion v² = u² + 2gh, with u=0, we get v² = 2gh.
So, v = √(2gh). This means velocity is proportional to the square root of the height (v ∝ √h).
If the height is quadrupled (h' = 4h), the new velocity v' = √(2g * 4h) = √4 * √(2gh) = 2v.

A. The solid sphere

Explanation: The solid sphere has a smaller moment of inertia (I = 2/5 MR²) compared to the hollow sphere (I = 2/3 MR²). When rolling, potential energy is converted into both translational and rotational kinetic energy. The object with the smaller moment of inertia will convert more energy into translational motion, leading to a greater linear acceleration and reaching the bottom first.

**10. Water flows through a horizontal pipe of varying cross-section. At a point where the speed of water is 1.0 m/s, the pressure is P. At another point where the speed is 2.0 m/s, what is the pressure? (Density of water = 1000 kg/m ³) **

27. A 100g block of a metal at 100°C is dropped into 200g of water at 25°C in a lagged calorimeter. If the final temperature is 30°C, what is the specific heat capacity of the metal? [Specific heat capacity of water = 4200 J/kg°C]
A. 300 J/kg°C
B. 600 J/kg°C
C. 900 J/kg°C
D. 1200 J/kg°C

Answer: B
Explanation: Principle of mixtures: Heat lost by metal = Heat gained by water.
(m_m * c_m * ΔT_m) = (m_w * c_w * ΔT_w)
m_m = 100g = 0.1 kg; ΔT_m = 100 - 30 = 70°C.
m_w = 200g = 0.2 kg; ΔT_w = 30 - 25 = 5°C.
(0.1 * c_m * 70) = (0.2 * 4200 * 5)
7 * c_m = 4200
c_m = 4200 / 7 = 600 J/kg°C.

4. The terminal velocity of a small sphere falling through a viscous fluid is reached when:
A. The upthrust is equal to the weight of the sphere.
B. The viscous drag is equal to the weight of the sphere.
C. The sum of the upthrust and viscous drag equals the weight of the sphere.
D. The sphere stops accelerating due to its low mass.

Answer: C
Explanation: When an object falls through a fluid, three forces act on it:

Weight (W), acting downwards.

Upthrust (U), acting upwards (Archimedes' principle).

Viscous Drag (Fv), acting upwards, opposing motion.
The net force on the sphere is F_net = W - (U + Fv). According to Newton's second law, F_net = ma.
Terminal velocity is the constant velocity reached when the acceleration 'a' is zero. This happens when the net force is zero.
So, 0 = W - (U + Fv), which means W = U + Fv.
Therefore, terminal velocity is reached when the downward force (weight) is balanced by the sum of the upward forces (upthrust and viscous drag).
