### **Chapter 1: Earth’s Structure and Plate Tectonics**

1.  The boundary separating the Earth's crust from the mantle is known as the...

    **Answer: B) Mohorovičić Discontinuity**
    **Explanation:** The Mohorovičić Discontinuity (Moho) is the boundary identified by a sudden increase in seismic wave velocity, marking the change in composition from the crust to the denser mantle. The Gutenberg Discontinuity separates the mantle and the core.

2.  Which layer of the Earth is in a liquid state and is primarily responsible for generating the Earth's magnetic field?
    A) Inner Core
    B) Outer Core
    C) Mantle
    D) Asthenosphere
    **Answer: B) Outer Core**
    **Explanation:** The Outer Core is composed of liquid iron and nickel. The convection currents within this liquid, influenced by the Earth's rotation, generate the planet's magnetic field through the geodynamo process.

3.  Continental crust is primarily composed of less dense, granitic rocks referred to as...
    A) SiMa
    B) FeNi
    C) SiAl
    D) Peridotite

4.  The theory that the lithosphere is broken into rigid plates that "float" on the more plastic asthenosphere is known as...
    A) Continental Drift

    **Explanation:** Plate Tectonics is the comprehensive theory that describes the movement of lithospheric plates over the asthenosphere and the interactions at their boundaries.

5.  At a divergent plate boundary, which of the following is most likely to occur?

    **Explanation:** At divergent boundaries, plates move apart, allowing magma from the mantle to rise, cool, and solidify, forming new oceanic crust in a process called seafloor spreading.

    A) Effusive volcanic activity

    C) A central rift valley
    D) The formation of new oceanic lithosphere
    **Answer: B) Intense folding, faulting, and crustal thickening**
    **Explanation:** These mountains are formed by immense compressional forces during continental-continental collisions, leading to complex folding, thrust faulting, and a significant thickening of the continental crust.

### **Chapter 2: Weather, Climate, and Atmosphere**

16. The most abundant permanent gas in the Earth's atmosphere by volume is...
    A) Oxygen
    B) Carbon Dioxide
    C) Argon
    D) Nitrogen
    **Answer: D) Nitrogen**
    **Explanation:** Nitrogen (N₂) makes up approximately 78% of the Earth's dry atmosphere.

17. In which layer of the atmosphere do virtually all weather phenomena occur?
    A) Troposphere
    B) Stratosphere
    C) Mesosphere
    D) Thermosphere
    **Answer: A) Troposphere**
    **Explanation:** The troposphere is the lowest layer, containing most of the atmosphere's mass and water vapor, and is where weather systems, clouds, and storms form.

18. The temperature inversion in the stratosphere is caused by the...
    A) absorption of ultraviolet radiation by the ozone layer
    B) distance from the Earth's surface
    C) high concentration of water vapor
    D) burning of meteors
    **Answer: A) absorption of ultraviolet radiation by the ozone layer**
    **Explanation:** The ozone layer within the stratosphere absorbs incoming UV radiation from the sun, which heats this layer, causing temperature to increase with altitude.

19. Lines on a weather map connecting points of equal atmospheric pressure are called...
    A) Isotherms
    B) Isobars
    C) Isohyets
    D) Contours
    **Answer: B) Isobars**
    **Explanation:** Isobars connect points of equal pressure. Isotherms connect points of equal temperature, and isohyets connect points of equal rainfall.

20. A low-pressure system (cyclone) in the Northern Hemisphere is characterized by...
    A) Sinking air and clockwise circulation
    B) Rising air and clockwise circulation
    C) Sinking air and counter-clockwise circulation
    D) Rising air and counter-clockwise circulation
    **Answer: D) Rising air and counter-clockwise circulation**
    **Explanation:** Low-pressure systems feature rising air, which leads to cloud formation and precipitation. Due to the Coriolis effect, winds circulate counter-clockwise into a low in the Northern Hemisphere.

21. The type of rainfall caused by the heating of the Earth's surface, leading to rising warm, moist air, is known as...
    A) Orographic rainfall
    B) Frontal rainfall
    C) Convectional rainfall
    D) Advectional rainfall
    **Answer: C) Convectional rainfall**
    **Explanation:** Convectional rainfall is caused by solar heating of the ground, which heats the air above it, causing it to rise, cool, and condense into clouds, often leading to heavy showers.

22. A rain shadow effect is most likely to be found on the...
    A) Windward side of a mountain
    B) Leeward side of a mountain
    C) Summit of a mountain
    D) Coastal plain
    **Answer: B) Leeward side of a mountain**
    **Explanation:** As moist air rises over a mountain (windward side), it cools and precipitates. The air that descends on the other side (leeward side) is dry, creating a drier region known as a rain shadow.

23. The climate zone characterized by hot, humid conditions year-round with high annual rainfall and no distinct dry season is the...
    A) Tropical Savanna
    B) Tropical Monsoon
    C) Tropical Rainforest (Equatorial)
    D) Arid Desert
    **Answer: C) Tropical Rainforest (Equatorial)**
    **Explanation:** The Tropical Rainforest (Equatorial) climate (Af) is defined by consistently high temperatures and high rainfall distributed throughout the year.

24. A key characteristic of a Mediterranean climate is...
    A) Wet summers and dry winters
    B) Dry summers and wet winters
    C) Wet conditions year-round
    D) Dry conditions year-round
    **Answer: B) Dry summers and wet winters**
    **Explanation:** Mediterranean climates (Csa, Csb) are unique for having their rainy season during the mild winter, while summers are typically hot and dry.

25. The El Niño-Southern Oscillation (ENSO) is a phenomenon that occurs in the...
    A) Equatorial Atlantic Ocean
    B) Equatorial Pacific Ocean
    C) Equatorial Indian Ocean
    D) Arctic Ocean
    **Answer: B) Equatorial Pacific Ocean**
    **Explanation:** ENSO is a coupled ocean-atmosphere phenomenon involving changes in sea surface temperatures and atmospheric pressure across the equatorial Pacific Ocean.

26. During an El Niño event, which of the following typically occurs?
    A) Strengthening of the easterly trade winds
    B) Increased upwelling of cold water off the coast of Peru
    C) Drought in Indonesia and Australia
    D) Heavy rainfall in the western Pacific
    **Answer: C) Drought in Indonesia and Australia**
    **Explanation:** During El Niño, the easterly trade winds weaken, allowing warm water to shift eastward. This shifts the zone of rising air and rainfall to the central/eastern Pacific, causing drought in the western Pacific (Indonesia, Australia).

27. A tropical cyclone is known as a typhoon in the...
    A) Atlantic Ocean
    B) South Pacific Ocean
    C) Indian Ocean
    D) Northwest Pacific Ocean
    **Answer: D) Northwest Pacific Ocean**
    **Explanation:** The same storm system is called a hurricane in the Atlantic, a cyclone in the Indian and South Pacific Oceans, and a typhoon in the Northwest Pacific Ocean.

28. The primary energy source for a tropical cyclone is...
    A) The Coriolis effect
    B) Latent heat released from condensing warm, moist air
    C) Strong upper-level winds
    D) Frictional drag over the ocean surface
    **Answer: B) Latent heat released from condensing warm, moist air**
    **Explanation:** Tropical cyclones are fueled by the immense energy (latent heat) released when water vapor, evaporated from warm ocean waters, condenses into clouds and rain.

29. Which climate is characterized by permafrost, low-growing vegetation, and long, cold winters?
    A) Taiga
    B) Tundra
    C) Ice Cap
    D) Highland
    **Answer: B) Tundra**
    **Explanation:** The Tundra climate (ET) is defined by a warmest month average temperature between 0°C and 10°C, which is too cold for trees. It is characterized by permafrost (permanently frozen subsoil) and low vegetation like mosses and sedges.

30. The dry, dusty northeasterly wind that blows from the Sahara over West Africa during the dry season is called the...
    A) Monsoon
    B) Sirocco
    C) Harmattan
    D) Föhn
    **Answer: C) Harmattan**
    **Explanation:** The Harmattan is the cool, dry, and dusty continental trade wind that affects West Africa from approximately November to March, bringing the dry season.

### **Chapter 3: Fluvial and Coastal Systems**

31. The process by which rock particles carried by a river collide with each other, becoming smaller and more rounded, is called...
    A) Hydraulic action
    B) Abrasion
    C) Attrition
    D) Solution
    **Answer: C) Attrition**
    **Explanation:** Attrition is the wearing down of the river's load as particles knock against each other. Abrasion is the load scouring the bed and banks.

32. A V-shaped valley is a characteristic landform of a river's...
    A) Upper course
    B) Middle course
    C) Lower course
    D) Mouth
    **Answer: A) Upper course**
    **Explanation:** In the steep upper course, vertical erosion is dominant, cutting a deep, narrow valley. Weathering and mass movement of the valley sides create the characteristic V-shape.

33. An oxbow lake is formed from a...
    A) Cut-off meander loop
    B) Glacial valley
    C) Volcanic crater
    D) Subsided fault block
    **Answer: A) Cut-off meander loop**
    **Explanation:** An oxbow lake forms when a river cuts through the narrow neck of a highly sinuous meander, abandoning the old loop, which is then often sealed off by deposition.

34. A fan-shaped deposit of alluvium at the mouth of a river where it enters a sea or lake is called a...
    A) Levee
    B) Floodplain
    C) Delta
    D) Tombolo
    **Answer: C) Delta**
    **Explanation:** A delta is formed when a river deposits its sediment load as its velocity decreases upon entering a standing body of water.

35. The zigzag movement of sediment along a coastline is known as...
    A) Backwash
    B) Longshore drift
    C) Swash
    D) Tidal current
    **Answer: B) Longshore drift**
    **Explanation:** Longshore drift occurs when waves approach the beach at an angle, causing swash to move sediment up the beach at an angle and backwash to pull it straight down, resulting in a net movement along the shore.

36. An elongated ridge of sand projecting from the land into the sea is a...
    A) Stack
    B) Bar
    C) Spit
    D) Cliff
    **Answer: C) Spit**
    **Explanation:** A spit is a depositional landform created by longshore drift, typically where the coastline changes direction.

37. A sequence of coastal erosion features on a headland is correctly ordered as...
    A) Stack -> Cave -> Arch -> Stump
    B) Cave -> Arch -> Stack -> Stump
    C) Arch -> Cave -> Stump -> Stack
    D) Cave -> Stack -> Arch -> Stump
    **Answer: B) Cave -> Arch -> Stack -> Stump**
    **Explanation:** Wave erosion first forms a cave in a headland. The cave may erode through to form an arch. The arch eventually collapses, leaving a stack, which is then eroded down to a stump.

38. A wave-cut platform is formed by...
    A) The deposition of sand by constructive waves
    B) The retreat of a cliff due to wave erosion at its base
    C) The collapse of a sea arch
    D) The accumulation of shingle from longshore drift
    **Answer: B) The retreat of a a cliff due to wave erosion at its base**
    **Explanation:** As waves undercut a cliff, the cliff retreats, leaving behind a gently sloping rock surface at its base, known as a wave-cut platform.

39. The transport of large boulders rolled along a river bed is known as...
    A) Suspension
    B) Solution
    C) Saltation
    D) Traction
    **Answer: D) Traction**
    **Explanation:** Traction is the process of rolling or dragging the heaviest particles of the river's load along the bed.

40. Natural embankments formed along the banks of a river in its lower course are called...
    A) Deltas
    B) Oxbows
    C) Levees
    D) Interlocking spurs
    **Answer: C) Levees**
    **Explanation:** Levees are raised banks formed by the deposition of coarser sediment along the sides of the river channel during floods.

### **Chapter 4: Biomes and Ecosystems**

41. The living components of an ecosystem, such as plants and animals, are referred to as...
    A) Abiotic components
    B) Biotic components
    C) Trophic levels
    D) The biosphere
    **Answer: B) Biotic components**
    **Explanation:** Biotic components are all the living or once-living organisms in an ecosystem. Abiotic components are the non-living physical and chemical factors.

42. Organisms that produce their own food through photosynthesis are known as...
    A) Consumers
    B) Decomposers
    C) Heterotrophs
    D) Autotrophs
    **Answer: D) Autotrophs**
    **Explanation:** Autotrophs (or producers) are organisms that can create their own food from inorganic sources, primarily sunlight. Heterotrophs (consumers and decomposers) obtain energy by feeding on other organisms.

43. Which biome is characterized by high biodiversity, dense multi-layered evergreen trees, and high rainfall year-round?
    A) Temperate Deciduous Forest
    B) Tropical Rainforest
    C) Tropical Savanna
    D) Coniferous Forest (Taiga)
    **Answer: B) Tropical Rainforest**
    **Explanation:** The Tropical Rainforest biome has the highest species diversity and is defined by its dense, tall, evergreen vegetation and consistently hot, wet climate.

44. Permafrost is a characteristic feature of which biome?
    A) Desert
    B) Savanna
    C) Tundra
    D) Temperate Grassland
    **Answer: C) Tundra**
    **Explanation:** Permafrost, or permanently frozen subsoil, is a defining characteristic of the Tundra biome, restricting root growth and causing waterlogged conditions in summer.

45. The gradual process of change in the species composition of a community over time is called...
    A) A food web
    B) Ecological succession
    C) Nutrient cycling
    D) Biodiversity
    **Answer: B) Ecological succession**
    **Explanation:** Ecological succession is the predictable process of change in an ecosystem's community structure following a disturbance or creation of new habitat.

46. The biome dominated by coniferous trees like spruce, fir, and pine, and characterized by long, cold winters is the...
    A) Tundra
    B) Temperate Rainforest
    C) Chaparral
    D) Taiga (Boreal Forest)
    **Answer: D) Taiga (Boreal Forest)**
    **Explanation:** The Taiga, or Boreal Forest, is the vast coniferous forest biome of the high northern latitudes.

47. In a food chain, an organism that eats a primary consumer is called a...
    A) Producer
    B) Secondary consumer
    C) Decomposer
    D) Herbivore
    **Answer: B) Secondary consumer**
    **Explanation:** A primary consumer (herbivore) eats producers. A secondary consumer eats the primary consumer.

48. The shallow, well-lit waters close to the shore of a lake, where rooted plants are abundant, is known as the...
    A) Limnetic zone
    B) Profundal zone
    C) Benthic zone
    D) Littoral zone
    **Answer: D) Littoral zone**
    **Explanation:** The littoral zone is the near-shore area of a lake or pond where sunlight penetrates all the way to the sediment and allows aquatic plants (macrophytes) to grow.

49. Coral reefs are most commonly found in...
    A) Cold, deep polar waters
    B) Warm, shallow, clear tropical waters
    C) Temperate, turbid coastal waters
    D) Brackish estuaries
    **Answer: B) Warm, shallow, clear tropical waters**
    **Explanation:** Coral polyps require warm temperatures and clear water for the symbiotic algae (zooxanthellae) to photosynthesize, so they thrive in shallow tropical seas.

50. The Fynbos of South Africa is an example of which type of biome?
    A) Tropical Savanna
    B) Temperate Grassland
    C) Mediterranean Scrubland
    D) Desert
    **Answer: C) Mediterranean Scrubland**
    **Explanation:** Fynbos is a unique and highly biodiverse type of vegetation found in the Mediterranean climate of South Africa's Western Cape, often referred to as a type of scrubland or chaparral.

### **Chapter 5: Soil Formation, Classification, and Distribution**

51. The vertical cross-section of soil from the surface down to the parent material is called a...
    A) Soil horizon
    B) Soil texture
    C) Soil profile
    D) Soil structure
    **Answer: C) Soil profile**
    **Explanation:** A soil profile is the vertical section through all the soil horizons.

52. The uppermost mineral soil horizon, enriched with humus, is the...
    A) O Horizon
    B) A Horizon
    C) B Horizon
    D) C Horizon
    **Answer: B) A Horizon**
    **Explanation:** The A Horizon, or topsoil, is the zone of mixing of mineral soil and decomposed organic matter (humus). The O horizon is almost purely organic matter.

53. According to the CLORPT model of soil formation, the 'R' stands for...
    A) Rainfall
    B) Relief
    C) Rocks
    D) Rotation
    **Answer: B) Relief**
    **Explanation:** The CLORPT model states that soil formation is a function of Climate, Organisms, Relief (topography), Parent material, and Time.

54. Lateritic soils, which are deeply weathered and rich in iron and aluminum oxides, are typically found in which climate zone?
    A) Polar
    B) Temperate Continental
    C) Hot, humid tropical
    D) Mediterranean
    **Answer: C) Hot, humid tropical**
    **Explanation:** The high temperatures and heavy rainfall in tropical regions lead to intense chemical weathering and leaching, which forms deep, reddish, nutrient-poor lateritic soils (Oxisols).

55. The process by which bacteria convert atmospheric nitrogen gas into a form usable by plants is called...
    A) Denitrification
    B) Nitrification
    C) Nitrogen fixation
    D) Ammonification
    **Answer: C) Nitrogen fixation**
    **Explanation:** Nitrogen fixation is the crucial first step of the nitrogen cycle, making atmospheric nitrogen available to the ecosystem.

56. Soils that are considered ideal for agriculture because they contain a balanced mixture of sand, silt, and clay are called...
    A) Sandy soils
    B) Clayey soils
    C) Loamy soils
    D) Peaty soils
    **Answer: C) Loamy soils**
    **Explanation:** Loam soils combine the good drainage of sand with the nutrient and water retention of silt and clay, providing the best properties for most agricultural crops.

57. The accumulation of salts in the upper layers of soil, often due to improper irrigation in arid regions, is known as...
    A) Leaching
    B) Desertification
    C) Salinization
    D) Laterization
    **Answer: C) Salinization**
    **Explanation:** Salinization occurs when irrigation water containing salts evaporates, leaving the salts behind in the soil, which can inhibit plant growth.

58. Chernozems, which are deep, black, fertile soils rich in humus, are typically associated with which biome?
    A) Tropical Rainforest
    B) Temperate Grassland (Prairie/Steppe)
    C) Tundra
    D) Coniferous Forest
    **Answer: B) Temperate Grassland (Prairie/Steppe)**
    **Explanation:** The decomposition of the dense root systems of grasses in temperate climates leads to the formation of a thick, dark, nutrient-rich A horizon, characteristic of chernozems (Mollisols).

59. The B horizon of a soil profile is often referred to as the zone of...
    A) Eluviation
    B) Illuviation
    C) Humification
    D) Weathering
    **Answer: B) Illuviation**
    **Explanation:** The B horizon is the zone of accumulation (illuviation), where materials like clay, iron oxides, and carbonates leached from the overlying A and E horizons are deposited. Eluviation is the process of removal from the A/E horizons.

60. Land degradation in arid and semi-arid regions resulting from factors like overgrazing and climate change is called...
    A) Deforestation
    B) Soil erosion
    C) Desertification
    D) Salinization
    **Answer: C) Desertification**
    **Explanation:** Desertification is the broad process of fertile land turning into desert-like land in dryland areas, losing its biological productivity.

### **Chapter 6: Population and Settlement Geography**

61. A population pyramid with a very wide base and narrow top indicates a...
    A) Slowly growing population
    B) Rapidly growing population
    C) Stable population
    D) Declining population
    **Answer: B) Rapidly growing population**
    **Explanation:** A wide base represents a large number of young people, which signifies high birth rates and rapid population growth (typical of Stage 2 or 3 of the DTM).

62. In the Demographic Transition Model, the "population explosion" occurs in...
    A) Stage 1
    B) Stage 2
    C) Stage 3
    D) Stage 4
    **Answer: B) Stage 2**
    **Explanation:** In Stage 2, death rates fall dramatically due to improvements in health and sanitation, while birth rates remain high, leading to a very high rate of natural increase and rapid population growth.

63. The main reason for rapid urbanization in many developing countries is...
    A) High urban death rates
    B) Government resettlement programs
    C) Rural-urban migration
    D) High rates of international immigration
    **Answer: C) Rural-urban migration**
    **Explanation:** The movement of people from rural areas to cities in search of better economic opportunities and services is the primary driver of urbanization in the developing world.

64. A rural settlement pattern where houses are clustered together in a village with farmland surrounding it is described as...
    A) Dispersed
    B) Linear
    C) Nucleated
    D) Isolated
    **Answer: C) Nucleated**
    **Explanation:** Nucleated (or clustered) settlements involve dwellings concentrated in a central point, often for reasons of defense, social interaction, or access to a common resource like water.

65. A "push factor" for migration is a factor that...
    A) attracts a person to a new location
    B) drives a person away from their current location
    C) facilitates the process of moving
    D) prevents a person from moving
    **Answer: B) drives a person away from their current location**
    **Explanation:** Push factors are negative conditions at the point of origin, such as poverty, lack of jobs, or conflict, that encourage people to leave. Pull factors are positive conditions at the destination.

66. The total population of a country divided by its total land area gives the...
    A) Physiological density
    B) Agricultural density
    C) Arithmetic density
    D) Optimum density
    **Answer: C) Arithmetic density**
    **Explanation:** Arithmetic (or crude) population density is the simplest measure, showing the number of people per unit of total land area.

67. A major problem associated with rapid urban growth in developing countries is...
    A) A surplus of housing
    B) Under-utilized public services
    C) The growth of informal settlements (slums)
    D) A decrease in traffic congestion
    **Answer: C) The growth of informal settlements (slums)**
    **Explanation:** When urban growth outpaces the provision of formal housing and services, large numbers of people are forced to live in overcrowded, substandard informal settlements.

68. The location of a settlement in relation to its surrounding area and other features is its...
    A) Site
    B) Situation
    C) Pattern
    D) Form
    **Answer: B) Situation**
    **Explanation:** Situation (or relative location) describes where a settlement is located in relation to things like transport routes, other settlements, or resources. Site refers to the actual physical characteristics of the land on which it is built.

69. In Stage 4 of the Demographic Transition Model, a country typically has...
    A) High birth rates and low death rates
    B) High birth rates and high death rates
    C) Low birth rates and low death rates
    D) Low birth rates and high death rates
    **Answer: C) Low birth rates and low death rates**
    **Explanation:** Stage 4 is characterized by a stabilization of the population as both birth and death rates become low, leading to slow or zero population growth.

70. A settlement pattern arranged in a line along a road or river is best described as...
    A) Clustered
    B) Nucleated
    C) Dispersed
    D) Linear
    **Answer: D) Linear**
    **Explanation:** Linear or ribbon settlements are elongated in shape, following a transport route or a physical feature like a river or coastline.

### **Chapter 7: Economic and Resource Geography**

71. The economic sector that involves the extraction of raw materials from the natural environment is the...
    A) Primary sector
    B) Secondary sector
    C) Tertiary sector
    D) Quaternary sector
    **Answer: A) Primary sector**
    **Explanation:** The primary sector includes activities like agriculture, mining, fishing, and forestry, which are all based on harvesting natural resources.

72. The conversion of raw materials into finished goods, such as in a car factory, is part of the...
    A) Primary sector
    B) Secondary sector
    C) Tertiary sector
    D) Quaternary sector
    **Answer: B) Secondary sector**
    **Explanation:** The secondary sector involves all manufacturing, processing, and construction activities that add value to raw materials.

73. In most developed countries, the largest sector of the economy in terms of employment and GDP is the...
    A) Primary sector
    B) Secondary sector
    C) Tertiary sector
    D) Agricultural sector
    **Answer: C) Tertiary sector**
    **Explanation:** Developed economies are typically dominated by the service sector (tertiary and quaternary), which includes activities like retail, finance, healthcare, and education.

74. A farming system where production is primarily for the consumption of the farmer's family is known as...
    A) Commercial farming
    B) Plantation agriculture
    C) Subsistence farming
    D) Extensive farming
    **Answer: C) Subsistence farming**
    **Explanation:** Subsistence farming is characterized by production for personal use, with little or no surplus for sale.

75. According to Weber's theory of industrial location, a weight-losing industry is most likely to locate near the...
    A) Market
    B) Source of labor
    C) Source of raw materials
    D) Source of energy
    **Answer: C) Source of raw materials**
    **Explanation:** In a weight-losing industry, the final product is lighter than the raw materials. To minimize transport costs, it is cheaper to process the materials at their source and transport the lighter finished product to market.

76. The benefits gained by firms locating close to each other, such as a shared skilled labor pool and specialized suppliers, are called...
    A) Economies of scale
    B) Agglomeration economies
    C) Comparative advantage
    D) Vertical integration
    **Answer: B) Agglomeration economies**
    **Explanation:** Agglomeration economies are the cost savings and other advantages that arise when firms in the same or related industries cluster together in a single location.

77. Plantation agriculture is a type of...
    A) Subsistence farming focused on staples
    B) Commercial farming focused on a single cash crop
    C) Mixed farming with crops and livestock
    D) Nomadic herding
    **Answer: B) Commercial farming focused on a single cash crop**
    **Explanation:** Plantations are large-scale commercial farms that specialize in growing a single cash crop (e.g., tea, coffee, rubber, bananas) for export.

78. The increasing economic interdependence of countries through cross-border flows of goods, services, and capital is known as...
    A) Protectionism
    B) Regionalism
    C) Globalization
    D) Nationalism
    **Answer: C) Globalization**
    **Explanation:** Globalization refers to the integration and interdependence of national economies on a global scale.

79. The quaternary sector of the economy is primarily based on...
    A) Manual labor
    B) Raw material extraction
    C) Manufacturing
    D) Information and knowledge
    **Answer: D) Information and knowledge**
    **Explanation:** The quaternary sector involves knowledge-based activities like research and development (R&D), IT services, and specialized consultancy.

80. A large-scale cattle ranching operation in Australia is an example of...
    A) Intensive subsistence farming
    B) Extensive commercial farming
    C) Intensive commercial farming
    D) Shifting cultivation
    **Answer: B) Extensive commercial farming**
    **Explanation:** It is commercial because the cattle are raised for sale, and extensive because it uses vast areas of land with low inputs per unit area.

### **Chapter 8: Cartography and Map Interpretation**

81. A map with a scale of 1:25,000 is a...
    A) Larger scale map than 1:1,000,000
    B) Smaller scale map than 1:1,000,000
    C) Map with no scale
    D) Map where 1 cm represents 25,000 km
    **Answer: A) Larger scale map than 1:1,000,000**
    **Explanation:** A large-scale map shows a small area in great detail. The representative fraction 1/25,000 is a larger number than 1/1,000,000, hence it is a larger scale.

82. On a topographic map, closely spaced contour lines indicate a...
    A) Gentle slope
    B) Steep slope
    C) Flat plain
    D) Valley floor
    **Answer: B) Steep slope**
    **Explanation:** Contour lines connect points of equal elevation. When they are close together, it means that elevation is changing rapidly over a short horizontal distance, indicating a steep slope.

83. Which map projection is well-known for preserving direction but grossly distorting areas near the poles?
    A) Peters Projection
    B) Mercator Projection
    C) Conical Projection
    D) Azimuthal Projection
    **Answer: B) Mercator Projection**
    **Explanation:** The Mercator projection was designed for navigation because lines of constant bearing (rhumb lines) are straight. However, this is achieved by stretching areas in a north-south direction, causing massive area distortion near the poles.

84. A thematic map that uses different colors or shades to represent data values within predefined areas (like countries or states) is called a...
    A) Dot distribution map
    B) Isoline map
    C) Choropleth map
    D) Proportional symbol map
    **Answer: C) Choropleth map**
    **Explanation:** Choropleth maps are used to show the magnitude of a variable (e.g., population density) by shading areal units.

85. In a six-figure grid reference such as 345562, the numbers '345' refer to the...
    A) Easting
    B) Northing
    C) Altitude
    D) Bearing
    **Answer: A) Easting**
    **Explanation:** The rule for grid references is "read right up." The first half of the reference (345) refers to the Easting (the vertical grid lines), and the second half (562) refers to the Northing (the horizontal grid lines).

86. When contour lines cross a river, they form a 'V' shape that points...
    A) Downstream
    B) Upstream
    C) Towards the south
    D) Towards the north
    **Answer: B) Upstream**
    **Explanation:** A river flows in the bottom of a valley. Since contour lines must stay at the same elevation, they have to bend up the valley to cross the river, so the 'V' points towards higher ground (upstream).

87. A bearing of 225° is in which cardinal direction?
    A) North-East
    B) South-East
    C) South-West
    D) North-West
    **Answer: C) South-West**
    **Explanation:** In a whole circle bearing, North is 0°, East is 90°, South is 180°, and West is 270°. 225° lies exactly between South (180°) and West (270°), which is South-West.

88. The ratio 1:50,000 on a map is an example of which type of scale?
    A) Statement scale
    B) Graphic scale
    C) Representative Fraction (RF)
    D) Linear scale
    **Answer: C) Representative Fraction (RF)**
    **Explanation:** A Representative Fraction (RF) or ratio scale expresses the map scale as a unitless ratio, such as 1:50,000.

89. A map designed to show the distribution of a specific theme, such as rainfall or population density, is called a...
    A) Topographic map
    B) Thematic map
    C) General purpose map
    D) Physical map
    **Answer: B) Thematic map**
    **Explanation:** Thematic maps are designed to illustrate a particular topic or "theme," in contrast to general purpose maps like topographic maps which show a variety of features.

90. The angular distance of a place north or south of the Equator is its...
    A) Longitude
    B) Bearing
    C) Latitude
    D) Altitude
    **Answer: C) Latitude**
    **Explanation:** Latitude measures the angular distance from the Equator (0°) to the poles (90° North or South). Longitude measures angular distance east or west of the Prime Meridian.

### **Chapter 9: GIS, Remote Sensing, and GPS**

91. A computer system designed to capture, store, analyze, and display geographically referenced data is called...
    A) GPS
    B) GIS
    C) Remote Sensing
    D) A digital map
    **Answer: B) GIS**
    **Explanation:** Geographic Information System (GIS) is the comprehensive term for the computer system that handles all aspects of spatial data.

92. In GIS, representing features like roads and rivers as a sequence of connected points is an example of the...
    A) Raster data model
    B) Vector data model
    C) Attribute data model
    D) Elevation data model
    **Answer: B) Vector data model**
    **Explanation:** The vector data model uses points, lines, and polygons to represent discrete geographic features with clear boundaries. The raster model uses a grid of cells.

93. The science of obtaining information about the Earth's surface from a distance, using satellites or aircraft, is known as...
    A) Cartography
    B) Geocoding
    C) Remote Sensing
    D) Surveying
    **Answer: C) Remote Sensing**
    **Explanation:** Remote Sensing is the acquisition of information about an object or phenomenon without making physical contact with it.

94. Which of the following is an example of an active remote sensing system?
    A) A camera on an airplane
    B) A multispectral scanner on the Landsat satellite
    C) The human eye
    D) RADAR
    **Answer: D) RADAR**
    **Explanation:** Active sensors, like RADAR and LiDAR, emit their own energy and detect the reflected return. Passive sensors, like cameras, detect naturally available energy (e.g., sunlight).

95. In GIS analysis, creating a zone of a specified distance around a feature (e.g., 1 km around a school) is called...
    A) Overlay
    B) Buffering
    C) Network analysis
    D) Geocoding
    **Answer: B) Buffering**
    **Explanation:** Buffering is a proximity analysis tool that creates a polygon (a buffer) around a point, line, or polygon feature.

96. The Global Positioning System (GPS) works by...
    A) Taking pictures of the Earth from satellites
    B) A receiver calculating its distance from multiple satellites
    C) Bouncing radio waves off the ionosphere
    D) Using a magnetic compass to find North
    **Answer: B) A receiver calculating its distance from multiple satellites**
    **Explanation:** A GPS receiver determines its location through trilateration, by precisely measuring the time it takes for signals from at least four different satellites to arrive.

97. A key advantage of satellite remote sensing for environmental monitoring is its ability to...
    A) provide very high-resolution imagery of small areas only
    B) provide regular, repetitive coverage of large areas
    C) physically collect soil and water samples
    D) operate without the need for computer analysis
    **Answer: B) provide regular, repetitive coverage of large areas**
    **Explanation:** Satellites in orbit can repeatedly image the same location, which is crucial for monitoring changes over time, such as deforestation, urban growth, or the effects of a flood.

98. The Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI) is commonly used in remote sensing to assess...
    A) Soil moisture
    B) Water temperature
    C) Vegetation health and density
    D) Urban heat island effect
    **Answer: C) Vegetation health and density**
    **Explanation:** NDVI is calculated from the red and near-infrared bands of a satellite image. Healthy, dense vegetation reflects a lot of near-infrared light and absorbs red light, resulting in a high NDVI value.

99. In GIS, the ability to combine different thematic layers like soils, slope, and land use to find suitable locations for a new development is an example of...
    A) Buffering
    B) Geocoding
    C) Network analysis
    D) Overlay analysis
    **Answer: D) Overlay analysis**
    **Explanation:** Overlay analysis is a fundamental GIS capability that involves integrating multiple data layers to create a new layer with combined information.

100. A primary application of GPS in fieldwork is for...
    A) Analyzing soil chemistry
    B) Measuring wind speed
    C) Collecting precise location coordinates of features
    D) Interviewing local residents
    **Answer: C) Collecting precise location coordinates of features**
    **Explanation:** GPS is used to accurately record the latitude and longitude of sample points, transects, or observed features, which can then be mapped and analyzed in a GIS.

### **Chapter 10: Physical and Economic Features of West Africa**

101. The principal river of West Africa, which flows in a large arc through Mali and Nigeria, is the...
    A) Volta River
    B) Senegal River
    C) Gambia River
    D) Niger River
    **Answer: D) Niger River**
    **Explanation:** The Niger River is the longest and most important river in West Africa, known for its distinctive "Niger Bend" or arc shape.

102. The Fouta Djallon highlands in Guinea are significant because they are...
    A) the highest mountain range in Africa
    B) a major source region for West Africa's main rivers
    C) the location of the largest oil reserves in the region
    D) a dense tropical rainforest biome
    **Answer: B) a major source region for West Africa's main rivers**
    **Explanation:** The Fouta Djallon plateau acts as the "water tower" of West Africa, being the source of the Niger, Senegal, and Gambia rivers.

103. The climatic and vegetation zones in West Africa are generally arranged in belts that run...
    A) North to South
    B) East to West
    C) Diagonally from SW to NE
    D) Radially from the center
    **Answer: B) East to West**
    **Explanation:** Due to the influence of latitude and the ITCZ, West Africa exhibits distinct climatic and vegetation belts (Equatorial, Savanna, Sahel, Desert) that are oriented in an east-west direction, transitioning from wet in the south to dry in the north.

104. The seasonal reversal of winds in West Africa, bringing moist SW winds in the wet season and dry NE winds in the dry season, is driven by the...
    A) Coriolis effect
    B) Altitude of the Jos Plateau
    C) Migration of the Intertropical Convergence Zone (ITCZ)
    D) Influence of the Benguela current
    **Answer: C) Migration of the Intertropical Convergence Zone (ITCZ)**
    **Explanation:** The seasonal north-south movement of the low-pressure ITCZ belt draws in moist monsoon winds from the Atlantic when it's in the north (wet season) and allows the dry Harmattan winds from the Sahara to dominate when it's in the south (dry season).

105. The vegetation belt located immediately south of the Sahara Desert, characterized by thorn scrub and short grasses, is the...
    A) Guinea Savanna
    B) Sudan Savanna
    C) Sahel Savanna
    D) Tropical Rainforest
    **Answer: C) Sahel Savanna**
    **Explanation:** The Sahel is the semi-arid transitional zone between the Sahara Desert to the north and the Sudan Savanna to the south.

106. Nigeria is the largest African producer of which of the following resources?
    A) Gold
    B) Bauxite
    C) Petroleum (Crude Oil)
    D) Copper
    **Answer: C) Petroleum (Crude Oil)**
    **Explanation:** Nigeria is a major member of OPEC and Africa's leading producer and exporter of petroleum, which is primarily found in the Niger Delta region.

107. Lake Volta in Ghana is a very large...
    A) Natural freshwater lake
    B) Alkaline Rift Valley lake
    C) Artificial lake (reservoir) created by a dam
    D) Endorheic (internal drainage) lake
    **Answer: C) Artificial lake (reservoir) created by a dam**
    **Explanation:** Lake Volta was formed by the construction of the Akosombo Dam on the Volta River and is one of the largest man-made lakes in the world.

108. The dominant type of land use in the Sahel region is...
    A) Plantation agriculture
    B) Shifting cultivation
    C) Nomadic pastoralism
    D) Commercial grain farming
    **Answer: C) Nomadic pastoralism**
    **Explanation:** The arid and semi-arid conditions of the Sahel are not suitable for most forms of intensive agriculture, so the dominant livelihood is the herding of livestock like cattle, sheep, goats, and camels.

109. Which West African country is a world-leading producer of bauxite?
    A) Nigeria
    B) Ghana
    C) Guinea
    D) Senegal
    **Answer: C) Guinea**
    **Explanation:** Guinea possesses some of the world's largest and highest-quality reserves of bauxite, the ore from which aluminum is produced.

110. The environmental problem of land degradation in drylands, leading to the expansion of desert-like conditions, is a major threat in the...
    A) Niger Delta
    B) Guinea coastal region
    C) Sahel region
    D) Jos Plateau
    **Answer: C) Sahel region**
    **Explanation:** The Sahel is highly vulnerable to desertification due to its low and variable rainfall, and pressures from overgrazing and climate change.

111. The seasonal movement of livestock between wet and dry season pastures in West Africa is an example of...
    A) Shifting cultivation
    B) Transhumance
    C) Crop rotation
    D) Mixed farming
    **Answer: B) Transhumance**
    **Explanation:** Transhumance is a form of pastoralism that involves the seasonal migration of livestock and herders between different grazing areas.

112. The climate of the southern coastal belt of West Africa (e.g., Lagos, Accra) is best described as...
    A) Tropical Savanna
    B) Tropical Desert
    C) Equatorial/Tropical Monsoon
    D) Mediterranean
    **Answer: C) Equatorial/Tropical Monsoon**
    **Explanation:** This region experiences high temperatures and humidity year-round with high rainfall, often with two peak rainy seasons, characteristic of an equatorial or tropical monsoon climate.

113. The Jos Plateau in Nigeria is notable for its...
    A) Higher altitude and cooler climate than the surrounding plains
    B) Extensive oil and gas reserves
    C) Location within the Great Rift Valley
    D) Dense tropical rainforest vegetation
    **Answer: A) Higher altitude and cooler climate than the surrounding plains**
    **Explanation:** The Jos Plateau is a significant highland area that stands out from the surrounding savanna plains, resulting in a cooler, temperate-like climate. It was also historically important for tin mining.

114. The Benue River is a major tributary of the...
    A) Volta River

115. Ghana was formerly known by its colonial name "Gold Coast" due to its rich deposits of...

    C) Lake Victoria

    **Answer: C) Lake Victoria**

### **Chapter 12: Environmental Degradation and Land Use Change**

131. The leading cause of deforestation globally is...
    A) Forest fires
    B) Commercial logging
    C) Conversion of forests to agriculture
    D) Urban sprawl
    **Answer: C) Conversion of forests to agriculture**
    **Explanation:** Clearing land for crops and livestock pasture is the single largest driver of deforestation worldwide.

132. The removal of a thin, uniform layer of topsoil by flowing water is called...
    A) Gully erosion
    B) Rill erosion
    C) Sheet erosion
    D) Rain splash erosion
    **Answer: C) Sheet erosion**
    **Explanation:** Sheet erosion is the less conspicuous but widespread removal of soil in thin layers from the land surface, often a precursor to more severe rill and gully erosion.

133. The uncontrolled expansion of low-density urban development over rural land is known as...
    A) Urbanization
    B) Urban renewal
    C) Urban sprawl
    D) Gentrification
    **Answer: C) Urban sprawl**
    **Explanation:** Urban sprawl specifically refers to the spreading, low-density, often car-dependent pattern of development at the edges of cities.

134. Which of the following is a major consequence of deforestation?
    A) Increased soil fertility
    B) A decrease in atmospheric carbon dioxide
    C) An increase in biodiversity
    D) An increased risk of soil erosion and landslides
    **Answer: D) An increased risk of soil erosion and landslides**
    **Explanation:** The root systems of trees bind the soil together and the canopy protects it from rain. Removing forests exposes the soil, making it highly susceptible to erosion and increasing the likelihood of landslides on steep slopes.

135. The 3Rs in waste management stand for...
    A) Rebuild, Reuse, Reclaim
    B) Reduce, Reuse, Recycle
    C) Remove, Relocate, Reclaim
    D) Restore, Reforest, Reclaim
    **Answer: B) Reduce, Reuse, Recycle**
    **Explanation:** The 3Rs form the core of the waste hierarchy, prioritizing waste prevention (Reduce), followed by using items again (Reuse), and then processing materials into new products (Recycle).

136. A major cause of sea-level rise due to climate change is...
    A) Increased rainfall into the oceans
    B) The melting of sea ice
    C) The thermal expansion of ocean water
    D) Increased evaporation from the ocean surface
    **Answer: C) The thermal expansion of ocean water**
    **Explanation:** As the oceans absorb heat and warm up, the water itself expands, increasing its volume. This, along with the melting of land-based ice (glaciers and ice sheets), is a primary driver of sea-level rise. Melting sea ice does not significantly raise sea level as it is already displacing its own mass in the water.

137. The absorption of excess atmospheric CO₂ by the oceans is leading to...
    A) Ocean warming only
    B) Ocean acidification
    C) An increase in marine biodiversity
    D) A decrease in sea level
    **Answer: B) Ocean acidification**
    **Explanation:** When CO₂ dissolves in seawater, it forms carbonic acid, which lowers the ocean's pH. This acidification harms marine organisms with calcium carbonate shells, like corals and shellfish.

138. Replanting trees on land that was previously forested is called...
    A) Afforestation
    B) Reforestation
    C) Agroforestry
    D) Deforestation
    **Answer: B) Reforestation**
    **Explanation:** Reforestation is the restoration of forests on land that was once forested. Afforestation is planting forests on land that was not previously forested.

139. Which of the following is a key principle of sustainable agriculture?
    A) Monocropping on a large scale
    B) Minimizing soil erosion through practices like contour farming
    C) Maximizing the use of chemical pesticides
    D) Removing all crop residues from the field
    **Answer: B) Minimizing soil erosion through practices like contour farming**
    **Explanation:** Sustainable agriculture aims to conserve natural resources. Minimizing soil erosion is a fundamental aspect of this, achieved through techniques that protect the soil.

140. Areas like national parks and wildlife reserves designated to protect biodiversity are known as...
    A) Urban areas
    B) Agricultural zones
    C) Protected areas
    D) Industrial estates
    **Answer: C) Protected areas**
    **Explanation:** Protected areas are managed specifically for the long-term conservation of nature with associated ecosystem services and cultural values.

### **Chapter 14 & 15: Fieldwork and GIS-Based Exercises**

151. In designing a field investigation, a specific, testable prediction about the outcome is called a...
    A) Aim
    B) Objective
    C) Hypothesis
    D) Conclusion
    **Answer: C) Hypothesis**
    **Explanation:** A hypothesis is a clear statement of expected results that can be supported or refuted through the collection and analysis of data.

152. A sampling method where every item in the population has an equal chance of being selected is called...
    A) Systematic sampling
    B) Stratified sampling
    C) Random sampling
    D) Quota sampling
    **Answer: C) Random sampling**
    **Explanation:** Random sampling is designed to be unbiased by giving every possible sample point an equal probability of selection.

153. A square frame used to sample vegetation in a defined area is called a...
    A) Transect
    B) Quadrat
    C) Pitfall trap
    D) Clinometer
    **Answer: B) Quadrat**
    **Explanation:** A quadrat is a standard tool in ecology and geography for sampling the abundance, density, or percentage cover of plants or other sessile organisms.

154. To study how vegetation changes along an environmental gradient, such as from a shoreline inland, the most appropriate method would be to use a...
    A) Series of random quadrats
    B) Transect
    C) Soil auger
    D) Questionnaire
    **Answer: B) Transect**
    **Explanation:** A transect is a line or belt along which data is collected systematically. It is ideal for investigating changes across a gradient.

155. In a fieldwork report, the section where you describe in detail how you collected your data is the...
    A) Introduction
    B) Results
    C) Methodology
    D) Conclusion
    **Answer: C) Methodology**
    **Explanation:** The methodology section must provide a clear and detailed account of the procedures, equipment, and sampling strategies used so that the study could be replicated.

156. In a fieldwork report, the section where you interpret your findings and explain what they mean is the...
    A) Methodology
    B) Results and Data Presentation
    C) Discussion and Analysis
    D) Appendices
    **Answer: C) Discussion and Analysis**
    **Explanation:** The discussion section is where you move beyond just presenting the data (Results) to analyzing patterns, explaining them with geographical theory, and interpreting their significance.

157. A pilot study is conducted before the main fieldwork in order to...
    A) Collect the majority of the data
    B) Test and refine the data collection methods
    C) Write the final conclusion
    D) Analyze data from other researchers
    **Answer: B) Test and refine the data collection methods**
    **Explanation:** A pilot study is a small-scale trial run that helps identify any problems with the methodology, equipment, or data recording sheets before committing to the full-scale investigation.

158. Which of the following is an example of primary data?
    A) A topographic map of the study area
    B) Census statistics from a government website
    C) River depth measurements you took yourself in the field
    D) A research article about the study area
    **Answer: C) River depth measurements you took yourself in the field**
    **Explanation:** Primary data is original data collected firsthand by the researcher for the specific purpose of their investigation. The other options are examples of secondary data.

159. Using GIS to find all areas that are both forested AND on a slope of less than 15 degrees is an example of...
    A) Buffering
    B) Overlay analysis
    C) Geocoding
    D) Network analysis
    **Answer: B) Overlay analysis**
    **Explanation:** This task involves combining (overlaying) a land cover data layer and a slope data layer to create new information that meets both criteria.

160. Using a smartphone's GPS to record the location of each questionnaire respondent is an example of...
    A) Qualitative data analysis
    B) Integrating GIS into field data collection
    C) Secondary data sourcing
    D) Laboratory analysis
    **Answer: B) Integrating GIS into field data collection**
    **Explanation:** This is a modern fieldwork technique where GPS technology is used to capture the spatial component of primary data, which can then be mapped and analyzed in a GIS.

161. The asthenosphere is the layer upon which lithospheric plates move. It is best described as...
    A) A rigid solid
    B) A low-density gas
    C) A liquid metal alloy
    D) A plastic, partially molten solid
    **Answer: D) A plastic, partially molten solid**
    **Explanation:** The asthenosphere is solid rock, but due to intense heat and pressure, it can flow very slowly over geological time (exhibiting plasticity), allowing the rigid lithosphere to move on top of it.

162. A good example of a continental rift valley is the...
    A) Mid-Atlantic Ridge
    B) East African Rift Valley
    C) Mariana Trench
    D) San Andreas Fault
    **Answer: B) East African Rift Valley**
    **Explanation:** The East African Rift Valley is a major active divergent boundary developing within the African continent, causing it to stretch and break apart.

163. The fact that fossils of the freshwater reptile *Mesosaurus* were found in both South America and Africa was used by Wegener as evidence for...
    A) Seafloor spreading
    B) The formation of the Ring of Fire
    C) Continental drift
    D) The existence of a land bridge
    **Answer: C) Continental drift**
    **Explanation:** Wegener argued that the presence of an identical freshwater reptile on two continents now separated by a vast ocean strongly suggested the continents were once connected.

164. The instrument used to measure wind speed is an...
    A) Anemometer
    B) Wind vane
    C) Barometer
    D) Hygrometer
    **Answer: A) Anemometer**
    **Explanation:** An anemometer measures wind speed, while a wind vane measures wind direction.

165. The deflection of wind to the right in the Northern Hemisphere is caused by the...
    A) Pressure Gradient Force
    B) Coriolis Effect
    C) Frictional Force
    D) Gravitational Force
    **Answer: B) Coriolis Effect**
    **Explanation:** The Coriolis effect is an apparent force caused by the Earth's rotation that deflects moving objects (like wind and ocean currents) to the right in the Northern Hemisphere and to the left in the Southern Hemisphere.

166. A climograph shows a city with high temperatures year-round and a very distinct wet and dry season. This city is most likely located in which climate zone?
    A) Tropical Rainforest
    B) Tropical Savanna
    C) Mediterranean
    D) Tundra
    **Answer: B) Tropical Savanna**
    **Explanation:** The key characteristics of a Tropical Savanna climate are consistently high temperatures and a pronounced seasonal difference in rainfall.

167. A tombolo is a depositional landform that connects...
    A) Two headlands
    B) A sea stack to a cliff
    C) An island to the mainland
    D) The two banks of a river
    **Answer: C) An island to the mainland**
    **Explanation:** A tombolo is a spit or bar that has grown outwards from the coast to connect with a nearby island.

168. The primary role of decomposers like bacteria and fungi in an ecosystem is...
    A) To produce energy through photosynthesis
    B) To control the population of herbivores
    C) To recycle nutrients from dead organic matter
    D) To form the base of the food web
    **Answer: C) To recycle nutrients from dead organic matter**
    **Explanation:** Decomposers are essential for breaking down dead organisms and waste products, returning vital nutrients to the soil and water to be used again by producers.

169. The zone of a lake where there is insufficient light for photosynthesis is the...
    A) Littoral zone
    B) Limnetic zone
    C) Profundal zone
    D) Epilimnion
    **Answer: C) Profundal zone**
    **Explanation:** The profundal (or aphotic) zone is the deep, dark region of a lake below the depth of light penetration.

170. Which of the following soil-forming factors is most influential at a broad, global scale?
    A) Time
    B) Relief
    C) Parent Material
    D) Climate
    **Answer: D) Climate**
    **Explanation:** Climate (temperature and precipitation) is the dominant factor that determines the major soil types found across the globe (e.g., lateritic soils in the tropics, podzols in cold, wet regions, desert soils in arid regions).

171. A country with a very high physiological population density indicates that...
    A) It has a very large total land area
    B) Its population is evenly distributed
    C) There is a large number of farmers
    D) There is significant pressure on its arable land
    **Answer: D) There is significant pressure on its arable land**
    **Explanation:** Physiological density (Total Population / Arable Land) relates population size to the amount of land available for farming. A high value means many people depend on each unit of farmland.

172. A major characteristic of shifting cultivation is that it...
    A) Uses heavy machinery and chemical fertilizers
    B) Is a form of commercial agriculture for export
    C) Involves farming a plot for a few years and then leaving it fallow
    D) Is practiced primarily in arid and semi-arid regions
    **Answer: C) Involves farming a plot for a few years and then leaving it fallow**
    **Explanation:** Shifting cultivation is a traditional farming system where land is cleared, farmed until fertility declines, and then abandoned for a period (fallow) to allow natural vegetation and soil fertility to recover.

173. The European Union (EU) and the African Continental Free Trade Area (AfCFTA) are examples of...
    A) Military alliances
    B) Regional trade blocs
    C) Global environmental agencies
    D) Cultural exchange programs
    **Answer: B) Regional trade blocs**
    **Explanation:** These are agreements between countries in a geographic region to reduce or eliminate trade barriers (like tariffs) among themselves to promote trade and economic integration.

174. On a map with a scale of 1:100,000, a distance of 4 cm on the map represents what distance on the ground?
    A) 400 m
    B) 4 km
    C) 40 km
    D) 400 km
    **Answer: B) 4 km**
    **Explanation:** 1 cm on the map represents 100,000 cm on the ground. 100,000 cm = 1,000 m = 1 km. Therefore, 4 cm on the map represents 4 x 1 km = 4 km.

175. The back bearing of 060° is...
    A) 120°
    B) 240°
    C) 300°
    D) 060°
    **Answer: B) 240°**
    **Explanation:** To find the back bearing, you add or subtract 180°. Since 60° is less than 180°, you add 180°. 60° + 180° = 240°.

176. Which GIS data model is best suited for representing continuous data like elevation or temperature?
    A) Vector
    B) Raster
    C) Point
    D) Polygon
    **Answer: B) Raster**
    **Explanation:** The raster data model uses a grid of cells, where each cell holds a value, making it ideal for representing phenomena that vary continuously across a surface.

177. The Inland Niger Delta is a vast area of lakes and marshes located in the country of...
    A) Nigeria
    B) Niger
    C) Mali
    D) Guinea
    **Answer: C) Mali**
    **Explanation:** The Inland Niger Delta is a unique feature where the Niger River slows and spreads out in the semi-arid region of central Mali, creating a large, productive wetland.

178. The "Resource Curse" or "Paradox of Plenty" refers to the observation that countries with abundant natural resources often...
    A) have the most diversified economies
    B) experience slower economic growth and more conflict
    C) are the most politically stable
    D) have the lowest levels of environmental pollution
    **Answer: B) experience slower economic growth and more conflict**
    **Explanation:** The "Resource Curse" describes the phenomenon where resource-rich countries can suffer from issues like neglect of other sectors, corruption, and instability, leading to poor development outcomes despite their wealth.

179. A key difference between the lakes of the Eastern and Western Rift Valleys in Africa is that the Eastern Rift lakes are often...
    A) Deeper and larger
    B) Shallower and more alkaline (soda lakes)
    C) Colder and ice-covered
    D) Man-made reservoirs
    **Answer: B) Shallower and more alkaline (soda lakes)**
    **Explanation:** The Eastern Rift is a closed drainage basin in many parts, so high evaporation rates concentrate salts, making many of its lakes shallow and alkaline (e.g., Lake Nakuru, Lake Magadi), famous for flamingos. The Western Rift lakes are typically deeper freshwater lakes.

180. The altitudinal zonation of vegetation on a high mountain is an example of a...
    A) Global biome
    B) Latitudinal climate pattern
    C) Microclimate
    D) Highland climate effect
    **Answer: D) Highland climate effect**
    **Explanation:** As altitude increases, temperature decreases, creating different climate zones on a mountain's slopes, which in turn support different belts of vegetation, similar to moving from the equator towards the poles.

181. A major impact of climate change on low-lying coastal areas and small island nations is...
    A) Increased risk of drought
    B) An increase in soil fertility
    C) A decrease in extreme weather events
    D) Increased coastal flooding due to sea-level rise
    **Answer: D) Increased coastal flooding due to sea-level rise**
    **Explanation:** Sea-level rise makes these areas more vulnerable to permanent inundation, coastal erosion, and more severe flooding during storm surges.

182. A sampling strategy that involves dividing a population into subgroups and then sampling from each subgroup is...
    A) Random
    B) Systematic
    C) Stratified
    D) Opportunistic
    **Answer: C) Stratified**
    **Explanation:** Stratified sampling is used to ensure that all distinct subgroups within a population are adequately represented in the sample.

183. The lithosphere is composed of the...
    A) crust and the asthenosphere
    B) crust and the rigid upper part of the mantle
    C) asthenosphere and the lower mantle
    D) entire mantle and the core
    **Answer: B) crust and the rigid upper part of the mantle**
    **Explanation:** The lithosphere is the solid, rigid outer shell of the Earth, which includes the crust and the uppermost, brittle layer of the mantle. It is this layer that is broken into tectonic plates.

184. Pillow lavas are characteristic features of...
    A) Continental-continental collisions
    B) Subaerial volcanic eruptions
    C) Submarine volcanic eruptions at mid-ocean ridges
    D) Transform fault boundaries
    **Answer: C) Submarine volcanic eruptions at mid-ocean ridges**
    **Explanation:** When basaltic lava erupts underwater, its surface cools rapidly, forming distinctive rounded, pillow-like shapes. This is common at divergent boundaries.

185. A Stevenson Screen is used in meteorology to...
    A) Measure rainfall
    B) House thermometers to get an accurate air temperature reading
    C) Measure wind direction
    D) Record atmospheric pressure
    **Answer: B) House thermometers to get an accurate air temperature reading**
    **Explanation:** A Stevenson Screen is a louvered white box that shields thermometers from direct sunlight and precipitation while allowing air to circulate freely, ensuring a standardized and accurate measurement of air temperature.

186. The formation of a spit that grows completely across a bay, cutting it off from the sea, creates a...
    A) Tombolo
    B) Lagoon
    C) Wave-cut platform
    D) Sea stack
    **Answer: B) Lagoon**
    **Explanation:** When a spit extends across the mouth of a bay, it forms a baymouth bar, and the body of water trapped behind it is called a lagoon.

187. In the context of soil pH, most essential plant nutrients are most available in a range that is...
    A) Strongly acidic (pH 4-5)
    B) Slightly acidic to neutral (pH 6-7)
    C) Strongly alkaline (pH 8-9)
    D) Extremely acidic (pH < 4)
    **Answer: B) Slightly acidic to neutral (pH 6-7)**
    **Explanation:** In this pH range, the solubility and availability of most macronutrients and micronutrients are optimized for plant uptake.

188. In many developed countries, the shape of the population pyramid is best described as a...
    A) Wide-based pyramid
    B) Beehive or column
    C) Inverted pyramid
    D) Narrow-based pyramid
    **Answer: B) Beehive or column**
    **Explanation:** Countries in Stage 4 of the DTM have low birth and death rates, resulting in a population pyramid with straighter sides (a beehive or column shape), indicating slow or stable growth and a more even age distribution.

189. Which of the following is a weight-gaining industry?
    A) Copper smelting
    B) Sawmilling
    C) Beverage bottling
    D) Sugar refining
    **Answer: C) Beverage bottling**
    **Explanation:** Beverage bottling is a classic weight-gaining industry. The final product (bottled drink) is much heavier and bulkier than the main inputs (syrup, concentrate), so it is cheaper to locate the bottling plant near the market to reduce transport costs of the finished product.

190. Which of the following map scales would be most suitable for a detailed map of a school campus?
    A) 1:1,000,000
    B) 1:250,000
    C) 1:50,000
    D) 1:1,000
    **Answer: D) 1:1,000**
    **Explanation:** To show a small area like a school campus in great detail, you need a very large-scale map. 1:1,000 is the largest scale among the options (1 cm represents only 10 meters).

191. Geocoding is the process of...
    A) Assigning geographic coordinates to a street address
    B) Creating a buffer around a point
    C) Calculating the distance between two points
    D) Collecting soil samples in the field
    **Answer: A) Assigning geographic coordinates to a street address**
    **Explanation:** Geocoding is a GIS function that converts descriptive location data, like addresses or place names, into geographic coordinates (latitude/longitude or grid coordinates) so they can be mapped.

192. A major environmental concern associated with the Niger Delta is...
    A) Desertification
    B) Acid rain from industrial emissions
    C) Pollution from oil spills and gas flaring
    D) Over-extraction of groundwater for irrigation
    **Answer: C) Pollution from oil spills and gas flaring**
    **Explanation:** Decades of oil extraction in the Niger Delta have led to severe environmental degradation, including frequent oil spills that contaminate land and water, and the flaring of associated natural gas which causes air pollution.

193. The Kalahari is best described as a...
    A) Large, hot desert in North Africa
    B) Coastal desert in East Africa
    C) High-altitude cold desert
    D) Large semi-arid sandy savanna in Southern Africa
    **Answer: D) Large semi-arid sandy savanna in Southern Africa**
    **Explanation:** While called a desert, the Kalahari receives more rainfall than a true desert and supports significant vegetation, making it more accurately described as a semi-desert or arid savanna. It covers large parts of Botswana, Namibia, and South Africa.

    **Explanation:** It emphasizes that the reconstruction phase after a disaster is an opportunity to rebuild infrastructure and social systems in a way that reduces vulnerability to future hazards.

    B) A V-shaped valley
    C) A beach

    **Answer: C) A beach**

198. The most significant greenhouse gas in terms of its increasing concentration due to human activities is...
    A) Water vapor
    B) Carbon dioxide
    C) Nitrogen
    D) Argon
    **Answer: B) Carbon dioxide**
    **Explanation:** While water vapor is a potent greenhouse gas, its concentration is not directly driven by human emissions in the same way as carbon dioxide (from burning fossil fuels and deforestation), which is the primary driver of modern climate change.

199. A dispersed settlement pattern is most likely to be found in areas of...
    A) Intensive subsistence farming with a need for defense
    B) Extensive commercial farming on large, individual farmsteads
    C) A scarcity of water resources forcing clustering
    D) A linear transport route
    **Answer: B) Extensive commercial farming on large, individual farmsteads**
    **Explanation:** In regions like the North American prairies or Australian outback, where farms are very large and land is abundant, farmhouses are often scattered far from one another, each on its own property.

200. A map with a verbal scale of "One centimeter to two kilometers" has a Representative Fraction (RF) of...
    A) 1:2,000
    B) 1:20,000
    C) 1:200,000
    D) 1:2,000,000
    **Answer: C) 1:200,000**
    **Explanation:** To find the RF, both units must be the same. 2 km = 2,000 meters = 200,000 centimeters. So, 1 cm represents 200,000 cm, giving an RF of 1:200,000.

201. The sinking of a dense oceanic plate beneath a less dense plate is called...
    A) Rifting
    B) Subduction
    C) Spreading
    D) Transformation
    **Answer: B) Subduction**
    **Explanation:** Subduction is the process that occurs at convergent plate boundaries where one plate is forced to slide beneath another into the mantle.

202. The aurora borealis (Northern Lights) occurs in which layer of the atmosphere?
    A) Troposphere
    B) Stratosphere
    C) Mesosphere
    D) Thermosphere
    **Answer: D) Thermosphere**
    **Explanation:** The auroras are caused by charged particles from the sun interacting with atoms and molecules in the thermosphere (specifically the ionosphere part), causing them to emit light.

203. A waterfall is often formed where...
    A) A river flows over a band of hard rock onto a band of softer rock
    B) A river meanders across a wide floodplain
    C) A river deposits sediment to form a delta
    D) A river is joined by a major tributary
    **Answer: A) A river flows over a band of hard rock onto a band of softer rock**
    **Explanation:** The softer rock is eroded more quickly than the harder rock, creating a vertical drop over which the river flows, forming a waterfall.

204. Which of the following is an example of an abiotic component of an ecosystem?
    A) A tree
    B) A fungus
    C) A deer
    D) Sunlight
    **Answer: D) Sunlight**
    **Explanation:** Abiotic components are the non-living chemical and physical parts of the environment that affect living organisms. Sunlight, water, soil, and temperature are all abiotic.

205. The process of mineralization in the nitrogen cycle is carried out by...
    A) Plants
    B) Herbivores
    C) Decomposers
    D) Nitrogen-fixing bacteria
    **Answer: C) Decomposers**
    **Explanation:** Mineralization (or ammonification) is the process where decomposers (like bacteria and fungi) break down organic nitrogen from dead organisms and waste into inorganic ammonium (NH₄⁺), which plants can then use.

206. A key feature of Stage 3 of the Demographic Transition Model is that...
    A) Birth rates and death rates are both high
    B) Death rates fall while birth rates remain high
    C) Birth rates begin to fall, and population growth slows down
    D) Birth rates and death rates are both low and stable
    **Answer: C) Birth rates begin to fall, and population growth slows down**
    **Explanation:** Stage 3 is characterized by declining birth rates (due to factors like urbanization, education, and access to contraception), which causes the rate of population growth to decrease from its peak in Stage 2.

207. Tourism, banking, and retail are all part of which economic sector?
    A) Primary
    B) Secondary
    C) Tertiary
    D) Extractive
    **Answer: C) Tertiary**
    **Explanation:** These are all service-based activities, which are the defining feature of the tertiary sector.

208. A line on a map connecting points of equal rainfall is an...
    A) Isobar
    B) Isotherm

    D) Isogon
    **Answer: C) Isohyet**
    **Explanation:** Isohyets are used on climatic maps to show rainfall distribution.

209. In GIS, the descriptive information about a geographic feature (e.g., the name or surface type of a road) is stored as...
    A) Vector data
    B) Raster data
    C) A data layer
    D) Attribute data
    **Answer: D) Attribute data**
    **Explanation:** Attribute data is the non-spatial information that is linked to the spatial data (the points, lines, or polygons). It is typically stored in a table.

210. Lake Chad has been shrinking dramatically in recent decades primarily due to...
    A) Tectonic uplift
    B) Volcanic activity
    C) Climate change and over-extraction of water

    **Answer: C) Climate change and over-extraction of water**
    **Explanation:** A combination of reduced rainfall due to climate change and increased demand for water for irrigation from the rivers that feed it has caused the dramatic shrinking of Lake Chad.

212. Coral bleaching is a phenomenon caused primarily by...

    **Answer: C) Basalt**

217. The layer of the atmosphere where most meteors burn up is the...
    A) Troposphere
    B) Stratosphere
    C) Mesosphere
    D) Thermosphere
    **Answer: C) Mesosphere**

218. The hydraulic action of a river refers to the...

    **Answer: C) Sheer force of the moving water dislodging material**

228. The first step in designing a fieldwork investigation is typically to...

230. A tectonic plate is a segment of the...
    A) Asthenosphere
    B) Lithosphere

    D) Lower Mantle
    **Answer: B) Lithosphere**
    **Explanation:** The Earth's rigid outer shell, the lithosphere (crust + upper mantle), is broken into the segments we call tectonic plates.

231. The formation of deep ocean trenches is characteristic of...
    A) Divergent boundaries

### **Geography MCQ Practice Questions (WAEC Standard)**

#### **Part I: Physical Geography (Chapters 1-3)**

**Chapter 1: Earth’s Structure and Plate Tectonics**

1.  **Question:** The layer of the Earth responsible for generating the planet's magnetic field is the...
    *   A) Inner Core
    *   B) Outer Core
    *   C) Mantle
    *   D) Crust
    *   **Answer:** B) Outer Core
    *   **Explanation:** The Earth's magnetic field is generated by the movement (convection currents) of molten iron and nickel in the liquid outer core. This process is known as the geodynamo theory.

2.  **Question:** The boundary that separates the Earth's crust from the mantle is known as the...
    *   A) Gutenberg Discontinuity
    *   B) Lehman Discontinuity
    *   C) Mohorovičić Discontinuity
    *   D) Conrad Discontinuity
    *   **Answer:** C) Mohorovičić Discontinuity
    *   **Explanation:** The Mohorovičić Discontinuity (or Moho) is defined by a sudden increase in the velocity of seismic waves, indicating a change in composition and density from the crust to the mantle.

3.  **Question:** A major difference between continental and oceanic crust is that continental crust is...
    *   A) thinner and denser
    *   B) thicker and less dense
    *   C) younger and basaltic
    *   D) thinner and granitic
    *   **Answer:** B) thicker and less dense
    *   **Explanation:** Continental crust is primarily granitic (rich in Silica and Alumina - SiAl), thicker (35-70 km), and less dense (avg. 2.7 g/cm³) than oceanic crust, which is basaltic (rich in Silica and Magnesia - SiMa), thinner (5-10 km), and denser (avg. 3.0 g/cm³).

4.  **Question:** The theory of plate tectonics is primarily driven by...
    *   A) centrifugal forces from Earth's rotation
    *   B) tidal forces from the Moon and Sun
    *   C) convection currents in the mantle
    *   D) solar winds interacting with the magnetosphere
    *   **Answer:** C) convection currents in the mantle
    *   **Explanation:** The slow movement of hotter, less dense material rising and cooler, denser material sinking within the mantle creates convection currents. These currents, along with slab pull and ridge push, are the primary driving forces of plate motion.

5.  **Question:** Which type of plate boundary is characterized by the formation of new oceanic lithosphere?
    *   A) Convergent
    *   B) Transform
    *   C) Divergent
    *   D) Subduction
    *   **Answer:** C) Divergent
    *   **Explanation:** At divergent boundaries, tectonic plates move apart, allowing magma from the mantle to rise, cool, and solidify, creating new crust. This process is known as seafloor spreading.

6.  **Question:** The Andes Mountains and the Peru-Chile Trench are features associated with which type of plate convergence?
    *   A) Oceanic-Oceanic
    *   B) Continental-Continental
    *   C) Oceanic-Continental
    *   D) Continental-Transform
    *   **Answer:** C) Oceanic-Continental
    *   **Explanation:** When a denser oceanic plate collides with a less dense continental plate, the oceanic plate subducts, forming a deep ocean trench. The magma generated from this process rises to form a continental volcanic arc, like the Andes Mountains.

7.  **Question:** The Himalayas were formed by the collision of the...
    *   A) Pacific Plate and North American Plate
    *   B) Indian Plate and Eurasian Plate
    *   C) South American Plate and Nazca Plate
    *   D) African Plate and Arabian Plate
    *   **Answer:** B) Indian Plate and Eurasian Plate
    *   **Explanation:** The Himalayan mountain range is a classic example of a fold mountain system created by the ongoing continental-continental collision between the Indian and Eurasian plates.

8.  **Question:** The San Andreas Fault in California is a well-known example of a...
    *   A) mid-ocean ridge
    *   B) transform boundary
    *   C) subduction zone
    *   D) rift valley
    *   **Answer:** B) transform boundary
    *   **Explanation:** At the San Andreas Fault, the Pacific Plate and the North American Plate slide horizontally past each other, which is characteristic of a transform plate boundary.

9.  **Question:** Earthquakes that occur at a subduction zone and range from shallow to deep focus are found within the...
    *   A) Moho discontinuity
    *   B) Asthenosphere
    *   C) Wadati-Benioff zone
    *   D) Lithosphere
    *   **Answer:** C) Wadati-Benioff zone
    *   **Explanation:** The Wadati-Benioff zone is the dipping planar zone of earthquakes that is produced by the interaction of a downgoing oceanic crustal plate with a continental plate.

10. **Question:** The "Pacific Ring of Fire" is a zone of frequent earthquakes and volcanic eruptions associated with...
    *   A) divergent plate boundaries
    *   B) hotspots and mantle plumes
    *   C) convergent plate boundaries
    *   D) continental rift valleys
    *   **Answer:** C) convergent plate boundaries
    *   **Explanation:** The Pacific Ring of Fire outlines the Pacific Plate, where it converges with several other plates, leading to extensive subduction zones that cause intense volcanic and seismic activity.

11. **Question:** Alfred Wegener's theory of continental drift was initially rejected primarily because he could not...
    *   A) prove the continents were once joined
    *   B) explain the fossil evidence
    *   C) provide a plausible mechanism for continental movement
    *   D) match the rock types across continents
    *   **Answer:** C) provide a plausible mechanism for continental movement
    *   **Explanation:** Despite compelling evidence like the jigsaw fit of continents and fossil distribution, Wegener's theory was not accepted because his proposed mechanisms (centrifugal force, tidal forces) were too weak to move continents.

12. **Question:** The symmetrical pattern of magnetic stripes on either side of a mid-ocean ridge is strong evidence for...
    *   A) continental rifting
    *   B) seafloor spreading
    *   C) hotspot volcanism
    *   D) isostatic adjustment
    *   **Answer:** B) seafloor spreading
    *   **Explanation:** As new crust forms at the mid-ocean ridge, it records the Earth's magnetic polarity at that time. The crust then spreads out, creating a symmetrical pattern of normal and reversed magnetic stripes, confirming the seafloor spreading hypothesis.

13. **Question:** The Hawaiian Islands were formed by...
    *   A) oceanic-oceanic convergence
    *   B) a mid-ocean ridge
    *   C) a continental rift
    *   D) a mantle plume or hotspot
    *   **Answer:** D) a mantle plume or hotspot
    *   **Explanation:** The Hawaiian Islands are an example of intraplate volcanism. They formed as the Pacific Plate moved over a stationary hotspot, a plume of hot rock rising from deep within the mantle.

14. **Question:** The inability of S-waves (shear waves) to pass through the outer core suggests that it is...
    *   A) solid
    *   B) liquid
    *   C) semi-molten
    *   D) gaseous
    *   **Answer:** B) liquid
    *   **Explanation:** S-waves can only travel through solid materials. Their disappearance at the core-mantle boundary is primary evidence that the outer core is liquid.

15. **Question:** A rift valley, such as the East African Rift Valley, is formed at a...
    *   A) convergent boundary
    *   B) transform boundary
    *   C) divergent boundary on a continent
    *   D) divergent boundary in the ocean
    *   **Answer:** C) divergent boundary on a continent
    *   **Explanation:** When a divergent boundary develops within a continental landmass, tensional forces stretch and fracture the crust, causing blocks of land to drop down and form a rift valley.

16. **Question:** The force that pulls a subducting plate into the mantle due to its own weight is known as...
    *   A) ridge push
    *   B) slab pull
    *   C) mantle drag
    *   D) trench suction
    *   **Answer:** B) slab pull
    *   **Explanation:** Slab pull is the force exerted by the weight of the cold, dense subducting lithospheric plate as it sinks into the mantle. It is now considered a dominant driving force of plate tectonics.

17. **Question:** What is the supercontinent that Alfred Wegener proposed existed about 200-300 million years ago?
    *   A) Gondwanaland

    *   C) Pangaea

    *   **Explanation:** Wegener named the single supercontinent Pangaea, from the Greek words for "all lands." He hypothesized that it later broke apart into the continents we see today.

18. **Question:** The rigid, brittle outer layer of the Earth, consisting of the crust and the uppermost mantle, is the...
    *   A) asthenosphere
    *   B) mesosphere
    *   C) lithosphere
    *   D) biosphere

    *   **Explanation:** The lithosphere is the rigid outer part of the Earth, which is broken into tectonic plates. It "floats" on the more plastic, flowing asthenosphere beneath it.

    *   **Explanation:** Paleomagnetism and the discovery of magnetic stripes on the seafloor came decades after Wegener's death and were key evidence for the theory of seafloor spreading, which then led to the modern theory of plate tectonics.

20. **Question:** Effusive, non-explosive volcanic eruptions of basaltic lava are most common at...
    *   A) subduction zones
    *   B) continental collision zones
    *   C) mid-ocean ridges
    *   D) continental transform faults
    *   **Answer:** C) mid-ocean ridges

**Chapter 3: Fluvial and Coastal Systems**

41. **Question:** The process by which a river's load of rocks and pebbles collide with each other, becoming smaller and more rounded, is called...
    *   A) Hydraulic action
    *   B) Abrasion
    *   C) Attrition
    *   D) Solution
    *   **Answer:** C) Attrition
    *   **Explanation:** Attrition is the wearing down of the transported sediment itself. Abrasion is the wearing down of the river bed and banks by the sediment load.

42. **Question:** Large boulders and rocks are rolled along the river bed by a process known as...
    *   A) Suspension
    *   B) Saltation
    *   C) Solution
    *   D) Traction
    *   **Answer:** D) Traction
    *   **Explanation:** Traction is the transport mechanism for the largest, heaviest parts of a river's load, which are too heavy to be lifted from the bed.

43. **Question:** A V-shaped valley is a characteristic landform of a river's...
    *   A) upper course
    *   B) middle course
    *   C) lower course
    *   D) mouth
    *   **Answer:** A) upper course
    *   **Explanation:** In the steep, high-energy upper course, vertical erosion (downcutting) is dominant, leading to the formation of deep, narrow, V-shaped valleys.

44. **Question:** A crescent-shaped lake formed when a meander of a river is cut off is known as...
    *   A) a levee
    *   B) a delta
    *   C) an oxbow lake
    *   D) a braided channel
    *   **Answer:** C) an oxbow lake
    *   **Explanation:** During a flood, a river may cut across the narrow neck of a meander to create a straighter course. Deposition then seals off the old meander loop, forming an oxbow lake.

45. **Question:** A fan-shaped deposit of alluvium at the mouth of a river where it enters a sea or lake is called a...
    *   A) floodplain
    *   B) levee
    *   C) delta
    *   D) tombolo
    *   **Answer:** C) delta
    *   **Explanation:** A delta forms when a river deposits its sediment load faster than the sea or lake can remove it, as the river's velocity drops upon entering the standing body of water.

46. **Question:** The zigzag movement of sediment along a coastline is known as...
    *   A) backwash
    *   B) longshore drift
    *   C) swash
    *   D) tidal current
    *   **Answer:** B) longshore drift
    *   **Explanation:** Longshore drift occurs when waves approach the shore at an angle, carrying sediment up the beach (swash) at that angle, while the backwash carries it straight back down, resulting in a net movement of sediment along the coast.

47. **Question:** A pillar of rock standing in the sea, separated from a headland, is called a...
    *   A) spit
    *   B) bar
    *   C) stack
    *   D) cave
    *   **Answer:** C) stack
    *   **Explanation:** A stack is a feature of coastal erosion, formed when a sea arch collapses, leaving an isolated rock pillar. A stump is the remnant of a stack after further erosion.

48. **Question:** An elongated ridge of sand or shingle projecting from the land out into the sea is a...
    *   A) cliff
    *   B) wave-cut platform
    *   C) tombolo
    *   D) spit
    *   **Answer:** D) spit
    *   **Explanation:** Spits are depositional landforms created by longshore drift, typically where the coastline changes direction.

49. **Question:** Which river process involves the dissolving of soluble rocks like limestone?
    *   A) Attrition
    *   B) Abrasion
    *   C) Solution
    *   D) Hydraulic action
    *   **Answer:** C) Solution
    *   **Explanation:** Solution (or corrosion) is a chemical weathering process where the river water, which is a weak acid, dissolves minerals from the bedrock, especially carbonate rocks like limestone and chalk.

50. **Question:** In a river meander, erosion is most active on the...
    *   A) inner bank
    *   B) outer bank
    *   C) river bed
    *   D) slip-off slope
    *   **Answer:** B) outer bank
    *   **Explanation:** In a meander, the water flows fastest on the outer bank, causing greater erosion and forming a river cliff. Deposition occurs on the slower-flowing inner bank, forming a slip-off slope or point bar.

51. **Question:** The tidal mouth of a river where fresh and salt water mix is called...
    *   A) a delta
    *   B) a lagoon
    *   C) an estuary
    *   D) a fjord
    *   **Answer:** C) an estuary
    *   **Explanation:** An estuary is a semi-enclosed coastal body of brackish water with one or more rivers or streams flowing into it, and with a free connection to the open sea.

52. **Question:** Waterfalls are often formed where a river flows over...
    *   A) a band of hard rock overlying a band of soft rock
    *   B) a wide, flat floodplain
    *   C) an oxbow lake
    *   D) unconsolidated alluvial deposits
    *   **Answer:** A) a band of hard rock overlying a band of soft rock
    *   **Explanation:** The river erodes the softer rock more quickly than the resistant hard rock, creating a step or vertical drop in the river's long profile, which forms a waterfall.

53. **Question:** A ridge of sand that connects an island to the mainland is a...
    *   A) baymouth bar
    *   B) spit
    *   C) tombolo
    *   D) levee
    *   **Answer:** C) tombolo
    *   **Explanation:** A tombolo is a depositional landform where a spit or bar grows outwards from the coast to connect with a nearby island.

54. **Question:** Raised banks of alluvium on either side of a river channel in its lower course are known as...
    *   A) floodplains
    *   B) deltas
    *   C) levees
    *   D) terraces
    *   **Answer:** C) levees
    *   **Explanation:** During floods, a river deposits its coarsest sediment first along the channel banks as it loses energy. Over time, these deposits build up to form natural levees.

55. **Question:** Which of the following is an example of a coastal landform created primarily by erosion?
    *   A) Beach
    *   B) Spit
    *   C) Wave-cut platform
    *   D) Salt marsh
    *   **Answer:** C) Wave-cut platform
    *   **Explanation:** A wave-cut platform is the gently sloping rock surface left at the base of a cliff as it retreats due to wave erosion. Beaches, spits, and salt marshes are depositional features.

56. **Question:** The main agent of erosion, transport, and deposition in a desert environment is...
    *   A) rivers
    *   B) glaciers
    *   C) wind
    *   D) waves
    *   **Answer:** C) wind
    *   **Explanation:** While water can cause significant erosion during rare flash floods, wind (aeolian processes) is the dominant and persistent agent shaping desert landscapes, creating features like sand dunes.

57. **Question:** The competence of a river refers to the...
    *   A) total amount of load it can carry
    *   B) speed of the water flow
    *   C) maximum particle size it can transport
    *   D) volume of water passing a point per second
    *   **Answer:** C) maximum particle size it can transport
    *   **Explanation:** Competence is a measure of the largest size of sediment a river can carry, which is determined by its velocity. Capacity is the total amount of load it can carry.

58. **Question:** Which of the following is NOT a process of river transport?
    *   A) Saltation
    *   B) Suspension
    *   C) Corrasion
    *   D) Traction
    *   **Answer:** C) Corrasion
    *   **Explanation:** Corrasion is another term for abrasion, which is a process of river *erosion*, not transport. Saltation, suspension, and traction are all methods of sediment transport.

59. **Question:** Interlocking spurs are a common feature of the...
    *   A) upper course of a river
    *   B) coastal shoreline
    *   C) lower course of a river
    *   D) glaciated valley
    *   **Answer:** A) upper course of a river
    *   **Explanation:** In its upper course, a river lacks the power to erode laterally and winds its way around ridges of hard rock, creating interlocking spurs.

60. **Question:** Constructive waves are characterized by a...
    *   A) strong backwash and weak swash
    *   B) weak backwash and strong swash
    *   C) high frequency and steep wave front
    *   D) high energy and erosional power
    *   **Answer:** B) weak backwash and strong swash
    *   **Explanation:** Constructive waves have a powerful swash that carries sediment up the beach and a weaker backwash, leading to a net deposition of material and the building up of beaches.

#### **Part II: Biogeography and Soil Geography (Chapters 4-5)**

61. **Question:** The living components of an ecosystem, such as plants and animals, are referred to as...
    *   A) Abiotic components
    *   B) Biotic components
    *   C) Trophic levels
    *   D) Climatic factors
    *   **Answer:** B) Biotic components
    *   **Explanation:** The biotic components of an ecosystem are all the living or once-living organisms. Abiotic components are the non-living physical and chemical factors like sunlight, water, and soil.

62. **Question:** In a food chain, organisms that feed on producers are called...
    *   A) Secondary consumers
    *   B) Decomposers
    *   C) Primary consumers
    *   D) Tertiary consumers
    *   **Answer:** C) Primary consumers
    *   **Explanation:** Primary consumers, or herbivores, are organisms that obtain energy by eating producers (plants or algae).

63. **Question:** A large-scale ecosystem characterized by a dominant vegetation type and climate is a...
    *   A) habitat
    *   B) niche
    *   C) biome
    *   D) community
    *   **Answer:** C) biome
    *   **Explanation:** A biome is a major global community of organisms, classified according to the predominant vegetation and characterized by adaptations of organisms to that particular environment (e.g., Tropical Rainforest, Tundra, Desert).

64. **Question:** Which biome is characterized by long, cold winters, short, cool summers, and coniferous trees like spruce and fir?
    *   A) Temperate Deciduous Forest
    *   B) Tundra
    *   C) Tropical Savanna
    *   D) Boreal Forest (Taiga)
    *   **Answer:** D) Boreal Forest (Taiga)
    *   **Explanation:** The Boreal Forest, or Taiga, is found in high northern latitudes and is dominated by evergreen, needle-leaved coniferous trees adapted to cold conditions.

65. **Question:** The gradual process of change in an ecosystem's community structure over time is called...
    *   A) eutrophication
    *   B) biodiversity
    *   C) ecological succession
    *   D) nutrient cycling
    *   **Answer:** C) ecological succession
    *   **Explanation:** Ecological succession is the process by which the mix of species and habitat in an area changes over time, for example, after a disturbance (secondary succession) or on new land (primary succession).

66. **Question:** Which of the following represents a type of freshwater biome?
    *   A) Coral reef
    *   B) Estuary
    *   C) River
    *   D) Intertidal zone
    *   **Answer:** C) River
    *   **Explanation:** Rivers are lotic (flowing water) freshwater ecosystems. Coral reefs and intertidal zones are marine (saltwater), and estuaries are brackish (mixed fresh and salt water).

67. **Question:** The uppermost soil horizon, rich in organic matter and humus, is the...
    *   A) A Horizon
    *   B) B Horizon
    *   C) C Horizon
    *   D) O Horizon
    *   **Answer:** D) O Horizon
    *   **Explanation:** The O Horizon is the surface layer composed of organic material, such as decomposing leaves and twigs. The A Horizon (topsoil) is the uppermost *mineral* layer, enriched with humus from the O Horizon.

68. **Question:** The soil formation factor concerned with the shape of the land surface, including slope and aspect, is...
    *   A) Climate
    *   B) Organisms
    *   C) Relief
    *   D) Parent Material
    *   **Answer:** C) Relief
    *   **Explanation:** Relief (or topography) influences soil formation by affecting drainage, erosion, and the amount of solar radiation received, leading to different soil types on slopes versus flat areas.

69. **Question:** Deeply weathered, reddish, nutrient-poor soils found in hot, humid tropical regions are known as...
    *   A) Alluvial soils
    *   B) Black soils (Chernozems)
    *   C) Lateritic soils (Latosols)
    *   D) Loamy soils
    *   **Answer:** C) Lateritic soils (Latosols)
    *   **Explanation:** Lateritic soils are formed by intense weathering and leaching in tropical climates, which removes soluble nutrients and silica, leaving behind iron and aluminum oxides that give the soil a red color.

70. **Question:** The process by which nutrients are washed out of the upper soil horizons by percolating water is called...
    *   A) Humification
    *   B) Leaching
    *   C) Illuviation
    *   D) Bioturbation
    *   **Answer:** B) Leaching
    *   **Explanation:** Leaching (also known as eluviation) is the downward movement of dissolved or suspended material from one soil layer to another. Illuviation is the accumulation of this material in a lower layer.

71. **Question:** Which biome is characterized by grasses, scattered trees, and distinct wet and dry seasons?
    *   A) Tropical Rainforest
    *   B) Temperate Grassland
    *   C) Tropical Savanna
    *   D) Desert
    *   **Answer:** C) Tropical Savanna
    *   **Explanation:** Tropical savannas have a grass-dominated landscape with scattered trees, adapted to a climate with a pronounced dry season and seasonal fires.

72. **Question:** In the nitrogen cycle, the conversion of atmospheric nitrogen gas into ammonia by bacteria is called...
    *   A) Nitrification
    *   B) Denitrification
    *   C) Ammonification
    *   D) Nitrogen fixation
    *   **Answer:** D) Nitrogen fixation
    *   **Explanation:** Nitrogen fixation is the crucial process that converts inert atmospheric nitrogen (N₂) into a form that plants can use (ammonia/ammonium), primarily carried out by nitrogen-fixing bacteria.

73. **Question:** The variety of life on Earth at all its levels, from genes to ecosystems, is termed...
    *   A) ecological succession
    *   B) a food web
    *   C) biodiversity
    *   D) a biome
    *   **Answer:** C) biodiversity
    *   **Explanation:** Biodiversity, or biological diversity, encompasses genetic diversity (within species), species diversity (number of species), and ecosystem diversity (variety of ecosystems).

74. **Question:** Which soil type is considered ideal for agriculture due to its balanced mixture of sand, silt, and clay?
    *   A) Sandy soil
    *   B) Clayey soil
    *   C) Peaty soil
    *   D) Loamy soil
    *   **Answer:** D) Loamy soil
    *   **Explanation:** Loamy soils have good drainage and aeration (from sand), good water and nutrient retention (from clay and silt), and are easy to work, making them highly desirable for farming.

75. **Question:** The CLORPT model of soil formation stands for...
    *   A) Clay, Loam, Organic, Rock, Plant, Time
    *   B) Climate, Organisms, Relief, Parent Material, Time
    *   C) Carbon, Light, Oxygen, Rain, Pressure, Temperature
    *   D) Climate, Latitude, Orogeny, Runoff, Plant-life, Tectonics
    *   **Answer:** B) Climate, Organisms, Relief, Parent Material, Time
    *   **Explanation:** The CLORPT model, developed by Hans Jenny, identifies the five major independent factors that control the formation of soil.

76. **Question:** Which of these ecosystems is known as the "rainforest of the sea" due to its extremely high biodiversity?
    *   A) Mangrove forest
    *   B) Coral reef
    *   C) Kelp forest
    *   D) Estuary
    *   **Answer:** B) Coral reef
    *   **Explanation:** Coral reefs are complex structures built by coral animals that support a vast array of marine life, making them one of the most biodiverse ecosystems on Earth.

77. **Question:** Land degradation in arid and semi-arid regions, leading to the expansion of desert-like conditions, is called...
    *   A) Deforestation
    *   B) Salinization
    *   C) Desertification
    *   D) Laterization
    *   **Answer:** C) Desertification
    *   **Explanation:** Desertification is a process of land degradation in drylands caused by a combination of human activities (like overgrazing) and climate change, resulting in a loss of biological productivity.

78. **Question:** The B horizon in a soil profile is also known as the zone of...
    *   A) eluviation
    *   B) accumulation
    *   C) decomposition
    *   D) weathering
    *   **Answer:** B) accumulation
    *   **Explanation:** The B horizon (or subsoil) is the zone of illuviation, where materials like clay, iron oxides, and carbonates that have been leached from the upper horizons (A and E) accumulate.

79. **Question:** Organisms like bacteria and fungi that break down dead organic matter are known as...
    *   A) producers
    *   B) carnivores
    *   C) herbivores
    *   D) decomposers
    *   **Answer:** D) decomposers
    *   **Explanation:** Decomposers (or detritivores) play a crucial role in nutrient cycling by breaking down dead plants and animals and returning essential nutrients to the soil for producers to use.

80. **Question:** Primary succession would most likely occur on...
    *   A) an abandoned farm field
    *   B) a forest clear-cut
    *   C) a recently cooled lava flow
    *   D) a field after a fire
    *   **Answer:** C) a recently cooled lava flow
    *   **Explanation:** Primary succession occurs on brand new substrates where no soil or life existed previously, such as bare rock from a lava flow or land exposed by a retreating glacier. The other options are examples of secondary succession, where soil already exists.

81. **Question:** A soil with a pH value of 4.5 would be considered...
    *   A) acidic
    *   B) neutral
    *   C) alkaline
    *   D) saline
    *   **Answer:** A) acidic
    *   **Explanation:** A pH of 7 is neutral. Values below 7 are acidic, and values above 7 are alkaline (or basic). A pH of 4.5 is strongly acidic.

82. **Question:** Alluvial soils are transported and deposited by...
    *   A) wind
    *   B) glaciers
    *   C) rivers
    *   D) waves
    *   **Answer:** C) rivers
    *   **Explanation:** Alluvial soils are formed from alluvium, which is the sediment (silt, sand, clay) deposited by rivers in floodplains and deltas. They are typically very fertile.

83. **Question:** Fynbos is a unique shrubland biome with extremely high biodiversity found in...
    *   A) the Mediterranean basin
    *   B) California
    *   C) Southwest Australia
    *   D) the Western Cape of South Africa
    *   **Answer:** D) the Western Cape of South Africa
    *   **Explanation:** The Fynbos biome is part of the Cape Floral Kingdom, a world heritage site renowned for its exceptional diversity and high number of endemic plant species.

84. **Question:** In the context of an ecosystem, what flows in one direction and does not cycle?
    *   A) Water
    *   B) Carbon
    *   C) Nitrogen
    *   D) Energy
    *   **Answer:** D) Energy
    *   **Explanation:** Energy flows from the sun to producers, then to consumers, with a significant loss of heat at each trophic level. It does not cycle back to the sun. Nutrients like water, carbon, and nitrogen are continuously cycled within the ecosystem.

85. **Question:** Vertisols, or black cotton soils, are characterized by a high content of...
    *   A) sand and gravel
    *   B) organic matter and humus
    *   C) shrink-swell clays
    *   D) iron and aluminum oxides
    *   **Answer:** C) shrink-swell clays
    *   **Explanation:** Vertisols are rich in smectite clays that swell dramatically when wet and shrink when dry, forming deep cracks. This makes them difficult to work but they can be fertile.

86. **Question:** The well-lit, open surface water zone in a lake, away from the shore, is the...
    *   A) Littoral zone
    *   B) Limnetic zone
    *   C) Profundal zone
    *   D) Benthic zone
    *   **Answer:** B) Limnetic zone
    *   **Explanation:** The limnetic zone is the sunlit top layer of water where photosynthesis can occur, dominated by phytoplankton. The littoral zone is the shallow near-shore area.

87. **Question:** Which factor is NOT part of the CLORPT model for soil formation?
    *   A) Time
    *   B) Latitude
    *   C) Relief
    *   D) Climate
    *   **Answer:** B) Latitude
    *   **Explanation:** While latitude strongly influences climate and organisms, it is not one of the five primary, independent factors in the CLORPT model (Climate, Organisms, Relief, Parent Material, Time).

88. **Question:** Xerophytic plants are those adapted to live in...
    *   A) waterlogged conditions
    *   B) saline conditions
    *   C) dry conditions
    *   D) nutrient-poor conditions
    *   **Answer:** C) dry conditions
    *   **Explanation:** Xerophytes are plants with adaptations like deep roots, small waxy leaves, or water-storing stems (succulence) that allow them to survive in arid environments like deserts.

89. **Question:** The accumulation of salts in the upper layers of soil, often due to improper irrigation in arid regions, is called...
    *   A) Leaching
    *   B) Laterization
    *   C) Podsolization
    *   D) Salinization
    *   **Answer:** D) Salinization
    *   **Explanation:** Salinization occurs when irrigation water containing dissolved salts evaporates, leaving the salts behind in the soil, which can inhibit plant growth.

90. **Question:** Which of the following is an example of a decomposer?
    *   A) Grass
    *   B) Lion
    *   C) Fungus
    *   D) Deer
    *   **Answer:** C) Fungus
    *   **Explanation:** Fungi and bacteria are the primary decomposers in most ecosystems, breaking down dead organic matter and recycling nutrients.

#### **Part III: Human Geography (Chapters 6-7)**

91. **Question:** The number of people per unit of arable land is known as...
    *   A) Arithmetic density
    *   B) Agricultural density
    *   C) Physiological density
    *   D) Population density
    *   **Answer:** C) Physiological density
    *   **Explanation:** Physiological density gives a better indication of the pressure of population on the land resources available for food production than arithmetic density (total population / total land area).

92. **Question:** The Demographic Transition Model describes the shift from...
    *   A) rural to urban populations
    *   B) high birth/death rates to low birth/death rates
    *   C) subsistence to commercial agriculture
    *   D) primary to secondary economic activities
    *   **Answer:** B) high birth/death rates to low birth/death rates
    *   **Explanation:** The DTM shows the transition in population growth patterns as a country develops, starting with high birth and death rates (Stage 1) and moving to low birth and death rates (Stage 4).

93. **Question:** A population pyramid with a very wide base and narrow top indicates a...
    *   A) rapidly growing population
    *   B) stable population
    *   C) declining population
    *   D) aging population
    *   **Answer:** A) rapidly growing population
    *   **Explanation:** A wide base signifies a large proportion of young people and a high birth rate, characteristic of a rapidly expanding population (typical of Stage 2 or 3 of the DTM).

94. **Question:** Which of the following is a "pull" factor in rural-urban migration?
    *   A) Poverty in rural areas
    *   B) Land scarcity
    *   C) Better employment opportunities in cities
    *   D) Crop failure
    *   **Answer:** C) Better employment opportunities in cities
    *   **Explanation:** Pull factors are positive attributes that attract people to a destination. Push factors (like poverty and crop failure) are negative attributes that drive people away from their origin.

95. **Question:** A settlement pattern where houses are clustered together in a village with farmland surrounding it is described as...
    *   A) Dispersed
    *   B) Linear
    *   C) Nucleated
    *   D) Isolated
    *   **Answer:** C) Nucleated
    *   **Explanation:** Nucleated (or clustered) settlements often form for reasons of defense, social interaction, or access to a central water source.

96. **Question:** Economic activities that involve the extraction of raw materials, such as mining and farming, belong to the...
    *   A) Primary sector
    *   B) Secondary sector
    *   C) Tertiary sector
    *   D) Quaternary sector
    *   **Answer:** A) Primary sector
    *   **Explanation:** The primary sector is concerned with obtaining raw materials directly from the natural environment.

97. **Question:** The transformation of raw materials into finished goods, such as car manufacturing, is part of the...
    *   A) Primary sector
    *   B) Secondary sector
    *   C) Tertiary sector
    *   D) Quaternary sector
    *   **Answer:** B) Secondary sector
    *   **Explanation:** The secondary sector includes all manufacturing, processing, and construction activities that add value to raw materials.

98. **Question:** A farming system where crops are grown mainly for the consumption of the farmer's family is known as...
    *   A) Commercial farming
    *   B) Plantation agriculture
    *   C) Subsistence farming
    *   D) Mixed farming
    *   **Answer:** C) Subsistence farming
    *   **Explanation:** Subsistence agriculture is characterized by production for personal use, with little or no surplus for sale.

99. **Question:** According to Weber's industrial location theory, a weight-losing industry is most likely to locate near...
    *   A) the market
    *   B) the source of raw materials
    *   C) a source of cheap labor
    *   D) a major transport hub
    *   **Answer:** B) the source of raw materials
    *   **Explanation:** In a weight-losing industry (e.g., ore processing, sawmilling), the final product is lighter than the raw materials. Locating near the raw material source minimizes the transport cost of the heaviest components.

100. **Question:** The provision of services, such as banking, tourism, and healthcare, falls under which economic sector?
    *   A) Primary sector
    *   B) Secondary sector
    *   C) Tertiary sector
    *   D) Agricultural sector
    *   **Answer:** C) Tertiary sector
    *   **Explanation:** The tertiary sector is the service industry, which has become the dominant sector in most developed economies.

101. **Question:** The actual piece of land on which a settlement is built is its...
    *   A) Site
    *   B) Situation
    *   C) Location
    *   D) Pattern
    *   **Answer:** A) Site
    *   **Explanation:** Site refers to the physical characteristics of the immediate land where a settlement is located (e.g., on a hill, by a river crossing). Situation refers to its location relative to its surroundings.

102. **Question:** In which stage of the Demographic Transition Model does the death rate fall rapidly while the birth rate remains high, leading to a population explosion?
    *   A) Stage 1
    *   B) Stage 2
    *   C) Stage 3
    *   D) Stage 4
    *   **Answer:** B) Stage 2
    *   **Explanation:** In Stage 2, improvements in medicine, sanitation, and food supply cause a dramatic drop in death rates, but birth rates stay high due to cultural norms, resulting in a very high rate of natural increase.

103. **Question:** The increasing interdependence and integration of economies across the world is known as...
    *   A) Urbanization
    *   B) Industrialization
    *   C) Globalization
    *   D) Colonization
    *   **Answer:** C) Globalization
    *   **Explanation:** Economic globalization involves the growth of cross-border trade, capital flows, and the influence of multinational corporations, linking national economies into a global system.

104. **Question:** Which of the following is a characteristic of commercial agriculture?
    *   A) Production for family consumption
    *   B) Use of simple tools and high labor input
    *   C) Specialization in one or a few crops for profit
    *   D) Small, fragmented land holdings
    *   **Answer:** C) Specialization in one or a few crops for profit
    *   **Explanation:** Commercial farming is market-oriented and typically involves specialization (monoculture), high capital investment, and mechanization to maximize profit.

105. **Question:** A settlement pattern stretched along a road or river is described as...
    *   A) Nucleated
    *   B) Dispersed
    *   C) Linear
    *   D) Clustered
    *   **Answer:** C) Linear
    *   **Explanation:** Linear or ribbon settlements develop along a transport route or physical feature that provides an advantage for access or resources.

106. **Question:** The term "urbanization" refers to the...
    *   A) growth in the absolute number of people in cities
    *   B) physical spread of urban areas
    *   C) increase in the proportion of a country's population living in urban areas
    *   D) development of infrastructure within a city
    *   **Answer:** C) increase in the proportion of a country's population living in urban areas
    *   **Explanation:** Urbanization specifically refers to the demographic shift of a population from rural to urban, measured as the percentage of the total population residing in urban areas.

107. **Question:** The quaternary economic sector is primarily concerned with...
    *   A) manufacturing and construction
    *   B) retail and tourism
    *   C) farming and mining
    *   D) information, research, and technology
    *   **Answer:** D) information, research, and technology
    *   **Explanation:** The quaternary sector is the knowledge-based part of the economy, including activities like IT services, research and development (R&D), and higher education.

108. **Question:** Heavily populated urban areas with substandard housing and inadequate access to basic services are known as...
    *   A) suburbs
    *   B) central business districts
    *   C) slums or informal settlements
    *   D) industrial estates
    *   **Answer:** C) slums or informal settlements
    *   **Explanation:** Slums are a major challenge of rapid, unplanned urbanization in many developing countries, characterized by poverty, overcrowding, and lack of secure tenure or services.

109. **Question:** A country in Stage 5 of the Demographic Transition Model would likely experience...
    *   A) high birth rates and high death rates
    *   B) rapid population growth
    *   C) zero population growth
    *   D) a decline in its total population
    *   **Answer:** D) a decline in its total population
    *   **Explanation:** Stage 5 is a proposed stage where the birth rate falls below the death rate, leading to a negative rate of natural increase and population decline (e.g., Japan, Germany).

110. **Question:** The benefits gained by firms locating close to each other, such as sharing infrastructure and a skilled labor pool, are called...
    *   A) economies of scale
    *   B) agglomeration economies
    *   C) comparative advantage
    *   D) diseconomies of scale
    *   **Answer:** B) agglomeration economies
    *   **Explanation:** Agglomeration economies are external benefits that encourage industries to cluster together, reducing costs and fostering innovation.

111. **Question:** A dispersed settlement pattern is most commonly associated with...
    *   A) areas where defense is a primary concern
    *   B) intensive subsistence farming
    *   C) extensive commercial farming in regions like North America
    *   D) settlements around a scarce water source
    *   **Answer:** C) extensive commercial farming in regions like North America
    *   **Explanation:** In areas with large, individually owned farms, such as the Great Plains of the USA or Canadian Prairies, farmhouses are often scattered across the landscape, typical of a dispersed pattern.

112. **Question:** Which region of the world has the highest concentration of population?
    *   A) North America
    *   B) Sub-Saharan Africa
    *   C) East and South Asia
    *   D) Western Europe
    *   **Answer:** C) East and South Asia
    *   **Explanation:** The regions of East Asia (China, Japan, Korea) and South Asia (India, Pakistan, Bangladesh) are the two most densely populated macro-regions on Earth.

113. **Question:** Thomas Malthus argued that population tends to grow...
    *   A) arithmetically, while food supply grows exponentially
    *   B) exponentially, while food supply grows arithmetically
    *   C) at the same rate as food supply
    *   D) logistically, while food supply grows arithmetically
    *   **Answer:** B) exponentially, while food supply grows arithmetically
    *   **Explanation:** Malthus's core argument was that population, if unchecked, increases at a geometric (exponential) rate, while the food supply increases at an arithmetic rate, leading to eventual checks on population like famine and disease.

114. **Question:** Which of these is NOT a primary function of a modern city?
    *   A) Commercial and financial center
    *   B) Administrative and political center
    *   C) Center for subsistence agriculture
    *   D) Transportation and communication hub

121. **Question:** On a map with a scale of 1:50,000, a distance of 4 cm on the map represents what actual distance on the ground?
    *   A) 200 m

    *   **Answer:** B) 2 km

    *   B) a steep slope
    *   C) a flat plain
    *   D) a valley floor

    *   **Explanation:** Contour lines connect points of equal elevation. When they are close together, it means that elevation is changing rapidly over a short horizontal distance, indicating a steep slope.

    *   A) pixels

    *   A) Raster model

130. **Question:** The Global Positioning System (GPS) determines a receiver's location using a method called...
    *   A) Triangulation
    *   B) Trilateration
    *   C) Trajectory
    *   D) Transformation

    *   **Explanation:** A GPS receiver calculates its distance from multiple satellites and uses these distances to determine its precise position on Earth through a geometric process called trilateration (or more accurately, multilateration).

131. **Question:** When contour lines cross a river, they form a 'V' shape that points...
    *   A) downstream
    *   B) upstream
    *   C) towards the south
    *   D) towards the ocean
    *   **Answer:** B) upstream
    *   **Explanation:** A river flows in a valley, which is a low point. Contour lines must bend "uphill" to cross the valley floor, so the 'V' always points towards the higher elevation, which is upstream.

132. **Question:** A map projection that preserves shape locally but distorts area, such as the Mercator projection, is called...

    *   C) Equidistant
    *   D) Azimuthal
    *   **Answer:** B) Conformal
    *   **Explanation:** Conformal (or orthomorphic) projections maintain the correct shapes of small areas, making them useful for navigation. However, this is achieved at the expense of distorting the size of areas, especially near the poles.

    *   C) Network analysis

    *   **Explanation:** Buffering is a GIS process that creates a zone of a specified distance around a point, line, or polygon feature.

134. **Question:** The back bearing of 120° is...
    *   A) 060°
    *   B) 240°
    *   C) 300°
    *   D) 030°
    *   **Answer:** C) 300°
    *   **Explanation:** To find the back bearing, you add 180° to the forward bearing if it is less than 180°, or subtract 180° if it is more than 180°. So, 120° + 180° = 300°.

    *   A) magnetic bearing

    *   C) magnetic declination
    *   D) compass error
    *   **Answer:** C) magnetic declination
    *   **Explanation:** Magnetic declination (or variation) is the angle between magnetic north (the direction the north end of a compass needle points) and true north. This value is important for accurate navigation.

137. **Question:** An active remote sensing system, like RADAR, differs from a passive system because it...
    *   A) can only be used during the day
    *   B) only detects visible light
    *   C) emits its own energy and detects the reflection
    *   D) is mounted on aircraft instead of satellites
    *   **Answer:** C) emits its own energy and detects the reflection
    *   **Explanation:** Active sensors (like RADAR and LiDAR) send out their own signal (e.g., a microwave or laser pulse) and measure the return. Passive sensors (like cameras) simply detect natural energy, such as reflected sunlight.

138. **Question:** The Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI) is calculated using which two spectral bands to assess plant health?

    *   **Answer:** C) Red and Near-Infrared
    *   **Explanation:** Healthy vegetation strongly reflects near-infrared (NIR) light and absorbs red light. The NDVI uses the difference and sum of these two reflectances to create an index of vegetation vigor.

139. **Question:** Which of the following is an example of raster data?
