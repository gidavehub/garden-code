Which statement most accurately explains why the asthenosphere is crucial for plate tectonics?
a) Its liquid state allows for the generation of Earth's magnetic field.
b) It is rigid and brittle, allowing it to fracture and form plate boundaries.
c) Its high silica content makes it buoyant, pushing the plates upwards.
d) Its property of plasticity allows the overlying rigid lithospheric plates to move across it.

Answer: d) Its property of plasticity allows the overlying rigid lithospheric plates to move across it.
Explanation: The key to this question is understanding the physical states of Earth's layers. The asthenosphere is not liquid (that's the outer core - choice a), nor is it rigid and brittle (that's the lithosphere - choice b). Its composition is rich in iron and magnesium, not exceptionally high in silica (choice c). Its crucial feature, as described in section 1.1, is plasticity—behaving as a very viscous, deformable fluid over geological timescales—which facilitates the movement of the lithospheric plates.

b) Convection currents rising directly beneath a mid-ocean ridge.
c) The subduction of an oceanic plate beneath another plate at a convergent boundary.

Answer: d) Extensive cattle ranching.

Answer: d) Upstream, towards the river's source.

**1. The primary reason S-waves are not detected in a 'shadow zone' on the opposite side of the Earth from an earthquake's epicenter is that:**

Why is a continental volcanic arc (like the Cascades) associated with an oceanic-continental convergent boundary, but NOT a continental-continental convergent boundary (like the Himalayas)?
a) Continental-continental convergence is not powerful enough to generate magma.
b) Two colliding continents are both too buoyant to subduct, so there is no subducting slab to release water and trigger flux melting in the mantle wedge.
c) Continental-continental boundaries only produce shield volcanoes.
d) Magma is generated at both, but it cannot penetrate the thickened crust of the Himalayas.

Answer: b) Two colliding continents are both too buoyant to subduct, so there is no subducting slab to release water and trigger flux melting in the mantle wedge.

Explanation: Volcanic arc formation depends on magma generation via flux melting. This requires water from a subducting oceanic slab to lower the mantle's melting point. In a continental-continental collision, neither buoyant plate subducts. Without subduction, there is no water release to trigger melting, so no volcanic arc is formed.
