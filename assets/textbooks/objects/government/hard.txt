8. Which of the following constitutional arrangements is logically impossible?

B) A unitary, constitutional monarchy.

Answer: D) A rigid, unwritten constitution.

Explanation: The text defines a "rigid" constitution as one that is difficult to amend, requiring a special, complex procedure. This procedure must be formally laid out. Explanation: Disclosure laws are a key tool for promoting transparency. 27. The concept of "Negative Liberty" is most closely associated with:
A) The freedom to achieve one's potential, requiring state support
B) The freedom from external coercion and government interference

Answer: B) The freedom from external coercion and government interference
Explanation: Negative liberty is defined as "freedom from." It emphasizes the absence of obstacles, barriers, or constraints imposed by others, especially the state. 32. The principle of "Responsibility to Protect" (R2P) most directly creates a tension with which traditional concept of international law?

B) Balance of Power
C) Westphalian Sovereignty (non-intervention)
D) Collective Security

Answer: C) Westphalian Sovereignty (non-intervention)
Explanation: The core of Westphalian sovereignty is the principle of non-intervention in the internal affairs of other states. R2P posits that sovereignty is not absolute and that the international community has a right, and even a responsibility, to intervene (as a last resort, militarily) in a state that is failing to protect its population from mass atrocities. This is a direct challenge to the non-intervention norm.

B) Realism, because it acknowledges the primacy of great power politics and interests

Answer: B) Realism, because it acknowledges the primacy of great power politics and interests
Explanation: The veto power is the ultimate expression of Realist thinking within the UN system. It acknowledges that the organization's effectiveness in matters of peace and security depends on the consent of the world's most powerful states. It prioritizes great power interests over the liberal ideal of the legal equality of all states, essentially ensuring the UN cannot take enforcement action against a P5 member or its close allies.

46. The principle of subsidiarity in local governance suggests that:

50. The most significant difference between a specific purpose grant and a general purpose grant from a central government to a local government is the:

51. A state's constitution guarantees judicial independence but its legislature controls the judiciary's annual budget and can reduce it in response to unfavorable court rulings. This situation creates a fundamental weakness in which mechanism for judicial independence?
A) Security of Tenure

52. The rise of transnational terrorist groups who operate across borders and challenge state authority most directly complicates the Westphalian concept of:
A) A state's monopoly on the legitimate use of force within its territory
B) The legal equality of states in international law
C) The balance of power between nation-states
D) The role of diplomacy in conflict resolution

Answer: A) A state's monopoly on the legitimate use of force within its territory
Explanation: A core element of sovereignty, as articulated by Max Weber and central to the Westphalian state, is that the state is the only entity with the legitimate right to use violence/force within its borders. Transnational terrorist groups directly challenge this by using force themselves, operating outside the state's control and undermining its authority over its own territory.

53. Which of the following policy actions is the clearest example of a government pursuing "Positive Liberty" for its citizens?
A) Repealing a law that censored the press
B) Establishing a public university system with subsidized tuition
C) Reducing the powers of domestic intelligence agencies
D) Guaranteeing the right to a jury trial

Answer: B) Establishing a public university system with subsidized tuition
Explanation: Options A, C, and D are all examples of protecting negative liberty (freedom from interference, censorship, surveillance, unfair trial). Positive liberty is about "freedom to"—the capacity and opportunity to achieve one's potential. Providing education (B) is a classic example of the state taking action to empower individuals and give them the capacity to pursue their goals, which is the essence of positive liberty.

54. 56. The "iron triangle" is a political science concept describing a powerful, mutually beneficial relationship between which three actors in the policy-making process?
A) The President, the Speaker of the House, and the Chief Justice
B) A legislative committee, a bureaucratic agency, and an interest group
C) A political party, the media, and a foreign government
D) A state government, a local government, and a traditional authority

Answer: B) A legislative committee, a bureaucratic agency, and an interest group
Explanation: The "iron triangle" (or sub-government) describes a closed, stable, and powerful alliance focused on a specific policy area. The interest group provides electoral support and campaign contributions to the legislative committee, which in turn provides funding and favorable legislation for the bureaucratic agency. The agency then implements policies and regulations in a way that benefits the interest group, creating a self-reinforcing loop of power that can be difficult for outsiders to break.

57. The concept of "goal displacement" in a bureaucracy occurs when:
A) The bureaucracy successfully achieves its primary policy objective
B) The political leadership changes the goals of a policy mid-implementation
C) Adherence to rules and procedures becomes more important than achieving the organization's mission
D) A public agency is privatized and adopts profit-making as its new goal

Answer: C) Adherence to rules and procedures becomes more important than achieving the organization's mission
Explanation: Goal displacement is a common bureaucratic dysfunction. It describes a situation where the means (following the rules, filling out the forms) become the ends in themselves, and the original, substantive purpose of the organization's work (e.g., helping citizens, improving health) is lost or neglected.

58. A Prime Minister's threat to dissolve parliament and call a snap election is a tool to enforce discipline within their own party primarily because:

Explanation: This is a key power of a Prime Minister in many parliamentary systems. The threat of dissolution forces MPs in the governing party to weigh their rebellion against the risk and expense of an election campaign they might lose. This fear of losing their seat is a powerful incentive for them to fall in line and support the PM's agenda.

59. In a federal constitution, the "residual list" of powers refers to powers that are:
A) Exclusively held by the central government
B) Held concurrently by both central and state governments
C) Exclusively held by the state governments
D) Not explicitly assigned to either level, and are allocated to one by default

Answer: D) Not explicitly assigned to either level, and are allocated to one by default
Explanation: Federal constitutions typically have an "exclusive list" (for the center), a "concurrent list" (for both), and sometimes a "state list." The residual list refers to any subject matter not mentioned in these lists. 60. A government that increases public spending during an economic recession to stimulate demand is primarily employing:
A) Monetary policy
B) Fiscal policy
C) Trade policy
D) Regulatory policy

Answer: B) Fiscal policy
Explanation: Fiscal policy refers to the use of government spending and taxation to influence the economy. 61. The concept of a "security dilemma" in Realist IR theory describes a situation where:
A) Two allied states cannot agree on how to respond to a common threat
B) A state must choose between economic prosperity and military strength

62. Which of the following is the BEST example of a non-tariff barrier to trade?

63. The function of a political party known as "interest aggregation" involves:

Explanation: "Interest articulation" is simply expressing a demand. "Interest aggregation" is the more complex process of taking the many, often conflicting, demands of different social groups (e.g., farmers, urban workers, business owners, environmentalists) and bundling them into a broad, reasonably coherent set of policies or a party platform. This is a key function of large, successful political parties.

66. The "democratic peace theory," a central tenet of Liberal IR theory, posits that:

67. A servant-leader, when faced with a major organizational decision, is most likely to prioritize:

69. The main purpose of creating specialized courts, such as a Labour Court or an Environmental Tribunal, is to:

71. In a presidential system, the cabinet's primary role is to:

72. The concept that the international system is "anarchic" means that:
A) It is chaotic, violent, and without any rules
B) There is no overarching, legitimate world government with authority over sovereign states
C) Non-state actors have more power than nation-states
D) All states are moving towards a state of lawlessness

Answer: B) There is no overarching, legitimate world government with authority over sovereign states
Explanation: In IR theory, "anarchy" is a technical term. It does not mean chaos. It simply means the absence of a higher authority (a world government) to enforce rules, make laws, and resolve disputes. It is this structural condition of anarchy that realists, in particular, believe dictates the competitive nature of state behavior.

73. A key advantage of a bicameral legislature over a unicameral one is its ability to:
A) Pass laws more quickly and efficiently
B) Ensure the executive branch is always dominant
C) Provide for more thorough deliberation and a check on the power of the majority in the lower house
D) Reduce the overall cost of governance

Answer: C) Provide for more thorough deliberation and a check on the power of the majority in the lower house
Explanation: A primary justification for a second chamber (bicameralism) is that it provides a "house of review." It allows for legislation to be scrutinized more carefully and can prevent a single chamber, perhaps driven by popular passions, from passing hasty or poorly drafted laws. It adds a layer of checks and balances within the legislature itself. This deliberation process usually makes law-making slower, not faster (A).

74. The "resource curse" is a developmental theory suggesting that countries with abundant natural resources often experience:

69. The standard of proof in a civil law case ("preponderance of the evidence") is different from a criminal law case ("beyond a reasonable doubt") because:
A) Civil cases are more serious and require a higher burden of proof.
B) The potential penalty in a criminal case (e.g., imprisonment) is far more severe, thus requiring a higher degree of certainty.
C) Criminal cases are decided by judges, while civil cases are decided by juries.
D) Civil law is based on written codes, while criminal law is based on precedent.

Answer: B)
Explanation: Chapter 6.3 outlines these two standards. The reason for the much higher standard in criminal law is the gravity of the consequences. Civil cases, which typically result in monetary damages, use a lower, "more likely than not" standard.
